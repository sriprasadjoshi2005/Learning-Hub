# Electronics syllabus plan (50 lessons, foundation to university entrance-exam level)

**Phase 1: Electrical and electronic foundations (Lessons 1–10):** Introduction to electronics and its applications, fundamental electrical quantities and SI units, electric charge and current, voltage and electromotive force, resistance and conductance, Ohm’s law and electrical power, series and parallel circuits, Kirchhoff’s current and voltage laws, electrical energy and circuit measurements, and basic circuit analysis using equivalent resistance and network reduction.

**Phase 2: Circuit theory and network analysis (Lessons 11–20):** Electrical sources and source transformations, voltage and current dividers, mesh and nodal analysis, Thevenin’s theorem, Norton’s theorem and superposition, maximum power transfer theorem, capacitors and capacitance, inductors and inductance, transient response of RC and RL circuits, and sinusoidal alternating signals and their characteristics.

**Phase 3: Semiconductor devices and analog electronics (Lessons 21–30):** Semiconductor fundamentals and energy-band theory, intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors, PN junction diodes and their characteristics, diode applications including rectifiers and filters, Zener diodes and voltage regulation, bipolar junction transistors and their operating principles, transistor configurations and characteristics, transistor biasing and stabilization, transistor amplifiers and small-signal concepts, and field-effect transistors including JFETs and MOSFETs.

**Phase 4: Analog, digital and integrated electronics (Lessons 31–40):** Operational amplifiers and ideal op-amp characteristics, inverting and non-inverting amplifiers, summing amplifiers and comparator circuits, active filters and signal conditioning, oscillators and waveform generators, number systems and binary arithmetic, Boolean algebra and logic gates, combinational logic circuits including adders, subtractors, multiplexers and decoders, sequential logic circuits including latches and flip-flops, and counters, registers and memory fundamentals.

**Phase 5: Advanced electronics, systems and applications (Lessons 41–50):** Digital integrated circuits and logic families, microprocessors and microcontrollers, interfacing and peripheral devices, embedded systems and sensor integration, communication electronics and modulation techniques, power electronics including controlled rectifiers and DC-DC converters, feedback and control systems, electronic instrumentation and measurement systems, PCB design, circuit simulation, troubleshooting and practical electronics, and a comprehensive electronics capstone covering advanced topics and emerging technologies.

The sequence progresses from fundamental electrical quantities and circuit analysis to semiconductor physics, analog electronics, digital logic, integrated circuits, embedded systems, communication, power electronics and practical electronic engineering. It incorporates conceptual explanations, mathematical derivations, circuit diagrams, numerical problems, component characteristics, simulation methods, experimental procedures and real-world applications.

The final lesson consolidates important areas that cannot be explored individually within the 50-lesson structure, including CMOS and VLSI concepts, programmable logic devices, FPGA fundamentals, digital signal processing, power-management systems, optoelectronic devices such as LEDs and photodiodes, fibre-optic electronics, wireless and RF systems, advanced sensors and transducers, IoT hardware, robotics electronics, advanced PCB design, reliability engineering, electromagnetic compatibility and emerging semiconductor technologies. This ensures that the curriculum provides a broad introduction to both foundational and advanced electronics without exceeding the 50-lesson limit.

The examination-oriented content is designed to develop the mathematical, analytical and practical foundations needed for university-level electronics, electrical engineering and related entrance examinations. It also supports preparation for electronics-related topics in examinations such as JEE Main, JEE Advanced and CUET, where applicable. Check the latest official syllabus for the examination being attempted; this is a broad electronics learning pathway, not an official examination syllabus.
