## Learning objective

Master quadratic equations through factorisation, completing the square, the quadratic formula and the discriminant.

## Core concepts

- A quadratic equation is ax²+bx+c=0 with a≠0. Its roots are x=[−b±√(b²−4ac)]/(2a).
- The discriminant D=b²−4ac determines the nature of real roots: D>0 gives two distinct real roots; D=0 gives equal real roots; D<0 gives no real roots.
- For roots α and β, α+β=−b/a and αβ=c/a. These relations allow equations to be formed from known roots.
- The graph y=ax²+bx+c is a parabola. It opens upward for a>0 and downward for a<0. Its axis is x=−b/(2a).
- Completing the square rewrites a quadratic in vertex form and reveals its minimum or maximum.

## Essential formulae and results

x=[−b±√(b²−4ac)]/(2a); D=b²−4ac.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Solve x²−5x+6=0: (x−2)(x−3)=0, so x=2 or x=3.
**Example 2.** For x²+4x+1=0, x=[−4±√(16−4)]/2=−2±√3.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- The discriminant is b²−4ac, not 4ac−b².
- A quadratic may have complex roots when D<0; do not claim it has no roots over the complex numbers.
- The vertex x-coordinate is −b/(2a), not −b/a.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quiz

1. The discriminant of x²−4x+4=0 is:
- [ ] 16
- [ ] 8
- [x] 0
- [ ] −4
> D=(−4)²−4(1)(4)=16−16=0.

2. The roots of x²−7x+12=0 are:
- [ ] 2, 6
- [x] 3, 4
- [ ] −3, −4
- [ ] 1, 12
> Factorise as (x−3)(x−4)=0.

3. For y=−2x²+1, the parabola opens:
- [ ] Upward
- [x] Downward
- [ ] Sideways
- [ ] It is a line
> A negative coefficient of x² means the parabola opens downward.
