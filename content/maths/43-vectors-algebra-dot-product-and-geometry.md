## Learning objective

Represent directed quantities as vectors and use components, magnitude, unit vectors and scalar products.

## Core concepts

- A vector has magnitude and direction; a scalar has magnitude only. In 2D, a= a₁i+a₂j; in 3D, add a₃k.
- Magnitude is |a|=√(a₁²+a₂²+a₃²). A unit vector in the direction of non-zero a is a/|a|.
- Vector addition is component-wise. Scalar multiplication changes magnitude and may reverse direction if the scalar is negative.
- Dot product: a·b=a₁b₁+a₂b₂+a₃b₃=|a||b|cos θ. Perpendicular non-zero vectors have dot product zero.
- Projection of a onto b has scalar component (a·b)/|b|. The vector projection is [(a·b)/|b|²]b.

## Essential formulae and results

a·b=|a||b|cosθ; |a|=√(a₁²+a₂²+a₃²).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** For a=3i+4j, |a|=5 and its unit vector is (3/5)i+(4/5)j.
**Example 2.** For a=(1,2,0), b=(2,−1,0), a·b=2−2=0, so they are perpendicular.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- A vector's components can be negative; magnitude cannot.
- The dot product is a scalar, not a vector.
- The zero vector has no defined direction or unit vector.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.
