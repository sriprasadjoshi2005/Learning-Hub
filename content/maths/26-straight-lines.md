## Learning objective

Study line equations, slope, parallel and perpendicular conditions, and distances involving lines.

## Core concepts

- The slope through two distinct points is m=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁), when x₂≠x₁. A vertical line has undefined slope.
- Slope-intercept form is y=mx+c. Point-slope form is y−y₁=m(x−x₁). General form is Ax+By+C=0.
- Parallel non-vertical lines have equal slopes. Perpendicular non-vertical lines have m₁m₂=−1.
- The angle θ between lines with slopes m₁,m₂ satisfies tan θ=|(m₂−m₁)/(1+m₁m₂)| when the denominator is non-zero.
- Distance from (x₁,y₁) to Ax+By+C=0 is |Ax₁+By₁+C|/√(A²+B²).

## Essential formulae and results

Slope m=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁); point-line distance=|Ax₁+By₁+C|/√(A²+B²).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Through (2,3) with slope 4: y−3=4(x−2), so y=4x−5.
**Example 2.** The line 2x+3y−6=0 has intercepts (3,0) and (0,2).

## Common mistakes to avoid

- A vertical line has equation x=k, not y=k.
- For perpendicular lines, handle vertical/horizontal cases separately.
- Simplify coefficients before comparing general-form line equations.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quiz

1. The slope of y=−3x+7 is:
- [ ] 7
- [x] −3
- [ ] 3
- [ ] −7
> The coefficient of x in slope-intercept form is the slope.

2. A line perpendicular to slope 2 has slope:
- [ ] 2
- [ ] −2
- [ ] 1/2
- [x] −1/2
> The product of perpendicular slopes is −1.

3. Distance from (0,0) to 3x+4y−10=0 is:
- [x] 2
- [ ] 10
- [ ] 5
- [ ] 4
> Distance = |−10|/√(9+16)=10/5=2.
