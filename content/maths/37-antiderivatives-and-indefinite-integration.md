## Learning objective

Treat integration as reverse differentiation and learn standard antiderivatives and substitution.

## Core concepts

- An antiderivative F of f satisfies F'=f. The indefinite integral is ∫f(x)dx=F(x)+C, where C is an arbitrary constant.
- Power rule: ∫x^n dx=x^(n+1)/(n+1)+C for n≠−1. The exceptional case is ∫1/x dx=ln|x|+C.
- Standard forms include ∫e^x dx=e^x+C, ∫cos x dx=sin x+C and ∫sin x dx=−cos x+C.
- Substitution reverses the chain rule: choose u=g(x), compute du=g'(x)dx and rewrite the integral in u.
- Linearity permits constants to be factored out and sums to be integrated term by term.

## Essential formulae and results

∫x^n dx=x^(n+1)/(n+1)+C (n≠−1); ∫1/x dx=ln|x|+C.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** ∫(3x²−4x+2)dx=x³−2x²+2x+C.
**Example 2.** ∫2x cos(x²)dx: let u=x², du=2x dx; the integral is ∫cos u du=sin(x²)+C.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Always include +C in an indefinite integral.
- The power rule does not apply to x^−1.
- Check an antiderivative by differentiating it.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quiz

- [ ] 3x²+C
- [x] x⁴/4+C
- [ ] x⁴+C
- [ ] x²/2+C
> Increase the exponent by one and divide by the new exponent.

2. ∫1/x dx equals:
- [ ] x²/2+C
- [x] ln|x|+C
- [ ] 1/x²+C
- [ ] e^x+C
> This is the logarithmic antiderivative.

3. ∫cos x dx equals:
- [ ] −sin x+C
- [x] sin x+C
- [ ] −cos x+C
- [ ] tan x+C
> The derivative of sin x is cos x.
