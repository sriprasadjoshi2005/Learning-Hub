## Learning objective

Understand periodic trigonometric graphs and the restricted domains used to define inverse trigonometric functions.

## Core concepts

- The graph of sin x has range [−1,1] and period 2π; cos x has the same range and period. tan x has period π and vertical asymptotes at x=π/2+nπ.
- For y=a sin(bx+c), amplitude is |a| and period is 2π/|b|. For y=a cos(bx+c), the period is the same.
- Inverse trigonometric functions return principal values: sin⁻¹x∈[−π/2,π/2], cos⁻¹x∈[0,π], tan⁻¹x∈(−π/2,π/2).
- The notation sin⁻¹x means inverse sine, not 1/sin x; the reciprocal is cosec x.
- Use identities such as sin⁻¹x+cos⁻¹x=π/2 for x∈[−1,1].

## Essential formulae and results

Period of sin(bx) and cos(bx)=2π/|b|; period of tan(bx)=π/|b|.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** The period of y=sin 3x is 2π/3.
**Example 2.** sin⁻¹(1/2)=π/6, while cos⁻¹(1/2)=π/3 because the principal ranges differ.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Inverse trigonometric outputs are restricted principal values, not all angles with that ratio.
- Amplitude is |a|, never negative.
- Do not confuse inverse functions with reciprocal ratios.

## Quiz

- [ ] All real numbers
- [x] [−1,1]

- [ ] (−∞,1]
> Sine values lie between −1 and 1 inclusive.

2. The period of sin 4x is:
- [ ] 2π
- [ ] π
- [x] π/2
- [ ] π/4
> Period = 2π/4 = π/2.

3. tan⁻¹(1), principal value, is:
- [ ] 0
- [ ] π/6
- [x] π/4
- [ ] π/2
