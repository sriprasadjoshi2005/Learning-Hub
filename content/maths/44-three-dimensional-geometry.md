## Learning objective

Extend coordinate geometry to space using distance, direction ratios, lines and planes.

## Core concepts

- A point in 3D is (x,y,z). Distance between two points is √[(x₂−x₁)²+(y₂−y₁)²+(z₂−z₁)²].
- A line through point a with direction vector d has vector form r=a+λd. In symmetric form, (x−x₁)/l=(y−y₁)/m=(z−z₁)/n when direction ratios are non-zero.
- A plane has equation ax+by+cz+d=0, with normal vector (a,b,c).
- The angle between two planes is the angle between their normal vectors. The angle between a line and a plane is complementary to the angle between the line direction and plane normal.
- Distance from point (x₁,y₁,z₁) to plane ax+by+cz+d=0 is |ax₁+by₁+cz₁+d|/√(a²+b²+c²).

## Essential formulae and results

3D distance=√[(Δx)²+(Δy)²+(Δz)²]; point-plane distance=|ax₁+by₁+cz₁+d|/√(a²+b²+c²).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Distance between (1,2,3) and (4,6,3) is √(9+16)=5.
**Example 2.** Distance from (1,0,0) to x+2y+2z−3=0 is |1−3|/√9=2/3.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- The plane's normal is given by the coefficients of x,y,z.
- Do not confuse direction ratios with direction cosines; direction cosines have squared sum 1.
- Use absolute value in point-to-plane distance.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quiz

- [ ] 2
- [x] 3
- [ ] 5
- [ ] √5
> √(1+4+4)=3.

2. A normal vector to 2x−y+3z=4 is:
- [x] (2,−1,3)
- [ ] (4,0,0)
- [ ] (1,2,3)
- [ ] (2,1,−3)
> The plane coefficients give a normal vector.

3. The distance formula from a point to a plane has denominator:
- [ ] a+b+c
- [x] √(a²+b²+c²)
- [ ] a²+b²+c²
- [ ] 1
> It is the magnitude of the plane's normal vector.
