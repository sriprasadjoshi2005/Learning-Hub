## Learning objective

Recognise sequences, derive nth terms and calculate sums of arithmetic progressions.

## Core concepts

- A sequence is an ordered list of terms. An arithmetic progression (AP) has a constant common difference d between consecutive terms.
- The nth term of an AP is a_n=a+(n−1)d, where a is the first term.
- The sum of the first n terms is S_n=n/2[2a+(n−1)d]=n(a+l)/2, where l is the nth (last included) term.
- An AP can be increasing, decreasing or constant depending on whether d is positive, negative or zero.
- To insert k arithmetic means between two numbers, form an AP with k+2 terms and determine d from the endpoints.

## Essential formulae and results

AP: a_n=a+(n−1)d; S_n=n[2a+(n−1)d]/2.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** For 5, 8, 11, ..., a=5 and d=3. The 20th term is 5+19×3=62.
**Example 2.** The sum of the first 10 terms is 10/2[2(5)+9(3)]=5×37=185.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Use n−1 common differences to reach the nth term.
- The last term in a sum formula must be the actual nth term, not the next term.
- Check whether the question asks for a term or a sum.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.
