## Learning objective

Understand the hyperbola, its foci, asymptotes and eccentricity, and distinguish it from an ellipse.

## Core concepts

- A hyperbola is the locus for which the absolute difference of distances from two fixed foci is constant.
- For x²/a²−y²/b²=1, c²=a²+b², foci=(±c,0), eccentricity e=c/a>1 and asymptotes are y=±(b/a)x.
- For y²/a²−x²/b²=1, foci=(0,±c), and asymptotes are y=±(a/b)x.
- The transverse axis has length 2a; the conjugate axis has length 2b. The latus rectum length is 2b²/a.
- Unlike an ellipse, a hyperbola has two separate branches and eccentricity greater than one.

## Essential formulae and results

Hyperbola: c²=a²+b²; e=c/a; for horizontal transverse axis, asymptotes y=±(b/a)x.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** For x²/9−y²/16=1, a=3, b=4, c=5, foci=(±5,0), e=5/3, asymptotes y=±(4/3)x.
**Example 2.** For y²/16−x²/9=1, the transverse axis is vertical and asymptotes are y=±(4/3)x.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Use c²=a²+b² for a hyperbola and a²−b² for an ellipse.
- The positive term determines the transverse-axis direction.
- Do not confuse asymptotes with axes.

- [ ] 0<e<1
- [ ] e=1
- [x] e>1
- [ ] e=0
> A hyperbola has eccentricity greater than one.

3. The asymptotes of x²/a²−y²/b²=1 are:
- [ ] y=±(a/b)x
- [x] y=±(b/a)x
- [ ] x=±a
- [ ] y=±b
> Set the quadratic terms to balance: y=±(b/a)x.
