Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a branch of computer science concerned with creating systems that can perform tasks commonly associated with human intelligence. These tasks include learning, reasoning, problem-solving, understanding language, recognizing patterns, and making decisions.

AI systems use algorithms, data, mathematical models, and computational resources to produce intelligent behaviour. Some AI systems follow predefined rules, while others learn patterns from examples and improve their performance through experience.

## 1. Meaning and Definition of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence is the study and development of machines and software capable of performing tasks that normally require human intelligence.

Examples include:
- Voice assistants understanding spoken commands.
- Recommendation systems suggesting movies and music.
- Navigation systems selecting efficient routes.
- Computer vision systems identifying objects in images.
- Medical systems assisting with disease detection.
- Language models generating and interpreting text.

The objective of AI is not necessarily to reproduce the human brain. It is to develop systems capable of performing intelligent tasks effectively.

## 2. Characteristics of Artificial Intelligence

Important characteristics of AI systems include:

**Learning:** The ability to improve performance using data or experience.

**Reasoning:** The ability to draw conclusions from available information.

**Problem-solving:** The ability to identify solutions to complex problems.

**Perception:** The ability to interpret information from the environment, such as images, sounds, and sensor readings.

**Natural language processing:** The ability to understand, interpret, and generate human language.

**Planning:** The ability to determine a sequence of actions to achieve a particular objective.

**Decision-making:** The ability to select an action based on available information and objectives.

Not every AI system possesses all these characteristics.

## 3. Traditional Programming and Artificial Intelligence

Traditional programming generally relies on explicitly written instructions. AI systems may additionally learn patterns from data and use those patterns to make predictions or decisions.

| Traditional programming | AI-based approach |
|---|---|
| Primarily follows explicitly written rules | May learn patterns from data |
| Behaviour is largely determined by programmed instructions | Behaviour may change through training |
| Suitable for clearly defined procedures | Useful for complex pattern-based tasks |
| Often produces deterministic outputs | May produce probabilistic outputs |

Example:

A traditional spam filter might identify messages using manually defined rules, such as detecting specific words.

An AI-based spam filter can learn from previously classified messages and identify more complicated patterns associated with spam.

AI does not eliminate traditional programming. Many AI applications combine conventional software with learned models.

## 4. Major Areas of Artificial Intelligence

AI includes several important subfields.

**Machine Learning (ML):** Enables systems to learn patterns from data and make predictions or decisions.

**Deep Learning:** A branch of machine learning that uses neural networks containing multiple processing layers.

**Natural Language Processing (NLP):** Deals with understanding and generating human language.

**Computer Vision:** Enables machines to interpret visual information such as photographs and videos.

**Robotics:** Combines AI with mechanical systems, sensors, and control mechanisms to perform physical tasks.

**Expert Systems:** Use stored knowledge and reasoning rules to solve problems within a specific domain.

**Speech Recognition:** Converts spoken language into machine-readable representations.

**Planning and Search:** Finds sequences of actions that help an intelligent system achieve a goal.

## 5. Types of Artificial Intelligence Based on Capability

AI is commonly classified into three theoretical categories.

**Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI):**

Designed to perform specific tasks within a limited domain.

Examples:
- Facial recognition systems.
- Chess-playing programs.
- Recommendation algorithms.
- Voice assistants.

Most AI systems currently in practical use belong to this category.

**Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):**

A hypothetical form of AI capable of understanding, learning, and performing a broad range of intellectual tasks across different domains with generality comparable to human intelligence.

AGI has not been conclusively achieved.

**Artificial Superintelligence (ASI):**

A hypothetical form of intelligence that would substantially exceed human intellectual capabilities across many domains.

ASI remains a theoretical concept.

## 6. Types of AI Based on Functionality

Another theoretical classification considers how an AI system operates.

**Reactive Machines:** Respond to current inputs without storing memories of past experiences. A basic chess-playing system that evaluates the current board position is a common simplified example.

**Limited Memory AI:** Uses information from previous observations or stored data to make decisions. Many modern AI applications use this approach.

**Theory of Mind AI:** A proposed form of AI that would understand the beliefs, intentions, emotions, and mental states of others.

**Self-Aware AI:** A hypothetical form of AI possessing consciousness and awareness of its own existence.

Theory of Mind and Self-Aware AI are conceptual categories rather than established capabilities of present-day systems.

AI is used in many industries.

| Field | Application |
|---|---|

| Finance | Fraud detection and risk assessment |
| Transportation | Route optimization and autonomous driving research |

## 8. Advantages and Limitations of AI

**Advantages:**
- Automates repetitive tasks.
- Processes large amounts of information quickly.
- Identifies patterns that may be difficult for humans to detect.
- Provides consistent performance in appropriately designed tasks.
- Supports decision-making and scientific research.
- Operates continuously without ordinary human fatigue.

**Limitations:**
- Depends on the quality and suitability of its data.
- Can produce incorrect or misleading outputs.
- May inherit biases present in training data.
- Can require significant computing resources.
- Often lacks genuine human understanding.
- Raises concerns involving privacy, security, accountability, and employment.

## 9. Artificial Intelligence and Human Intelligence

AI and human intelligence differ in important ways.

| Artificial intelligence | Human intelligence |
|---|---|
| Implemented through computational systems | Arises from biological processes |
| Can process information at high speed | Often excels at contextual understanding |
| Learns through algorithms and data | Learns through experience and social interaction |
| Can perform repetitive calculations consistently | Can become tired or distracted |
| Does not necessarily possess consciousness | Associated with human conscious experience |

AI can outperform humans in specific tasks without possessing the full range of human intellectual abilities.

## Common Exam Traps

- Artificial Intelligence is broader than machine learning.
- Deep learning is a subset of machine learning.
- ANI is the dominant category of AI in practical applications.
- AGI and ASI are theoretical concepts, not established achievements.
- AI does not necessarily require human-like consciousness.
- A system producing intelligent behaviour does not automatically possess human understanding.
