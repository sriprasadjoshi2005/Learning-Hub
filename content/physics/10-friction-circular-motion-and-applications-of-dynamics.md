Friction and circular motion are important applications of Newton's laws of motion.

Friction is a contact force that opposes relative motion or the tendency of relative motion between surfaces. It allows objects to walk, vehicles to accelerate and brakes to stop moving wheels.

Circular motion occurs when an object moves along a circular path. Even when its speed remains constant, its velocity changes because its direction continuously changes.

This lesson develops the physics of friction, inclined planes, centripetal acceleration, circular forces, banking of roads and vertical circular motion.

## 1. Friction and Its Nature

Friction is a contact force that acts parallel to the surfaces in contact.

It opposes relative motion or the tendency of relative motion between the surfaces.

Friction arises from microscopic interactions between surfaces, including surface irregularities and molecular interactions.

Examples:

- Walking requires friction between shoes and the ground.
- Car tyres require friction to accelerate and brake.
- A book sliding across a table experiences friction.
- A rope can grip a surface because of friction.

Friction is not always undesirable. It is essential for many everyday activities.

## 2. Types of Friction

Friction can be classified into several types.

**Static friction:**

Acts between surfaces that are not sliding relative to each other.

It prevents the surfaces from beginning to slide.

**Limiting friction:**

The maximum value of static friction just before slipping begins.

**Kinetic or sliding friction:**

Acts when two surfaces slide relative to each other.

**Rolling resistance:**

Opposes the rolling motion of an object. It is generally much smaller than sliding friction for suitable wheels and surfaces.

## 3. Laws of Dry Friction

For many idealised dry-friction problems, the following approximate relationships are used.

### Static friction

Static friction adjusts according to the applied force, up to a maximum value.

\[
0\leq f_s\leq\mu_sN
\]

Where:

- \(f_s\) = static friction.
- \(\mu_s\) = coefficient of static friction.
- N = normal reaction.

The limiting value is:

\[
f_{s,max}=\mu_sN
\]

### Kinetic friction

When sliding occurs:

\[
f_k=\mu_kN
\]

Where:

- \(f_k\) = kinetic friction.
- \(\mu_k\) = coefficient of kinetic friction.

For many common dry surfaces:

\[
\mu_k<\mu_s
\]

The coefficients are dimensionless.

### Important distinction

Static friction is not always equal to \(\mu_sN\).

It takes the value required to prevent slipping, up to its limiting value.

Kinetic friction is approximately \(\mu_kN\) under the standard model.

## 4. Factors Affecting Friction

In the elementary dry-friction model, friction depends primarily on:

- The nature of the contacting surfaces.
- The normal reaction.
- The condition of the surfaces.

The coefficient of friction represents the frictional characteristics of the surface pair.

Real friction may also depend on speed, temperature, lubrication, deformation and surface conditions.

The simple Coulomb friction model is an approximation, not a universal description of every contact.

## 5. Friction on a Horizontal Surface

Consider a block of mass m resting on a horizontal surface.

Its weight is:

\[
W=mg
\]

If no vertical forces other than weight and normal reaction act:

\[
N=mg
\]

Therefore, maximum static friction is:

\[
f_{s,max}=\mu_smg
\]

Kinetic friction is:

\[
f_k=\mu_kmg
\]

### Example

A 10 kg block rests on a horizontal surface with coefficient of static friction 0.4.

Take g = 10 m/s².

Normal reaction:

\[
N=mg=100\ \text{N}
\]

Maximum static friction:

\[
f_{s,max}=0.4(100)
\]

\[
f_{s,max}=40\ \text{N}
\]

An applied horizontal force of 20 N will not move the block because static friction can balance it.

An applied force greater than 40 N can initiate slipping under the ideal model.

## 6. Friction on an Inclined Plane

Consider a block resting on an inclined plane at angle θ.

The weight acts vertically downward.

Its components are:

Parallel to the plane:

\[
mg\sin\theta
\]

Perpendicular to the plane:

\[
mg\cos\theta
\]

The normal reaction is:

\[
N=mg\cos\theta
\]

if there is no acceleration perpendicular to the plane.

Maximum static friction:

\[
f_{s,max}=\mu_smg\cos\theta
\]

The block begins to slide downward when:

\[
mg\sin\theta>\mu_smg\cos\theta
\]

At the limiting condition:

\[
mg\sin\theta=\mu_smg\cos\theta
\]

Therefore:

\[
\tan\theta=\mu_s
\]

The angle satisfying this condition is called the angle of repose.

\[
\boxed{\tan\theta_r=\mu_s}
\]

## 7. Motion on an Inclined Plane with Friction

Suppose a block slides down an inclined plane.

The component of weight down the plane is:

\[
mg\sin\theta
\]

Kinetic friction acts upward:

\[
f_k=\mu_kmg\cos\theta
\]

Applying Newton's second law along the plane:

\[
mg\sin\theta-\mu_kmg\cos\theta=ma
\]

Dividing by m:

\[
a=g(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta)
\]

This expression assumes the block slides downward and the only forces along the plane are gravity and kinetic friction.

## 8. Circular Motion

Circular motion occurs when an object follows a circular path.

Examples include:

- A stone tied to a string and rotated.
- A car moving around a circular track.
- A rotating fan.
- A satellite moving in an approximately circular orbit.

Circular motion can be uniform or non-uniform.

**Uniform circular motion:**

The speed remains constant, but velocity changes continuously because its direction changes.

**Non-uniform circular motion:**

Both speed and direction may change.

## 9. Angular Displacement and Angular Velocity

Angular displacement describes the angle through which an object rotates.

It is commonly measured in radians.

One complete revolution corresponds to:

\[
2\pi\ \text{radians}
\]

Angular velocity is the rate of change of angular displacement.

\[
\omega=\frac{d\theta}{dt}
\]

For uniform circular motion:

\[
\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}
\]

Where T is the time period.

Since:

\[
f=\frac{1}{T}
\]

We also have:

\[
\omega=2\pi f
\]

SI unit:

\[
\text{rad/s}
\]

## 10. Relationship Between Linear and Angular Quantities

For an object moving along a circle of radius r:

Arc length:

\[
s=r\theta
\]

Linear speed:

\[
v=r\omega
\]

For angular acceleration:

\[
\alpha=\frac{d\omega}{dt}
\]

Tangential acceleration:

\[
a_t=r\alpha
\]

These relationships connect rotational motion with ordinary linear motion.

## 11. Centripetal Acceleration

An object moving in a circular path experiences acceleration directed towards the centre of the circle.

This is called centripetal acceleration.

Its magnitude is:

\[
a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}
\]

Using:

\[
v=r\omega
\]

We obtain:

\[
a_c=r\omega^2
\]

The direction is always towards the centre for ideal circular motion.

Even when speed is constant, the direction of velocity changes, so acceleration is non-zero.

## 12. Centripetal Force

Centripetal force is the net inward force required to produce centripetal acceleration.

\[
F_c=ma_c
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{F_c=\frac{mv^2}{r}}
\]

Or:

\[
F_c=mr\omega^2
\]

Centripetal force is not a separate fundamental force.

It is the name given to the net inward force resulting from actual forces such as:

- Tension.
- Gravitational force.
- Friction.
- Normal reaction.
- Their appropriate combinations.

### Example

A 2 kg object moves in a circle of radius 4 m at a speed of 6 m/s.

\[
F_c=\frac{mv^2}{r}
\]

\[
F_c=\frac{2(36)}{4}
\]

\[
F_c=18\ \text{N}
\]

The net inward force is 18 N.

## 13. Banking of Roads

Roads are sometimes banked at curves so that the outer edge is raised relative to the inner edge.

Banking allows a component of the normal reaction to provide the required centripetal force.

For an ideal frictionless banked road:

\[
N\cos\theta=mg
\]

\[
N\sin\theta=\frac{mv^2}{r}
\]

Dividing:

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{v^2}{rg}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{v=\sqrt{rg\tan\theta}}
\]

This is the ideal design speed for a given banking angle without requiring friction.

## 14. Circular Motion on a Flat Road

For a vehicle travelling around a flat circular road, static friction between the tyres and road can provide the centripetal force.

At the limiting condition:

\[
f_{s,max}=\mu_sN
\]

For a horizontal road:

\[
N=mg
\]

Therefore:

\[
\mu_smg=\frac{mv^2}{r}
\]

Cancelling mass:

\[
v_{max}=\sqrt{\mu_srg}
\]

This is the maximum speed for idealised flat-road circular motion before slipping begins.

## 15. Vertical Circular Motion

Vertical circular motion occurs when an object moves along a circular path in a vertical plane.

Examples include:

- A ball attached to a string and rotated vertically.
- A roller coaster moving through a vertical loop.
- A bucket of water rotated in a vertical circle.

The gravitational force changes its contribution to the radial force at different positions.

### At the top

For a body attached to a string:

\[
T+mg=\frac{mv^2}{r}
\]

Therefore:

\[
T=\frac{mv^2}{r}-mg
\]

### At the bottom

\[
T-mg=\frac{mv^2}{r}
\]

Therefore:

\[
T=\frac{mv^2}{r}+mg
\]

The tension is generally greater at the bottom than at the top for a body undergoing a complete vertical circle under gravity.

### Minimum speed at the top

For a string to remain just taut at the top:

\[
T=0
\]

Therefore:

\[
mg=\frac{mv_{top}^2}{r}
\]

\[
\boxed{v_{top,min}=\sqrt{gr}}
\]

This is the minimum speed at the top for the limiting case of a complete vertical circle with a flexible string.

## 16. Non-Inertial Reference Frames and Centrifugal Force

In a rotating reference frame, a fictitious force called centrifugal force is introduced to describe motion using Newton's laws in that frame.

Its magnitude is:

\[
F_{cf}=m\omega^2r
\]

Its direction is radially outward from the axis of rotation.

Centrifugal force is not an additional real interaction in an inertial frame.

For example, a passenger in a turning vehicle may feel pushed outward. In an inertial frame, the passenger's inertia resists the inward change in direction. In the rotating vehicle frame, this can be represented using an outward centrifugal pseudo force.

## 17. Solved Numerical Problems

### Example 1: Friction

A 5 kg block rests on a horizontal surface with coefficient of kinetic friction 0.2. Take g = 10 m/s².

Find kinetic friction.

Normal reaction:

\[
N=mg=50\ \text{N}
\]

Friction:

\[
f_k=\mu_kN
\]

\[
f_k=0.2(50)
\]

\[
f_k=10\ \text{N}
\]

### Example 2: Centripetal acceleration

A body moves in a circle of radius 2 m with speed 4 m/s.

\[
a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}
\]

\[
a_c=\frac{16}{2}
\]

\[
a_c=8\ \text{m/s}^2
\]

### Example 3: Banking angle

A vehicle travels at 10 m/s around a curve of radius 20 m. Take g = 10 m/s².

For ideal banking:

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{v^2}{rg}
\]

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{100}{20(10)}
\]

\[
\tan\theta=0.5
\]

Therefore:

\[
\theta=\tan^{-1}(0.5)
\]

\[
\theta\approx26.6^\circ
\]

## 18. Integrated Problem-Solving Strategy

When solving a friction or circular-motion problem:

1. Identify the object or system.
2. Draw a free-body diagram.
3. Choose suitable coordinate axes.
4. Identify the direction of acceleration.
5. Resolve forces into components.
6. Apply Newton's second law.
7. Use the correct friction model or centripetal acceleration relationship.
8. Solve algebraically.
9. Check units and signs.
10. Verify that the result is physically possible.

For circular motion, always identify which actual force or combination of forces provides the required inward resultant force.

## Common Exam Traps

- Static friction is self-adjusting and is not always equal to its maximum value.
- Kinetic friction acts during sliding.
- The normal reaction on an inclined plane is generally mg cos(theta), not mg.
- Friction opposes relative slipping or its tendency, not necessarily the overall direction of motion.
- Centripetal force is the net inward force, not an additional independent force.
- Uniform circular motion has non-zero acceleration even when speed is constant.
- Centripetal acceleration is directed towards the centre.
- Centrifugal force is a pseudo force used in a rotating reference frame.
- The ideal banking formula assumes no friction is needed at the design speed.
- The minimum-speed condition at the top of a vertical circle depends on the constraint; the string must remain taut in the limiting case.

## Quiz

1. A 10 kg block rests on a horizontal surface with coefficient of static friction 0.3. Take g = 10 m/s². What is the maximum static friction?

- [ ] 10 N
- [ ] 20 N
- [x] 30 N
- [ ] 100 N

> Maximum static friction = μsN. Since N = mg = 100 N, the maximum friction is 0.3 × 100 = 30 N.

2. A body moves at 5 m/s in a circle of radius 5 m. What is its centripetal acceleration?

- [ ] 1 m/s²
- [x] 5 m/s²
- [ ] 10 m/s²
- [ ] 25 m/s²

> Centripetal acceleration = v²/r = 25/5 = 5 m/s², directed towards the centre.

3. What provides the centripetal force for a vehicle moving around an ideal flat circular road without skidding?

- [ ] Gravitational force alone
- [ ] Normal reaction alone
- [x] Static friction between the tyres and road
- [ ] Centrifugal force

> Static friction provides the inward net force required for circular motion on an ideal flat road.
