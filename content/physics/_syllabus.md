# Physics syllabus plan (50 lessons, foundation to university entrance-exam level)

**Phase 1: Foundations and classical mechanics (Lessons 1–12):** Introduction to physics and the nature of physical laws, physical quantities and SI units, dimensional analysis, measurement and experimental errors, significant figures, scalars and vectors, mathematical tools for physics, motion in one dimension, motion in two dimensions and projectile motion, relative motion and motion graphs, Newton’s laws of motion and free-body diagrams, friction and circular motion, work, energy and power, and conservation of mechanical energy.

**Phase 2: Mechanics, properties of matter and thermal physics (Lessons 13–24):** Linear momentum and impulse, collisions and centre of mass, rotational motion and torque, angular momentum and rotational dynamics, gravitation and satellite motion, elasticity and mechanical properties of solids, fluid mechanics and mechanical properties of fluids, temperature and thermal expansion, heat transfer and calorimetry, thermodynamics and the laws of thermodynamics, kinetic theory of gases, and oscillations and simple harmonic motion.

**Phase 3: Waves, electricity and magnetism (Lessons 25–33):** Wave motion and sound, superposition of waves and standing waves, electrostatics and electric charge, electric fields and Gauss’s law, electric potential and capacitance, current electricity and electrical circuits, magnetic fields and magnetic forces, magnetic fields due to electric currents and electromagnetic induction, alternating current and AC circuits, transformers and electrical power, and electromagnetic waves and the electromagnetic spectrum.

**Phase 4: Optics and modern physics (Lessons 34–45):** Geometrical optics including reflection and refraction, spherical mirrors and thin lenses, optical instruments and human vision, wave optics including Huygens’ principle and interference, diffraction and polarisation, quantum physics and the photoelectric effect, atomic models and atomic spectra, nuclear physics and radioactivity, nuclear reactions including fission and fusion, semiconductor physics and diodes, transistors and digital electronics, and communication systems and signal transmission.

**Phase 5: Advanced applications, practical physics and examination mastery (Lessons 46–50):** Special relativity and modern physics concepts, applications of physics in medicine, environmental science and space exploration, experimental physics and practical skills including measurements and data analysis, integrated problem-solving and university entrance-examination strategies, and a comprehensive physics capstone covering advanced topics and important remaining areas of physics.

The sequence progresses from fundamental measurements, mathematical reasoning and classical mechanics to thermal physics, waves, electricity, magnetism, optics and modern physics. It incorporates conceptual explanations, mathematical derivations, numerical problems, dimensional analysis, graphs, experimental methods and real-world applications.

The final lesson consolidates important areas that cannot be explored individually within the 50-lesson structure, including conceptual foundations of Maxwell’s equations, advanced electromagnetism, plasma physics, condensed-matter physics, superconductivity, particle physics, quantum mechanics, astrophysics and cosmology. This ensures that the curriculum provides a broad introduction to both classical and modern physics without exceeding the 50-lesson limit.

The examination-oriented content is designed to build a foundation for university entrance examinations such as JEE Main, JEE Advanced and CUET, alongside other physics-based admission tests. Check the latest official syllabus for the examination being attempted; this is a broad physics learning pathway, not an official examination syllabus.
