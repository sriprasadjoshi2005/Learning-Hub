# Lesson 50: Full Mathematics Mock Test and Revision Roadmap

## Learning objective

Consolidate the complete learning path with a mixed diagnostic test, a revision method and a realistic progression toward board and entrance examinations.

## Core concepts

- A balanced revision cycle revisits foundations, algebra, trigonometry, coordinate geometry, calculus, vectors/3D, probability and statistics.
- Use three passes: first learn definitions and standard results; second solve worked examples without looking; third attempt timed mixed questions.
- Maintain an error log with categories: concept gap, formula recall, algebraic slip, misread condition and time management. Re-solve each missed question after a delay.
- For board-style preparation, practise complete written solutions, diagrams, derivations and clear steps. For JEE Main/state entrance preparation, practise timed objective questions and multi-concept applications.
- Do not skip prerequisites. If a calculus problem fails because of algebra or trigonometry, repair that foundation before repeating the calculus problem.

## Essential formulae and results

Use an error log: concept gap, formula recall, algebra slip, misread condition, or time management.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** A student scores 18/30 on a diagnostic test. The score alone is not a study plan: classify the 12 errors, identify the largest recurring category and schedule targeted practice.
**Example 2.** For a 60-minute practice set of 30 questions, the average available time is 2 minutes per question. Allocate more time only when the question's expected value justifies it, and return to skipped questions.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Do not revise only favourite chapters; use a syllabus checklist and error log.
- Avoid memorising a formula without knowing its variables, domain and conditions.
- A mock score is diagnostic information, not a prediction or a measure of fixed ability.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Revision checklist

- [ ] I can explain the main definitions without looking at the notes.
- [ ] I can reproduce the essential formulae and state their conditions.
- [ ] I can solve the worked examples independently.
- [ ] I can explain why each incorrect quiz option is wrong.
- [ ] I can solve a fresh board-level question and an entrance-style question on this topic.
