# Lesson 40: Area Under Curves

## Learning objective

Use definite integrals to find geometric areas between curves and the coordinate axes.

## Core concepts

- If y=f(x)≥0 on [a,b], area under the curve and above the x-axis is ∫_a^b f(x)dx.
- If the curve crosses the x-axis, split at each intersection or integrate |f(x)| to obtain total geometric area.
- The area between upper curve y=f(x) and lower curve y=g(x) is ∫_a^b [f(x)−g(x)]dx, provided f≥g on the interval.
- Alternatively, integrate with respect to y using rightmost minus leftmost curve when horizontal slices are simpler.
- Find intersection points first; they usually determine the integration limits.

## Essential formulae and results

Area between upper f and lower g: ∫_a^b[f(x)−g(x)]dx.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Area under y=x from x=0 to 2 is ∫_0^2 x dx=2.
**Example 2.** Between y=2x and y=x² on [0,2], area=∫_0^2(2x−x²)dx=[x²−x³/3]_0^2=4/3.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Choose upper minus lower, not the reverse.
- A signed integral may be negative even though geometric area is non-negative.
- Check which curve is above over the entire interval.

## Quick quiz

**1. Area under y=3 from x=0 to 2 is:**
- [ ] 3
- [ ] 5
- [x] 6
- [ ] 9
> **Explanation:** The rectangle has width 2 and height 3, so area is 6.

**2. For area between two curves using vertical slices, integrate:**
- [ ] Lower minus upper
- [x] Upper minus lower
- [ ] Their product
- [ ] Their derivatives
> **Explanation:** The vertical height is upper y minus lower y.

**3. If a curve crosses the x-axis, total area is found by:**
- [ ] One signed integral only
- [x] Splitting at crossings or integrating absolute value
- [ ] Differentiating
- [ ] Ignoring negative parts
> **Explanation:** Geometric area counts all regions positively.
