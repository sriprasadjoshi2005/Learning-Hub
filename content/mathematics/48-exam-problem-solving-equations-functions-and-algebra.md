# Lesson 48: Exam Problem-Solving: Equations, Functions and Algebra

## Learning objective

Combine algebraic tools in multi-step problems and practise efficient, verifiable methods appropriate to board and entrance examinations.

## Core concepts

- Start by identifying the mathematical structure: equation, inequality, function, sequence, polynomial or counting problem.
- Use substitutions to simplify repeated expressions. For reciprocal-symmetric expressions, consider t=x+1/x when x≠0; record the allowed range of t for real x.
- For parameter questions, use discriminants, root relations, domains and sign analysis rather than testing random values.
- For function equations, check domains before composing or inverting. For inequalities, preserve sign conditions when multiplying by expressions of unknown sign.
- A strong exam solution is concise but justified: state the method, show decisive steps and verify restrictions or extraneous roots.

## Essential formulae and results

For ax²+bx+c=0 to have real roots, b²−4ac≥0.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** If x+1/x=3 with x≠0, then x²−3x+1=0. Its real roots are (3±√5)/2; both are positive.
**Example 2.** If x²−5x+k=0 has real roots, its discriminant must satisfy 25−4k≥0, so k≤25/4.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Do not divide by a variable expression without checking whether it can be zero.
- Squaring both sides may create extraneous roots; substitute candidates into the original equation.
- Parameter restrictions often come from discriminants and domains.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quick quiz

**1. For x+1/x with real x≠0, the expression can never be:**
- [ ] 2
- [ ] 3
- [ ] −2
- [x] 0
> **Explanation:** By AM-GM for positive x, or by x+1/x=0 giving x²+1=0, zero is impossible over the reals.

**2. The quadratic x²−4x+k=0 has real roots when:**
- [ ] k>4
- [x] k≤4
- [ ] k<−4
- [ ] k≥4
> **Explanation:** D=16−4k≥0 gives k≤4.

**3. If f is one-one, f(a)=f(b) implies:**
- [ ] a≠b
- [x] a=b
- [ ] a=0
- [ ] b=0
> **Explanation:** This is the defining implication for injectivity.
