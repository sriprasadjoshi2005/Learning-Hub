# Lesson 17: Complex Numbers

## Learning objective

Extend the real number system using i, and learn algebra, conjugates, modulus and the Argand plane.

## Core concepts

- The imaginary unit satisfies i²=−1. A complex number is z=a+ib, where a,b are real; a is the real part and b is the imaginary part.
- Powers of i repeat every four: i, −1, −i, 1. Reduce exponents modulo 4.
- The conjugate is z̄=a−ib. The modulus is |z|=√(a²+b²), and z z̄=|z|².
- Division is simplified by multiplying numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.
- On the Argand plane, a+ib corresponds to point (a,b). The modulus is its distance from the origin.

## Essential formulae and results

z=a+ib; z̄=a−ib; |z|=√(a²+b²); i²=−1.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** (3+2i)+(1−5i)=4−3i.
**Example 2.** (2+i)/(1−i): multiply by (1+i), giving (2+i)(1+i)/2=(1+3i)/2.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Remember i²=−1, not i.
- The conjugate changes the sign of the imaginary part only.
- The modulus is non-negative and is zero only for z=0.

## Quick quiz

**1. i^6 equals:**
- [ ] 1
- [x] −1
- [ ] i
- [ ] −i
> **Explanation:** i^6=i^4×i²=1×(−1)=−1.

**2. The conjugate of 4−7i is:**
- [ ] −4+7i
- [x] 4+7i
- [ ] −4−7i
- [ ] 7+4i
> **Explanation:** Change only the sign of the imaginary part.

**3. The modulus of 3+4i is:**
- [ ] 7
- [ ] 1
- [x] 5
- [ ] 25
> **Explanation:** √(3²+4²)=5.
