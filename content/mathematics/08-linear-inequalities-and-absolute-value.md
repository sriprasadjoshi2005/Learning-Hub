# Lesson 8: Linear Inequalities and Absolute Value

## Learning objective

Extend equation-solving methods to inequalities and understand distance from zero through absolute value.

## Core concepts

- An inequality compares values using <, >, ≤ or ≥. Adding or subtracting the same number preserves its direction.
- Multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.
- The solution of 2x−3<7 is x<5. On a number line, an open endpoint represents a strict inequality.
- |x| is the distance of x from zero: |x|=x if x≥0 and |x|=−x if x<0.
- For a>0, |x|<a means −a<x<a, while |x|>a means x<−a or x>a. Equality variants include endpoints.

## Essential formulae and results

Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number reverses its direction.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Solve −3x≥12. Divide by −3 and reverse the sign: x≤−4.
**Example 2.** Solve |x−2|≤3: −3≤x−2≤3, hence −1≤x≤5.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Forgetting to reverse the sign when dividing by a negative number.
- |x|=−x is valid only when x≤0.
- A compound 'and' inequality gives an intersection, not a union.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.

## Quick quiz

**1. Solve −2x<8.**
- [ ] x<−4
- [x] x>−4
- [ ] x<4
- [ ] x>4
> **Explanation:** Divide by −2 and reverse the inequality.

**2. The solution of |x|<3 is:**
- [ ] x<3
- [ ] x>−3
- [x] −3<x<3
- [ ] x<−3 or x>3
> **Explanation:** Distance from zero less than 3 gives the open interval.

**3. Solve 5−x≤2.**
- [ ] x≤3
- [x] x≥3
- [ ] x≤−3
- [ ] x≥−3
> **Explanation:** Subtract 5: −x≤−3; multiply by −1 and reverse.
