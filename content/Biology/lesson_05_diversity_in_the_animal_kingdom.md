## title: Diversity in the Animal Kingdom
level: foundation

Kingdom Animalia encompasses multicellular, eukaryotic, heterotrophic organisms that lack cell walls. Animals are classified into major phyla based on fundamental body plans, tissue organisation, germ layers, body cavities, and developmental patterns.

## Levels of Organisation and Body Symmetry

Animals are broadly categorised by their structural complexity and spatial symmetry:

1. **Levels of Organisation**: 
   - *Cellular level*: Loose cell aggregates with minimal functional division of labour (Phylum Porifera).
   - *Tissue level*: Cells performing the same function are arranged into tissues (Phylum Coelenterata / Ctenophora).
   - *Organ / Organ-system level*: Tissues aggregate into distinct organs and functional organ systems (Phylum Platyhelminthes to Chordata).

2. **Germ Layers**:
   - *Diploblastic*: Two embryonic layers: outer **ectoderm** and inner **endoderm** separated by non-cellular **mesoglea** (e.g., Cnidaria, Ctenophora).
   - *Triploblastic*: Three embryonic layers: **ectoderm**, **mesoderm**, and **endoderm** (e.g., Platyhelminthes to Chordata).

3. **Body Cavity (Coelom)**:

$$
\text{Blastocoel} \xrightarrow{\text{Mesodermal Lining}} \begin{cases} \text{Acoelomate} & \text{(No body cavity: Platyhelminthes)} \\ \text{Pseudocoelomate} & \text{(Scattered mesodermal pouches: Aschelminthes)} \\ \text{Coelomate / Eucoelomate} & \text{(True coelom lined by mesoderm: Annelida to Chordata)} \end{cases}
$$

## Overview of Major Animal Phyla

| Phylum | Symmetry | Coelom Status | Distinctive Features | Representative Examples |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Porifera** | Asymmetrical | Acoelomate | Ostia/osculum pore system, choanocytes (collar cells), spicules | *Sycon*, *Spongilla*, *Euspongia* |
| **Cnidaria (Coelenterata)** | Radial | Acoelomate | Cnidocytes (stinging cells), polyp and medusa body forms | *Hydra*, *Aurelia*, *Physalia* |
| **Ctenophora** | Radial | Acoelomate | Eight rows of ciliated comb plates, bioluminescence | *Pleurobrachia*, *Ctenoplana* |
| **Platyhelminthes** | Bilateral | Acoelomate | Dorso-ventrally flattened body, flame cells for excretion | *Taenia*, *Fasciola*, *Planaria* |
| **Aschelminthes (Nematoda)** | Bilateral | Pseudocoelomate | Circular cross-section, complete digestive tract | *Ascaris*, *Wuchereria*, *Ancylostoma* |
| **Annelida** | Bilateral | Coelomate | Metameric segmentation, nephridia for excretion | *Nereis*, *Pheretima* (Earthworm), *Hirudinaria* |
| **Arthropoda** | Bilateral | Coelomate | Chitinous exoskeleton, jointed appendages, open circulatory system | *Apis*, *Bombyx*, *Anopheles*, *Periplaneta* |
| **Mollusca** | Bilateral | Coelomate | Soft body, calcareous shell, muscular foot, mantle cavity, radula | *Pila*, *Pinctada*, *Octopus* |
| **Echinodermata** | Radial (Adult) / Bilateral (Larva) | Coelomate | Water vascular system, tube feet, spiny skin, endoskeleton of calcareous ossicles | *Asterias* (Starfish), *Echinus* |
| **Hemichordata** | Bilateral | Coelomate | Worm-like, stomochord, proboscis, collar and trunk | *Balanoglossus*, *Saccoglossus* |
| **Chordata** | Bilateral | Coelomate | Notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, paired pharyngeal gill slits, post-anal tail | *Ascidia*, *Branchiostoma*, *Labeo*, *Rana*, *Panthera* |

## Common Exam Traps

* **Symmetry Shift in Echinoderms**: Adult echinoderms exhibit **radial symmetry**, but their larvae display **bilateral symmetry**.
* **Pseudocoelomate distinction**: Aschelminthes (roundworms) are the **only** major phylum with a pseudocoelom (mesoderm present as scattered pouches instead of lining the coelom).
* **Nerve Cord Position**: Chordates have a **dorsal, hollow, single** nerve cord, whereas non-chordates (like Arthropods and Annelids) have a **ventral, solid, double** nerve cord.

## Quiz

1. An organism collected from marine waters displays radial symmetry as an adult, possesses an endoskeleton of calcareous ossicles, and moves using a specialized water vascular system. How should its larval stage be characterized regarding symmetry?

* \[ \] Asymmetrical
* \[ \] Radial
* \[x\] Bilateral
* \[ \] Biradial

> Echinoderms exhibit a unique developmental pattern where adults show secondary radial symmetry, whereas their free-swimming larvae are bilaterally symmetrical.

2. Which of the following diagnostic features correctly distinguishes Phylum Aschelminthes from Phylum Platyhelminthes?

* \[ \] Presence of triploblastic germ layers
* \[ \] Presence of bilateral symmetry
* \[x\] Presence of a pseudocoelom and complete digestive tract
* \[ \] Absence of a respiratory system

> Platyhelminthes are acoelomate with an incomplete digestive tract (blind-sac plan), whereas Aschelminthes are pseudocoelomate with a complete digestive system (tube-within-a-tube plan). Both phyla are triploblastic and bilaterally symmetrical.

3. During an examination of an unknown invertebrate, a biologist notes metameric segmentation, a true coelom lined by mesoderm, paired nephridia, and a closed circulatory system. To which phylum does this animal belong?

* \[ \] Arthropoda
* \[x\] Annelida
* \[ \] Mollusca
* \[ \] Hemichordata

> Annelids are characterized by true metameric segmentation, a coelom, nephridia for excretion/osmoregulation, and a closed circulatory system (unlike Arthropods and Molluscs, which have open circulatory systems).
