## title: Chemical Coordination and Integration
level: intermediate → advanced

The endocrine system coordinates long-term metabolic and physiological functions by releasing chemical messengers (**hormones**) directly into the bloodstream.

## Key Human Endocrine Glands and Hormones

| Endocrine Gland | Primary Hormones | Physiological Functions / Target Organs | Deficiency / Hypersecretion Disorders |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Hypothalamus** | Releasing ($\mathrm{GnRH}$) and Inhibiting ($\mathrm{GIH}$) hormones; Synthesizes $\mathrm{ADH}$ & Oxytocin | Controls anterior pituitary gland secretions | Disruption affects overall pituitary axis |
| **Pituitary (Anterior)** | $\mathrm{GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, Prolactin}$ | Master gland regulating thyroid, adrenal cortex, gonads | Gigantism/Acromegaly (High $\mathrm{GH}$); Dwarfism (Low $\mathrm{GH}$) |
| **Pituitary (Posterior)** | Stores/releases $\mathrm{ADH}$ and Oxytocin | $\mathrm{ADH}$ (water reabsorption); Oxytocin (uterine contraction/milk ejection) | Diabetes Insipidus (Low $\mathrm{ADH}$ leading to polyuria) |
| **Thyroid** | Thyroxine ($\mathrm{T_4}$), Triiodothyronine ($\mathrm{T_3}$), Thyrocalcitonin ($\mathrm{TCT}$) | Basal Metabolic Rate ($\text{BMR}$), $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ lowering ($\mathrm{TCT}$) | Goitre, Cretinism (Hypo); Grave's disease (Hyper) |
| **Parathyroid** | Parathyroid Hormone ($\mathrm{PTH}$) | **Hypercalcemic** hormone (increases blood $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ levels) | Hypocalcemic tetany |
| **Adrenal Cortex** | Glucocorticoids (Cortisol), Mineralocorticoids (Aldosterone) | Metabolism, immune suppression (Cortisol); $\mathrm{Na^+/K^+}$ balance (Aldosterone) | Addison's disease (Hypo); Cushing's syndrome (Hyper) |
| **Adrenal Medulla** | Adrenaline (Epinephrine), Noradrenaline | Emergency "Fight or Flight" hormones ($\text{3F}$: Fear, Fight, Flight) | Alterations in sympathetic response |
| **Pancreas (Islets)** | Insulin ($\beta$-cells), Glucagon ($\alpha$-cells) | Insulin lowers blood glucose; Glucagon increases blood glucose | Diabetes Mellitus (Insulin deficiency/resistance) |

## Mechanism of Hormone Action

```
                              Hormone Binding
                                     |
         -----------------------------------------------------------
         |                                                         |
 Water-Soluble Hormones                                   Lipid-Soluble Hormones
 (Peptides, Amines; e.g., Insulin, Epinephrine)          (Steroids, Thyroid hormones; e.g., Estrogen)
         |                                                         |
 Binds cell-surface membrane receptor                     Crosses membrane into cytoplasm/nucleus
         |                                                         |
 Generates 2nd Messengers (cAMP, IP₃, Ca²⁺)              Binds intracellular nuclear receptor
         |                                                         |
 Downstream biochemical cascade                           Directly alters gene expression (DNA)
```

## Common Exam Traps

* **$\mathrm{PTH}$ vs $\mathrm{TCT}$ Antagonism**: Parathyroid Hormone ($\mathrm{PTH}$) **increases** blood $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ (hypercalcemic), while Thyrocalcitonin ($\mathrm{TCT}$) **decreases** blood $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ (hypocalcemic).
* **Posterior Pituitary Storage**: Posterior pituitary (Neurohypophysis) does **not** synthesize $\mathrm{ADH}$ or Oxytocin; it only stores and releases hormones produced by the **Hypothalamus**.

## Quiz

1. A patient presents with persistent hyperglycemia, glucosuria (glucose in urine), ketonuria, and excessive thirst (polydipsia). Which endocrine pancreatic dysfunction is responsible for these clinical signs?

* \[ \] Hypersecretion of Glucagon by $\alpha$-cells
* \[x\] Deficiency of Insulin production by $\beta$-cells (Diabetes Mellitus)
* \[ \] Deficiency of $\text{ADH}$ secretion by posterior pituitary
* \[ \] Hypersecretion of Thyrocalcitonin

> Diabetes Mellitus results from insufficient insulin production by pancreatic $\beta$-cells (or tissue resistance), causing high blood glucose, glucosuria, and ketone body accumulation.

2. Which hormone acts as a hypercalcemic factor by stimulating bone resorption (osteoclastic breakdown), enhancing renal reabsorption of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, and increasing intestinal calcium absorption?

* \[ \] Thyrocalcitonin ($\text{TCT}$)
* \[x\] Parathyroid Hormone ($\text{PTH}$)
* \[ \] Aldosterone
* \[ \] Cortisol

> Parathyroid Hormone ($\text{PTH}$) increases blood calcium levels by mobilizing $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ from bones, reabsorbing it in renal tubules, and promoting vitamin D-mediated intestinal uptake.

3. How do steroid hormones such as Estrogen and Testosterone exert their metabolic effects on target cells?

* \[ \] Binding cell-surface membrane receptors to activate secondary messenger cyclic $\text{AMP}$ ($\text{cAMP}$)
* \[x\] Diffusing across plasma membranes to bind intracellular nuclear receptors, directly altering gene transcription
* \[ \] Opening ligand-gated sodium channels to induce membrane depolarization
* \[ \] Activating extracellular G-protein coupled cascades

> Lipid-soluble steroid hormones cross cell membranes easily and bind to intracellular/nuclear receptors, forming a complex that binds $\text{DNA}$ to regulate gene expression.
