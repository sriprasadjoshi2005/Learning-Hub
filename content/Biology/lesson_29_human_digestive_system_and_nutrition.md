## title: Human Digestive System and Nutrition
level: intermediate → advanced

The human digestive system comprises the alimentary canal and associated digestive glands (salivary glands, liver, pancreas) that convert complex food macromolecules into absorbable monomeric units.

## Anatomy of Alimentary Canal and Histology

$$
\text{Mouth} \rightarrow \text{Pharynx} \rightarrow \text{Oesophagus} \rightarrow \text{Stomach} \rightarrow \text{Small Intestine (Duodenum, Jejunum, Ileum)} \rightarrow \text{Large Intestine} \rightarrow \text{Anus}
$$

### Gut Wall Histology (Outermost to Innermost)
1. **Serosa**: Outermost thin mesothelium layer.
2. **Muscularis**: Smooth muscles (outer longitudinal, inner circular).
3. **Sub-mucosa**: Loose connective tissue containing nerves, blood, and lymph vessels (Brunner's glands present in duodenum).
4. **Mucosa**: Innermost lining with villi/microvilli to expand surface area.

## Enzymatic Digestion Breakdown

| Site | Secretion / Source | Enzyme | Substrate $\rightarrow$ Product |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Oral Cavity** | Saliva ($\mathrm{pH\ 6.8}$) | Salivary Amylase (Ptyalin) | Starch $\rightarrow$ Maltose ($30\%$ breakdown) |
| **Stomach** | Gastric Juice ($\mathrm{pH\ 1.8}$) | Pepsin (from Pepsinogen via $\mathrm{HCl}$) | Proteins $\rightarrow$ Proteoses and Peptones |
| **Stomach (Infants)** | Gastric Juice | Rennin | Milk protein (Casein) digestion |
| **Duodenum** | Pancreatic Juice ($\mathrm{pH\ 7.8}$) | Trypsin, Chymotrypsin, Carboxypeptidase | Proteins/Peptides $\rightarrow$ Dipeptides |
| **Duodenum** | Pancreatic Juice | Pancreatic Lipase (Steapsin) | Emulsified Fats $\rightarrow$ Diglycerides $\rightarrow$ Monoglycerides |
| **Small Intestine** | Intestinal Juice (*Succus Entericus*) | Dipeptidases, Maltase, Lactase, Sucrase | Final monomeric absorption (Amino acids, Glucose, Fructose) |

## Role of Bile in Digestion

Bile synthesized by the liver and stored in the **gallbladder** contains **bile salts** (sodium glycocholate/taurocholate) and **bile pigments** (bilirubin, biliverdin). Bile contains **no enzymes**, but performs essential **emulsification of fats** and creates an alkaline $\mathrm{pH}$ for pancreatic enzymes.

## Absorption of Nutrients

* **Glucose and Amino Acids**: Absorbed via active transport or facilitated diffusion into blood capillaries.
* **Fatty Acids and Glycerol**: Insoluble; re-esterified into **chylomicrons** (lipoprotein globules) inside mucosal cells and transported into lymphatic capillaries (**lacteals**).

## Common Exam Traps

* **Bile has no digestive enzymes**: Bile facilitates lipid digestion strictly through physical emulsification and $\mathrm{pH}$ neutralization.
* **Rennin vs Renin**: **Rennin** (with double 'n') is a milk-curdling digestive enzyme in infant stomach; **Renin** (single 'n') is a hormone/enzyme secreted by kidney juxtaglomerular cells for blood pressure regulation.

## Quiz

1. Fatty acids and glycerol cannot be absorbed directly into blood capillaries. Into what specialized microscopic structures inside intestinal villi are chylomicrons absorbed?

* \[ \] Hepatic portal veins
* \[x\] Lacteals (Lymphatic capillaries)
* \[ \] Crypts of Lieberkühn
* \[ \] Brunner's glands

> Water-insoluble fats are packaged into lipoprotein particles called chylomicrons inside enterocytes and released into lacteals (central lymphatic capillaries of villi).

2. What is the physiological activation mechanism for converting inactive zymogen pepsinogen into active protein-digesting pepsin within the gastric mucosa?

* \[ \] Cleavage by enterokinase enzyme
* \[ \] Emulsification by bile salts
* \[x\] Exposure to Hydrochloric Acid ($\text{HCl}$) creating $\text{pH } 1.8$
* \[ \] Phosphorylation by pancreatic lipase

> Gastric parietal cells secrete $\text{HCl}$, which creates an acidic environment ($\text{pH } 1.5 - 2.0$) that cleaves inactive pepsinogen into active pepsin.

3. Which digestive juice secreted into the duodenum contains no digestive enzymes yet plays a mandatory role in lipid emulsification and $\text{pH}$ optimization?

* \[ \] Pancreatic juice
* \[ \] Succus entericus
* \[x\] Bile
* \[ \] Gastric juice

> Bile contains bile salts and phospholipids but zero digestive enzymes. It physically emulsifies fats into micro-droplets and neutralizes acidic stomach chyme.
