## title: Cell Cycle and Cell Division
level: intermediate

The cell cycle is an ordered series of events involved in cell growth and division. It ensures the accurate replication of genetic material and its equal distribution into daughter cells.

## Phases of the Cell Cycle

The cell cycle consists of two primary phases: **Interphase** (preparatory phase, $95\%$ of cycle duration) and **M-Phase** (Mitotic phase).

$$
\text{Interphase } (\mathrm{G_1} \rightarrow \mathrm{S} \rightarrow \mathrm{G_2}) \longrightarrow \text{M-Phase } (\text{Karyokinesis} \rightarrow \text{Cytokinesis})
$$

### Interphase Breakdown
1. **$\mathrm{G_1}$ Phase (Gap 1)**: Active cell growth, organelle duplication, RNA and protein synthesis. $\mathrm{DNA}$ content is $2\mathrm{C}$ ($2n$ chromosomes).
2. **$\mathrm{S}$ Phase (Synthesis)**: Replication of nuclear $\mathrm{DNA}$ and duplication of the centrosome in cytoplasm. Chromosome count remains $2n$, but $\mathrm{DNA}$ content doubles to $4\mathrm{C}$.
3. **$\mathrm{G_2}$ Phase (Gap 2)**: Tubulin protein synthesis and preparation for spindle apparatus assembly. $\mathrm{DNA}$ content is $4\mathrm{C}$.
4. **$\mathrm{G_0}$ Phase (Quiescent Stage)**: Non-dividing metabolic state entered by cells exiting $\mathrm{G_1}$ (e.g., mature neurons, cardiac muscle cells).

## Mitosis vs Meiosis Comparison

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Divisions** | Single nuclear division | Two sequential divisions (Meiosis I & Meiosis II) |
| **Daughter Cells** | $2$ genetically identical diploid ($2n$) cells | $4$ genetically diverse haploid ($n$) cells |
| **Synapsis & Crossing Over** | Absent | Occurs during **Prophase I** (Pachytene stage) |
| **Biological Role** | Growth, repair, somatic tissue regeneration | Gametes formation, introducing genetic variation |

## Stages of Meiotic Prophase I

Prophase I is prolonged and divided into five distinct sub-stages:

1. **Leptotene**: Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes.
2. **Zygotene**: Homologous chromosomes pair up (**synapsis**) to form bivalents/tetrads via the **synaptonemal complex**.
3. **Pachytene**: **Crossing over** (exchange of genetic material) occurs between non-sister chromatids of homologous pairs, mediated by the enzyme **recombinase**.
4. **Diplotene**: Dissolution of synaptonemal complex; homologous chromosomes remain attached at cross-over sites called **chiasmata**.
5. **Diakinesis**: Terminalisation of chiasmata; nuclear envelope breaks down.

## Common Exam Traps

* **Chromosome vs Chromatid Number**: In $\mathrm{S}$ phase, $\mathrm{DNA}$ content doubles ($2\mathrm{C} \rightarrow 4\mathrm{C}$), but the total number of chromosomes remains the **same** ($2n$).
* **Anaphase I vs Anaphase II**: Homologous pairs separate during Anaphase I (reducing chromosome number from $2n$ to $n$), whereas sister chromatids separate during Anaphase II.
