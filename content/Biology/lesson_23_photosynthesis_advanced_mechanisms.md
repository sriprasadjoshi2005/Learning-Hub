## title: Photosynthesis — Advanced Mechanisms
level: advanced

Photosynthesis proceeds through two coupled phases: the **Light Reactions** (Photochemical phase generating $\mathrm{ATP}$ and $\mathrm{NADPH}$) and the **Dark Reactions** (Biochemical phase assimilating $\mathrm{CO_2}$ into carbohydrates).

## Light-Dependent Reactions & Z-Scheme

Photosynthesis uses two photosystems embedded in thylakoid membranes: **Photosystem II (PS II, $\mathrm{P_{680}}$)** and **Photosystem I (PS I, $\mathrm{P_{700}}$)**.

### Non-Cyclic Photophosphorylation (Z-Scheme)
Flow of electrons from water to $\mathrm{NADP^+}$:

$$
\mathrm{2H_2O} \xrightarrow[\text{Complex}]{\text{Oxygen-Evolving}} \mathrm{4H^+ + 4e^- + O_2} \longrightarrow \text{PS II (P}_{680}\text{)} \xrightarrow{\text{ETC}} \text{PS I (P}_{700}\text{)} \xrightarrow{\text{NADP}^+ \text{Reductase}} \mathrm{NADPH}
$$

* **Products**: $\mathrm{ATP}$, $\mathrm{NADPH}$, and $\mathrm{O_2}$.

### Cyclic Photophosphorylation
Occurs in stroma lamellae (lacks PS II and $\text{NADP}^+$ reductase). Electrons cycle back from PS I to the electron transport chain.
* **Product**: $\mathrm{ATP}$ only (No $\mathrm{NADPH}$ or $\mathrm{O_2}$ formed).

## Chemiosmotic Hypothesis (ATP Synthesis)

Proposed by Peter Mitchell: $\mathrm{ATP}$ synthesis is driven by a **proton gradient** across the thylakoid membrane:
1. Protons ($\mathrm{H^+}$) accumulate inside the **thylakoid lumen** due to water splitting and cytochrome transport.
2. Breakdown of the proton gradient as $\mathrm{H^+}$ flows through the $\mathrm{F_0-F_1\ ATP\ Synthase}$ complex drives $\mathrm{ATP}$ synthesis in the stroma.

## Dark Reactions (Carbon Fixation Pathways)

### 1. $\mathrm{C_3}$ Pathway (Calvin Cycle)
* **Primary $\mathrm{CO_2}$ Acceptor**: Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate ($\mathrm{RuBP}$, $5\text{C}$ sugar).
* **Key Enzyme**: $\mathrm{RuBisCO}$ (Ribulose bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase).
* **First Stable Product**: 3-phosphoglyceric acid ($\mathrm{3-PGA}$, $3\text{C}$ compound).
* **Cost per Glucose**: $18\mathrm{\ ATP} + 12\mathrm{\ NADPH}$.

### 2. $\mathrm{C_4}$ Pathway (Hatch-Slack Pathway)
Found in tropical plants (Maize, Sugarcane) displaying **Kranz Anatomy** (large bundle sheath cells surrounding vascular bundles).

```
  Mesophyll Cell (No RuBisCO)                  Bundle Sheath Cell (Has RuBisCO)
  ---------------------------                  ----------------------------------
  PEP (3C) + CO₂  ---> OAA (4C)                Malate (4C) ---> Pyruvate (3C) + CO₂
        (PEP Case)                                                 |
                                                                   v
                                                             Calvin Cycle (C₃)
```

* **Primary $\mathrm{CO_2}$ Acceptor**: Phosphoenolpyruvate ($\mathrm{PEP}$, $3\text{C}$).
* **First Stable Product**: Oxaloacetic acid ($\mathrm{OAA}$, $4\text{C}$).
* **Advantage**: Eliminates **Photorespiration** ($\mathrm{C_2}$ cycle), maximizing photosynthetic efficiency at high temperatures.

## Common Exam Traps

* **$\mathrm{RuBisCO}$ Dual Activity**: $\mathrm{RuBisCO}$ acts as an **oxygenase** at high $\mathrm{O_2}$ concentrations and elevated temperatures, initiating wasteful photorespiration where no $\mathrm{ATP}$ or $\mathrm{NADPH}$ is synthesized.
* **Location of Enzymes**: $\mathrm{PEP}$ carboxylase is present in **mesophyll cells**, whereas $\mathrm{RuBisCO}$ is confined to **bundle sheath cells** in $\mathrm{C_4}$ plants.

## Quiz

1. How many total molecules of $\text{ATP}$ and $\text{NADPH}$ are consumed in the stroma of a $\text{C}_3$ plant to synthesize one molecule of glucose via the Calvin Cycle?

* \[ \] $12\ \text{ATP}$ and $12\ \text{NADPH}$
* \[x\] $18\ \text{ATP}$ and $12\ \text{NADPH}$
* \[ \] $30\ \text{ATP}$ and $12\ \text{NADPH}$
* \[ \] $18\ \text{ATP}$ and $18\ \text{NADPH}$

> Fixing $1\text{ CO}_2$ requires $3\text{ ATP}$ and $2\text{ NADPH}$. Synthesizing $1\text{ glucose}$ ($6\text{C}$) requires 6 turns of the cycle: $6 \times 3 = 18\text{ ATP}$ and $6 \times 2 = 12\text{ NADPH}$.

2. Tropical plants like maize and sugarcane avoid photorespiratory carbon loss by concentrating $\text{CO}_2$ around $\text{RuBisCO}$ in specialized bundle sheath cells. What structural feature characterizes this adaptation?

* \[ \] Aerenchyma
* \[x\] Kranz anatomy
* \[ \] Velamen tissue
* \[ \] Bulliform cells

> Kranz anatomy features large, thick-walled bundle sheath cells packed with chloroplasts arranged in a wreath-like ring around vascular bundles, preventing photorespiration in $\text{C}_4$ plants.
