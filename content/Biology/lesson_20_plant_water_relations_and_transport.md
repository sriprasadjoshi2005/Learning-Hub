## title: Plant Water Relations and Transport
level: intermediate

Plants transport water, inorganic minerals, and organic nutrients over long distances using specialized physical gradients and vascular tissues.

## Water Potential ($\Psi_w$)

Water potential is the measure of free energy of water in a system. Pure water at standard temperature and pressure has a water potential of **zero** ($\Psi_w = 0$). Adding solutes **lowers** the water potential (makes it negative).

$$
\Psi_w = \Psi_s + \Psi_p
$$

* $\Psi_s$: Solute potential (always **negative** for solutions).
* $\Psi_p$: Pressure potential (usually **positive** in turgid cells due to wall pressure).

## Osmotic Movements in Plant Cells

* **Plasmolysis**: Shrinkage of protoplast away from the cell wall when placed in a **hypertonic** solution.
* **Turgor Pressure**: Hydrostatic pressure built up against the cell wall in a **hypotonic** solution.

## Long-Distance Water Transport: Ascent of Sap

Water moves from soil to root xylem via two pathways:
1. **Apoplast Pathway**: Non-living cell walls and intercellular spaces (blocked at the endodermis by the **Casparian strip** composed of **suberin**).
2. **Symplast Pathway**: Interconnected living protoplasts via **plasmodesmata**.

### Cohesion-Tension-Transpiration Pull Model
Proposed by Dixon and Joly, this model explains water movement up tall trees ($>100\text{ m}$):
* **Cohesion**: Mutual attraction between water molecules.
* **Adhesion**: Attraction between water molecules and lignified xylem elements.
* **Transpiration Pull**: Tension created by stomatal evaporation driving unbroken water columns upward.

## Phloem Translocation: Mass Flow Hypothesis

Münch's Pressure Flow Hypothesis models sucrose transport from **Source** (photosynthetic leaves) to **Sink** (storage organs/growing tips):

$$
\text{Source (High Hydrostatic Pressure)} \xrightarrow{\text{Phloem Bulk Flow}} \text{Sink (Low Hydrostatic Pressure)}
$$

## Common Exam Traps

* **Max Water Potential**: The maximum possible value for pure water at atmospheric pressure is **zero**. All aqueous solutions have a negative $\Psi_w$.
* **Casparian Strip Barrier**: The Casparian strip forces water moving along the apoplast pathway to cross the selectively permeable cell membrane into the **symplast pathway**.

## Quiz

1. A plant cell with a solute potential ($\Psi_s$) of $-0.8\text{ MPa}$ and a pressure potential ($\Psi_p$) of $+0.3\text{ MPa}$ is placed in an open beaker containing a solution with a water potential ($\Psi_w$) of $-0.7\text{ MPa}$. What will be the net direction of water movement?

* \[ \] Net water movement into the cell
* \[x\] Net water movement out of the cell into the beaker
* \[ \] No net movement of water
* \[ \] Cell will undergo immediate lysis

> Cell $\Psi_w = \Psi_s + \Psi_p = -0.8 + 0.3 = -0.5\text{ MPa}$. The beaker solution has $\Psi_w = -0.7\text{ MPa}$. Water moves from higher $\Psi_w$ ($-0.5\text{ MPa}$) to lower $\Psi_w$ ($-0.7\text{ MPa}$), so water leaves the cell.

2. The hydrophobic, suberized structural barrier in the endodermal cell walls of plant roots that blocks apoplastic movement of water and forces solute entry into the symplast is called the:

* \[ \] Plasmodesmata
* \[x\] Casparian strip
* \[ \] Tonoplast membrane
* \[ \] Middle lamella

> The Casparian strip is a band of suberin in root endodermal walls that prevents apoplastic water movement, forcing water and solutes to cross the plasma membrane into the symplast.

3. According to the Münch Pressure Flow Hypothesis, what mechanism generates the hydrostatic pressure gradient required for sucrose transport through sieve tubes?

* \[x\] Active loading of sucrose at the source lowers phloem water potential, driving osmotic entry of water.
* \[ \] Transpiration pull from stomata creates negative pressure inside phloem vessels.
* \[ \] Capillary action inside phloem sieve elements moves sucrose upward.
* \[ \] Passive diffusion of sucrose down a concentration gradient without water movement.

> Active loading of sucrose into phloem sieve tubes at the source lowers the solute potential, drawing water in from adjacent xylem by osmosis. This builds high hydrostatic turgor pressure that drives mass flow toward the sink.
