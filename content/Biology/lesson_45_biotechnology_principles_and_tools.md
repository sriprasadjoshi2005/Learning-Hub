## title: Biotechnology — Principles and Tools
level: advanced

Biotechnology uses living organisms or enzymes to produce products beneficial to humans. **Recombinant DNA ($\text{rDNA}$) technology** involves genetic engineering to alter genetic material *in vitro*.

## Key Steps in Recombinant DNA Technology

$$
\text{Isolation of DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Restriction Enzyme}} \text{Gene of Interest} \xrightarrow{\text{Ligation into Vector}} \text{Recombinant DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Transformation}} \text{Host Cell} \rightarrow \text{Expression}
$$

## Major Molecular Tools

1. **Restriction Endonucleases ("Molecular Scissors")**:
   * Cut double-stranded $\mathrm{DNA}$ at specific **palindromic nucleotide sequences**.
   * Produce **sticky ends** (overhanging single strands) or **blunt ends**.
   * *Example*: $\mathrm{EcoRI}$ recognizes sequence $5'\text{-GAATTC-}3'$ and cuts between $\text{G}$ and $\text{A}$.
2. **Cloning Vectors ("Molecular Vehicles")**:
   * Carry foreign $\mathrm{DNA}$ into host cells. Key components:
     * **Origin of Replication (ori)**: Controls vector replication and copy number.
     * **Selectable Marker**: Identifies transformants (e.g., antibiotic resistance genes $amp^R, tet^R$).
     * **Cloning Sites**: Unique recognition sites for restriction enzymes.
   * *Example*: $\mathrm{pBR322}$ plasmid vector.
3. **DNA Ligase ("Molecular Glue")**: Joins cut $\mathrm{DNA}$ fragments via phosphodiester bonds.

## Core Techniques

* **Gel Electrophoresis**: Separates $\mathrm{DNA}$ fragments based on size. $\mathrm{DNA}$ is negatively charged and moves toward the **anode** through an **agarose gel**. Visualized under $\text{UV}$ light using **Ethidium Bromide (EtBr)** (orange bands). Extracting bands from gel is called **Elution**.
* **Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)**: Amplifies specific $\mathrm{DNA}$ sequences *in vitro* over three steps:

$$
\text{Denaturation } (94^\circ\mathrm{C}) \longrightarrow \text{Annealing } (54^\circ\mathrm{C}) \longrightarrow \text{Extension } (72^\circ\mathrm{C, Taq Polymerase})
$$

> Uses heat-stable **$\mathrm{Taq}$ Polymerase** isolated from thermophilic bacterium *Thermus aquaticus*.

## Common Exam Traps

* **Restriction Enzyme Naming**: In $\mathrm{EcoRI}$, **$\text{E}$** = Genus (*Escherichia*), **$\text{co}$** = Species (*coli*), **$\text{R}$** = Strain ($\text{RY 13}$), **$\text{I}$** = Order of discovery.
* **PCR Polymerase Thermostability**: Standard $\mathrm{DNA}$ polymerase denatures at high temperatures; $\mathrm{PCR}$ requires heat-stable **$\text{Taq}$ Polymerase** to withstand the $94^\circ\mathrm{C}$ denaturation step.

## Quiz

1. A genetic engineer uses the restriction enzyme $\text{EcoRI}$ to digest human genomic $\text{DNA}$. At which specific 6-base-pair palindromic sequence does this endonuclease cleave double-stranded $\text{DNA}$?

* \[ \] $5'\text{-GGCC-}3'$
* \[x\] $5'\text{-GAATTC-}3'$
* \[ \] $5'\text{-AAGCTT-}3'$
* \[ \] $5'\text{-CTCGAG-}3'$

> $\text{EcoRI}$ recognizes the palindromic sequence $5'\text{-GAATTC-}3'$ and cuts between $\text{G}$ and $\text{A}$, producing complementary sticky ends.

2. Following agarose gel electrophoresis, $\text{DNA}$ fragments cannot be seen under visible light. What staining dye and illumination source are required to visualize separated $\text{DNA}$ bands?

* \[ \] Methylene blue under infrared light
* \[x\] Ethidium bromide (EtBr) under ultraviolet (UV) light
* \[ \] Acetocarmine under white fluorescent light
* \[ \] Crystal violet under laser light

> $\text{DNA}$ fragments stained with Ethidium Bromide ($\text{EtBr}$) emit bright orange fluorescence when exposed to ultraviolet ($\text{UV}$) light.

3. Which heat-tolerant enzyme is essential for carrying out the repeated thermal cycles of extension during Polymerase Chain Reaction ($\text{PCR}$)?

* \[ \] $\text{DNA}$ Polymerase I from *E. coli*
* \[x\] $\text{Taq}$ Polymerase from *Thermus aquaticus*
* \[ \] Reverse transcriptase from retrovirus
* \[ \] Helicase from bacteriophage T4

> $\text{Taq}$ Polymerase isolated from the thermophilic bacterium *Thermus aquaticus* remains active at high temperatures ($72^\circ\text{C}$), allowing automated $\text{PCR}$ amplification.
