## title: Cell Organelles and Their Functions
level: foundation

Eukaryotic cells are compartmentalized by membrane-bound structures called organelles. This spatial segregation allows distinct, incompatible biochemical reactions to take place simultaneously within specialized microenvironments.

## Eukaryotic Organelles Overview

| Organelle | Membrane Layers | Primary Function | Distinctive Features |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Nucleus** | Double | Storage of genetic information, site of transcription and ribosome assembly | Contains nucleolus and chromatin network |
| **Mitochondria** | Double | Site of aerobic respiration and $\mathrm{ATP}$ synthesis via oxidative phosphorylation | Inner membrane forms **cristae**; contains matrix, circular $\mathrm{DNA}$, and $\mathrm{70S}$ ribosomes |
| **Chloroplast** | Double | Site of photosynthesis (light and dark reactions) | Stroma contains **thylakoid** stacks (**grana**), circular $\mathrm{DNA}$, and $\mathrm{70S}$ ribosomes |
| **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)** | Single | **RER**: Protein synthesis and folding.<br>**SER**: Lipid/steroid synthesis and $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ storage | RER is studded with ribosomes; SER lacks ribosomes |
| **Golgi Apparatus** | Single | Modification, sorting, and packaging of proteins and lipids | Consists of flattened sacs (**cisternae**) with distinct *cis* (forming) and *trans* (maturing) faces |
| **Lysosomes** | Single | Intracellular digestion and organelle turnover | Rich in acid hydrolases (active at $\mathrm{pH} \approx 5.0$); known as "suicide bags" |
| **Peroxisomes** | Single | $\mathrm{H_2O_2}$ metabolism and fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation | Contains catalase and oxidases |
| **Vacuoles** | Single | Storage of water, sap, excretory products, and maintenance of turgor pressure | Membrane is called the **tonoplast**; occupies up to $90\%$ of plant cell volume |

## Endomembrane System

The endomembrane system consists of organelles whose functions are coordinated:

$$
\text{Rough ER} \xrightarrow{\text{Transport Vesicle}} \text{Golgi (Cis } \rightarrow \text{ Trans)} \xrightarrow{\text{Secretory Vesicle}} \text{Plasma Membrane / Lysosome}
$$

> *Note*: Mitochondria, chloroplasts, and peroxisomes are **not** part of the endomembrane system because their functions are not coordinated with the ER-Golgi network.

## Common Exam Traps

* **Ribosome Membrane Status**: Ribosomes are **non-membrane-bound** organelles present in both prokaryotes and eukaryotes.
* **Organelle Genomes**: Mitochondria and chloroplasts are semi-autonomous organelles possessing their own circular $\mathrm{DNA}$ and $\mathrm{70S}$ ribosomes, supporting the **Endosymbiotic Theory**.

## Quiz

1. Biochemical analysis of an organelle isolated from animal tissue reveals a low internal $\text{pH}$ ($\sim 5.0$) and high concentrations of hydrolytic enzymes including lipases, proteases, and nucleases. Which organelle was isolated?

* \[ \] Peroxisome
* \[x\] Lysosome
* \[ \] Golgi apparatus
* \[ \] Glyoxysome

> Lysosomes contain acid hydrolases (optimal activity around $\text{pH } 5.0$) that break down proteins, lipids, nucleic acids, and carbohydrates during intracellular digestion.

2. Which group of cellular organelles operates coordinately as part of the eukaryotic endomembrane system?

* \[ \] Mitochondria, Chloroplasts, and Peroxisomes
* \[x\] Endoplasmic Reticulum, Golgi Apparatus, Lysosomes, and Vacuoles
* \[ \] Nucleus, Mitochondria, and Ribosomes
* \[ \] Chloroplasts, Glyoxysomes, and Centrosomes

> The endomembrane system includes the Endoplasmic Reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, and vacuoles. Their functions are tightly integrated in protein processing, packaging, and intracellular transport.

3. Which feature of mitochondria and chloroplasts supports the endosymbiotic theory of their evolutionary origin?

* \[ \] They are bounded by a single phospholipid bilayer.
* \[ \] They synthesize proteins using $\text{80S}$ cytoplasmic ribosomes attached to their outer surface.
* \[x\] They possess their own circular double-stranded $\text{DNA}$ and $\text{70S}$ ribosomes.
* \[ \] They originate via de novo assembly from cytosol components.

> Both mitochondria and chloroplasts contain prokaryote-like circular $\text{DNA}$ and $\text{70S}$ ribosomes, supporting the theory that they evolved from engulfed ancestral prokaryotic endosymbionts.
