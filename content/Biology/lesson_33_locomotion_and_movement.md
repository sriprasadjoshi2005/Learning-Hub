## title: Locomotion and Movement
level: intermediate → advanced

Movement is a fundamental feature of living organisms. Humans rely on skeletal muscle contraction integrated with an endoskeleton of $206\text{ bones}$ for locomotion.

## Human Skeletal Division

```
                      Human Skeleton (206 Bones)
                                  |
         --------------------------------------------------
         |                                                |
   Axial Skeleton (80 Bones)                      Appendicular Skeleton (126 Bones)
 (Skull, Vertebral Column,                      (Pectoral & Pelvic Girdles,
  Sternum, Ribs)                                 Limb Bones: Arms & Legs)
```

* **Joint Types**:
  1. *Fibrous / Synarthrosis*: Immovable (e.g., Sutures of skull).
  2. *Cartilaginous / Amphiarthrosis*: Slightly movable (e.g., Intervertebral discs, Pubic symphysis).
  3. *Synovial / Diarthrosis*: Freely movable with fluid-filled cavity (Ball & Socket, Hinge, Pivot, Gliding, Saddle).

## Microscopic Structure of Skeletal Muscle

Each muscle fibre contains functional contractile units called **Sarcomeres** bounded by **Z-lines**:

$$
\text{Sarcomere} = \text{Z-line} \longleftrightarrow \text{I-band (Actin)} \longleftrightarrow \text{A-band (Myosin + Actin)} \longleftrightarrow \text{H-zone (Myosin only)} \longleftrightarrow \text{Z-line}
$$

* **Thin Filaments (Actin)**: Filamentous Actin (F-actin) coiled with **Tropomyosin** and regulatory protein **Troponin**.
* **Thick Filaments (Myosin)**: Polymerized protein composed of **Monomeric Myosins** (Heavy Meromyosin head + Light Meromyosin tail).

## Sliding Filament Theory of Muscle Contraction

Proposed by Huxley and Hanson:

1. Action potential reaches neuromuscular junction $\rightarrow$ Release of **Acetylcholine (ACh)**.
2. $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ released from **Sarcoplasmic Reticulum** into sarcoplasm.
3. $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ binds to **Troponin C**, causing a conformational change that uncovers active myosin-binding sites on actin.
4. Myosin head hydrolyzes $\mathrm{ATP}$ ($\mathrm{ADP + P_i}$), forms a **Cross-Bridge** with actin, and executes a **Power Stroke** pulling actin inward.
5. Sarcomere shortens: **I-band shortens**, **H-zone disappears**, while **A-band length remains constant**.

## Common Exam Traps

* **Band Behavior During Contraction**: During sarcomere contraction, the **A-band length does NOT change**. Only the **I-band** and **H-zone** shorten/disappear.
* **Myosin Head Binding**: The Myosin head acts as an active $\mathrm{ATP}$ase enzyme and has binding sites for both $\mathrm{ATP}$ and actin.

## Quiz

1. According to the Sliding Filament Theory of skeletal muscle contraction, what structural change occurs to the length of the anisotropic A-band when a sarcomere shortens?

* \[ \] A-band shortens proportionally to the I-band.
* \[ \] A-band expands as filaments overlap.
* \[x\] A-band length remains completely unchanged.
* \[ \] A-band completely disappears.

> During muscle contraction, actin thin filaments slide over myosin thick filaments toward the center. The length of the thick filament (A-band) remains constant, while the I-band and H-zone shorten.

2. During excitation-contraction coupling in skeletal muscle, calcium ions ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum bind directly to which regulatory protein complex?

* \[ \] Tropomyosin
* \[x\] Troponin (subunit Troponin C)
* \[ \] F-actin
* \[ \] Myosin light chain

> $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ binds to Troponin C on thin filaments, altering its conformation to move tropomyosin away from the myosin-binding sites on actin.
