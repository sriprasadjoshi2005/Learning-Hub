## title: Advanced Biological Problem Solving
level: entrance preparation

University entrance examinations require integrated mathematical calculations, multi-step genetics problems, and analytical data interpretation across biological domains.

## Core Problem-Solving Formulas

### 1. Population Genetics (Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium)

$$
p + q = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1
$$

* Allele frequencies: $p$ (Dominant), $q$ (Recessive).
* Genotype frequencies: $p^2$ (Homozygous dominant), $2pq$ (Heterozygous), $q^2$ (Homozygous recessive).

### 2. Genetics and Recombination Frequency

$$
\text{Recombination Frequency (RF \%)} = \frac{\text{Total Recombinant Progeny}}{\text{Total Progeny}} \times 100
$$

$$
1\%\ \text{Recombination} = 1\ \text{Map Unit (mu)} = 1\ \text{Centimorgan (cM)}
$$

### 3. Molecular Genetics & DNA Calculations

* Base pair distance: $0.34\text{ nm} = 0.34 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$.
* Total length of $\mathrm{dsDNA}$:

$$
\text{Length of DNA (meters)} = (\text{Total Base Pairs}) \times (0.34 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m})
$$

### 4. Eco-Energetics ($10\%$ Energy Transfer Rule)

$$
\text{Energy at Trophic Level } n = \text{Energy at Producer Level } (T_1) \times (0.10)^{n-1}
$$

## Common Exam Traps

* **$\mathrm{DNA}$ Content vs Chromosome Number**: Remember that in $\mathrm{S}$-phase, $\mathrm{DNA}$ mass doubles ($2\mathrm{C} \rightarrow 4\mathrm{C}$), but the chromosome count ($2n$) remains **unchanged**.
* **Base Pair Counting**: For linear $\mathrm{DNA}$, $\text{Number of Phosphodiester Bonds} = 2 \times (\text{Base Pairs}) - 2$. For circular $\mathrm{DNA}$, $\text{Number of Phosphodiester Bonds} = 2 \times (\text{Base Pairs})$.

## Quiz

1. A mammalian diploid cell ($2n = 24$) with $40\text{ pg}$ of nuclear $\text{DNA}$ enters Meiosis. What will be the total chromosome number and $\text{DNA}$ content per individual cell at the completion of Telophase I?

* \[ \] 24 chromosomes; $40\text{ pg}\ \text{DNA}$
* \[x\] 12 chromosomes; $40\text{ pg}\ \text{DNA}$
* \[ \] 12 chromosomes; $20\text{ pg}\ \text{DNA}$
* \[ \] 24 chromosomes; $20\text{ pg}\ \text{DNA}$

> Before meiosis, $\text{DNA}$ replicates in $\text{S}$-phase to $80\text{ pg}$ ($2n=24$). During Meiosis I, homologous pairs separate, halving the chromosome number to $n=12$. Cell division divides the $80\text{ pg}$ of $\text{DNA}$ into two daughter cells, resulting in $12$ chromosomes and $40\text{ pg}$ of $\text{DNA}$ per cell.

2. In a genetic mapping cross, a test cross of a dihybrid ($AaBb \times aabb$) produces 420 $AB$, 410 $ab$, 85 $Ab$, and 85 $aB$ offspring. What is the map distance separating gene locus $A$ and gene locus $B$?

* \[ \] $8.5\ \text{cM}$
* \[x\] $17.0\ \text{cM}$
* \[ \] $41.5\ \text{cM}$
* \[ \] $34.0\ \text{cM}$

> Total progeny $= 420 + 410 + 85 + 85 = 1000$. Recombinant offspring $= 85 + 85 = 170$. $\text{Recombination Frequency} = \left(\frac{170}{1000}\right) \times 100 = 17\%$. Therefore, map distance $= 17.0\ \text{cM}$.

3. If the primary producers in a grassland ecosystem assimilate $40,000\text{ Joules}$ of solar energy into net primary biomass, how much energy will be available to the tertiary consumer level (apex predator) according to Lindeman's Efficiency Law?

* \[ \] $4,000\text{ Joules}$
* \[ \] $400\text{ Joules}$
* \[x\] $40\text{ Joules}$
* \[ \] $4\text{ Joules}$

> Trophic level breakdown: $T_1$ (Producers) $= 40,000\text{ J} \rightarrow T_2$ (Primary Consumers) $= 4,000\text{ J} \rightarrow T_3$ (Secondary Consumers) $= 400\text{ J} \rightarrow T_4$ (Tertiary Consumers) $= 40\text{ J}$.
