## title: Introduction to Genetics and Heredity
level: intermediate

Genetics is the branch of biology that studies **heredity** (the transmission of traits from parents to offspring) and **variation** (the structural or physiological differences shown by individuals of the same species).

## Key Terminology in Genetics

* **Gene**: The basic functional unit of inheritance; a segment of $\mathrm{DNA}$ encoding a functional polypeptide or $\mathrm{RNA}$.
* **Allele**: Alternative forms of a gene occupying the same locus on homologous chromosomes (e.g., $T$ for tallness, $t$ for dwarfness).
* **Genotype**: The complete genetic makeup of an organism ($TT$, $Tt$, or $tt$).
* **Phenotype**: The observable physical or physiological expression of a genotype (e.g., Tall vs Short).
* **Homozygous**: An organism carrying two identical alleles for a given gene ($TT$ or $tt$).
* **Heterozygous**: An organism carrying two different alleles for a given gene ($Tt$).
* **Dominant Trait**: An allele that expresses its phenotype in both homozygous and heterozygous conditions ($T$).
* **Recessive Trait**: An allele whose phenotypic expression is masked in the presence of a dominant allele, expressing only when homozygous ($tt$).

## Gregor Mendel’s Experimental Advantage

Gregor Mendel ("Father of Genetics") conducted hybridization experiments on the garden pea plant (*Pisum sativum*) from 1856 to 1863.

### Why *Pisum sativum*?
1. Possesses clear, contrasting binary traits (e.g., Tall/Dwarf, Yellow/Green seeds, Smooth/Wrinkled seeds).
2. Short life cycle, producing numerous progeny per cross.
3. Naturally self-pollinating, making it easy to establish pure-breeding true lines, yet easily cross-pollinated manually.

| Trait | Dominant Expression | Recessive Expression |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Stem Height** | Tall | Dwarf |
| **Seed Shape** | Round | Wrinkled |
| **Seed Color** | Yellow | Green |
| **Flower Color** | Violet | White |
| **Pod Shape** | Inflated | Constricted |
| **Pod Color** | Green | Yellow |
| **Flower Position** | Axial | Terminal |

## Common Exam Traps

* **Confusing Seed Color and Pod Color**: Dominant seed color is **Yellow**, whereas dominant pod color is **Green**.
* **Phenotype does not dictate Genotype**: A dominant phenotype (e.g., Tall plant) can correspond to either a homozygous ($TT$) or heterozygous ($Tt$) genotype.

* \[ \] $0\%$
* \[x\] $25\%$
* \[ \] $50\%$
* \[ \] $75\%$

> Violet flower color is dominant ($V$) over white ($v$). The $\text{F}_1$ heterozgotes ($Vv$) yield an $\text{F}_2$ phenotypic ratio of $3\text{ Violet} : 1\text{ White}$ upon selfing, meaning $25\%$ will be homozygous recessive ($vv$) white-flowered plants.

* \[ \] Cistrons
* \[x\] Alleles
* \[ \] Transposons
* \[ \] Mutons

> Alleles are alternative sequence variants of a given gene located at the same chromosomal locus.

3. In garden peas (*Pisum sativum*), which pair of contrasting character states represents a dominant trait paired with its corresponding recessive state?

* \[ \] Green seed color (Dominant) / Yellow seed color (Recessive)
* \[x\] Axial flower position (Dominant) / Terminal flower position (Recessive)
* \[ \] Constricted pod shape (Dominant) / Inflated pod shape (Recessive)
* \[ \] Yellow pod color (Dominant) / Green pod color (Recessive)

> In Mendel's pea experiments, axial flower position is dominant over terminal flower position. (Yellow seeds are dominant over green seeds, and green pods are dominant over yellow pods).
