## title: Cell Membrane and Transport Mechanisms
level: foundation → intermediate

The cell membrane is a selectively permeable barrier that regulates the entry and exit of substances, maintaining cellular homeostasis. The accepted framework for membrane architecture is the **Fluid Mosaic Model** proposed by Singer and Nicolson (1972).

## The Fluid Mosaic Model Structure

* **Phospholipid Bilayer**: Amphipathic molecules with hydrophilic (polar) phosphate heads facing outward toward aqueous environments and hydrophobic (nonpolar) fatty acid tails facing inward.
* **Membrane Proteins**:
  * *Integral / Intrinsic Proteins*: Embedded partially or fully within the bilayer (e.g., channel proteins, transmembrane receptors).
  * *Peripheral / Extrinsic Proteins*: Attached loosely to the outer or inner membrane surface.
* **Cholesterol**: Intercalated between phospholipids; acts as a temperature buffer maintaining optimal membrane fluidity.
* **Carbohydrates**: Glycoproteins and glycolipids on the external surface involved in cell-cell recognition and cell signaling.

## Bulk Transport Mechanisms

* **Endocytosis**: Ingestion of macromolecular material via membrane invagination.
  * *Phagocytosis*: Cellular eating (ingestion of solid particles).
  * *Pinocytosis*: Cellular drinking (ingestion of extracellular fluid droplets).
* **Exocytosis**: Fusion of intracellular vesicles with the plasma membrane to release contents outside the cell.

## Common Exam Traps

* **Facilitated Diffusion Saturation**: Facilitated diffusion requires carrier proteins, meaning its transport rate hits a maximum threshold ($\mathrm{V_{max}}$) when all carriers are saturated, unlike simple diffusion.
* **Direction of $\mathrm{Na^+/K^+}$ Pump**: The pump moves $3\ \mathrm{Na^+}$ ions **out of** the cell and $2\ \mathrm{K^+}$ ions **into** the cell per $\mathrm{ATP}$ hydrolyzed, creating an electrogenic net positive charge movement outward.

> In a hypotonic solution, the water potential outside the cell is higher than inside. Water enters the cell down its potential gradient via osmosis, causing the cell to swell and burst.

2. Which characteristic distinguishes carrier-mediated facilitated diffusion from simple diffusion across a plasma membrane?

* \[ \] Facilitated diffusion moves solutes against their concentration gradient.
* \[ \] Facilitated diffusion requires direct metabolic consumption of $\text{ATP}$.
* \[x\] Facilitated diffusion exhibits saturation kinetics ($\text{V}_{max}$) as solute concentration increases.
* \[ \] Facilitated diffusion occurs exclusively through lipid bilayer dissolution without membrane proteins.

> Because facilitated diffusion relies on a finite number of membrane transport proteins, the transport rate reaches a saturation plateau ($\text{V}_{max}$) when all binding sites are occupied. Simple diffusion rates scale linearly with concentration.
