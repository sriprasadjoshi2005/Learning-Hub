## title: Gene Regulation, Mutation and Genetic Disorders
level: intermediate → advanced

Gene expression is tightly regulated to adapt to environmental changes and drive cellular differentiation. Disruptions in genetic sequences through mutations can lead to metabolic errors or inherited genetic disorders.

## Regulation of Gene Expression: The Lac Operon

In *E. coli*, the **Lac Operon** (Jacob & Monod) regulates lactose breakdown via polycistronic transcriptional control:

$$
\text{Promoter (P)} - \text{Operator (O)} - \text{LacZ} - \text{LacY} - \text{LacA}
$$

* **Structural Genes**:
  * $\text{LacZ}$: Encodes $\beta$-galactosidase (hydrolyzes lactose to glucose + galactose).
  * $\text{LacY}$: Encodes Permease (increases membrane permeability to $\beta$-galactosides).
  * $\text{LacA}$: Encodes Transacetylase.
* **Inducible System**: In the **absence** of lactose, the Repressor protein (encoded by $i$ gene) binds to the Operator ($\text{O}$), blocking $\text{RNA}$ polymerase. In the **presence** of lactose (allolactose inducer), the repressor is inactivated, allowing transcription.

## Types of Gene Mutations

1. **Point Mutation**: Single base substitution (e.g., Sickle-cell anemia: $\text{GAG} \rightarrow \text{GUG}$ in $\beta$-globin chain replacing Glutamic acid with Valine).
2. **Frameshift Mutation**: Insertion or deletion of bases (not multiples of 3), altering the reading frame downstream.

## Human Genetic Disorders

| Disorder Category | Examples | Mode of Inheritance / Cause | Key Symptoms / Characteristics |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Mendelian Disorder** | Haemophilia | X-linked recessive | Defective blood clotting factor (VIII or IX) |
| **Mendelian Disorder** | Phenylketonuria (PKU) | Autosomal recessive | Deficiency of phenylalanine hydroxylase enzyme; mental retardation |
| **Mendelian Disorder** | Thalassemia | Autosomal recessive | Quantitative defect in $\alpha$- or $\beta$-globin chain synthesis |
| **Chromosomal Disorder** | Down Syndrome | Aneuploidy ($2n+1$) | Trisomy of chromosome 21; broad palm, mental retardation |
| **Chromosomal Disorder** | Klinefelter Syndrome | Aneuploidy ($2n+1$) | Genotype $47, \text{XXY}$; sterile male with gynaecomastia |
| **Chromosomal Disorder** | Turner Syndrome | Aneuploidy ($2n-1$) | Genotype $45, \text{XO}$; sterile female with rudimented ovaries |

## Common Exam Traps

* **Sickle-cell vs Thalassemia**: Sickle-cell anemia is a **qualitative** protein synthesis defect (incorrect amino acid), whereas Thalassemia is a **quantitative** protein synthesis defect (reduced amount of normal globin chains).
* **Induce vs Repress**: The Lac operon is an **inducible** operon under negative control; lactose acts as an inducer by inactivating the repressor.

## Quiz

1. In the *E. coli* Lac operon, what occurs at the molecular level when allolactose binds to the repressor protein?

* \[ \] The repressor binds more tightly to the operator region, stopping transcription.
* \[x\] The repressor changes shape and loses its ability to bind to the operator, initiating transcription.
* \[ \] $\text{RNA}$ polymerase is inhibited from binding to the promoter region.
* \[ \] $\text{LacZ}$ gene undergoes a frameshift mutation.

> Lactose (allolactose) acts as an inducer. Binding to the repressor causes a conformational change that prevents it from binding to the operator, allowing $\text{RNA}$ polymerase to transcribe structural genes.

* \[ \] Nullisomy of autosomes
* \[x\] Autosomal trisomy
* \[ \] Sex chromosomal aneuploidy

> Down syndrome is caused by autosomal trisomy of chromosome 21 ($2n+1 = 47$), resulting from non-disjunction of homologous chromosome pair 21 during meiosis.

* \[ \] $\text{AUG} \rightarrow \text{UAG}$
* \[x\] $\text{GAG} \rightarrow \text{GUG}$
* \[ \] $\text{UAA} \rightarrow \text{UGA}$
* \[ \] $\text{CUC} \rightarrow \text{CAC}$

> The single base substitution on the $\text{DNA}$ template changes the $6^{\text{th}}$ codon of the $\beta$-globin $\text{mRNA}$ from $\text{GAG}$ (encoding Glutamic acid) to $\text{GUG}$ (encoding Valine).
