**Taxonomy** ($\mathrm{taxis}$ = arrangement, $\mathrm{nomos}$ = law) is the branch of biology concerned with the identification, nomenclature, and classification of organisms. Modern taxonomy relies on **systematics**, which integrates evolutionary relationships (phylogeny) into classification systems.

## Taxonomic Hierarchy

Organisms are classified in a hierarchical series of categories (taxa) in ascending or descending order. As you move from **Species to Kingdom**, the number of common characteristics **decreases**, while the number of included organisms **increases**.

$$
\text{Species} \longrightarrow \text{Genus} \longrightarrow \text{Family} \longrightarrow \text{Order} \longrightarrow \text{Class} \longrightarrow \text{Phylum / Division} \longrightarrow \text{Kingdom}
$$

| Rank | Definition / Notes | Example (Human) | Example (Housefly) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kingdom** | Highest taxonomic category combining related phyla. | Animalia | Animalia |
| **Phylum / Division** | Grouping of related classes (*Division* is used for plants). | Chordata | Arthropoda |
| **Class** | Grouping of related orders. | Mammalia | Insecta |
| **Order** | Assembly of families showing similar traits. | Primates | Diptera |
| **Family** | Group of related genera with fewer similarities than a genus. | Hominidae | Muscidae |
| **Genus** | Group of closely related species with common structural features. | *Homo* | *Musca* |
| **Species** | Basic unit of classification; individuals capable of interbreeding. | *Homo sapiens* | *Musca domestica* |

## Binomial Nomenclature

Developed by **Carl Linnaeus**, binomial nomenclature provides a universally accepted two-word scientific name for every species:

1. The first word is the **Genus** name (starts with a capital letter).
2. The second word is the **Specific Epithet** (starts with a lowercase letter).
3. Names are written in *Italics* when printed, or **underlined separately** when handwritten.
4. Names are derived from Latin or are latinised regardless of their origin.

> Example: *Mangifera indica* L. (The "L." denotes Linnaeus, the author who first described the species).

## Taxonomic Aids

Tools and resources that help in the identification and classification of organisms include:

- **Herbarium**: A repository of dried, pressed, and preserved plant specimens mounted on sheets.
- **Botanical Gardens**: Collection of living plants maintained for reference and conservation (e.g., Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew).
- **Museums**: Collections of preserved plant and animal specimens stored in jars with preservative solutions, or mounted as skeletons.
- **Zoological Parks**: Ex-situ conservation sites where wild animals are kept in protected environments under human care.
- **Taxonomic Keys**: Diagnostic tools using contrasting characters in pairs called **couplets**, where each statement is a **lead**.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Confusing similarity trends**: Common characteristics **increase** as you move down the hierarchy toward Species, but overall diversity **increases** as you move up toward Kingdom.
- **Capitalising the specific epithet**: Writing *Homo Sapiens* is incorrect; it must be *Homo sapiens*.
- **Confusing botanical and zoological terminology**: Plants use the rank **Division**, whereas animals use **Phylum**.

## Quiz

1. Which of the following taxonomic ranks contains organisms with the highest number of shared structural characteristics?
  - [ ] Kingdom
  - [ ] Phylum
  - [ ] Family
  - [x] Genus
   > As you move down the taxonomic hierarchy toward species, the number of shared (common) characteristics increases. Among the choices listed, Genus is the lowest taxonomic rank.

2. A student collects a plant specimen, presses it, dries it, and mounts it on a standard sheet along with a label detailing date, location, collector's name, and family. What taxonomic aid is being prepared?
  - [x] Herbarium
  - [ ] Botanical Garden
  - [ ] Museum
  - [ ] Monograph
   > Herbarium sheets store dried, pressed, and preserved plant specimens along with standard labels containing technical and geographical data for reference.

3. In taxonomic keys, a pair of contrasting choices used to identify an organism is known as a __________, and each individual statement within it is called a __________.
  - [ ] lead; couplet
  - [x] couplet; lead
  - [ ] taxon; rank
  - [ ] key; monograph
   > A taxonomic key uses contrasting pairs of traits called a couplet. Acceptance of one choice leads to rejection of the other, and each individual statement in the couplet is called a lead.
