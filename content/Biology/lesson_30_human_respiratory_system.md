## title: Human Respiratory System
level: intermediate → advanced

Respiration involves atmospheric gas exchange across alveolar membranes, circulatory gas transport, and cellular respiration to produce energy.

## Breathing Mechanism and Pressure Dynamics

* **Inspiration (Active Process)**:
  * Diaphragm contracts (flattens) + External Intercostal muscles contract.
  * Thoracic volume increases $\rightarrow$ Intra-pulmonary pressure drops below atmospheric pressure $\rightarrow$ Air flows **into** lungs.
* **Expiration (Passive Process)**:
  * Diaphragm relaxes (becomes dome-shaped) + External Intercostal muscles relax.
  * Thoracic volume decreases $\rightarrow$ Intra-pulmonary pressure rises above atmospheric pressure $\rightarrow$ Air flows **out of** lungs.

## Respiratory Volumes and Capacities

| Respiratory Volume / Capacity | Abbreviation | Normal Value (Adult Human) | Definition |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Tidal Volume** | TV | $\sim 500\text{ mL}$ | Volume of air inspired or expired during normal quiet breathing |
| **Inspiratory Reserve Volume** | IRV | $2500 - 3000\text{ mL}$ | Additional volume of air inspired by forceful inspiration |
| **Expiratory Reserve Volume** | ERV | $1000 - 1100\text{ mL}$ | Additional volume of air expired by forceful expiration |
| **Residual Volume** | RV | $1100 - 1200\text{ mL}$ | Volume of air remaining in lungs even after forcible expiration |
| **Vital Capacity** | VC | $3500 - 4500\text{ mL}$ | Maximum volume of air a person can breathe in after forced expiration ($\mathrm{VC = ERV + TV + IRV}$) |
| **Total Lung Capacity** | TLC | $5000 - 6000\text{ mL}$ | Total volume of air accommodated in lungs after forced inspiration ($\mathrm{TLC = VC + RV}$) |

## Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide Transport

### 1. Oxygen ($\mathrm{O_2}$) Transport
* $97\%$ bound to Hemoglobin as **Oxyhemoglobin** ($\mathrm{Hb_4O_8}$). Each Hb molecule binds up to $4\text{ molecules of } \mathrm{O_2}$.
* **Oxygen-Hemoglobin Dissociation Curve**: Sigmoid curve.
  * *Shift to Right* (Enhanced dissociation/delivery at tissues): High $\mathrm{pCO_2}$, High $[\mathrm{H^+}]$, High Temperature, Low $\mathrm{pO_2}$ (**Bohr Effect**).

### 2. Carbon Dioxide ($\mathrm{CO_2}$) Transport
* $70\%$ transported as **Bicarbonate ions** ($\mathrm{HCO_3^-}$) in plasma via erythrocyte **Carbonic Anhydrase**.
* $20-25\%$ transported bound to hemoglobin as **Carbamino-hemoglobin**.
* $7\%$ dissolved directly in blood plasma.

$$
\mathrm{CO_2 + H_2O} \xrightleftharpoons[\text{Anhydrase}]{\text{Carbonic}} \mathrm{H_2CO_3} \xrightleftharpoons{} \mathrm{HCO_3^- + H^+}
$$

## Common Exam Traps

* **Residual Volume Measurement**: Residual Volume ($\mathrm{RV}$) cannot be measured directly using a standard **Spirometer**.
* **Carbonic Anhydrase Location**: Carbonic anhydrase enzyme is present in extremely high concentrations inside **RBCs**, with only minute traces in blood plasma.

## Quiz

1. A patient undergoes spirometry evaluation. Which respiratory volume cannot be directly measured using standard spirometric testing?

* \[ \] Tidal Volume (TV)
* \[ \] Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV)
* \[x\] Residual Volume (RV)
* \[ \] Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV)

> Residual volume (the air remaining in lungs after maximal forced expiration) cannot be exhaled into a spirometer and must be measured using helium dilution or body plethysmography.

2. Which systemic physiological condition promotes the dissociation of oxygen from oxyhemoglobin at tissue capillary beds, shifting the Oxygen-Hemoglobin Dissociation Curve to the right (Bohr Effect)?

* \[ \] Low partial pressure of carbon dioxide ($\text{pCO}_2$)
* \[x\] High $\text{pCO}_2$, elevated temperature, and increased $[\text{H}^+]$ (lower $\text{pH}$)
* \[ \] High partial pressure of oxygen ($\text{pO}_2$) in systemic arterioles
* \[ \] Decreased concentration of 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG)

> High $\text{pCO}_2$, high acidity ($[\text{H}^+]$), and elevated temperature decrease hemoglobin's affinity for oxygen, shifting the curve to the right to unload oxygen to metabolically active tissues.

3. In human blood, what primary chemical form accounts for the transport of approximately $70\%$ of all metabolic carbon dioxide from peripheral tissues to the respiratory alveoli?

* \[ \] Dissolved $\text{CO}_2$ gas in blood plasma
* \[ \] Carbamino-hemoglobin bound to amino groups
* \[x\] Bicarbonate ions ($\text{HCO}_3^-$) dissolved in plasma
* \[ \] Carboxyhemoglobin bound to heme iron

> About $70\%$ of $\text{CO}_2$ diffuses into RBCs, where carbonic anhydrase converts it to carbonic acid, which dissociates into bicarbonate ions ($\text{HCO}_3^-$) transported in plasma.
