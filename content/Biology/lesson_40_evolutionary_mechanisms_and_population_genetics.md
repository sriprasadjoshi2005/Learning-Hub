## title: Evolutionary Mechanisms and Population Genetics
level: intermediate → advanced

Evolution occurs when allele frequencies within a population change over generations driven by natural selection, genetic drift, gene flow, and mutations.

## Theories of Evolution

1. **Lamarckism**: Theory of Use and Disuse of Organs; inheritance of acquired characters (disproven).
2. **Darwinism**: Evolution by **Natural Selection** based on overproduction, struggle for existence, variation, and survival of the fittest.
3. **Mutation Theory (Hugo de Vries)**: Proposed that speciation is caused by sudden, large single-step mutations called **Saltation**, rather than minor continuous variations as Darwin posited.

## Hardy-Weinberg Principle of Genetic Equilibrium

In a large, randomly mating population, allele and genotypic frequencies remain constant from generation to generation in the absence of evolutionary forces.

$$
p + q = 1 \quad \implies \quad p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1
$$

* $p$: Frequency of dominant allele ($A$).
* $q$: Frequency of recessive allele ($a$).
* $p^2$: Frequency of homozygous dominant genotype ($AA$).
* $2pq$: Frequency of heterozygous genotype ($Aa$).
* $q^2$: Frequency of homozygous recessive genotype ($aa$).

### Five Factors Distrupting Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium
1. Gene Migration / Gene Flow
2. Genetic Drift (Random change in small populations; includes **Founder Effect** and **Bottleneck Effect**)
3. Mutation
4. Genetic Recombination
5. Natural Selection

## Common Exam Traps

* **Genetic Drift Population Size**: Genetic drift operates significantly only in **small isolated populations**, whereas Natural Selection operates in populations of any size.
* **Saltation Concept**: Hugo de Vries defined **Saltation** as a single-step, large-scale mutation causing sudden speciation.

## Quiz

1. In a genetic population at Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, the frequency of a recessive allele ($a$) is determined to be $0.3$. What is the expected proportion of heterozygous individuals ($Aa$) within this population?

* \[ \] $0.09$
* \[ \] $0.49$
* \[x\] $0.42$
* \[ \] $0.21$

> Recessive allele frequency $q = 0.3$. Dominant allele frequency $p = 1 - q = 0.7$. Heterozygote frequency $2pq = 2 \times 0.7 \times 0.3 = 0.42$ ($42\%$).

2. Following a volcanic eruption, a small group of birds is blown off course to a remote island, establishing a new colony whose gene pool differs dramatically from the original mainland population. What genetic phenomenon is illustrated?

* \[ \] Stabilizing selection
* \[x\] Genetic drift (Founder effect)
* \[ \] Directional mutation pressure
* \[ \] Gene flow equilibrium

> The Founder effect is a form of genetic drift occurring when a small isolated subset of a population colonizes a new habitat, carrying a different allele frequency from the source population.

3. During the industrial revolution in England, dark-colored peppered moths (*Biston betularia*) became predominant over light-colored moths due to soot covering tree trunks. What mode of natural selection was demonstrated?

* \[ \] Stabilizing selection
* \[x\] Directional selection
* \[ \] Disruptive selection
* \[ \] Balancing selection

> Selection favored one extreme phenotype (dark melanic moths) over another due to environmental change, shifting allele frequencies in a single direction (Directional selection).
