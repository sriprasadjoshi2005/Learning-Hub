## title: Mendelian Genetics and Inheritance Patterns
level: intermediate

Mendel's cross-breeding experiments established the core laws of inheritance. Post-Mendelian discoveries later expanded these principles to include non-Mendelian inheritance patterns.

## Monohybrid and Dihybrid Cross Ratios

### Monohybrid Cross (One Trait)
Cross between parents differing in one pair of contrasting traits ($TT \times tt$):
* **$\mathrm{F_1}$ Generation**: All heterozygous tall ($Tt$).
* **$\mathrm{F_2}$ Phenotypic Ratio**: $3\ \text{Tall} : 1\ \text{Dwarf}$ ($3:1$).
* **$\mathrm{F_2}$ Genotypic Ratio**: $1\ TT : 2\ Tt : 1\ tt$ ($1:2:1$).

### Dihybrid Cross (Two Traits)
Cross between parents differing in two traits ($RRYY \times rryy$):
* **$\mathrm{F_2}$ Phenotypic Ratio**: $9\ \text{Round Yellow} : 3\ \text{Round Green} : 3\ \text{Wrinkled Yellow} : 1\ \text{Wrinkled Green}$ ($9:3:3:1$).

## Fundamental Mendelian Laws

1. **Law of Dominance**: Characters are controlled by discrete units called factors (genes) occurring in pairs. In a dissimilar pair of factors, one masks the expression of the other.
2. **Law of Segregation (Purity of Gametes)**: Alleles of a pair separate during gamete formation, so that each gamete receives only one allele. (Universal law with **no exceptions**).
3. **Law of Independent Assortment**: Alleles of two different gene pairs segregate independently of one another during gamete formation (Applies to genes located on different chromosomes).

## Diagnostic Crosses

* **Test Cross**: Crossing an organism with an unknown dominant phenotype with a **homozygous recessive** parent ($T\_ \times tt$).
  * If all progeny are dominant $\rightarrow$ Unknown is homozygous ($TT$).
  * If dominant : recessive is $1:1 \rightarrow$ Unknown is heterozygous ($Tt$).

## Common Exam Traps

* **Matching Phenotypic and Genotypic Ratios**: In cases of **incomplete dominance** and **codominance**, the $\mathrm{F_2}$ phenotypic ratio ($1:2:1$) matches the genotypic ratio ($1:2:1$).
* **Law of Segregation Has No Exceptions**: Unlike the Laws of Dominance and Independent Assortment (which have exceptions like codominance and linkage), the Law of Segregation is universally true for diploid organisms during meiosis.

## Quiz

1. A botanist crosses a true-breeding red-flowered snapdragon (*Antirrhinum majus*) with a true-breeding white-flowered plant. All $\text{F}_1$ offspring produce pink flowers. Self-pollination of the $\text{F}_1$ plants yields an $\text{F}_2$ phenotypic ratio of 1 Red : 2 Pink : 1 White. What mode of inheritance is shown here?

* \[ \] Complete dominance
* \[x\] Incomplete dominance
* \[ \] Codominance
* \[ \] Epistasis

> In incomplete dominance, neither allele is completely dominant, producing an intermediate phenotype (pink) in heterozygotes. The $\text{F}_2$ phenotypic and genotypic ratios are identical ($1:2:1$).

2. An individual with blood group type $\text{AB}$ marries a partner with blood group type $\text{O}$ ($ii$). What are the possible blood group phenotypes of their biological children?

* \[ \] $100\%\ \text{AB}$
* \[ \] $50\%\ \text{AB}\ \text{and}\ 50\%\ \text{O}$
* \[x\] $50\%\ \text{A}\ \text{and}\ 50\%\ \text{B}$
* \[ \] $25\%\ \text{A},\ 25\%\ \text{B},\ 25\%\ \text{AB},\ \text{and}\ 25\%\ \text{O}$

> The type $\text{AB}$ parent produces gametes carrying $I^A$ or $I^B$. The type $\text{O}$ parent produces only $i$ gametes. The offspring genotypes will be $I^A i$ (Type A) and $I^B i$ (Type B) in a $1:1$ ratio.

3. What is the expected phenotypic ratio resulting from a diagnostic test cross of a heterozygous tall, heterozygous round pea plant ($TtRr$) with a homozygous dwarf, wrinkled parent ($ttrr$)?

* \[ \] $9:3:3:1$
* \[ \] $3:1$
* \[x\] $1:1:1:1$
* \[ \] $1:2:1$

> A dihybrid test cross ($TtRr \times ttrr$) yields offspring with four phenotypes in equal proportions: 1 Tall Round : 1 Tall Wrinkled : 1 Dwarf Round : 1 Dwarf Wrinkled ($1:1:1:1$).
