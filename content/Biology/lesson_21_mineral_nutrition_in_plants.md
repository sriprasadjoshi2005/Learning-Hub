## title: Mineral Nutrition in Plants
level: intermediate

Plants require essential inorganic mineral elements absorbed by roots from the soil to maintain growth, metabolism, and cellular structure.

## Essential Criteria and Mineral Classification

An element is deemed **essential** if:
1. The plant cannot complete its life cycle in its absence.
2. The element's action is specific and non-replaceable by another element.
3. The element is directly involved in plant metabolism.

### Macronutrients vs Micronutrients

* **Macronutrients** (Required in concentrations $>10\text{ mmole/kg}$ of dry matter): Carbon ($\mathrm{C}$), Hydrogen ($\mathrm{H}$), Oxygen ($\mathrm{O}$), Nitrogen ($\mathrm{N}$), Phosphorus ($\mathrm{P}$), Sulfur ($\mathrm{S}$), Potassium ($\mathrm{K}$), Calcium ($\mathrm{Ca}$), Magnesium ($\mathrm{Mg}$).
* **Micronutrients** (Required in trace amounts $<10\text{ mmole/kg}$ of dry matter): Iron ($\mathrm{Fe}$), Manganese ($\mathrm{Mn}$), Copper ($\mathrm{Cu}$), Molybdenum ($\mathrm{Mo}$), Zinc ($\mathrm{Zn}$), Boron ($\mathrm{B}$), Chlorine ($\mathrm{Cl}$), Nickel ($\mathrm{Ni}$).

## Specific Physiological Roles and Deficiency Symptoms

| Element | Physiological Function | Deficiency Symptoms |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Nitrogen ($\mathrm{N}$)** | Constituent of amino acids, proteins, nucleic acids, chlorophyll | Chlorosis in older leaves first |
| **Potassium ($\mathrm{K}$)** | Osmotic regulation, stomatal opening/closing, enzyme activator | Marginal leaf necrosis |
| **Calcium ($\mathrm{Ca}$)** | Synthesis of middle lamella (calcium pectate), mitotic spindle formation | Stunted growth in young meristems |
| **Magnesium ($\mathrm{Mg}$)** | Central atom of chlorophyll ring, activates photosynthetic/respiratory enzymes | Interveinal chlorosis in older leaves |
| **Boron ($\mathrm{B}$)** | Pollen germination, carbohydrate translocation, cell elongation | Brown heart disease, loss of apical dominance |
| **Molybdenum ($\mathrm{Mo}$)** | Constituent of **nitrogenase** and **nitrate reductase** enzymes | Whiptail disease in cauliflower |

## Biological Nitrogen Fixation

Conversion of atmospheric nitrogen ($\mathrm{N_2}$) into ammonia ($\mathrm{NH_3}$):

$$
\mathrm{N_2 + 8H^+ + 8e^- + 16\ ATP} \xrightarrow{\text{Nitrogenase}} \mathrm{2NH_3 + H_2 + 16\ ADP + 16\ P_i}
$$

* **Nitrogenase Complex**: Contains $\mathrm{Mo-Fe}$ protein; highly sensitive to oxygen ($\mathrm{O_2}$).
* **Leghemoglobin**: Oxygen scavenger present in legume root nodules that keeps $\mathrm{O_2}$ levels low to protect nitrogenase.

## Common Exam Traps

* **Mobility of Deficiency Symptoms**: Deficiencies of **mobile** elements ($\mathrm{N, K, Mg}$) show up first in **older leaves**. Deficiencies of **immobile** elements ($\mathrm{Ca, S}$) appear first in **young tissues**.
* **Nitrogenase Sensitivity**: Nitrogenase requires **anaerobic** micro-conditions to function, despite symbiotic *Rhizobium* being aerobic when free-living in soil.

## Quiz

1. A farmer notes interveinal chlorosis appearing first in the older, senescent leaves of a crop plant. Which mineral deficiency is most likely responsible for this symptom?

* \[ \] Calcium
* \[x\] Magnesium
* \[ \] Boron
* \[ \] Iron

> Magnesium is a constituent of chlorophyll and is mobile within the plant. When deficient, it is mobilized from older tissues to young leaves, causing chlorosis in mature leaves first.

2. Which enzyme complex catalyzes the reduction of atmospheric nitrogen gas ($\text{N}_2$) to ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) during biological nitrogen fixation in legume root nodules?

* \[ \] Nitrate reductase
* \[ \] Nitrite reductase
* \[x\] Nitrogenase
* \[ \] Glutamine synthetase

> The Nitrogenase enzyme complex (containing Molybdenum-Iron proteins) catalyzes the energy-intensive reduction of $\text{N}_2$ gas to $2\text{NH}_3$.

3. What is the physiological function of leghemoglobin in the nitrogen-fixing root nodules of leguminous plants?

* \[ \] Direct transport of sucrose from leaves to bacteroids
* \[ \] Catalysis of ATP hydrolysis during nitrogen reduction
* \[x\] Scavenging free oxygen to maintain an anaerobic environment for nitrogenase
* \[ \] Storage of synthesized amino acids in root tissues

> Leghemoglobin is a strong oxygen scavenger that protects the oxygen-sensitive Nitrogenase enzyme by keeping free oxygen levels low inside the nodule.
