## title: Molecular Basis of Inheritance
level: intermediate → advanced

Nucleic acids ($\mathrm{DNA}$ and $\mathrm{RNA}$) are the primary informational macromolecules of life. $\mathrm{DNA}$ serves as the genetic repository in most organisms, stored and copied using its double-helical structure.

## DNA Double Helix Structure

Watson and Crick (1953) proposed the double helix model of $\mathrm{DNA}$ based on X-ray diffraction data from Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins, along with **Chargaff's Rules**:

$$
\text{Purines } (\mathrm{A + G}) = \text{Pyrimidines } (\mathrm{C + T}) \quad \implies \quad \frac{\mathrm{A}}{\mathrm{T}} = 1, \quad \frac{\mathrm{G}}{\mathrm{C}} = 1
$$

### Structural Properties of B-DNA
* Consists of two antiparallel polynucleotide chains ($5' \rightarrow 3'$ and $3' \rightarrow 5'$).
* Nitrogenous bases pair inside via hydrogen bonds: **Adenine pairs with Thymine** ($\mathrm{A = T}$, 2 hydrogen bonds); **Guanine pairs with Cytosine** ($\mathrm{G \equiv C}$, 3 hydrogen bonds).
* Helical pitch is $3.4\mathrm{\ nm}$ ($34\mathrm{\ \text{Å}}$) with approximately $10\text{ base pairs}$ per turn. Distance between adjacent base pairs is $0.34\mathrm{\ nm}$.

## Central Dogma of Molecular Biology

Proposed by Francis Crick, the central dogma describes the flow of genetic information:

$$
\text{DNA} \xrightarrow[\text{Replication}]{\text{DNA Polymerase}} \text{DNA} \xrightarrow[\text{Transcription}]{\text{RNA Polymerase}} \text{mRNA} \xrightarrow[\text{Translation}]{\text{Ribosome}} \text{Protein}
$$

> *Exception*: Retroviruses (e.g., $\mathrm{HIV}$) use **Reverse Transcriptase** to synthesize $\mathrm{DNA}$ from an $\mathrm{RNA}$ template ($\mathrm{RNA} \rightarrow \mathrm{DNA}$).

## DNA Replication Summary

* **Semi-Conservative Mechanism**: Demonstrated by Meselson and Stahl (1958) using heavy isotope nitrogen ($^{15}\mathrm{N}$ and $^{14}\mathrm{N}$) in *E. coli*.
* **Key Enzymes**:
  * *Helicase*: Unwinds double helix by breaking hydrogen bonds.
  * *DNA Polymerase III*: Synthesizes new $\mathrm{DNA}$ strand in $5' \rightarrow 3'$ direction only.
  * *Primase*: Synthesizes short $\mathrm{RNA}$ primers.
  * *DNA Ligase*: Joins **Okazaki fragments** on the discontinuous lagging strand.

## Common Exam Traps

* **Direction of Synthesis**: All polymerases ($\mathrm{DNA}$ and $\mathrm{RNA}$ polymerases) synthesize new nucleic acid strands exclusively in the **$5' \rightarrow 3'$ direction**, reading the template strand in the $3' \rightarrow 5'$ direction.
* **Chargaff’s Rule Limitation**: Chargaff's rules apply **only** to double-stranded nucleic acids ($\mathrm{dsDNA}$), not to single-stranded $\mathrm{DNA}$ ($\mathrm{ssDNA}$) or single-stranded $\mathrm{RNA}$.

## Quiz

1. A double-stranded $\text{DNA}$ sample is analyzed and found to contain $28\%$ Cytosine residues. According to Chargaff's rules, what is the percentage of Adenine in this $\text{DNA}$ molecule?

* \[ \] $28\%$
* \[x\] $22\%$
* \[ \] $44\%$
* \[ \] $56\%$

> In double-stranded $\text{DNA}$, $\% \text{Cytosine} = \% \text{Guanine} = 28\%$. Together, $\text{C} + \text{G} = 56\%$. The remaining $44\%$ is divided equally between Adenine and Thymine, giving $\% \text{Adenine} = 22\%$.

2. During $\text{DNA}$ replication in *E. coli*, which enzyme synthesizes short $\text{RNA}$ sequences required to provide a free $3'\text{-OH}$ group for $\text{DNA}$ polymerase initiation?

* \[ \] $\text{DNA}$ Ligase
* \[ \] Topoisomerase
* \[x\] Primase
* \[ \] Helicase

> $\text{DNA}$ polymerases cannot initiate synthesis *de novo*; they require a short $\text{RNA}$ primer provided by the enzyme Primase to supply a free $3'\text{-OH}$ group.

3. *E. coli* cells grown exclusively in $^{15}\text{N}$ media for many generations are transferred to standard $^{14}\text{N}$ media. After two complete rounds of replication ($2$ generations), what fraction of the double-stranded $\text{DNA}$ molecules will consist of hybrid ($^{15}\text{N}/^{14}\text{N}$) $\text{DNA}$?

* \[ \] $0\%$
* \[ \] $25\%$
* \[x\] $50\%$
* \[ \] $100\%$

> After Generation 1 in $^{14}\text{N}$, $100\%$ of $\text{DNA}$ is hybrid ($^{15}\text{N}/^{14}\text{N}$). After Generation 2, $50\%$ of the molecules will be hybrid ($^{15}\text{N}/^{14}\text{N}$) and $50\%$ will be light ($^{14}\text{N}/^{14}\text{N}$).
