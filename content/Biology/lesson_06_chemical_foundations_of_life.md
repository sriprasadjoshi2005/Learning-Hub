## title: Chemical Foundations of Life
level: foundation

All biological systems are governed by the laws of chemistry and physics. Life relies on a specific subset of chemical elements, unique molecular bonding properties, and the unusual physical properties of water to maintain homeostasis and cellular structure.

## Essential Elements of Life

Out of 92 naturally occurring elements, only about 25 are essential for life. Four elements make up approximately $96\%$ of living matter:

* **Bulk Elements**: Carbon ($\mathrm{C}$), Hydrogen ($\mathrm{H}$), Oxygen ($\mathrm{O}$), and Nitrogen ($\mathrm{N}$).
* **Macro-minerals**: Calcium ($\mathrm{Ca}$), Phosphorus ($\mathrm{P}$), Potassium ($\mathrm{K}$), Sulfur ($\mathrm{S}$), Sodium ($\mathrm{Na}$), Chlorine ($\mathrm{Cl}$), and Magnesium ($\mathrm{Mg}$).
* **Trace Elements**: Elements required in minute amounts ($<0.01\%$), such as Iron ($\mathrm{Fe}$), Iodine ($\mathrm{I}$), Zinc ($\mathrm{Zn}$), and Copper ($\mathrm{Cu}$).

## Chemical Bonds in Biological Systems

Intramolecular and intermolecular forces stabilize biomolecules and determine cellular architecture:

| Bond Type | Nature of Interaction | Relative Strength in Water | Biological Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Covalent Bond** | Sharing of electron pairs between atoms. | **Strongest** | Forms backbone of DNA, proteins, and carbohydrates. |
| **Ionic Bond** | Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. | Weakened by water molecules | Maintains protein tertiary structure, salt bridges. |
| **Hydrogen Bond** | Attraction between a hydrogen covalently bonded to an electronegative atom ($\mathrm{O}$ or $\mathrm{N}$) and another electronegative atom. | Weak individually, strong in bulk | Base pairing in DNA double helix; water cohesion; secondary protein structure ($\alpha$-helix, $\beta$-sheets). |
| **Van der Waals Forces** | Transient electrostatic interactions due to fluctuating electron densities. | Very Weak | Packing of hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails in cell membranes. |

## Unique Properties of Water and pH

Water ($\mathrm{H_2O}$) is a polar molecule capable of forming up to four hydrogen bonds with adjacent water molecules. Its key biological properties include:

1. **High Specific Heat Capacity**: Buffers cellular organisms against rapid temperature fluctuations.
2. **High Heat of Vaporisation**: Facilitates evaporative cooling (sweating in mammals, transpiration in plants).
3. **Cohesion and Adhesion**: High surface tension and capillary action facilitate ascent of sap in plants.
4. **Universal Solvent**: Dissolves polar and ionic substances by forming hydration shells around ions.

### pH and Biological Buffers

$$
\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\mathrm{H^+}]
$$

Organisms maintain internal $\text{pH}$ within narrow limits using buffer systems (e.g., the carbonic acid-bicarbonate buffer system in human blood):

$$
\mathrm{CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 \rightleftharpoons HCO_3^- + H^+}
$$

## Common Exam Traps

* **Bond Strength Context**: Ionic bonds are very strong in vacuum or dry state, but in aqueous cellular environments, **covalent bonds** are far stronger than ionic interactions.
* **pH Scale Logarithm**: A change of 1 unit on the $\text{pH}$ scale represents a **10-fold change** in $[\mathrm{H}^+]$ ion concentration, not a linear change.
* **Hydrophobic Interactions**: Hydrophobic molecules do not repel water chemically; rather, water molecules exclude nonpolar substances to maximize their own hydrogen bonding with each other.

## Quiz

1. Human blood maintains a tight $\text{pH}$ baseline near $7.40$. If metabolic acidosis causes the blood $\text{pH}$ to drop from $7.40$ to $6.40$, how has the free hydrogen ion concentration $[\mathrm{H}^+]$ changed?

* [ ] Decreased by a factor of 10
* [ ] Increased by a factor of 2
* [x] Increased by a factor of 10
* [ ] Increased by a factor of 100

> The $\text{pH}$ scale is logarithmic ($\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\mathrm{H}^+]$). A decrease of $1.0\text{ pH}$ unit corresponds to a $10^1 = 10$-fold increase in free $[\mathrm{H}^+]$ concentration.

2. Which chemical bond or interaction is primarily responsible for holding together the two antiparallel strands of a double-stranded $\text{DNA}$ molecule?

* [ ] Phosphodiester covalent bonds
* [ ] Ionic bonds between phosphate groups
* [x] Hydrogen bonds between complementary nitrogenous bases
* [ ] Disulfide linkages between deoxyribose sugars

> Phosphodiester covalent bonds build the single-strand backbone, but hydrogen bonds between complementary bases (Adenine-Thymine and Guanine-Cytosine) hold the two opposing strands together.

3. Water’s high heat of vaporisation is crucial for thermal regulation in terrestrial organisms. Which physical phenomenon directly explains why liquid water requires substantial thermal energy to transition to gas?

* [ ] Breaking of covalent $\mathrm{O-H}$ bonds within individual water molecules.
* [x] Disruption of extensive intermolecular hydrogen-bonding networks between adjacent water molecules.
* [ ] Formation of hydration shells around nonpolar solutes.
* [ ] High ionization tendency into $\mathrm{H}^+$ and $\mathrm{OH}^-$ ions at room temperature.

> Evaporating water requires breaking the dynamic network of intermolecular hydrogen bonds holding liquid water molecules together, not breaking the covalent bonds within the molecules themselves.
