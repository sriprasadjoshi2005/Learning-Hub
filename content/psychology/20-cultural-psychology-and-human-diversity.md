# Lesson 20: Cultural Psychology and Human Diversity

Cultural psychology examines how culture influences human thought, emotion, perception and behaviour. It investigates how individuals develop within cultural environments and how shared beliefs, values, language and social practices shape psychological experiences. Understanding cultural diversity is essential for interpreting human behaviour across different communities.

## 1. Meaning of Culture

Culture refers to the shared beliefs, values, customs, practices, knowledge and social expectations of a group.

Culture is transmitted through social interaction, education, communication and everyday experiences.

Examples include:
- Language.
- Food practices.
- Family structures.
- Religious and philosophical traditions.
- Social customs.
- Artistic expression.
- Communication styles.

Culture is dynamic and can change across generations.

## 2. Meaning of Cultural Psychology

Cultural psychology investigates the relationship between psychological processes and cultural environments.

It examines questions such as:
- How does culture influence self-concept?
- Do emotional expressions differ across cultures?
- How do cultural values influence decision-making?
- How does language influence cognition?
- How do social norms shape behaviour?

## 3. Cultural Transmission

Cultural transmission is the process through which knowledge, values and practices are passed between individuals and generations.

### 3.1 Vertical Transmission

Culture is transmitted from parents or older generations to younger generations.

### 3.2 Horizontal Transmission

Cultural information is transmitted between individuals of similar age or social position.

### 3.3 Oblique Transmission

Cultural information is transmitted from individuals of older generations who are not necessarily direct family members.

Examples include teachers, community members and mentors.

## 4. Individualism and Collectivism

Individualism and collectivism describe broad cultural orientations.

### 4.1 Individualism

Individualistic orientations emphasise:
- Personal autonomy.
- Individual goals.
- Independence.
- Personal choice.
- Individual achievement.

### 4.2 Collectivism

Collectivistic orientations emphasise:
- Group relationships.
- Interdependence.
- Shared responsibilities.
- Family and community obligations.
- Group harmony.

These are broad tendencies rather than fixed descriptions of every individual in a society.

Many cultures and individuals demonstrate both orientations depending on the situation.

## 5. Independent and Interdependent Self-Construal

Self-construal refers to how individuals understand and define themselves in relation to others.

### 5.1 Independent Self-Construal

The self is understood primarily through personal characteristics, preferences and individual goals.

### 5.2 Interdependent Self-Construal

The self is understood partly through relationships, social roles and group membership.

These approaches influence how individuals interpret responsibility, identity and social behaviour.

## 6. Cultural Influences on Cognition

Culture can influence how people process and organise information.

### 6.1 Attention

Research has investigated whether cultural backgrounds influence attention to objects and surrounding contexts.

Some studies have found differences in the extent to which individuals focus on central objects or contextual relationships.

### 6.2 Categorisation

Cultural learning can influence how people classify objects, events and social information.

### 6.3 Memory

Cultural practices and personal experiences can influence what individuals remember and how memories are organised.

### 6.4 Reasoning

Cultural traditions may influence preferences for analytical or context-sensitive reasoning in certain tasks.

These findings describe tendencies in particular research contexts and should not be used to assume how every person from a culture thinks.

## 7. Cultural Influences on Emotion

Culture can influence emotional expression and interpretation.

### 7.1 Display Rules

Display rules are cultural expectations about when and how emotions should be expressed.

For example, some social contexts encourage restrained emotional expression, while others encourage more open expression.

### 7.2 Emotion Vocabulary

Languages may contain words for emotional experiences that do not have exact equivalents in other languages.

Language can therefore influence how individuals describe and communicate emotions.

### 7.3 Cultural Context

The meaning of an emotional expression may depend on:
- Social relationships.
- Situational expectations.
- Cultural values.
- Communication norms.

## 8. Social Norms and Values

Social norms are shared expectations about appropriate behaviour.

They influence:
- Greetings.
- Communication.
- Dress.
- Family roles.
- Public behaviour.
- Social responsibilities.

Values are broader beliefs about what is important or desirable.

Examples include:
- Honesty.
- Respect.
- Cooperation.
- Achievement.
- Equality.
- Tradition.

Norms and values influence behaviour but are not followed identically by every member of a society.

## 9. Cultural Identity

Cultural identity refers to the sense of belonging associated with cultural groups and traditions.

It may involve:
- Language.
- Heritage.
- Shared history.
- Community relationships.
- Traditions.
- Cultural values.

Individuals may identify with multiple cultural groups.

Cultural identity can change through migration, education, relationships and personal experiences.

## 10. Acculturation

Acculturation refers to psychological and cultural changes that occur through sustained contact between different cultural groups.

John Berry proposed four broad acculturation strategies.

### 10.1 Assimilation

An individual adopts the dominant cultural practices while placing less emphasis on maintaining their original cultural identity.

### 10.2 Separation

An individual maintains their original cultural practices while having limited interaction with the wider dominant culture.

### 10.3 Integration

An individual maintains aspects of their original cultural identity while participating in the wider society.

### 10.4 Marginalisation

An individual experiences limited connection with both the original and dominant cultural groups.

These categories are theoretical frameworks. Real experiences are complex and may vary across situations.

## 11. Ethnocentrism and Cultural Relativism

### 11.1 Ethnocentrism

Ethnocentrism involves evaluating other cultures primarily through the standards and assumptions of one's own culture.

It may lead to misunderstanding and unfair judgement.

### 11.2 Cultural Relativism

Cultural relativism involves understanding cultural practices within their own social and historical contexts.

It encourages careful interpretation rather than assuming that one's own cultural standards are universal.

Cultural understanding does not require ignoring human rights or the consequences of harmful practices.

## 12. Prejudice and Stereotyping

Cultural differences may become associated with inaccurate assumptions.

### 12.1 Stereotyping

Stereotyping involves applying generalised beliefs to members of a group.

### 12.2 Prejudice

Prejudice involves evaluations or attitudes towards individuals based on group membership.

### 12.3 Discrimination

Discrimination involves unequal treatment or behaviour based on group membership.

Reducing these problems requires accurate information, meaningful interaction and fair social practices.

## 13. Cross-Cultural Psychology

Cross-cultural psychology compares psychological processes across different cultural groups.

It investigates whether findings from one cultural population apply to another.

Important considerations include:

- Language differences.
- Cultural meaning.
- Sampling methods.
- Measurement equivalence.
- Social context.
- Historical influences.

A psychological test developed in one cultural setting may not automatically be appropriate in another.

## 14. Cultural Psychology in India

India contains substantial linguistic, regional, religious, social and cultural diversity.

Psychological experiences may be influenced by:
- Family structures.
- Regional traditions.
- Languages.
- Educational environments.
- Urban and rural settings.
- Social expectations.
- Community relationships.

Indian psychological research includes both universal psychological processes and culturally situated experiences.

It is important to avoid treating India's population as culturally uniform.

## 15. Applications of Cultural Psychology

### Education

Cultural understanding helps educators recognise different backgrounds, languages and learning experiences.

### Healthcare

Cultural awareness can improve communication and understanding of health-related beliefs.

### Organisations

Cultural psychology supports cooperation in diverse workplaces.

### Counselling

Culturally informed practice helps professionals understand clients within their social and cultural contexts.

### International Communication

Understanding cultural differences can reduce misunderstandings in global interactions.

## 16. Summary

- Culture includes shared beliefs, values, practices and knowledge.
- Cultural psychology examines interactions between culture and psychological processes.
- Cultural transmission occurs vertically, horizontally and obliquely.
- Individualism and collectivism describe broad cultural orientations.
- Independent and interdependent self-construals describe different ways of understanding identity.
- Culture can influence attention, memory, emotion and reasoning.
- Social norms and values guide behaviour.
- Cultural identity can involve multiple group memberships.
- Acculturation describes changes arising from sustained cultural contact.
- Ethnocentrism evaluates other cultures through one's own cultural standards.
- Cultural relativism encourages understanding practices within their context.
- Cross-cultural research requires careful attention to measurement and cultural differences.
