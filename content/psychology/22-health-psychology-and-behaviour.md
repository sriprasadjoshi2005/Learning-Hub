# Lesson 22: Health Psychology and Behaviour

Health psychology studies how psychological, behavioural, social and biological factors influence health and illness. It investigates health-related behaviour, stress, coping, treatment adherence and the psychological experience of illness. Its central perspective is that health cannot be understood through biological processes alone.

## 1. Meaning of Health Psychology

Health psychology is the scientific study of psychological influences on health, illness and healthcare.

It examines:
- Health-related decisions.
- Lifestyle behaviour.
- Stress and coping.
- Illness-related beliefs.
- Patient behaviour.
- Treatment adherence.
- Psychological adjustment to illness.

## 2. The Biopsychosocial Model

The biopsychosocial model, associated with George Engel, explains health through the interaction of three broad categories.

### 2.1 Biological Factors

These include:
- Genetics.
- Hormones.
- Immune functioning.
- Physical injury.
- Infection.
- Physiological processes.

### 2.2 Psychological Factors

These include:
- Thoughts.
- Emotions.
- Beliefs.
- Personality.
- Coping strategies.
- Behaviour.

### 2.3 Social Factors

These include:
- Family relationships.
- Education.
- Employment.
- Culture.
- Economic conditions.
- Social support.
- Access to healthcare.

The model recognises that these factors interact rather than operating independently.

## 3. Health Beliefs and Behaviour

Health beliefs influence how individuals interpret health risks and decide whether to adopt preventive behaviour.

### 3.1 Health Belief Model

The Health Belief Model explains health-related decisions through several perceptions.

**Perceived susceptibility:** Belief about the likelihood of experiencing a health problem.

**Perceived severity:** Belief about the seriousness of the condition.

**Perceived benefits:** Belief that an action can reduce risk or improve health.

**Perceived barriers:** Belief about obstacles to taking action.

**Cues to action:** Events or information that encourage behaviour.

**Self-efficacy:** Belief in one's ability to perform the recommended behaviour.

Example:

An individual may decide to undergo a health screening after recognising personal risk, understanding its benefits and receiving a reminder.

## 4. Health-Promoting Behaviour

Health-promoting behaviour includes actions intended to maintain or improve health.

Examples include:
- Regular physical activity.
- Balanced nutrition.
- Adequate sleep.
- Preventive healthcare.
- Avoidance of harmful substances.
- Stress management.
- Healthy social relationships.

Psychological factors influence whether individuals begin and maintain these behaviours.

## 5. Risk Behaviour

Risk behaviour involves actions that may increase the probability of negative health outcomes.

Examples include:
- Smoking.
- Substance misuse.
- Unsafe driving.
- Prolonged physical inactivity.
- Risky sexual behaviour.

Risk behaviour can be influenced by peer pressure, stress, impulsivity, social norms and perceived vulnerability.

Understanding these influences helps design preventive interventions.

## 6. Stress and Health

Stress is a response that occurs when individuals perceive demands as challenging or exceeding their available resources.

Stress may involve:
- Physiological arousal.
- Emotional reactions.
- Cognitive changes.
- Behavioural responses.

### 6.1 Acute Stress

Acute stress occurs in response to immediate demands or threats.

### 6.2 Chronic Stress

Chronic stress involves prolonged or repeated exposure to stressful conditions.

Long-term stress can affect sleep, mood, concentration and physiological functioning.

## 7. Physiological Responses to Stress

The body responds to stress through interconnected systems.

### 7.1 Sympathetic Nervous System

The sympathetic nervous system contributes to the fight-or-flight response.

Possible changes include:
- Increased heart rate.
- Increased alertness.
- Changes in breathing.
- Mobilisation of energy.

### 7.2 Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal Axis

The HPA axis involves communication between the hypothalamus, pituitary gland and adrenal glands.

During stress, it contributes to the release of cortisol.

Cortisol helps regulate energy availability and other physiological processes.

Prolonged dysregulation of stress systems may contribute to health problems, although outcomes vary between individuals.

## 8. General Adaptation Syndrome

Hans Selye proposed the General Adaptation Syndrome to describe a pattern of physiological responses to prolonged stress.

It includes three stages.

### 8.1 Alarm Stage

The body detects a stressor and activates physiological stress responses.

### 8.2 Resistance Stage

The body continues responding while attempting to adapt to the ongoing stressor.

### 8.3 Exhaustion Stage

Prolonged demands may reduce the body's capacity to maintain adaptation.

The model is historically important, but actual stress responses are more complex and do not always follow a fixed sequence.

## 9. Coping Strategies

Coping refers to cognitive and behavioural efforts used to manage stressful demands.

### 9.1 Problem-Focused Coping

This involves addressing the source of stress.

Examples:
- Organising work.
- Seeking information.
- Creating a practical plan.
- Solving a problem directly.

### 9.2 Emotion-Focused Coping

This involves managing the emotional response to stress.

Examples:
- Relaxation.
- Emotional expression.
- Mindfulness.
- Seeking emotional support.

### 9.3 Meaning-Focused Coping

This involves finding meaning, purpose or positive perspectives during difficult experiences.

The usefulness of a coping strategy depends on the situation and available resources.

## 10. Social Support and Health

Social support can influence psychological adjustment and health-related behaviour.

Types include:
- Emotional support.
- Instrumental support.
- Informational support.
- Appraisal support.

Supportive relationships may help individuals manage stress and maintain health-promoting habits.

## 11. Pain and Psychological Processes

Pain involves sensory and emotional experiences associated with actual or potential tissue damage.

Pain perception is influenced by:
- Biological processes.
- Attention.
- Emotional state.
- Previous experience.
- Expectations.
- Social context.

Psychological influences do not make pain imaginary. They are part of the complex processes through which pain is experienced.

## 12. Treatment Adherence

Treatment adherence refers to the extent to which individuals follow agreed healthcare recommendations.

Barriers may include:
- Financial limitations.
- Side effects.
- Complex treatment schedules.
- Misunderstanding.
- Forgetfulness.
- Low perceived benefit.
- Lack of support.

Clear communication and shared decision-making can improve adherence.

## 13. Health Psychology Interventions

Health psychology contributes to interventions such as:

### Behaviour Change Programmes

Support individuals in adopting healthier habits.

### Stress Management

Teach relaxation, coping and problem-solving techniques.

### Health Education

Improve understanding of health risks and preventive actions.

### Motivational Interviewing

A collaborative communication approach that helps individuals explore motivation and resolve uncertainty about behaviour change.

### Psychological Support

Assist individuals in adjusting to illness and healthcare experiences.

## 14. Summary

- Health psychology examines psychological influences on health and illness.
- The biopsychosocial model integrates biological, psychological and social factors.
- The Health Belief Model explains several influences on preventive behaviour.
- Stress involves cognitive, emotional and physiological responses.
- The sympathetic nervous system and HPA axis contribute to stress responses.
- Selye proposed alarm, resistance and exhaustion stages.
- Coping may be problem-focused, emotion-focused or meaning-focused.
- Social support can influence health behaviour and adjustment.
- Pain involves biological and psychological processes.
- Health psychology contributes to behaviour change and treatment adherence.
