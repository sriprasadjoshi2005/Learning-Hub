# Lesson 42: Stress, Health and Coping Psychology

Stress is a psychological and physiological response to demands that challenge or exceed an individual's perceived ability to cope. Health psychology examines how biological, psychological and social factors interact to influence health and illness. This lesson explores stress mechanisms, coping strategies and the relationship between psychological functioning and physical health.

## 1. Understanding Stress

### 1.1 Definition of Stress

Stress is a state of psychological or physiological tension that occurs when an individual evaluates a situation as demanding or threatening.

### 1.2 Stressors

Stressors are events or conditions that produce stress.

Examples include:

- Academic pressure.
- Financial difficulties.
- Relationship conflicts.
- Workplace demands.
- Health problems.
- Major life changes.

### 1.3 Eustress and Distress

**Eustress** refers to stress that can motivate individuals and improve performance.

**Distress** refers to stress that becomes overwhelming or negatively affects functioning.

## 2. Types of Stress

### 2.1 Acute Stress

Acute stress is a short-term response to an immediate challenge.

Examples include preparing for an important presentation or responding to a sudden emergency.

### 2.2 Episodic Acute Stress

Episodic acute stress involves frequent episodes of acute stress.

It may occur when individuals repeatedly experience demanding situations.

### 2.3 Chronic Stress

Chronic stress involves prolonged exposure to stressful conditions.

Examples include persistent financial insecurity, ongoing family conflict and long-term workplace pressure.

## 3. Physiological Stress Response

### 3.1 Sympathetic Nervous System

The sympathetic nervous system activates the body's immediate response to perceived threats.

It may produce:

- Increased heart rate.
- Faster breathing.
- Increased alertness.
- Sweating.
- Increased blood flow to skeletal muscles.

### 3.2 Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal Axis

The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis is an important hormonal system involved in the stress response.

Its major sequence is:

1. The hypothalamus releases corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH).
2. CRH stimulates the anterior pituitary gland.
3. The pituitary releases adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH).
4. ACTH stimulates the adrenal cortex.
5. The adrenal cortex releases cortisol.

### 3.3 Cortisol

Cortisol is a glucocorticoid hormone that helps regulate energy metabolism and the body's response to stress.

It supports the availability of energy during demanding situations.

Prolonged dysregulation of cortisol systems may be associated with adverse health effects.

## 4. General Adaptation Syndrome

### 4.1 Introduction

Hans Selye proposed the general adaptation syndrome (GAS) to describe the body's response to prolonged stress.

It consists of three stages.

### 4.2 Alarm Stage

The body recognises a stressor and activates physiological stress responses.

The fight-or-flight response may occur during this stage.

### 4.3 Resistance Stage

The body attempts to adapt to the continued presence of the stressor.

Physiological responses remain active as the individual tries to maintain functioning.

### 4.4 Exhaustion Stage

When prolonged demands exceed the body's adaptive resources, vulnerability to physical and psychological difficulties may increase.

The model is influential, although actual stress responses are more complex than a fixed three-stage sequence.

## 5. Psychological Theories of Stress

### 5.1 Cannon's Fight-or-Flight Theory

Walter Cannon described the immediate physiological response to perceived threats.

It emphasised the role of the sympathetic nervous system in preparing the body for action.

### 5.2 Lazarus and Folkman's Transactional Model

This model explains stress through the interaction between an individual and their environment.

It emphasises cognitive appraisal.

**Primary appraisal:** The individual evaluates whether an event is irrelevant, positive or stressful.

**Secondary appraisal:** The individual evaluates available resources and possible ways of coping.

**Reappraisal:** The individual reassesses the situation as new information or experiences become available.

### 5.3 Example

A person receiving an important examination notice may interpret it as a threat because they feel unprepared.

Another person may interpret the same examination as a manageable challenge because they have sufficient preparation and support.

## 6. Coping Strategies

### 6.1 Meaning of Coping

Coping refers to the cognitive and behavioural efforts individuals use to manage stressful situations.

### 6.2 Problem-Focused Coping

Problem-focused coping attempts to change or directly address the source of stress.

Examples include:

- Creating a study schedule.
- Seeking practical assistance.
- Solving a financial problem.
- Improving time management.

### 6.3 Emotion-Focused Coping

Emotion-focused coping aims to manage the emotional response to stress.

Examples include:

- Relaxation techniques.
- Talking to supportive individuals.
- Mindfulness.
- Emotional expression.

### 6.4 Avoidant Coping

Avoidant coping involves escaping, denying or disengaging from stressful situations.

It may provide temporary relief but can worsen problems when used persistently.

### 6.5 Adaptive and Maladaptive Coping

Adaptive coping supports effective functioning and long-term adjustment.

Maladaptive coping may temporarily reduce distress while increasing difficulties over time.

## 7. Psychological Effects of Chronic Stress

### 7.1 Cognitive Effects

Chronic stress may affect:

- Attention.
- Working memory.
- Decision-making.
- Learning.
- Concentration.

### 7.2 Emotional Effects

Possible effects include:

- Irritability.
- Anxiety.
- Emotional exhaustion.
- Low mood.
- Reduced emotional regulation.

### 7.3 Behavioural Effects

Stress may contribute to:

- Social withdrawal.
- Sleep disruption.
- Changes in eating patterns.
- Reduced productivity.
- Increased substance use.

## 8. Stress and Physical Health

### 8.1 Cardiovascular System

Prolonged stress is associated with increased cardiovascular risk through several biological and behavioural pathways.

### 8.2 Immune System

Chronic stress can influence immune functioning and inflammatory processes.

### 8.3 Digestive System

Stress may affect digestive functioning and worsen symptoms in individuals with certain gastrointestinal conditions.

### 8.4 Sleep

Stress can interfere with sleep initiation, sleep quality and regular sleep patterns.

### 8.5 Psychosomatic Processes

Psychosomatic processes describe interactions between psychological functioning and physical symptoms.

Physical symptoms are real, even when psychological factors contribute to their development or severity.

## 9. Health Psychology

### 9.1 Definition

Health psychology studies how psychological, behavioural and social factors influence physical health, illness and healthcare.

### 9.2 Health Belief Model

The Health Belief Model explains health-related decisions through several beliefs:

- Perceived susceptibility.
- Perceived severity.
- Perceived benefits.
- Perceived barriers.
- Cues to action.
- Self-efficacy.

### 9.3 Theory of Planned Behaviour

The Theory of Planned Behaviour proposes that behaviour is influenced by behavioural intentions.

Intentions are shaped by:

1. Attitudes towards the behaviour.
2. Subjective norms.
3. Perceived behavioural control.

### 9.4 Biopsychosocial Model of Health

The biopsychosocial model explains health through interactions among:

- Biological factors.
- Psychological factors.
- Social factors.

It provides a broader understanding than explanations based only on biological processes.

## 10. Stress Management

### 10.1 Physical Activity

Regular physical activity can support mood regulation and general health.

### 10.2 Relaxation Techniques

Relaxation techniques include:

- Progressive muscle relaxation.
- Controlled breathing.
- Meditation.
- Guided imagery.

### 10.3 Mindfulness

Mindfulness involves paying attention to present experiences with awareness and without automatic judgement.

### 10.4 Social Support

Supportive relationships can provide emotional reassurance and practical assistance.

### 10.5 Time Management

Setting priorities, breaking large tasks into smaller steps and maintaining realistic schedules can reduce avoidable stress.

### 10.6 Professional Support

Persistent or severe stress may require assessment and support from qualified mental health professionals.

## 11. Summary

Stress involves psychological and physiological responses to perceived demands. The HPA axis and sympathetic nervous system play important roles in stress responses. Cognitive appraisal influences how individuals experience stressful situations, while problem-focused and emotion-focused coping provide different ways of managing them. Health psychology examines the interaction between biological, psychological and social influences on health.
