# Lesson 24: Environmental Psychology and Human Behaviour

Environmental psychology studies interactions between people and their physical surroundings. It examines how natural and built environments influence perception, emotion, behaviour, health and social relationships. It also investigates how human behaviour affects the environment and how psychological principles can support sustainability.

## 1. Meaning of Environmental Psychology

Environmental psychology is the scientific study of relationships between individuals and their physical environments.

It examines:
- Natural environments.
- Buildings and architecture.
- Urban spaces.
- Noise and pollution.
- Crowding.
- Personal space.
- Environmental attitudes.
- Sustainable behaviour.

The environment includes both natural settings and human-designed surroundings.

## 2. Person-Environment Interaction

People influence their environments, while environmental conditions influence human behaviour.

Examples include:
- Room design affecting concentration.
- Noise influencing communication.
- Green spaces supporting recreation.
- Crowded environments affecting comfort.
- Lighting influencing alertness.

Environmental psychology studies these interactions rather than assuming that environments affect everyone identically.

## 3. Environmental Perception

Environmental perception involves interpreting information from surrounding spaces.

It includes:
- Visual organisation.
- Spatial awareness.
- Recognition of landmarks.
- Perception of distance.
- Navigation.
- Interpretation of environmental safety.

Past experience and cultural expectations can influence how environments are perceived.

## 4. Personal Space

Personal space refers to the area individuals maintain around themselves during social interaction.

Edward T. Hall introduced the concept of proxemics to study the use of interpersonal space.

He described four broad distance categories.

### 4.1 Intimate Distance

Used in close personal relationships and situations involving physical contact.

### 4.2 Personal Distance

Commonly used during interactions with friends and familiar individuals.

### 4.3 Social Distance

Often used in formal or less personal interactions.

### 4.4 Public Distance

Used when addressing larger groups or interacting across greater distances.

These categories are broad cultural observations, not universal fixed measurements.

## 5. Crowding and Density

Density refers to the number of individuals occupying a particular area.

Crowding refers to the subjective experience of insufficient space.

High density does not always produce crowding, because perception depends on context, expectations and available control.

Crowding may influence:
- Stress.
- Privacy.
- Social interaction.
- Concentration.
- Behaviour.

## 6. Noise and Human Behaviour

Noise is unwanted or disruptive sound.

Its psychological effects may include:
- Reduced concentration.
- Communication difficulties.
- Increased annoyance.
- Sleep disruption.
- Stress.

The effects depend on intensity, duration, predictability and individual sensitivity.

Environmental design can reduce noise through sound insulation, spatial planning and noise-control measures.

## 7. Lighting and Colour

Environmental lighting influences visual performance and can affect alertness and comfort.

Important factors include:
- Brightness.
- Contrast.
- Natural light.
- Glare.
- Colour temperature.

Colour can influence perception and emotional associations, but its psychological effects depend on culture, context and individual experience.

## 8. Natural Environments and Well-Being

Exposure to natural environments may support psychological restoration and well-being.

Examples include:
- Parks.
- Forests.
- Gardens.
- Lakesides.
- Natural landscapes.

### 8.1 Attention Restoration Theory

Attention Restoration Theory, associated with Rachel and Stephen Kaplan, proposes that certain natural environments can support recovery from directed attentional fatigue.

Natural settings may provide:
- A sense of being away.
- Extent or a coherent environment.
- Fascination.
- Compatibility with personal needs.

### 8.2 Stress Reduction Theory

Stress Reduction Theory, associated with Roger Ulrich, proposes that exposure to certain natural settings can contribute to reduced stress.

Research has examined emotional and physiological responses to natural environments.

## 9. Urban Environments

Urban environments influence daily life through:
- Population density.
- Transportation.
- Housing.
- Noise.
- Green spaces.
- Social opportunities.
- Accessibility.

Urban settings can provide access to services and social opportunities while also creating challenges related to crowding, pollution and noise.

## 10. Environmental Stressors

Environmental stressors are external conditions that may produce psychological or physiological strain.

Examples include:
- Air pollution.
- Excessive noise.
- Extreme temperatures.
- Poor housing.
- Traffic congestion.
- Natural disasters.

Responses vary according to exposure, personal resources and social support.

## 11. Environmental Attitudes

Environmental attitudes are beliefs and evaluations concerning environmental issues.

They can influence:
- Recycling.
- Energy conservation.
- Water use.
- Transportation choices.
- Consumption patterns.
- Environmental protection.

However, positive attitudes do not always lead to environmentally responsible behaviour.

Practical barriers, habits and social conditions also influence action.

## 12. Pro-Environmental Behaviour

Pro-environmental behaviour refers to actions intended to reduce environmental harm or protect natural resources.

Examples include:
- Reducing unnecessary consumption.
- Reusing materials.
- Recycling.
- Conserving electricity.
- Saving water.
- Using public transport.
- Supporting conservation initiatives.

Behavioural interventions can encourage these actions through feedback, incentives, accessible facilities and social norms.

## 13. Sustainable Behaviour

Sustainable behaviour involves choices that support environmental, social and economic well-being over time.

Psychological approaches can encourage sustainability by:
- Making sustainable options easier.
- Providing clear information.
- Developing supportive social norms.
- Encouraging long-term habits.
- Offering feedback about environmental impact.

## 14. Environmental Psychology in Design

Environmental psychology contributes to the design of:

### Schools

Spaces that support concentration, collaboration and accessibility.

### Hospitals

Environments that support comfort, privacy and recovery.

### Workplaces

Spaces that encourage productivity and reduce unnecessary stress.

### Public Spaces

Accessible environments that support social interaction and recreation.

### Residential Areas

Neighbourhoods that support safety, convenience and community life.

## 15. Summary

- Environmental psychology studies interactions between people and their physical surroundings.
- Environmental perception involves interpreting spatial and sensory information.
- Proxemics examines the use of interpersonal space.
- Crowding is a subjective experience, while density is a physical measure.
- Noise and environmental stressors can affect concentration and well-being.
- Natural environments may support psychological restoration.
- Attention Restoration Theory explains recovery from directed attentional fatigue.
- Environmental attitudes influence but do not guarantee sustainable behaviour.
- Environmental psychology contributes to the design of healthier and more accessible spaces.
