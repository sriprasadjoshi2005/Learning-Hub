# Lesson 23: Organisational and Industrial Psychology

Industrial and organisational psychology applies psychological principles to workplaces. It investigates employee selection, performance, motivation, leadership, job satisfaction, organisational culture and workplace well-being. Its goal is to understand how individuals and organisations can function effectively while maintaining healthy working conditions.

## 1. Meaning and Scope

Industrial and organisational psychology is the scientific study of human behaviour in work settings.

It is commonly divided into two connected areas.

**Industrial psychology:** Focuses on employee selection, training, performance evaluation and job analysis.

**Organisational psychology:** Focuses on workplace relationships, motivation, leadership, organisational culture and employee well-being.

## 2. Job Analysis

Job analysis is the systematic process of identifying the duties, responsibilities and requirements of a job.

It examines:

### 2.1 Job Description

A job description explains:
- Main responsibilities.
- Daily tasks.
- Reporting relationships.
- Working conditions.
- Expected duties.

### 2.2 Job Specification

A job specification identifies the qualifications and characteristics required for the role.

These may include:
- Education.
- Technical skills.
- Experience.
- Cognitive abilities.
- Communication skills.

## 3. Employee Selection

Employee selection involves evaluating applicants to identify individuals suitable for a position.

Common methods include:

### 3.1 Interviews

Interviews collect information about applicants' experience, skills and suitability.

### 3.2 Ability Tests

These assess relevant cognitive or practical abilities.

### 3.3 Personality Assessment

Personality measures may provide information about characteristics relevant to a role.

### 3.4 Work Samples

Applicants complete tasks similar to those required in the job.

### 3.5 Assessment Centres

Assessment centres use multiple exercises and evaluation methods to assess applicants.

Selection methods should be reliable, valid, fair and relevant to the actual job.

## 4. Employee Training and Development

Training improves skills and knowledge required for work.

Important stages include:

1. Identifying training needs.
2. Establishing learning objectives.
3. Selecting training methods.
4. Delivering training.
5. Evaluating training outcomes.

### 4.1 Training Methods

Examples include:
- Demonstrations.
- Workshops.
- Simulations.
- Mentoring.
- On-the-job training.
- Online learning.

Training should be connected to actual job requirements.

## 5. Work Motivation

Work motivation influences effort, persistence and goal-directed behaviour.

### 5.1 Maslow's Theory

Maslow's hierarchy can be applied to workplace needs such as security, belonging, recognition and personal development.

### 5.2 Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory

Frederick Herzberg distinguished between two categories.

**Hygiene factors:**

These include salary, working conditions, organisational policies and job security.

Their absence can produce dissatisfaction.

**Motivators:**

These include achievement, recognition, responsibility and opportunities for growth.

Their presence can increase job satisfaction.

### 5.3 Expectancy Theory

Victor Vroom's expectancy theory explains motivation through three relationships.

**Expectancy:** Belief that effort can produce successful performance.

**Instrumentality:** Belief that successful performance will lead to particular outcomes.

**Valence:** The value an individual places on those outcomes.

Motivation is influenced by how strongly these relationships are perceived.

## 6. Job Satisfaction

Job satisfaction refers to an individual's overall evaluation of their work.

It can be influenced by:
- Salary.
- Work environment.
- Leadership.
- Recognition.
- Job security.
- Work relationships.
- Opportunities for development.
- Autonomy.

Job satisfaction is related to performance and retention, but the relationship is not simple or identical across workplaces.

## 7. Leadership in Organisations

Leadership involves influencing individuals and coordinating activities towards organisational goals.

### 7.1 Transactional Leadership

Transactional leadership uses clear expectations, performance monitoring and rewards or corrective actions.

### 7.2 Transformational Leadership

Transformational leadership encourages employees through vision, inspiration and support for development.

### 7.3 Servant Leadership

Servant leadership emphasises supporting employees, encouraging development and prioritising the needs of the wider group.

### 7.4 Situational Leadership

Situational approaches propose adapting leadership behaviour to the task, circumstances and capabilities of employees.

## 8. Organisational Culture

Organisational culture refers to shared values, assumptions and practices within an organisation.

It influences:
- Communication.
- Decision-making.
- Leadership expectations.
- Workplace relationships.
- Attitudes towards change.
- Employee behaviour.

A healthy organisational culture can encourage trust, cooperation and accountability.

## 9. Teamwork and Group Dynamics

Work teams involve individuals coordinating their efforts towards shared objectives.

Effective teamwork often requires:
- Clear roles.
- Shared goals.
- Communication.
- Mutual trust.
- Accountability.
- Constructive conflict management.

Poor coordination can produce duplicated work, misunderstandings and reduced performance.

## 10. Workplace Stress

Workplace stress occurs when job demands are perceived as exceeding available resources or coping capacity.

Common sources include:
- Excessive workload.
- Unclear responsibilities.
- Job insecurity.
- Interpersonal conflict.
- Limited control.
- Poor working conditions.

### 10.1 Burnout

Burnout is an occupational phenomenon associated with chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed.

The World Health Organization describes three dimensions:
- Energy depletion or exhaustion.
- Increased mental distance or cynicism towards one's job.
- Reduced professional efficacy.

Burnout is specifically associated with the occupational context and should not be used as a synonym for every form of tiredness.

## 11. Work-Life Balance

Work-life balance concerns how individuals manage responsibilities across employment and personal life.

It can be influenced by:
- Working hours.
- Flexibility.
- Family responsibilities.
- Organisational expectations.
- Personal boundaries.
- Access to support.

Workplaces can support balance through reasonable scheduling and clear policies.

## 12. Performance Appraisal

Performance appraisal is the systematic evaluation of employee performance.

Methods include:
- Rating scales.
- Goal-based evaluation.
- Behavioural assessments.
- Peer feedback.
- Multi-source feedback.

### 12.1 360-Degree Feedback

360-degree feedback collects information from multiple sources, which may include supervisors, colleagues, subordinates and the employee themselves.

It can provide a broader perspective but requires confidentiality and careful interpretation.

## 13. Workplace Diversity and Inclusion

Workplace diversity concerns differences among employees, including backgrounds, experiences, abilities and perspectives.

Inclusion involves creating conditions in which employees can participate meaningfully and receive fair treatment.

Inclusive practices can improve collaboration and help organisations use a wider range of skills and experiences.

## 14. Applications of Organisational Psychology

Organisational psychologists may contribute to:
- Recruitment.
- Employee training.
- Performance evaluation.
- Leadership development.
- Organisational change.
- Workplace safety.
- Employee well-being.
- Conflict resolution.

## 15. Summary

- Industrial and organisational psychology studies behaviour in workplaces.
- Job analysis identifies job responsibilities and requirements.
- Employee selection uses interviews, tests and work samples.
- Training develops job-related skills.
- Herzberg distinguished hygiene factors from motivators.
- Expectancy theory explains motivation through expectancy, instrumentality and valence.
- Leadership and organisational culture influence employee behaviour.
- Teamwork depends on communication and coordination.
- Burnout is associated with chronic unmanaged workplace stress.
- Performance appraisal evaluates employee performance.
- Inclusion supports fair participation and organisational effectiveness.
