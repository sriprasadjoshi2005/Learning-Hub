# Psychology syllabus plan (50 lessons, foundation to university entrance-exam level)

**Phase 1: Foundations and basic psychological processes (Lessons 1–10):** Introduction to psychology and its applications, history and development of psychology, research methods in psychology, biological foundations of behaviour, sensation and sensory processes, perception and interpretation of sensory information, consciousness, sleep and dreams, learning and conditioning, memory and forgetting, and thinking, intelligence and problem-solving.

**Phase 2: Individual behaviour, development and cognition (Lessons 11–20):** Motivation and motivational theories, emotion and emotional processes, personality concepts and theories, personality assessment and individual differences, principles and theories of human development, prenatal development, infancy and childhood, adolescence and adulthood, language and communication, cognitive development and executive functions, and introduction to social psychology and social cognition.

**Phase 3: Social, educational and applied psychology (Lessons 21–30):** Social influence including conformity, compliance and obedience, attitudes and persuasion, groups and leadership, intergroup relations, prosocial behaviour and aggression, conflict and cooperation, culture and cross-cultural psychology, educational psychology and learning environments, health psychology and stress management, organisational and industrial psychology, psychological well-being and positive psychology, and applied psychology and professional ethics.

**Phase 4: Advanced psychological theories, assessment and mental health (Lessons 31–40):** Advanced perspectives in psychology, cognitive psychology and information processing, behavioural and cognitive-behavioural approaches, humanistic and existential psychology, psychodynamic theories and defence mechanisms, neuroscience and the relationship between brain and behaviour, psychological testing and psychometrics, intelligence theories and assessment, psychological disorders and their classification, and clinical psychology and psychological intervention.

**Phase 5: Clinical, specialised and contemporary psychology (Lessons 41–50):** Anxiety, obsessive-compulsive and trauma-related disorders, mood and psychotic disorders, personality and neurodevelopmental disorders, neurocognitive disorders and cognitive impairment, psychological assessment and clinical formulation, psychotherapy approaches and therapeutic techniques, biological and medical interventions in mental health, evidence-based practice and treatment evaluation, forensic psychology and psychology in society, and integrative psychology with contemporary issues and comprehensive examination practice.

The sequence progresses from fundamental psychological concepts and basic mental processes to individual differences, human development, social behaviour, applied psychology, clinical psychology and advanced contemporary topics. It incorporates conceptual explanations, major psychological theories, historical developments, experimental research, case studies, behavioural observations, psychological assessment methods, real-world applications and evidence-based interpretations. Mathematical and statistical concepts are introduced wherever necessary to develop an understanding of psychological research and data analysis.

The final lessons consolidate important areas that cannot be explored individually within the 50-lesson structure, including diagnostic classification systems, anxiety and trauma-related conditions, mood disorders, psychotic disorders, personality disorders, neurodevelopmental conditions, neurocognitive disorders, clinical case formulation, psychological testing, reliability and validity, cognitive-behavioural therapy, psychodynamic therapy, humanistic approaches, family and group therapy, psychopharmacology, neuroscience-based interventions, treatment evaluation, forensic applications, and contemporary debates in psychology. This ensures that the curriculum provides a broad introduction to foundational and advanced psychology without exceeding the 50-lesson limit.

The examination-oriented content is designed to develop the conceptual, analytical, research-based and scientific foundations needed for university-level psychology and related entrance examinations. It also supports preparation for psychology-related topics in examinations such as CUET UG, CUET PG and other university entrance examinations, where applicable. Check the latest official syllabus for the examination being attempted; this is a broad psychology learning pathway, not an official examination syllabus.
