# Lesson 19: Interpersonal Relationships and Communication

Interpersonal relationships are connections between individuals that develop through communication, emotional experiences and social interaction. Psychology investigates how relationships begin, develop and change, as well as the roles of attachment, attraction, trust, empathy and conflict.

## 1. Meaning of Interpersonal Relationships

An interpersonal relationship is a social connection between two or more individuals.

Relationships may include:
- Family relationships.
- Friendships.
- Romantic relationships.
- Professional relationships.
- Acquaintanceships.

Relationships differ in emotional closeness, commitment, duration and social expectations.

## 2. Importance of Relationships

Social relationships contribute to several aspects of human life.

They can provide:
- Emotional support.
- Belonging.
- Companionship.
- Practical assistance.
- Social learning.
- Identity development.
- Opportunities for cooperation.

The quality of relationships can influence psychological well-being and stress.

## 3. Social Attraction

Interpersonal attraction refers to positive feelings that encourage individuals to approach or maintain contact with others.

### 3.1 Proximity

Physical or social proximity can increase opportunities for interaction.

Repeated contact may encourage familiarity.

### 3.2 Similarity

Individuals may feel attracted to others who share similar interests, values or attitudes.

Similarity can support mutual understanding and social connection.

### 3.3 Reciprocity

Reciprocity involves responding positively to individuals who show interest, acceptance or affection.

### 3.4 Physical Attractiveness

Physical appearance can influence initial impressions, although attraction is shaped by many additional factors.

### 3.5 Familiarity

Repeated exposure can increase positive feelings towards a person or object under suitable conditions.

This is related to the mere exposure effect.

## 4. Theories of Interpersonal Attraction

### 4.1 Reinforcement-Affect Theory

This approach proposes that individuals may develop positive feelings towards people associated with rewarding experiences.

Positive interactions can strengthen attraction.

### 4.2 Social Exchange Theory

Social exchange theory examines relationships through perceived rewards and costs.

Individuals may evaluate:
- Emotional satisfaction.
- Support.
- Time.
- Effort.
- Conflict.
- Commitment.

The theory does not imply that every relationship is consciously calculated. It provides one framework for understanding relationship decisions.

### 4.3 Equity Theory

Equity theory focuses on perceived fairness in relationships.

Individuals may experience dissatisfaction when they believe that contributions and benefits are seriously unequal.

Fairness is subjective and depends on relationship expectations.

## 5. Attachment in Adult Relationships

Attachment patterns can influence expectations and behaviour in close relationships.

Adult attachment research commonly discusses four broad patterns.

### 5.1 Secure Attachment

Individuals generally feel comfortable with closeness and can maintain a balance between independence and connection.

### 5.2 Anxious Attachment

Individuals may strongly seek reassurance and become concerned about rejection or abandonment.

### 5.3 Avoidant Attachment

Individuals may prefer emotional independence and feel uncomfortable with excessive closeness.

### 5.4 Fearful-Avoidant Attachment

Individuals may desire closeness while also feeling concerned about vulnerability or rejection.

Attachment patterns are not fixed identities. They may vary across relationships and change through experience.

## 6. Communication

Communication is the process of exchanging information, thoughts, emotions and intentions.

It involves both verbal and nonverbal elements.

### 6.1 Verbal Communication

Verbal communication uses spoken or written language.

Effective verbal communication often requires:
- Clarity.
- Appropriate language.
- Organisation.
- Relevant information.
- Consideration of the audience.

### 6.2 Nonverbal Communication

Nonverbal communication includes:
- Facial expressions.
- Gestures.
- Posture.
- Eye contact.
- Personal space.
- Tone and rhythm of speech.

Nonverbal signals may communicate emotional information that is not explicitly expressed in words.

### 6.3 Active Listening

Active listening involves giving attention to another person and attempting to understand their message.

It includes:
- Avoiding unnecessary interruptions.
- Asking clarifying questions.
- Reflecting important points.
- Acknowledging emotions.
- Providing relevant responses.

## 7. Empathy

Empathy involves understanding or sharing aspects of another person's emotional experience.

Two commonly discussed forms are:

**Cognitive empathy:** Understanding another person's perspective or emotional state.

**Affective empathy:** Experiencing an emotional response related to another person's feelings.

Empathy can support communication, cooperation and conflict resolution.

## 8. Self-Disclosure

Self-disclosure involves sharing personal information, thoughts or feelings with another person.

Appropriate self-disclosure can:
- Increase trust.
- Strengthen relationships.
- Improve mutual understanding.
- Encourage emotional support.

However, the degree of disclosure should consider trust, privacy and context.

## 9. Relationship Development

Relationships often develop through several interacting processes.

### 9.1 Initial Contact

Individuals become aware of one another through shared environments or social interaction.

### 9.2 Familiarity

Repeated interactions can increase comfort and recognition.

### 9.3 Mutual Understanding

Individuals learn about each other's values, preferences and experiences.

### 9.4 Trust and Commitment

Consistent and supportive interactions may strengthen trust.

### 9.5 Maintenance

Relationships require communication, respect, cooperation and adjustment.

Not all relationships follow the same sequence.

## 10. Sternberg's Triangular Theory of Love

Robert Sternberg proposed that love involves three components.

### 10.1 Intimacy

Emotional closeness, trust and connection.

### 10.2 Passion

Physical attraction and motivational aspects of romantic connection.

### 10.3 Commitment

The decision to maintain a relationship over time.

Different combinations produce different forms of love.

| Components | Type |
|---|---|
| Intimacy | Liking |
| Passion | Infatuation |
| Commitment | Empty love |
| Intimacy + passion | Romantic love |
| Intimacy + commitment | Companionate love |
| Passion + commitment | Fatuous love |
| All three | Consummate love |

The theory is a conceptual framework rather than a universal classification of every relationship.

## 11. Conflict in Relationships

Conflict occurs when individuals have incompatible needs, expectations, interests or interpretations.

Common causes include:
- Miscommunication.
- Differences in values.
- Unequal responsibilities.
- Financial concerns.
- Jealousy.
- Unmet expectations.
- Lack of trust.

Conflict is not necessarily harmful. Constructive disagreement can help clarify needs and improve understanding.

## 12. Conflict Resolution

Useful approaches include:

### Open Communication

Discuss concerns clearly and respectfully.

### Perspective-Taking

Attempt to understand the other person's viewpoint.

### Negotiation

Identify possible compromises.

### Problem-Solving

Focus on the issue rather than attacking the individual.

### Boundaries

Establish reasonable limits that protect personal well-being and mutual respect.

## 13. Social Support

Social support refers to assistance received through relationships.

Types include:

**Emotional support:** Empathy, care and reassurance.

**Instrumental support:** Practical help and resources.

**Informational support:** Advice, guidance and useful knowledge.

**Appraisal support:** Feedback that helps individuals evaluate situations.

Social support can reduce the impact of stress and improve adjustment.

## 14. Summary

- Interpersonal relationships are social connections between individuals.
- Proximity, similarity, reciprocity and familiarity can influence attraction.
- Social exchange and equity theories examine rewards, costs and fairness.
- Attachment patterns influence expectations in close relationships.
- Communication includes verbal and nonverbal processes.
- Active listening supports effective communication.
- Empathy involves understanding or sharing emotional experiences.
- Sternberg's theory describes intimacy, passion and commitment.
- Conflict can arise from differences in needs and expectations.
- Social support can be emotional, instrumental, informational or appraisal-based.
