# Lesson 27: Anxiety, Stress-Related and Obsessive-Compulsive Disorders

Anxiety-related, stress-related and obsessive-compulsive disorders involve different patterns of fear, worry, intrusive thoughts and repetitive behaviour. These conditions can interfere with daily functioning and quality of life. Understanding their symptoms and psychological mechanisms helps distinguish ordinary emotional responses from clinically significant difficulties.

## 1. Meaning of Anxiety

Anxiety is an emotional state involving apprehension, worry and anticipation of possible future threats.

It may involve:
- Increased alertness.
- Muscle tension.
- Restlessness.
- Rapid heartbeat.
- Difficulty concentrating.
- Sleep disturbances.

Anxiety is a normal response to uncertainty and danger. It becomes clinically significant when it is excessive, persistent or associated with substantial distress or impairment.

## 2. Fear and Anxiety

Fear and anxiety are related but distinct.

| Fear | Anxiety |
|---|---|
| Often responds to an immediate threat | Often concerns a possible future threat |
| May produce rapid defensive responses | May involve prolonged anticipation and worry |
| Usually linked to a specific danger | May occur without a clearly defined immediate threat |

Both involve interacting cognitive, physiological and behavioural processes.

## 3. Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety disorders involve excessive fear or anxiety that causes significant distress or functional impairment.

Important categories include:

- Generalised anxiety disorder.
- Panic disorder.
- Specific phobia.
- Social anxiety disorder.
- Agoraphobia.
- Separation anxiety disorder.

## 4. Generalised Anxiety Disorder

Generalised anxiety disorder involves persistent and excessive worry about multiple areas of everyday life.

Common features include:
- Restlessness.
- Fatigue.
- Irritability.
- Muscle tension.
- Sleep difficulties.
- Difficulty controlling worry.
- Concentration problems.

Worry may concern work, health, family, finances or ordinary responsibilities.

## 5. Panic Disorder

Panic disorder involves recurrent unexpected panic attacks and persistent concern or behavioural changes related to further attacks.

### 5.1 Panic Attacks

A panic attack is a sudden episode of intense fear or discomfort accompanied by physical and cognitive symptoms.

Possible symptoms include:
- Palpitations.
- Sweating.
- Trembling.
- Shortness of breath.
- Chest discomfort.
- Dizziness.
- Fear of losing control.
- Fear of dying.

Panic attacks can occur in different psychological conditions and do not automatically indicate panic disorder.

## 6. Phobias

A phobia is an intense and persistent fear associated with a particular object or situation.

### 6.1 Specific Phobia

Specific phobia involves marked fear of a particular object or situation.

Examples include:
- Heights.
- Certain animals.
- Blood or injections.
- Flying.
- Enclosed spaces.

### 6.2 Social Anxiety Disorder

Social anxiety disorder involves significant fear of social situations in which an individual may be judged or negatively evaluated.

Examples include:
- Public speaking.
- Meeting unfamiliar people.
- Eating in public.
- Participating in group activities.

### 6.3 Agoraphobia

Agoraphobia involves fear or anxiety about situations in which escape may be difficult or help may not be available if panic-like or other incapacitating symptoms occur.

Examples may include:
- Public transport.
- Open spaces.
- Enclosed public places.
- Crowds.
- Being outside alone.

## 7. Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) involves obsessions, compulsions or both.

### 7.1 Obsessions

Obsessions are recurrent and persistent thoughts, urges or images that are experienced as intrusive and unwanted.

Examples include:
- Fear of contamination.
- Persistent doubts.
- Intrusive aggressive thoughts.
- A strong need for symmetry.

### 7.2 Compulsions

Compulsions are repetitive behaviours or mental acts performed in response to obsessions or according to rigid rules.

Examples include:
- Repeated handwashing.
- Checking locks.
- Counting.
- Repeating words mentally.
- Arranging objects.

### 7.3 Obsession-Compulsion Cycle

A common pattern involves:

1. An intrusive thought or urge appears.
2. Anxiety or discomfort increases.
3. A repetitive action or mental ritual is performed.
4. Temporary relief occurs.
5. The cycle may repeat.

Compulsions can become reinforced because they temporarily reduce distress.

OCD is not simply a preference for cleanliness or organisation.

## 8. Related Conditions

### 8.1 Body Dysmorphic Disorder

Body dysmorphic disorder involves persistent preoccupation with perceived defects in physical appearance that are not observable or appear slight to others.

It may involve repetitive behaviours such as mirror checking or excessive grooming.

### 8.2 Hoarding Disorder

Hoarding disorder involves persistent difficulty discarding possessions, regardless of their actual value, leading to accumulation and significant impairment.

These conditions are classified within the obsessive-compulsive and related disorders category.

## 9. Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders

These disorders involve difficulties associated with exposure to traumatic or stressful experiences.

### 9.1 Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) can develop following exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury or sexual violence.

Symptoms may include:

**Intrusion:** Unwanted memories, nightmares or flashbacks.

**Avoidance:** Avoiding reminders of the traumatic event.

**Negative changes in cognition and mood:** Persistent negative beliefs, emotional detachment or difficulty experiencing positive emotions.

**Changes in arousal and reactivity:** Irritability, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle responses or sleep disturbances.

PTSD requires a clinically significant pattern of symptoms and is not an inevitable response to trauma.

### 9.2 Acute Stress Disorder

Acute stress disorder involves significant trauma-related symptoms occurring during the period from three days to one month after a traumatic event.

Some individuals with acute stress disorder recover, while others may later develop PTSD.

### 9.3 Adjustment Disorder

Adjustment disorder involves emotional or behavioural symptoms arising in response to an identifiable stressor.

The reaction produces significant distress or impairment beyond what would be expected in the individual's context.

## 10. Psychological Explanations

Different approaches explain anxiety and related disorders through different mechanisms.

### 10.1 Behavioural Explanation

Fear may develop through classical conditioning.

Avoidance can be maintained through negative reinforcement when avoiding a feared situation reduces anxiety.

### 10.2 Cognitive Explanation

Anxiety may be influenced by:
- Overestimating danger.
- Underestimating coping ability.
- Catastrophic thinking.
- Selective attention to threats.
- Intolerance of uncertainty.

### 10.3 Biological Explanation

Biological research examines:
- Genetic vulnerability.
- Brain circuits involved in threat processing.
- Neurotransmitter systems.
- Stress-response systems.

No single biological factor explains every case.

### 10.4 Psychological and Social Factors

Developmental experiences, social support, stressful events and environmental conditions may influence vulnerability and recovery.

## 11. Treatment Approaches

Treatment depends on the specific condition, severity, individual needs and professional assessment.

### 11.1 Cognitive Behavioural Therapy

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) helps individuals identify and modify unhelpful thought patterns and behaviours.

It may involve:
- Cognitive restructuring.
- Behavioural experiments.
- Exposure-based methods.
- Skills development.

### 11.2 Exposure Therapy

Exposure therapy involves carefully planned contact with feared situations or stimuli.

It aims to reduce avoidance and support new learning about safety and coping.

### 11.3 Exposure and Response Prevention

Exposure and response prevention (ERP) is an important psychological treatment for OCD.

It involves exposure to triggers while gradually resisting compulsive responses.

### 11.4 Medication

Healthcare professionals may prescribe medication for certain anxiety and related disorders.

Treatment decisions require consideration of individual circumstances, benefits and potential risks.

## 12. The Role of Avoidance

Avoidance can provide immediate relief from anxiety.

However, repeated avoidance may prevent individuals from learning that a feared situation can be managed safely.

This can maintain anxiety over time.

Treatment may therefore focus on reducing avoidance through gradual, appropriate and professionally guided methods.

## 13. Summary

- Anxiety is a normal response that can become clinically significant when excessive or impairing.
- Fear generally concerns immediate threats, while anxiety often concerns anticipated threats.
- Anxiety disorders include generalised anxiety disorder, panic disorder and phobias.
- OCD involves obsessions, compulsions or both.
- Body dysmorphic disorder and hoarding disorder are related conditions.
- PTSD and acute stress disorder are trauma-related conditions.
- Behavioural, cognitive, biological and social factors can influence these disorders.
- CBT is an important treatment approach.
- Exposure and response prevention is particularly relevant to OCD.
- Avoidance may temporarily reduce anxiety while maintaining it over time.
