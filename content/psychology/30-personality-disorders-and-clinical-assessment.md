# Lesson 30: Personality Disorders and Clinical Assessment

Personality disorders involve enduring patterns of inner experience and behaviour that differ significantly from cultural expectations and create difficulties in important areas of functioning. Clinical assessment examines personality patterns, their development and their impact on relationships and daily life. This lesson introduces major personality disorder categories and the principles of responsible clinical evaluation.

## 1. Meaning of Personality Disorders

Personality disorders are characterised by persistent patterns of thinking, emotional experience, interpersonal functioning and behaviour that produce significant difficulties.

These patterns are generally:
- Pervasive across different situations.
- Relatively inflexible.
- Associated with distress or impairment.
- Evident over an extended period.
- Traceable to adolescence or early adulthood in many cases.

A personality disorder is not simply an unusual personality trait or a temporary reaction to stress.

## 2. Personality Traits and Personality Disorders

Personality traits exist along dimensions.

A person may be highly reserved, emotionally sensitive or strongly independent without having a personality disorder.

A disorder involves a broader pattern of difficulties affecting functioning.

Clinical evaluation considers:
- Severity.
- Stability.
- Flexibility.
- Cultural context.
- Relationships.
- Occupational functioning.
- Personal distress.

## 3. Classification of Personality Disorders

The DSM-5-TR groups ten personality disorders into three broad clusters.

### Cluster A: Odd or Eccentric Patterns

- Paranoid personality disorder.
- Schizoid personality disorder.
- Schizotypal personality disorder.

### Cluster B: Dramatic, Emotional or Erratic Patterns

- Antisocial personality disorder.
- Borderline personality disorder.
- Histrionic personality disorder.
- Narcissistic personality disorder.

### Cluster C: Anxious or Fearful Patterns

- Avoidant personality disorder.
- Dependent personality disorder.
- Obsessive-compulsive personality disorder.

These clusters are classification groupings and do not imply that all individuals within a cluster behave similarly.

## 4. Cluster A Personality Disorders

### 4.1 Paranoid Personality Disorder

This disorder involves a pervasive pattern of distrust and suspiciousness.

Possible characteristics include:
- Interpreting others' intentions as harmful.
- Doubting the loyalty of others.
- Reluctance to share personal information.
- Persistent suspicion without sufficient evidence.

### 4.2 Schizoid Personality Disorder

This disorder involves a persistent pattern of detachment from social relationships and restricted emotional expression.

Possible characteristics include:
- Preference for solitary activities.
- Limited desire for close relationships.
- Reduced interest in social interaction.
- Restricted outward emotional expression.

### 4.3 Schizotypal Personality Disorder

This disorder involves social and interpersonal difficulties along with unusual patterns of thinking, perception and behaviour.

Possible characteristics include:
- Unusual beliefs.
- Odd speech or behaviour.
- Social anxiety.
- Unusual perceptual experiences.
- Suspiciousness.

## 5. Cluster B Personality Disorders

### 5.1 Antisocial Personality Disorder

This disorder involves a pervasive pattern of disregard for and violation of the rights of others.

Possible characteristics include:
- Repeated unlawful behaviour.
- Deceitfulness.
- Impulsivity.
- Irritability or aggression.
- Reckless disregard for safety.
- Irresponsibility.
- Lack of remorse.

Diagnosis requires specific developmental criteria, including evidence of conduct disorder before adulthood.

### 5.2 Borderline Personality Disorder

Borderline personality disorder involves instability in relationships, self-image and emotional regulation, along with marked impulsivity.

Possible characteristics include:
- Intense fear of abandonment.
- Unstable relationships.
- Rapid emotional changes.
- Unstable self-image.
- Impulsive behaviour.
- Recurrent self-harm or suicidal behaviour in some individuals.
- Chronic feelings of emptiness.

Symptoms vary, and individuals may benefit substantially from appropriate treatment.

### 5.3 Histrionic Personality Disorder

This disorder involves a pervasive pattern of excessive emotionality and attention-seeking behaviour.

Possible characteristics include:
- Strong desire to be the centre of attention.
- Dramatic emotional expression.
- Behaviour influenced by social attention.
- Overestimation of relationship intimacy.

### 5.4 Narcissistic Personality Disorder

This disorder involves a pervasive pattern of grandiosity, need for admiration and difficulties involving empathy.

Possible characteristics include:
- Exaggerated sense of self-importance.
- Strong need for admiration.
- Sense of entitlement.
- Exploitative interpersonal behaviour.
- Difficulty recognising others' needs.

Narcissistic traits alone do not establish a personality disorder.

## 6. Cluster C Personality Disorders

### 6.1 Avoidant Personality Disorder

This disorder involves social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy and heightened sensitivity to negative evaluation.

Possible characteristics include:
- Avoidance of social activities.
- Fear of criticism.
- Reluctance to form relationships without reassurance.
- Feelings of inferiority.

### 6.2 Dependent Personality Disorder

This disorder involves an excessive need to be cared for, leading to submissive and dependent behaviour.

Possible characteristics include:
- Difficulty making everyday decisions independently.
- Excessive need for reassurance.
- Fear of losing support.
- Difficulty disagreeing with others.

### 6.3 Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder

This disorder involves a persistent pattern of preoccupation with orderliness, perfectionism and control.

Possible characteristics include:
- Excessive attention to rules.
- Rigid perfectionism.
- Difficulty delegating tasks.
- Excessive devotion to work.
- Difficulty adapting to unexpected changes.

Obsessive-compulsive personality disorder is different from obsessive-compulsive disorder.

## 7. Comparison: OCD and OCPD

| Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder | Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder |
|---|---|
| Involves obsessions, compulsions or both | Involves a pervasive personality pattern |
| Repetitive behaviours may be performed to reduce distress | Rigid standards and control are central characteristics |
| Symptoms may be experienced as unwanted | Patterns may be experienced as appropriate or necessary |
| Classified among obsessive-compulsive and related disorders | Classified among personality disorders |

The two conditions can sometimes occur together.

## 8. Development and Causes

Personality disorders are understood through interactions among biological, psychological and social influences.

### 8.1 Genetic Influences

Inherited characteristics may contribute to temperament and personality vulnerability.

### 8.2 Developmental Experiences

Early relationships and childhood experiences may influence interpersonal expectations and emotional regulation.

### 8.3 Environmental Factors

Social conditions, chronic stress and adverse experiences may contribute to developmental difficulties.

### 8.4 Cognitive and Emotional Processes

Persistent patterns of interpreting social situations and regulating emotions may contribute to personality-related difficulties.

No single factor explains every personality disorder.

## 9. Clinical Assessment

Clinical assessment involves collecting information about personality patterns and their effects on functioning.

Methods may include:

### 9.1 Clinical Interview

Explores personal history, relationships, behaviour and current difficulties.

### 9.2 Structured Diagnostic Interview

Uses standardised questions to assess diagnostic criteria systematically.

### 9.3 Personality Inventories

Standardised questionnaires measure personality characteristics and clinical patterns.

### 9.4 Behavioural Observation

Observations may provide information about communication, emotional expression and interpersonal behaviour.

### 9.5 Collateral Information

With appropriate consent and safeguards, information from relevant individuals may help clarify long-term patterns.

## 10. Differential Diagnosis

Personality disorder symptoms may overlap with other psychological conditions.

Assessment should consider:
- Mood disorders.
- Anxiety disorders.
- Trauma-related disorders.
- Psychotic disorders.
- Neurodevelopmental conditions.
- Substance-related conditions.
- Medical explanations.

Clinicians must distinguish enduring personality patterns from temporary changes caused by another condition.

## 11. Treatment Approaches

Treatment depends on the specific difficulties and individual needs.

### 11.1 Psychotherapy

Psychotherapy is an important treatment approach.

It may focus on:
- Emotional regulation.
- Interpersonal skills.
- Self-understanding.
- Cognitive patterns.
- Behavioural change.

### 11.2 Dialectical Behaviour Therapy

Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) was originally developed to help individuals experiencing severe emotional dysregulation and recurrent self-harm.

It emphasises:
- Mindfulness.
- Distress tolerance.
- Emotion regulation.
- Interpersonal effectiveness.

### 11.3 Schema Therapy

Schema therapy focuses on persistent patterns of thinking, feeling and behaving that develop through life experiences.

### 11.4 Mentalisation-Based Therapy

Mentalisation-based therapy aims to improve the ability to understand one's own and others' mental states.

### 11.5 Medication

Medication may be considered for specific co-occurring symptoms or conditions, but it does not directly eliminate an underlying personality pattern.

Treatment decisions require qualified clinical assessment.

## 12. Stigma and Responsible Language

Personality disorders are frequently associated with inaccurate stereotypes.

Responsible discussion should:
- Avoid reducing individuals to diagnostic labels.
- Recognise variation in symptoms.
- Respect personal dignity.
- Avoid assuming dangerousness.
- Recognise the possibility of improvement.
- Use evidence-based information.

A diagnosis should help guide understanding and support, not define an individual's entire identity.

## 13. Summary

- Personality disorders involve enduring and inflexible patterns that cause significant impairment.
- DSM-5-TR groups ten personality disorders into three clusters.
- Cluster A includes paranoid, schizoid and schizotypal personality disorders.
- Cluster B includes antisocial, borderline, histrionic and narcissistic personality disorders.
- Cluster C includes avoidant, dependent and obsessive-compulsive personality disorders.
- Obsessive-compulsive disorder is different from obsessive-compulsive personality disorder.
- Personality disorders involve interacting biological, developmental and social influences.
- Clinical assessment uses interviews, inventories and other relevant information.
- Differential diagnosis distinguishes personality patterns from other conditions.
- Psychotherapy can support emotional, cognitive and interpersonal improvement.
- Responsible language helps reduce stigma and preserve individual dignity.
