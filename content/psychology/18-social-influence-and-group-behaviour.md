# Lesson 18: Social Influence and Group Behaviour

Social influence refers to the ways in which individuals change their thoughts, feelings or behaviour in response to other people. Group behaviour examines how individuals act when they belong to or interact with groups. These processes help explain conformity, obedience, cooperation, leadership and collective decision-making.

## 1. Meaning of Social Influence

Social influence occurs when the presence, actions or expectations of other people affect an individual's behaviour or attitudes.

Examples include:
- Following group rules.
- Changing an opinion after discussion.
- Obeying an authority figure.
- Adopting social customs.
- Helping others because of group expectations.

Social influence may be direct or indirect.

## 2. Types of Social Influence

Three important forms are conformity, compliance and obedience.

### 2.1 Conformity

Conformity involves changing behaviour or attitudes to align with group expectations or perceived social norms.

Example:

An individual may adopt a particular style of behaviour because most members of their peer group follow it.

### 2.2 Compliance

Compliance occurs when an individual changes behaviour in response to a direct request.

The person may comply without necessarily agreeing internally.

Example:

A person agrees to complete a task after a colleague requests assistance.

### 2.3 Obedience

Obedience involves following instructions given by an authority figure.

Example:

An employee follows a supervisor's legitimate workplace instructions.

## 3. Asch's Conformity Experiment

Solomon Asch conducted experiments investigating conformity.

Participants were asked to compare the lengths of lines while surrounded by other individuals who were instructed to give incorrect answers on certain trials.

Some participants conformed to the incorrect group response even when the correct answer was visually apparent.

The research demonstrated the influence of group pressure on individual judgements.

### 3.1 Factors Influencing Conformity

Conformity may increase when:
- Group size increases, particularly from a small group.
- The group is highly united.
- The task is difficult or ambiguous.
- The individual lacks confidence.
- Public responses are required.

The presence of even one supportive person may reduce conformity pressure in certain situations.

## 4. Informational and Normative Social Influence

### 4.1 Informational Social Influence

Informational influence occurs when individuals follow others because they believe the group possesses useful or accurate information.

Example:

A person follows experienced hikers during an unfamiliar mountain route.

This influence is particularly likely in uncertain situations.

### 4.2 Normative Social Influence

Normative influence occurs when individuals conform to gain acceptance or avoid social rejection.

Example:

A person agrees with a group opinion to avoid embarrassment.

### 4.3 Comparison

| Informational influence | Normative influence |
|---|---|
| Based on the desire for accurate information | Based on the desire for social acceptance |
| Often involves uncertainty | Often involves social pressure |
| May produce private acceptance | May produce public compliance without private agreement |

## 5. Milgram's Obedience Studies

Stanley Milgram conducted experiments investigating obedience to authority.

Participants were instructed to administer what they believed were increasingly intense electric shocks to another person when incorrect answers were given.

The shocks were simulated, and the other person was an actor.

The experiments demonstrated that situational pressures and perceived authority can strongly influence behaviour.

### 5.1 Factors Influencing Obedience

Important factors included:
- Perceived legitimacy of authority.
- Physical proximity of the authority figure.
- Distance from the person affected.
- Institutional context.
- Presence of dissenting individuals.

The studies raised important ethical questions about deception, psychological stress and informed consent.

## 6. Group Behaviour

A group consists of two or more individuals who interact and influence one another.

Groups may be organised around:
- Shared goals.
- Relationships.
- Common interests.
- Social identity.
- Tasks and responsibilities.

Examples include families, sports teams, classrooms and workplace teams.

## 7. Group Norms and Roles

### 7.1 Group Norms

Group norms are shared expectations about appropriate behaviour within a group.

They can regulate:
- Communication.
- Dress.
- Responsibilities.
- Decision-making.
- Social interaction.

### 7.2 Group Roles

A group role refers to expected behaviour associated with a particular position within a group.

Examples include:
- Leader.
- Coordinator.
- Adviser.
- Mediator.
- Team member.

Clear roles can support coordination, while unclear roles may produce conflict.

## 8. Group Cohesion

Group cohesion refers to the forces that encourage members to remain connected to a group.

It may be influenced by:
- Shared goals.
- Trust.
- Positive relationships.
- Group identity.
- Mutual support.

High cohesion can improve cooperation, but it may also increase pressure to agree with group expectations.

## 9. Groupthink

Groupthink is a decision-making pattern in which the desire for group agreement discourages critical evaluation of alternatives.

Irving Janis developed the concept.

Possible symptoms include:
- Pressure on dissenting members.
- Self-censorship.
- Illusions of unanimity.
- Overconfidence in group decisions.
- Neglect of alternative explanations.

### 9.1 Preventing Groupthink

Possible strategies include:
- Encouraging disagreement.
- Assigning independent evaluators.
- Considering alternative explanations.
- Seeking external opinions.
- Allowing anonymous feedback.
- Dividing large groups into smaller discussion teams.

## 10. Social Facilitation

Social facilitation refers to changes in performance that occur in the presence of other people.

The presence of others may improve performance on well-learned or relatively simple tasks.

It may impair performance on difficult or unfamiliar tasks.

### 10.1 Evaluation Apprehension

Evaluation apprehension refers to concern about being judged by others.

It can influence performance when individuals believe that their actions are being evaluated.

## 11. Social Loafing

Social loafing occurs when individuals exert less effort on a collective task than they would when working individually.

It may increase when:
- Individual contributions are difficult to identify.
- Personal responsibility is unclear.
- The task seems unimportant.
- Group size is large.

Social loafing can be reduced through clear responsibilities, meaningful goals and individual accountability.

## 12. Deindividuation

Deindividuation refers to reduced self-awareness and reduced concern about individual evaluation in certain group situations.

It may occur when anonymity and group immersion reduce personal accountability.

Deindividuation does not automatically produce aggression. Its effects depend on group norms and situational conditions.

## 13. Leadership

Leadership involves influencing and coordinating individuals towards shared objectives.

### 13.1 Autocratic Leadership

The leader makes decisions with limited participation from group members.

This may be useful in urgent situations but can reduce participation.

### 13.2 Democratic Leadership

The leader encourages discussion and participation in decision-making.

This may improve involvement and acceptance of group decisions.

### 13.3 Laissez-Faire Leadership

The leader provides substantial independence to group members.

This can work when members are capable and motivated but may create confusion when guidance is needed.

### 13.4 Transformational Leadership

Transformational leaders encourage change through a shared vision, inspiration and support for development.

## 14. Cooperation and Competition

**Cooperation:** Individuals work together towards shared goals.

**Competition:** Individuals or groups pursue goals that may conflict with one another.

Both can influence performance and relationships.

Cooperation can encourage trust and collective problem-solving, while competition may increase effort in some situations but can also produce conflict.

## 15. Summary

- Social influence changes thoughts, attitudes or behaviour through social interaction.
- Conformity involves aligning with group expectations.
- Compliance involves responding to a request.
- Obedience involves following authority.
- Asch investigated conformity, while Milgram investigated obedience.
- Informational influence concerns accuracy, while normative influence concerns acceptance.
- Groups develop norms, roles and cohesion.
- Groupthink can weaken critical decision-making.
- Social facilitation concerns performance in the presence of others.
- Social loafing involves reduced individual effort in group tasks.
- Leadership styles influence group functioning.
- Cooperation and competition shape relationships and performance.
