# Lesson 7: Consciousness, Sleep and Dreams

Consciousness refers to awareness of oneself and the surrounding environment. It includes experiences such as thoughts, feelings, sensations, memories and perceptions. Sleep, dreams, attention and altered states of awareness provide important ways to investigate consciousness.

## 1. Meaning of Consciousness

Consciousness is the state of being aware of internal experiences and external surroundings.

It may involve:

- Awareness of the environment.
- Awareness of one's body.
- Thoughts and memories.
- Emotional experiences.
- Attention and subjective experiences.

Consciousness is a complex topic studied in psychology, neuroscience, philosophy and cognitive science.

## 2. Levels and States of Consciousness

Consciousness can vary in intensity and content.

Examples include:

**Alert wakefulness:** A state of active awareness and responsiveness.

**Relaxed wakefulness:** A state of reduced activity while remaining conscious.

**Sleep:** A naturally recurring state involving changes in awareness and responsiveness.

**Dreaming:** Experiences that may occur during sleep.

**Altered states:** Changes in ordinary awareness associated with certain conditions or practices.

## 3. Circadian Rhythms

Circadian rhythms are biological cycles that follow an approximately 24-hour pattern.

They influence:

- Sleep and wakefulness.
- Body temperature.
- Hormone release.
- Alertness.
- Metabolic processes.

### 3.1 Biological Clock

The suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN), located in the hypothalamus, plays a major role in regulating circadian rhythms.

Light information received through the eyes helps synchronise the biological clock with the external day-night cycle.

### 3.2 Melatonin

Melatonin is a hormone produced mainly by the pineal gland.

Its secretion generally increases in darkness and contributes to the regulation of sleep timing.

## 4. Sleep

Sleep is a naturally recurring biological state involving changes in brain activity, awareness and responsiveness.

Sleep supports several important functions, including:

- Memory consolidation.
- Emotional regulation.
- Physical restoration.
- Immune functioning.
- Learning and cognitive performance.

## 5. Stages of Sleep

Sleep is broadly divided into non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep and rapid eye movement (REM) sleep.

### 5.1 NREM Stage N1

N1 is the lightest stage of sleep.

Characteristics include:

- Transition from wakefulness to sleep.
- Reduced awareness of surroundings.
- Slow eye movements.
- Relatively easy awakening.

### 5.2 NREM Stage N2

N2 is a deeper stage of sleep than N1.

Characteristics include:

- Reduced awareness.
- Sleep spindles.
- K-complexes.
- Reduced responsiveness to the environment.

Sleep spindles and K-complexes are characteristic electrical patterns observed in brain recordings.

### 5.3 NREM Stage N3

N3 is commonly called slow-wave or deep sleep.

Characteristics include:

- Prominent slow delta waves.
- Reduced responsiveness.
- Physical restoration.
- Difficulty waking compared with lighter sleep stages.

### 5.4 REM Sleep

REM stands for rapid eye movement.

Characteristics include:

- Rapid eye movements.
- Increased brain activity.
- Vivid dreaming in many cases.
- Temporary reduction of most skeletal muscle activity.

REM sleep is important in several memory and emotional processes.

### 5.5 Sleep Cycles

Sleep stages occur in repeating cycles throughout the night.

A complete cycle commonly lasts approximately 90 minutes, although duration varies.

NREM and REM sleep alternate, and their proportions change throughout the night.

## 6. Dreams

Dreams are experiences involving images, thoughts, emotions and sensations during sleep.

They can occur during different sleep stages, although vivid and elaborate dreams are frequently associated with REM sleep.

### 6.1 Psychoanalytic Interpretation

Sigmund Freud proposed that dreams could express unconscious wishes and conflicts.

He distinguished between:

- **Manifest content:** The remembered storyline of a dream.
- **Latent content:** The underlying symbolic meaning proposed by Freud.

This is a historical theoretical interpretation rather than an established universal explanation of dreaming.

### 6.2 Activation-Synthesis Theory

The activation-synthesis theory proposes that dreams emerge partly from the brain's attempt to organise and interpret internally generated neural activity during sleep.

### 6.3 Cognitive Approaches

Cognitive approaches examine how dreams relate to memory, emotions, thinking and waking experiences.

No single theory fully explains every aspect of dreaming.

## 7. Sleep Deprivation

Sleep deprivation occurs when an individual receives insufficient sleep.

Possible effects include:

- Reduced attention.
- Slower reaction time.
- Difficulty remembering information.
- Irritability.
- Reduced decision-making ability.
- Increased risk of accidents.

Long-term insufficient sleep may also affect physical and mental health.

## 8. Sleep Disorders

### 8.1 Insomnia

Insomnia involves persistent difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep or obtaining restorative sleep despite adequate opportunity.

### 8.2 Sleep Apnea

Sleep apnea involves repeated interruptions or reductions in breathing during sleep.

It may cause fragmented sleep and daytime tiredness.

### 8.3 Narcolepsy

Narcolepsy is a neurological sleep disorder associated with excessive daytime sleepiness and, in some cases, sudden episodes of muscle weakness called cataplexy.

### 8.4 Sleepwalking

Sleepwalking involves performing certain behaviours while not fully awake, usually during NREM sleep.

### 8.5 Nightmares

Nightmares are disturbing dreams that may cause strong emotional reactions and awakening.

## 9. Altered States of Consciousness

Altered states involve changes in ordinary patterns of awareness.

Examples include:

- Meditation.
- Hypnosis.
- Deep relaxation.
- Certain sleep-related states.
- Effects of psychoactive substances.

These states differ in their characteristics and should not be treated as identical experiences.

## 10. Hypnosis

Hypnosis is a procedure involving focused attention, reduced awareness of peripheral distractions and increased responsiveness to suggestions.

It has been investigated in relation to pain management, attention and certain psychological treatments.

Hypnosis does not guarantee complete control over another person's behaviour and does not make individuals automatically reveal hidden truths.

## 11. Meditation

Meditation includes practices that train attention and awareness.

Common approaches include:

- Focused-attention meditation.
- Open-monitoring meditation.
- Mindfulness practices.

Meditation may help some individuals improve attention and manage stress.

Its effects vary depending on the person, practice and context.

## 12. Psychoactive Substances

Psychoactive substances influence brain activity and can alter consciousness, perception, mood or behaviour.

Broad categories include:

**Stimulants:** Increase certain forms of nervous system activity and alertness.

**Depressants:** Reduce nervous system activity.

**Hallucinogens:** Can produce significant changes in perception and experience.

**Opioids:** Act on opioid receptors and can reduce pain, but some carry a substantial risk of dependence and overdose.

The effects depend on the substance, amount, individual characteristics and circumstances.

## 13. Summary

- Consciousness involves awareness of internal and external experiences.
- Circadian rhythms regulate approximately 24-hour biological cycles.
- The suprachiasmatic nucleus plays a central role in regulating circadian timing.
- Sleep includes NREM and REM stages.
- NREM sleep includes N1, N2 and N3.
- Sleep supports memory, restoration and emotional regulation.
- Dreams have been explained through psychoanalytic, activation-synthesis and cognitive approaches.
- Sleep deprivation can impair attention and cognitive performance.
- Meditation and hypnosis can involve altered patterns of awareness.
- Psychoactive substances can influence mood, perception and consciousness.
