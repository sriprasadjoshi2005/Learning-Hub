# Lesson 38: Eating and Sleep-Wake Disorders

Eating and sleep are essential biological processes that influence physical health, emotional regulation and cognitive functioning. Psychological difficulties involving eating patterns and sleep can significantly affect everyday life. This lesson examines major eating disorders and sleep-wake disorders, their causes and treatment approaches.

## 1. Understanding Eating Behaviour

### 1.1 Biological Regulation of Eating

Eating behaviour is influenced by biological mechanisms that regulate hunger, appetite and energy balance.

Important influences include:

- Hunger signals.
- Satiety signals.
- Hormonal activity.
- Brain reward systems.
- Energy requirements.

### 1.2 Psychological Influences

Eating behaviour is also influenced by:

- Emotional states.
- Body image.
- Cultural expectations.
- Family environments.
- Learned habits.
- Social pressures.

### 1.3 Eating Disorders

Eating disorders involve persistent disturbances in eating behaviour or related patterns that significantly affect physical health and psychological functioning.

## 2. Anorexia Nervosa

### 2.1 Definition

Anorexia nervosa is an eating disorder involving persistent restriction of energy intake, significantly low body weight relative to the individual's circumstances, intense fear of gaining weight or persistent behaviour that interferes with weight gain, and disturbances in body image or recognition of the seriousness of low body weight.

### 2.2 Major Characteristics

Possible features include:

- Severe restriction of food intake.
- Intense fear of weight gain.
- Distorted perception of body shape or weight.
- Excessive concern about body image.
- Behaviours that prevent weight restoration.

### 2.3 Health Consequences

Anorexia nervosa can affect multiple body systems.

Possible complications include:

- Nutritional deficiencies.
- Hormonal disturbances.
- Reduced bone density.
- Cardiovascular complications.
- Weakness and fatigue.

It can become medically life-threatening.

## 3. Bulimia Nervosa

### 3.1 Definition

Bulimia nervosa involves recurrent episodes of binge eating followed by inappropriate compensatory behaviours intended to prevent weight gain.

### 3.2 Binge Eating

A binge-eating episode involves consuming an unusually large amount of food within a discrete period while experiencing a sense of loss of control.

### 3.3 Compensatory Behaviours

These may include:

- Self-induced vomiting.
- Misuse of laxatives.
- Fasting.
- Excessive exercise.

### 3.4 Possible Consequences

Bulimia nervosa may lead to:

- Electrolyte imbalances.
- Dental damage.
- Gastrointestinal problems.
- Dehydration.
- Cardiovascular complications.

## 4. Binge-Eating Disorder

### 4.1 Definition

Binge-eating disorder involves recurrent episodes of binge eating accompanied by significant distress.

Unlike bulimia nervosa, these episodes are not regularly followed by inappropriate compensatory behaviours.

### 4.2 Common Features

Possible characteristics include:

- Eating rapidly.
- Eating when not physically hungry.
- Eating until uncomfortably full.
- Eating alone because of embarrassment.
- Experiencing guilt or distress afterward.

### 4.3 Psychological Effects

Individuals may experience shame, emotional distress and difficulties with self-esteem.

## 5. Other Feeding and Eating Disorders

### 5.1 Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder

ARFID involves restricted food intake that results in nutritional deficiencies, weight difficulties or significant interference with everyday functioning.

Unlike anorexia nervosa, it is not primarily driven by concerns about body weight or shape.

### 5.2 Pica

Pica involves persistent consumption of non-food substances that are not appropriate for the individual's developmental level or cultural practices.

Examples may include paper, soil or other non-food materials.

### 5.3 Rumination Disorder

Rumination disorder involves repeated regurgitation of food after eating, with the food sometimes being rechewed, reswallowed or spat out.

## 6. Causes and Treatment of Eating Disorders

### 6.1 Biological Factors

Genetic vulnerability, hormonal processes and brain reward mechanisms may contribute to eating disorders.

### 6.2 Psychological Factors

Possible influences include:

- Body dissatisfaction.
- Perfectionism.
- Difficulties regulating emotions.
- Low self-esteem.
- Unhelpful beliefs about food and body image.

### 6.3 Social and Cultural Factors

Social comparison, unrealistic appearance standards and weight-related discrimination may contribute to vulnerability.

### 6.4 Treatment Approaches

Treatment may include:

- Nutritional rehabilitation.
- Cognitive behavioural therapy.
- Family-based treatment in appropriate cases.
- Medical monitoring.
- Treatment of co-occurring psychological conditions.

Severe eating disorders may require specialised medical care.

## 7. Understanding Sleep

### 7.1 Meaning of Sleep

Sleep is a naturally recurring biological state involving reduced responsiveness to the environment and changes in brain activity.

It is essential for:

- Memory consolidation.
- Learning.
- Emotional regulation.
- Physical recovery.
- Immune functioning.

### 7.2 Circadian Rhythm

The circadian rhythm is an internal biological cycle lasting approximately 24 hours.

It helps regulate sleep-wake timing, body temperature and hormone release.

### 7.3 Role of the Suprachiasmatic Nucleus

The suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN), located in the hypothalamus, acts as a major biological clock.

It receives information about environmental light and helps synchronise circadian rhythms.

### 7.4 Melatonin

Melatonin is a hormone involved in signalling biological night and supporting sleep timing.

Its release is influenced by the light-dark cycle.

## 8. Stages of Sleep

### 8.1 Non-Rapid Eye Movement Sleep

NREM sleep is divided into three stages.

**N1:** The transition from wakefulness to sleep.

**N2:** A deeper stage characterised by sleep spindles and K-complexes.

**N3:** Deep sleep, also called slow-wave sleep, which is associated with physical restoration.

### 8.2 Rapid Eye Movement Sleep

REM sleep is characterised by:

- Rapid eye movements.
- Increased brain activity.
- Vivid dreaming in many cases.
- Reduced skeletal muscle activity.

### 8.3 Sleep Cycles

Sleep cycles alternate between NREM and REM stages throughout the night.

A complete cycle commonly lasts approximately 90 to 110 minutes, although duration varies.

## 9. Major Sleep-Wake Disorders

### 9.1 Insomnia Disorder

Insomnia disorder involves persistent difficulty initiating sleep, maintaining sleep or waking earlier than intended, despite adequate opportunity for sleep.

It may cause daytime fatigue, irritability and concentration difficulties.

### 9.2 Hypersomnolence Disorder

Hypersomnolence disorder involves excessive daytime sleepiness despite a generally adequate main sleep period.

### 9.3 Narcolepsy

Narcolepsy is a neurological sleep disorder involving excessive daytime sleepiness and, in some individuals, cataplexy.

Cataplexy is a sudden loss of muscle tone often triggered by strong emotions.

### 9.4 Obstructive Sleep Apnea

Obstructive sleep apnea involves repeated episodes of partial or complete upper-airway obstruction during sleep.

It can cause disrupted sleep and reduced oxygen levels.

### 9.5 Circadian Rhythm Sleep-Wake Disorders

These disorders involve persistent misalignment between an individual's internal circadian rhythm and the required or desired sleep schedule.

Examples include delayed sleep-wake phase disorder and shift work disorder.

### 9.6 Parasomnias

Parasomnias involve unusual behaviours or experiences occurring during sleep or transitions between sleep and wakefulness.

Examples include:

- Sleepwalking.
- Night terrors.
- Nightmares.
- Sleep talking.

## 10. Treatment and Management

### 10.1 Cognitive Behavioural Therapy for Insomnia

CBT-I is a structured treatment for insomnia that addresses unhelpful thoughts and behaviours associated with sleep.

### 10.2 Sleep Hygiene

Helpful practices may include:

- Maintaining consistent sleep and wake times.
- Reducing excessive evening light exposure.
- Creating a comfortable sleep environment.
- Limiting late-day caffeine.
- Following a relaxing bedtime routine.

### 10.3 Medical Treatment

Certain sleep disorders may require medical assessment, medication or specialised interventions.

For example, obstructive sleep apnea may be treated using continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) therapy.

## 11. Summary

Eating disorders involve disturbances in eating behaviour and related psychological processes. Major conditions include anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder. Sleep-wake disorders involve difficulties with sleep quantity, timing or quality. Both groups of conditions may involve biological, psychological and environmental influences and require appropriate assessment and treatment.
