# Lesson 37: Neurodevelopmental Disorders

Neurodevelopmental disorders are conditions that begin during the developmental period and affect the development of the brain and nervous system. They may influence learning, communication, attention, behaviour and social functioning. This lesson examines major neurodevelopmental disorders and the factors that contribute to their development.

## 1. Understanding Neurodevelopment

### 1.1 Meaning of Neurodevelopment

Neurodevelopment refers to the processes through which the brain and nervous system develop and mature.

It involves:

- Formation of neural connections.
- Development of sensory and motor abilities.
- Growth of language skills.
- Development of memory and attention.
- Improvement in emotional and social functioning.

### 1.2 Neurodevelopmental Disorders

Neurodevelopmental disorders involve developmental differences or difficulties that affect everyday functioning.

They commonly emerge during childhood, although some may not be recognised until adolescence or adulthood.

### 1.3 Developmental Differences

Not every developmental difference represents a disorder.

Assessment must consider developmental history, individual strengths, environmental conditions and functional difficulties.

## 2. Autism Spectrum Disorder

### 2.1 Definition

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterised by persistent differences in social communication and interaction, together with restricted or repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests or activities.

### 2.2 Social Communication Differences

Possible characteristics include:

- Differences in conversational interaction.
- Difficulty interpreting certain social cues.
- Differences in nonverbal communication.
- Challenges developing or maintaining relationships.

### 2.3 Restricted and Repetitive Patterns

These may include:

- Repetitive movements or speech.
- Strong preference for routines.
- Highly focused interests.
- Repeated patterns of play or activity.
- Unusual sensory responses.

### 2.4 Sensory Processing

Some autistic individuals experience heightened or reduced sensitivity to sensory information.

Examples include sensitivity to bright lights, loud sounds, textures or certain smells.

### 2.5 Autism Spectrum

Autism is described as a spectrum because individuals have different combinations of strengths, support needs and characteristics.

Support should be adapted to the individual's circumstances.

## 3. Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder

### 3.1 Definition

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder involving persistent patterns of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that interfere with functioning.

### 3.2 Inattention

Possible features include:

- Difficulty sustaining attention.
- Frequent careless mistakes.
- Difficulty organising tasks.
- Forgetfulness.
- Easily becoming distracted.
- Difficulty completing activities.

### 3.3 Hyperactivity

Possible features include:

- Excessive movement.
- Difficulty remaining seated.
- Frequent talking.
- Restlessness.
- Difficulty engaging in quiet activities.

### 3.4 Impulsivity

Impulsivity may involve:

- Interrupting conversations.
- Acting without considering consequences.
- Difficulty waiting.
- Responding before questions are completed.

### 3.5 Presentations of ADHD

ADHD is commonly described through three presentations:

1. Predominantly inattentive presentation.
2. Predominantly hyperactive-impulsive presentation.
3. Combined presentation.

## 4. Specific Learning Disorder

### 4.1 Definition

Specific learning disorder involves persistent difficulties in acquiring and using particular academic skills despite appropriate learning opportunities.

### 4.2 Dyslexia

Dyslexia involves difficulties with accurate or fluent word recognition, decoding and spelling.

It primarily affects reading-related skills.

### 4.3 Dyscalculia

Dyscalculia is a commonly used term for persistent difficulties with mathematical understanding and calculation.

Individuals may struggle with number sense, arithmetic operations and mathematical reasoning.

### 4.4 Difficulties in Written Expression

Some individuals experience persistent problems with spelling, grammar, punctuation and organising written information.

### 4.5 Important Consideration

Specific learning disorders do not indicate low intelligence.

Individuals may have average or above-average intellectual abilities while experiencing significant difficulties in particular academic areas.

## 5. Intellectual Developmental Disorder

### 5.1 Definition

Intellectual developmental disorder involves significant limitations in intellectual functioning and adaptive functioning, with onset during the developmental period.

### 5.2 Intellectual Functioning

Intellectual functioning includes abilities such as:

- Reasoning.
- Problem-solving.
- Planning.
- Abstract thinking.
- Learning from experience.

### 5.3 Adaptive Functioning

Adaptive functioning refers to the skills required for everyday independence and social responsibility.

It includes:

- Conceptual skills.
- Social skills.
- Practical skills.

### 5.4 Levels of Support

Support requirements vary between individuals and should be determined by adaptive functioning rather than intelligence scores alone.

## 6. Communication Disorders

### 6.1 Language Disorder

Language disorder involves persistent difficulties understanding or using language.

It may affect vocabulary, sentence structure and communication.

### 6.2 Speech Sound Disorder

Speech sound disorder involves difficulties producing speech sounds clearly and correctly.

### 6.3 Childhood-Onset Fluency Disorder

Childhood-onset fluency disorder, commonly called stuttering, involves disruptions in the normal flow of speech.

These may include sound repetitions, prolongations and blocks.

### 6.4 Social Communication Disorder

Social communication disorder involves persistent difficulties using verbal and nonverbal communication appropriately in social situations.

## 7. Motor Disorders

### 7.1 Developmental Coordination Disorder

Developmental coordination disorder involves significant difficulties in acquiring and performing coordinated motor skills.

These difficulties may affect writing, sports, dressing and other everyday activities.

### 7.2 Stereotypic Movement Disorder

Stereotypic movement disorder involves repetitive, apparently driven and nonfunctional movements that interfere with functioning or may cause physical harm.

Examples include repeated hand movements or body rocking.

### 7.3 Tic Disorders

Tics are sudden, rapid and recurrent movements or vocalisations.

Examples include:

- Eye blinking.
- Shoulder shrugging.
- Throat clearing.
- Repeated vocal sounds.

Tourette's disorder involves multiple motor tics and at least one vocal tic during the course of the condition.

## 8. Causes and Risk Factors

### 8.1 Genetic Influences

Genetic factors contribute to vulnerability to several neurodevelopmental disorders.

### 8.2 Prenatal and Perinatal Factors

Possible influences include:

- Certain prenatal infections.
- Exposure to harmful substances during pregnancy.
- Premature birth.
- Certain complications affecting early development.

These factors do not independently establish a diagnosis.

### 8.3 Brain Development

Differences in brain development, neural connectivity and information processing may contribute to neurodevelopmental conditions.

### 8.4 Environmental Influences

Environmental conditions may influence development and functioning, although they do not explain every neurodevelopmental disorder.

## 9. Assessment and Support

### 9.1 Developmental Assessment

Assessment may involve:

- Developmental history.
- Behavioural observation.
- Interviews with caregivers.
- Cognitive and academic testing.
- Communication and adaptive functioning assessments.

### 9.2 Educational Support

Support may include:

- Individualised learning plans.
- Additional learning time.
- Accessible teaching materials.
- Structured instructions.
- Assistive technology.

### 9.3 Behavioural and Developmental Interventions

Interventions may focus on communication, independence, social participation and everyday skills.

### 9.4 Family and Community Support

Families and communities play an important role in providing understanding, accessibility and opportunities for participation.

## 10. Inclusion and Respect

Neurodevelopmental differences should be understood without reducing individuals to diagnostic labels.

Inclusive environments recognise individual strengths, provide appropriate accommodations and respect different ways of learning and communicating.

## 11. Summary

Neurodevelopmental disorders begin during the developmental period and may affect communication, attention, learning, intellectual functioning and motor abilities. Major conditions include autism spectrum disorder, ADHD, specific learning disorder, intellectual developmental disorder and communication and motor disorders. Early assessment, appropriate support and inclusive environments can improve everyday functioning and quality of life.
