# Lesson 39: Substance Use and Addictive Disorders

Substance use and addictive disorders involve patterns of behaviour related to the consumption of substances or engagement in rewarding activities that may become difficult to control. These conditions can affect brain functioning, physical health, relationships and everyday responsibilities. This lesson examines substance-related disorders, addiction mechanisms and treatment approaches.

## 1. Understanding Substance Use

### 1.1 Meaning of Psychoactive Substances

Psychoactive substances are chemicals that influence brain activity and alter mental processes such as mood, perception, consciousness and behaviour.

Examples include:

- Alcohol.
- Nicotine.
- Cannabis.
- Opioids.
- Stimulants.
- Sedatives.

### 1.2 Substance Use and Substance-Related Disorders

Substance use does not automatically indicate addiction.

A substance use disorder involves a problematic pattern of substance use that causes significant impairment or distress.

### 1.3 Risk and Protective Factors

Risk factors may include:

- Genetic vulnerability.
- Chronic stress.
- Early exposure to substances.
- Peer pressure.
- Trauma.
- Co-occurring psychological conditions.

Protective factors may include supportive relationships, healthy coping skills and access to appropriate healthcare.

## 2. Understanding Addiction

### 2.1 Meaning of Addiction

Addiction involves persistent engagement in substance use or certain behaviours despite harmful consequences and difficulties controlling the behaviour.

### 2.2 Compulsive Behaviour

Compulsive behaviour refers to repeated actions that become difficult to control, even when they interfere with personal goals and responsibilities.

### 2.3 Craving

Craving is a strong desire or urge to use a substance or engage in a particular behaviour.

It may be triggered by environmental cues, emotional states or memories associated with previous use.

## 3. The Brain's Reward System

### 3.1 Dopamine and Reward

Dopamine is a neurotransmitter involved in motivation, learning and reward processing.

It helps the brain learn which experiences and actions are associated with rewarding outcomes.

### 3.2 Mesolimbic Pathway

The mesolimbic dopamine pathway is an important neural circuit involved in reward and motivation.

It includes connections between the ventral tegmental area and the nucleus accumbens.

### 3.3 Reinforcement Learning

Substances can influence reward-related learning and strengthen associations between particular cues and substance use.

Repeated exposure may contribute to persistent cravings and difficulty controlling behaviour.

### 3.4 Neuroadaptation

Repeated substance exposure may produce changes in brain functioning.

These adaptations can contribute to tolerance, withdrawal and continued substance-seeking behaviour.

## 4. Important Concepts in Substance Use

### 4.1 Tolerance

Tolerance occurs when a person needs increasingly larger amounts of a substance to achieve an effect previously produced by a smaller amount, or experiences a reduced effect from the same amount.

### 4.2 Withdrawal

Withdrawal refers to physical or psychological symptoms that occur when substance use is reduced or stopped after repeated exposure.

Symptoms vary depending on the substance.

### 4.3 Physical Dependence

Physical dependence involves physiological adaptation to a substance, such that stopping or reducing it may produce withdrawal symptoms.

Physical dependence is not identical to addiction.

### 4.4 Psychological Dependence

Psychological dependence involves strong emotional or mental reliance on a substance or behaviour.

### 4.5 Relapse

Relapse refers to a return to a previous pattern of problematic substance use after a period of improvement or recovery.

Relapse can occur during recovery and does not mean that treatment has permanently failed.

## 5. Major Substance-Related Disorders

### 5.1 Alcohol Use Disorder

Alcohol use disorder involves a problematic pattern of alcohol consumption that causes significant impairment or distress.

Possible features include:

- Difficulty controlling consumption.
- Strong cravings.
- Continued use despite negative consequences.
- Neglecting responsibilities.
- Developing tolerance or withdrawal.

### 5.2 Tobacco and Nicotine Use Disorder

Nicotine is an addictive substance found in tobacco products.

Nicotine dependence may involve cravings, withdrawal symptoms and repeated unsuccessful attempts to stop using nicotine.

### 5.3 Cannabis Use Disorder

Cannabis use disorder involves problematic cannabis use that causes significant impairment or distress.

Possible features include difficulty reducing use, cravings and continued use despite negative effects.

### 5.4 Opioid Use Disorder

Opioid use disorder involves problematic use of opioid substances.

Opioids include certain prescription pain medications and illicit substances.

The disorder may involve strong cravings, tolerance, withdrawal and continued use despite harm.

### 5.5 Stimulant Use Disorder

Stimulants increase activity in certain brain systems and may affect alertness, energy and attention.

Problematic stimulant use can produce significant psychological and physical consequences.

## 6. Behavioural Addictions

### 6.1 Gambling Disorder

Gambling disorder involves persistent and recurrent problematic gambling behaviour that causes significant impairment or distress.

It may involve:

- Increasing amounts of money being gambled.
- Repeated unsuccessful attempts to stop.
- Chasing financial losses.
- Concealing gambling behaviour.
- Damaged relationships and financial difficulties.

### 6.2 Gaming Disorder

Gaming disorder is recognised in ICD-11.

It involves impaired control over gaming, increasing priority given to gaming over other activities and continued gaming despite negative consequences.

The pattern must be sufficiently severe to cause significant impairment in important areas of functioning.

### 6.3 Habit and Addiction

Not every repeated or time-consuming behaviour is an addiction.

Diagnosis requires evidence of impaired control, significant consequences and a persistent problematic pattern.

## 7. Psychological Explanations of Addiction

### 7.1 Classical Conditioning

Environmental cues may become associated with substance use.

For example, a particular location or social situation may trigger cravings because it was repeatedly associated with previous substance consumption.

### 7.2 Operant Conditioning

Substance use may be maintained through reinforcement.

Positive reinforcement occurs when a substance produces a rewarding experience.

Negative reinforcement occurs when substance use temporarily reduces unpleasant feelings or withdrawal symptoms.

### 7.3 Cognitive Factors

Beliefs and expectations can influence substance-related behaviour.

Examples include believing that a substance is necessary for relaxation or social confidence.

### 7.4 Social Learning

Individuals may learn substance-use behaviours by observing family members, peers or influential social groups.

## 8. Effects of Addiction

### 8.1 Physical Effects

Depending on the substance, consequences may include:

- Cardiovascular problems.
- Liver damage.
- Respiratory difficulties.
- Neurological impairment.
- Increased risk of accidents.

### 8.2 Psychological Effects

Substance-related disorders may be associated with:

- Anxiety.
- Depression.
- Sleep disturbances.
- Cognitive difficulties.
- Increased impulsivity.

### 8.3 Social Effects

Addiction may affect:

- Family relationships.
- Employment.
- Education.
- Financial stability.
- Social participation.

## 9. Prevention and Treatment

### 9.1 Psychological Treatment

Evidence-based approaches include:

- Cognitive behavioural therapy.
- Motivational interviewing.
- Relapse prevention.
- Contingency management.
- Family-based interventions.

### 9.2 Medication-Assisted Treatment

Certain substance use disorders may be treated using medication alongside psychological and social support.

Examples include medications used in the treatment of opioid and alcohol use disorders.

### 9.3 Detoxification

Medically supervised withdrawal management may be necessary for certain substances.

Abruptly stopping some substances, particularly alcohol or sedatives after prolonged heavy use, can be dangerous.

### 9.4 Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation programmes may help individuals develop coping skills, rebuild relationships and maintain recovery.

### 9.5 Prevention

Prevention strategies include:

- Mental health education.
- Early identification of risk factors.
- Healthy coping strategies.
- Supportive family environments.
- Access to treatment services.

## 10. Recovery and Relapse Prevention

Recovery is a continuing process that may involve:

- Recognising personal triggers.
- Developing alternative coping strategies.
- Building supportive relationships.
- Maintaining treatment participation.
- Creating healthy routines.
- Seeking help when cravings return.

A relapse should be treated as a signal to reassess support and treatment rather than as a reason for shame.

## 11. Summary

Substance use and addictive disorders involve difficulties controlling substance use or certain rewarding behaviours despite harmful consequences. Addiction is influenced by reward learning, reinforcement, brain adaptations and social and psychological factors. Treatment may combine psychotherapy, medication, rehabilitation and social support. Prevention and long-term recovery require individualised and compassionate approaches.
