# Lesson 44: Social and Cultural Psychology in Depth

Social and cultural psychology examine how individuals think, feel and behave within social groups and cultural environments. Social psychology focuses on interpersonal influence and group processes, while cultural psychology examines how cultural practices and meanings shape psychological functioning.

## 1. Understanding Social Psychology

### 1.1 Definition

Social psychology is the scientific study of how individuals' thoughts, emotions and behaviours are influenced by the actual, imagined or implied presence of others.

### 1.2 Major Areas

Social psychology examines:

- Social perception.
- Attitudes.
- Persuasion.
- Conformity.
- Obedience.
- Group behaviour.
- Prejudice.
- Interpersonal relationships.

## 2. Social Cognition

### 2.1 Meaning

Social cognition refers to the mental processes involved in perceiving, interpreting and remembering information about other people and social situations.

### 2.2 Schemas

Schemas are organised mental structures that help individuals interpret information.

Social schemas influence how people understand roles, relationships and social expectations.

### 2.3 Heuristics

Heuristics are mental shortcuts used to make judgements quickly.

Examples include:

- Availability heuristic.
- Representativeness heuristic.
- Anchoring heuristic.

These shortcuts can be useful but may sometimes produce systematic errors.

### 2.4 Attribution

Attribution refers to the process of explaining the causes of behaviour.

Individuals may attribute behaviour to personal characteristics or situational circumstances.

## 3. Attribution Theories

### 3.1 Internal Attribution

Internal attribution explains behaviour through personal characteristics such as personality, intentions or abilities.

### 3.2 External Attribution

External attribution explains behaviour through situational factors such as social pressure, environmental conditions or unexpected events.

### 3.3 Fundamental Attribution Error

The fundamental attribution error involves overemphasising personal characteristics and underestimating situational influences when explaining other people's behaviour.

### 3.4 Self-Serving Bias

Self-serving bias involves attributing successes to internal factors while explaining failures through external circumstances.

### 3.5 Actor-Observer Difference

The actor-observer difference describes a tendency to explain one's own behaviour through situational factors while explaining others' behaviour through personal characteristics.

## 4. Attitudes and Persuasion

### 4.1 Components of Attitudes

Attitudes are commonly described through three components:

- Cognitive: beliefs and thoughts.
- Affective: feelings and emotional responses.
- Behavioural: action tendencies.

### 4.2 Cognitive Dissonance

Leon Festinger proposed cognitive dissonance theory.

Cognitive dissonance refers to psychological discomfort arising from inconsistency between beliefs, attitudes or behaviours.

Individuals may attempt to reduce this discomfort by changing their attitudes, modifying their behaviour or rationalising the inconsistency.

### 4.3 Elaboration Likelihood Model

The elaboration likelihood model explains two major routes of persuasion.

**Central route:** Persuasion occurs through careful evaluation of arguments and evidence.

**Peripheral route:** Persuasion occurs through simpler cues, such as attractiveness, familiarity or the perceived credibility of a communicator.

## 5. Group Behaviour

### 5.1 Social Facilitation

Social facilitation refers to changes in performance that occur in the presence of others.

The presence of an audience may improve performance on well-learned tasks but impair performance on difficult or unfamiliar tasks.

### 5.2 Social Loafing

Social loafing occurs when individuals exert less effort while working collectively than when working individually.

### 5.3 Group Polarisation

Group polarisation occurs when group discussion strengthens the group's initial tendency towards a more extreme position.

### 5.4 Groupthink

Groupthink occurs when the desire for group agreement interferes with realistic evaluation of alternatives.

It may lead to poor decisions and insufficient consideration of risks.

## 6. Prejudice, Stereotypes and Discrimination

### 6.1 Stereotypes

Stereotypes are generalised beliefs about members of a particular social group.

### 6.2 Prejudice

Prejudice involves attitudes or evaluations directed towards individuals based on their group membership.

### 6.3 Discrimination

Discrimination refers to behaviour that unfairly disadvantages individuals because of their social group membership.

### 6.4 Social Identity Theory

Henri Tajfel and John Turner developed social identity theory.

It proposes that individuals derive part of their self-concept from membership in social groups.

This may contribute to in-group favouritism and out-group bias.

### 6.5 Reducing Prejudice

Potential approaches include:

- Meaningful intergroup contact.
- Cooperative goals.
- Equal-status interaction.
- Perspective-taking.
- Education and awareness.

## 7. Cultural Psychology

### 7.1 Definition

Cultural psychology examines how cultural practices, beliefs and social environments influence psychological processes.

### 7.2 Culture and Behaviour

Culture influences:

- Communication.
- Emotional expression.
- Social expectations.
- Parenting practices.
- Moral reasoning.
- Personal identity.

### 7.3 Individualism

Individualistic cultural orientations tend to emphasise personal autonomy, individual goals and independence.

### 7.4 Collectivism

Collectivistic cultural orientations tend to emphasise group belonging, social obligations and interdependence.

These are broad cultural tendencies, not fixed descriptions of every individual within a society.

## 8. Cross-Cultural Psychology

### 8.1 Definition

Cross-cultural psychology compares psychological processes across different cultural groups.

### 8.2 Cultural Universals

Cultural universals are psychological patterns or processes that appear across many different societies.

### 8.3 Cultural Relativism

Cultural relativism involves understanding behaviour within its cultural context rather than judging it exclusively through the standards of another culture.

### 8.4 Ethnocentrism

Ethnocentrism involves evaluating other cultures using the assumptions and standards of one's own culture.

### 8.5 Emic and Etic Approaches

**Emic approach:** Studies psychological processes within a particular cultural context.

**Etic approach:** Examines psychological processes using concepts intended to be compared across cultures.

Both approaches can contribute to a more complete understanding of human behaviour.

## 9. Cultural Influences on the Self

### 9.1 Independent Self-Construal

An independent self-construal emphasises personal characteristics, autonomy and individual identity.

### 9.2 Interdependent Self-Construal

An interdependent self-construal emphasises relationships, social roles and connections with others.

### 9.3 Cultural Identity

Cultural identity refers to an individual's sense of belonging to cultural groups and traditions.

It may involve language, values, customs and shared experiences.

## 10. Research Methods in Social and Cultural Psychology

### 10.1 Experiments

Experiments examine causal relationships by manipulating variables and observing their effects.

### 10.2 Surveys

Surveys collect information about attitudes, beliefs and behaviours from participants.

### 10.3 Cross-Cultural Comparisons

Researchers compare psychological measurements across cultural groups.

### 10.4 Measurement Equivalence

Measurement equivalence examines whether an assessment instrument measures the same construct in comparable ways across different groups.

Without adequate equivalence, differences in scores may reflect measurement problems rather than genuine psychological differences.

## 11. Applications

Social and cultural psychology contribute to:

- Education.
- Public health.
- Conflict resolution.
- Workplace behaviour.
- Advertising and communication.
- Intercultural relationships.
- Community development.

## 12. Summary

Social psychology examines how people influence one another through attitudes, attribution, group behaviour and social identity. Cultural psychology examines how cultural practices and meanings shape psychological processes. Cross-cultural research helps identify both shared human tendencies and meaningful cultural differences.
