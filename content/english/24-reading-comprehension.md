Reading comprehension is the active cognitive process of decoding, interpreting, analyzing, and synthesizing written texts. Mastering comprehension requires identifying main ideas, analyzing authorial tone, tracking text structures, and drawing valid inferences.

## Main Concept 1: Active Reading Strategies

Efficient reading requires moving beyond passive decoding to active analytical engagement with the text.

- **Definitions and terminology:**
  - *Skimming:* Fast-paced reading focused on headings, opening sentences, and conclusions to determine text macro-structure.
  - *Scanning:* Target-oriented visual searching for specific data points, dates, or terms.
  - *Inferences:* Logical deductions drawn by pairing explicit text statements with context and reasoning.
- **Important principles and rules:**
  - **Fact vs. Opinion:** Facts are verifiable statements; opinions are value judgments or interpretations.
  - **Author's Purpose:** The primary motivation behind the passage (*to inform, to persuade, to critique, to entertain*).

## Main Concept 2: Text Structures and Tone Analysis

### Subtopic 1: Structural Paragraph Markers and Transition Tracking

Authors organize prose using predictable structural patterns:
- **Cause and Effect:** Explains reasons and results (*consequently, as a result, led to*).
- **Compare and Contrast:** Evaluates similarities and differences (*conversely, similarly, whereas*).
- **Problem and Solution:** Posits a dilemma and evaluates resolutions.
- **Chronological / Sequential:** Details events in temporal order.

### Subtopic 2: Authorial Tone and Perspective

Tone reflects the author's emotional attitude toward the subject matter:
- **Objective / Neutral:** Informative, factual, devoid of emotional bias.
- **Skeptical / Critical:** Questioning, doubting, highlighting flaws in arguments.
- **Advocative / Enthusiastic:** Strongly supportive, persuasive.
- **Analytical:** Systematic breakdown of evidence without overt emotional bias.

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Question Category | Core Analytical Task | Typical Question Stem | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main Idea | Identify central claim | "Which statement best summarizes..." | Focus on intro, topic sentences, & conclusion. |
| Direct Detail | Locate explicit facts | "According to the passage,..." | Scan for keywords; match exact textual meaning. |
| Inference | Deduce unstated logic | "It can be inferred that..." | Combine explicit statements to form safe conclusions. |
| Tone / Attitude | Assess perspective | "The author's tone can best be described as..." | Analyze word connotations (positive/negative/neutral). |
| Vocabulary-in-Context | Decode context | "In line X, 'ephemeral' most nearly means..." | Substitute options back into the line context. |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Valid Inference Formulation:

$$\text{Explicit Textual Statement } A + \text{Explicit Textual Statement } B \implies \text{Valid Unstated Inference } C$$

$$\text{Rule}: \text{Inferences MUST stay strictly within the logical bounds of the text; avoid extreme extrapolations!}$$

Distorted Choice Elimination Rule:

$$\text{Option contains extreme qualifiers } (\textit{always}, \textit{never}, \textit{completely}) \implies \mathbf{\text{High Probability of Incorrect Answer!}}$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **The "True but Irrelevant" Trap:** Selecting an option that contains factually true statements not discussed in the text or irrelevant to the specific question asked.
- **Overly Broad or Overly Narrow Main Idea:** Choosing a summary option that covers only one paragraph (too narrow) or expands far beyond the text scope (too broad).
- **Extrapolating Beyond Textual Evidence:** Making wild assumptions based on personal outside knowledge rather than strict text evidence.

## Quiz

1. A reading passage presents factual data regarding rising ocean temperatures, followed by a detailed review of three competing climate models without taking a personal stance. How should the author's tone be described?
  - [ ] Highly biased and emotional
  - [x] Objective and analytical
  - [ ] Cynical and dismissive
  - [ ] Polemical and aggressive
   > Presenting factual data and reviewing competing models without emotional stance indicates an objective, analytical tone.

2. Which option strategy best avoids trap choices on Main Idea comprehension questions?
  - [ ] Selecting the option with the longest word count
  - [ ] Choosing an option that focuses on a single interesting statistic in paragraph 2
  - [x] Selecting the option that encompasses the entire passage's argument without being overly broad
  - [ ] Choosing an option that relies heavily on outside information not mentioned in the passage
   > The correct main idea choice synthesizes the central claim of the entire text while avoiding narrow details or unsupported extrapolations.

3. Consider the text: "All submitted manuscripts undergo double-blind peer review before publication. Manuscript X was published in yesterday's journal edition." What valid inference can be drawn?
  - [ ] Manuscript X was written by a world-renowned scientist.
  - [ ] Manuscript X took over two years to complete peer review.
  - [x] Manuscript X underwent a double-blind peer review process prior to publication.
  - [ ] Manuscript X is the most cited article in the journal's history.
   > Since ALL published manuscripts undergo double-blind review, and Manuscript X was published, it necessarily underwent double-blind review.
