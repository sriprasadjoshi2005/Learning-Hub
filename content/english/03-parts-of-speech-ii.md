Verbs and adjectives form the core engine of predication and modification in English syntax. Verbs establish action, state, and tense, while adjectives qualify nouns and pronouns to add precision.

## Main Concept 1: Verbs

A **verb** is a word or word phrase that expresses an action, an occurrence, or a state of being. Every complete English sentence must contain at least one finite verb.

- **Definitions and terminology:** A *finite verb* shows tense, person, and number, while a *non-finite verb* (infinitive, participle, gerund) does not change form for tense or subject.
- **Important principles and rules:** Verbs are categorized by transitiveness—whether they require an object to complete their meaning.
- **Classifications and subcategories:**
  - *Action Verbs (Dynamic):* Express physical or mental activities (*run, evaluate, consider*). Can be transitive or intransitive.
  - *Linking Verbs (Stative):* Connect the subject to a subject complement (adjective or noun) describing the subject (*be, seem, appear, become, feel*).
  - *Auxiliary Verbs (Helping):* Accompany main verbs to form complex tenses, voices, or moods. Includes primary auxiliaries (*be, do, have*) and modal auxiliaries (*can, must, should*).

### Principal Parts of Verbs
English verbs have five principal structural forms:

1. **Base Form ($V_1$):** *write, walk*
2. **Past Simple ($V_2$):** *wrote, walked*
3. **Past Participle ($V_3$):** *written, walked*
4. **Present Participle / Gerund ($V_4$):** *writing, walking*
5. **Third-Person Singular Present ($V_5$):** *writes, walks*

## Main Concept 2: Adjectives

An **adjective** is a modifier that qualifies, quantifies, or specifies a noun or pronoun. Adjectives answer questions such as *Which one? What kind? How many? How much?*

### Subtopic 1: Types of Adjectives

- **Descriptive Adjectives:** Express qualities or states (*a rigorous study, a ancient text*).
- **Quantitative / Limiting Adjectives:** Specify amounts or limits (*several options, few resources*).
- **Proper Adjectives:** Derived from proper nouns, requiring capitalization (*Shakespearean drama, Newtonian physics*).
- **Attributive vs. Predicative:** *Attributive adjectives* appear directly before the noun (*the quiet student*); *predicative adjectives* follow a linking verb (*the student remained quiet*).

### Subtopic 2: Degrees of Comparison

Adjectives modify across three scalar degrees:
1. **Positive Degree:** Base form modifying a single item (*bright*).
2. **Comparative Degree:** Compares two items, formed with *-er* or *more* (*brighter, more rigorous*).
3. **Superlative Degree:** Compares three or more items, formed with *-est* or *most* (*brightest, most rigorous*).

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Verb / Adjective Type | Function | Syntax Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transitive Verb | Requires a direct object | $\text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \text{Object}$ | She *analyzed* the data. |
| Intransitive Verb | Takes no direct object | $\text{Subject} + \text{Verb}$ | The sun *rose*. |
| Linking Verb | Connects subject to complement | $\text{Subject} + \text{Linking Verb} + \text{Complement}$ | The hypothesis *seems* valid. |
| Comparative Adjective | Compares 2 entities | $\text{Noun}_1 + \text{Verb} + \text{Adj-er/more Adj} + \text{than} + \text{Noun}_2$ | Plan A is *more viable than* Plan B. |
| Superlative Adjective | Identifies extreme in $\ge 3$ | $\text{The} + \text{Adj-est/most Adj} + \text{Noun}$ | It is the *most efficient* engine. |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Royal Order of Adjectives (when stacking multiple attributive adjectives before a noun):

$$\text{Determiner} \rightarrow \text{Opinion} \rightarrow \text{Size} \rightarrow \text{Age} \rightarrow \text{Shape} \rightarrow \text{Color} \rightarrow \text{Origin} \rightarrow \text{Material} \rightarrow \text{Purpose} \rightarrow \text{NOUN}$$

Example: *A (Determiner) beautiful (Opinion) small (Size) old (Age) square (Shape) blue (Color) French (Origin) wooden (Material) writing (Purpose) desk.*

## Common Exam Traps

- **Confusing Linking Verbs with Action Verbs:** Adverbs modify action verbs, but adjectives modify subjects following linking verbs (e.g., *The soup tastes delicious* [adjective], NOT *tastes deliciously* [adverb]).
- **Double Comparatives and Superlatives:** Combining *more/most* with *-er/-est* is non-standard (e.g., write *more efficient*, NOT *more more efficient* or *more faster*).
- **Irregular Adjective Forms:** Misapplying standard rules to irregular adjectives (*good $\rightarrow$ better $\rightarrow$ best*; *bad $\rightarrow$ worse $\rightarrow$ worst*).

## Quiz

1. Choose the sentence that correctly uses a predicative adjective following a linking verb.
  - [ ] The engine ran smooth during the entire test drive.
  - [x] The research findings remain consistent despite changing conditions.
  - [ ] She looked carefully at the old manuscript.
  - [ ] The choir sang sweet during the evening performance.
   > "Remain" is a linking verb connecting the subject "findings" to the predicative adjective "consistent". In the incorrect options, action verbs are modified by adjectives instead of adverbs.

2. What is the correct comparative form for the irregular adjective "bad"?
  - [ ] Badder
  - [ ] More bad
  - [x] Worse
  - [ ] Worst
   > "Worse" is the irregular comparative form of "bad" (positive: *bad*, comparative: *worse*, superlative: *worst*).

3. Identify the error in the adjective order of this phrase: "An Italian leather beautiful jacket."
  - [ ] Material should precede origin.
  - [x] Opinion ("beautiful") must precede origin ("Italian") and material ("leather").
  - [ ] Origin must follow the noun directly.
  - [ ] Material must be placed before opinion.
   > According to the Royal Order of Adjectives, opinion adjectives (*beautiful*) must precede origin (*Italian*) and material (*leather*).
