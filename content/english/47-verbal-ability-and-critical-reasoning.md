Verbal ability and critical reasoning sections evaluate your capacity to analyze arguments, identify implicit assumptions, strengthen or weaken claims, decode analogies, and resolve logical paradoxes.

## Main Concept 1: Structure of Critical Reasoning Arguments

Critical reasoning items feature short arguments comprised of premises, assumptions, and conclusions.

- **Definitions and terminology:**
  - *Premise:* Stated evidence or factual support.
  - *Conclusion:* The central claim supported by the premises.
  - *Assumption:* The unstated, necessary logical bridge connecting premise to conclusion.
- **Core Argument Equation:**

$$\text{Stated Premises } (P) + \mathbf{\text{Unstated Assumption } (A)} \implies \text{Conclusion } (C)$$

## Main Concept 2: Core Critical Reasoning Question Types

### Subtopic 1: Assumption, Strengthen, and Weaken Questions

- **Assumption Questions:** Identify the unwritten claim necessary for the argument to hold. Use the **Negation Test**: If you negate the correct option, the argument must completely fall apart.
- **Strengthen Questions:** Select an option that provides additional evidence supporting the unstated assumption or ruling out alternative causes.
- **Weaken Questions:** Select an option that attacks the unstated assumption, introduces alternative causes, or presents counter-evidence.

### Subtopic 2: Boldface and Paradox Questions

- **Boldface Sentence Questions:** Identify the structural roles played by specific bolded sentences (e.g., *First boldface is a premise; second is the main conclusion*).
- **Paradox / Discrepancy Questions:** Select an option that explains how two seemingly contradictory facts can both be true simultaneously.

```text
                      Critical Reasoning Pipeline
                                   │
  1. Deconstruct Argument: Isolate Premise(s) & Conclusion
                                   │
  2. Identify Logical Gap: What unstated assumption is needed?
                                   │
  3. Apply Question Type Strategy:
     - Assumption: Apply Negation Test
     - Weaken: Find alternative cause or gap exploitation
     - Strengthen: Validate assumption or rule out alternatives
```

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Question Type | Core Objective | Key Solving Method | Validation Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assumption | Find necessary unstated bridge | Apply Negation Test | Negated option destroys argument. |
| Strengthen | Support the argument | Rule out alternative causes | Boosts validity of unstated bridge. |
| Weaken | Undermine the argument | Introduce alternative cause / gap | Shows conclusion fails even with premise. |
| Paradox | Resolve apparent conflict | Find unifying context variable | Allows Fact $A$ & Fact $B$ to coexist. |
| Boldface | Identify structural roles | Classify statements (Fact/Claim) | Matches argument framework. |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

The Negation Test for Assumptions:

$$\text{Candidate Option } O \xrightarrow{\text{Negate (Make Negative)}} \text{Does the Main Conclusion collapse?} \begin{cases} \text{YES} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{CORRECT ASSUMPTION!}} \\ \text{NO} \longrightarrow \text{Eliminate} \end{cases}$$

Paradox Resolution Equation:

$$\text{Fact } A \text{ (High Input)} + \text{Fact } B \text{ (Low Output)} + \mathbf{\text{Unifying Factor } X} \implies \text{Logical Harmony}$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Choosing an Option that Strengthens when Asked to Weaken:** Selecting an option supporting the claim due to misreading the question stem.
- **Selecting Factually True External Facts:** Choosing an option that states a real-world truth but fails to address the specific logical gap in the passage argument.
- **Failing the Negation Test:** Choosing an assumption option whose negation does not actually destroy the author's core conclusion.

## Quiz

1. Consider the argument: "City Y installed automated traffic cameras in January. By December, traffic accidents dropped by 25%. Therefore, the cameras caused the reduction in accidents." Which option, if true, most severely weakens the argument?
  - [ ] Traffic cameras in City Z cost more to maintain than those in City Y.
  - [x] In January, City Y also doubled police road patrols and increased speeding fines significantly.
  - [ ] Automated traffic cameras photograph license plates accurately.
  - [ ] Most drivers in City Y are aware of the camera locations.
   > Introducing alternative simultaneous causes (doubled police patrols and higher fines) undermines the claim that the cameras alone caused the accident reduction.

2. What is the outcome when the Negation Test is applied to a genuinely necessary unstated assumption of an argument?
  - [ ] The conclusion becomes mathematically proven.
  - [x] The author's conclusion collapses and becomes logically invalid.
  - [ ] The premises are shown to be factually false.
  - [ ] The argument shifts from inductive to deductive.
   > A necessary assumption is required for the argument to stand; negating it causes the author's conclusion to fall apart.

3. Resolve the paradox: "A company introduced a mandatory health and wellness program for all workers. Six months later, employee sick leave requests increased by 15%."
  - [ ] Wellness programs are popular in tech companies.
  - [x] The program included free diagnostic screenings that detected previously unnoticed health conditions requiring medical leave.
  - [ ] Employees enjoyed the free healthy meals provided during the program.
  - [ ] Sick leave requests were processed by a new HR software system.
   > Option B provides the unifying factor explaining why a health program logically led to an increase in initial sick leave requests (detecting hidden conditions).
