Modal verbs are auxiliary verbs that express the speaker's attitude toward the necessity, probability, capability, permission, or advisability of an action. Modals modify the main verb without changing form for subject-verb agreement.

## Main Concept 1: Primary Modal Auxiliary Verbs

Primary modals include *can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will,* and *would*.

- **Definitions and terminology:** Modals are invariable (they take no *-s* for third-person singular, no *-ed* for past, and no *-ing* for participles) and are followed directly by a bare infinitive ($V_1$ without *to*).
- **Core Functional Categories:**
  1. **Ability:** *can* (present/general ability), *could* (past ability). (*She can speak four languages.*)
  2. **Permission:** *may* (formal), *can* (informal), *could* (polite request). (*May I review the document?*)
  3. **Possibility / Probability:** *might* (weak possibility), *may* (moderate possibility), *could* (theoretical possibility), *must* (strong logical deduction).
  4. **Necessity / Obligation:** *must* (strong internal obligation or logical certainty), *should / ought to* (advisability, duty, recommendation).

## Main Concept 2: Semi-Modals and Modal Perfects

### Subtopic 1: Semi-Modal Auxiliaries

Semi-modals function similarly to modals but can inflect for tense and subject:
- **Ought to:** Expresses moral duty or advice (*We ought to respect the regulations.*).
- **Need (to):** Expresses necessity. Functions as a modal in negative constructions (*You need not attend*) or as a main verb (*You do not need to attend*).
- **Used to:** Expresses past habits or discontinued states (*He used to work late*).
- **Have to / Has to / Had to:** Expresses external obligation imposed by rules or authorities (*Employees have to wear safety gear*).

### Subtopic 2: Modal Perfects ($\text{Modal} + \text{have} + V_3$)

Used to express past deductions, unfulfilled expectations, or regrets regarding completed events:

- **Must have + $V_3$:** Strong logical deduction about a past event (*The roads are wet; it must have rained last night*).
- **Should have + $V_3$:** Past obligation or regret that was **not** fulfilled (*I should have studied more for the exam*).
- **Could have + $V_3$:** Past capability or possibility that did not occur (*He could have passed, but he skipped the exam*).
- **May / Might have + $V_3$:** Speculation about a past event (*They might have missed the bus*).

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Modal Structure | Primary Function | Time Reference | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\text{Can} + V_1$ | Present ability / permission | Present / Future | She *can operate* the machinery. |
| $\text{Must} + V_1$ | Strong obligation / deduction | Present / Future | You *must submit* the form today. |
| $\text{Should} + V_1$ | Advice / Recommendation | Present / Future | We *should double-check* the calculations. |
| $\text{Must have} + V_3$ | Past logical deduction | Past | They *must have arrived* by now. |
| $\text{Should have} + V_3$ | Unfulfilled past obligation | Past | You *should have notified* the director. |
| $\text{Need not} + V_1$ | Absence of obligation | Present | You *need not bring* extra paper. |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Invariant Modal Rule:

$$\text{Subject} + \mathbf{\text{Modal Verb}} + \mathbf{V_1\ \text{(Bare Infinitive)}} \quad [\text{No } \textit{-s}, \text{no } \textit{-ed}, \text{no } \textit{to}]$$

$$\text{Example}: \text{He } \mathbf{\text{must}} \text{ submit } [\text{NOT } \text{musts}, \text{must to submit}, \text{or must submitted}]$$

Modal Perfect Formula:

$$\text{Subject} + \text{Modal} + \mathbf{\text{have}} + \mathbf{V_3\ \text{(Past Participle)}}$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Adding "To" After Pure Modals:** Placing *to* after pure modals (e.g., writing *She must to complete the assignment* instead of *She must complete the assignment*).
- **Confusing "Must Have" and "Should Have":** Using *must have* for past regrets instead of *should have* (e.g., saying *I must have studied harder* when expressing personal regret rather than logical deduction).
- **Pluralizing Modals for Third Person:** Adding *-s* to modals (e.g., writing *He cans speak English* instead of *He can speak English*).

## Quiz

1. Which modal construction correctly expresses a strong logical conclusion about a past event?
  - [ ] The ground is completely dry; it should have rained last night.
  - [x] The lights are off and the doors are locked; they must have left the office.
  - [ ] She can have completed the assignment yesterday.
  - [ ] They ought have notified us prior to arrival.
   > "Must have + $V_3$" is used to make strong logical deductions about past events based on available evidence.

2. Identify the correct sentence that avoids modal infinitive errors.
  - [ ] Candidates should to present their identification cards at the door.
  - [ ] Candidates may to present their identification cards at the door.
  - [x] Candidates must present their identification cards at the door.
  - [ ] Candidates can to present their identification cards at the door.
   > Pure modals (*must, should, may, can*) are followed immediately by the bare infinitive without "to".

3. Complete the sentence: "You _____ bought extra ink, as we already had five cartridges in stock."
  - [ ] must have
  - [ ] should have
  - [x] need not have
  - [ ] could not have
   > "Need not have + $V_3$" indicates that an action was performed in the past but was unnecessary.
