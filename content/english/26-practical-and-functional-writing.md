Practical and functional writing encompasses real-world written communication—such as business correspondence, formal emails, summary précis, and technical reports. Success in functional writing relies on clarity, conciseness, appropriate register, and audience awareness.

## Main Concept 1: Formal Letters and Professional Email Conventions

Professional communication requires structured formatting and polite, precise language tailored to organizational settings.

- **Definitions and terminology:**
  - *Salutation:* Formal greeting (*Dear Dr. Smith:, Dear Selection Committee:*). Note that formal business letters use a **colon** after salutations, whereas informal letters use a comma.
  - *Sign-off:* Formal closing (*Sincerely, Regards, Respectfully yours*).
- **Key Structural Components:**
  1. **Subject Line:** Concise, specific summary of purpose (*Application for Senior Research Associate Position*).
  2. **Opening Statement:** Immediate declaration of purpose (*I am writing to inquire about...*).
  3. **Body Paragraphs:** Concise background, details, or requests.
  4. **Call to Action / Next Steps:** Clear instructions or expectations.
  5. **Professional Sign-off and Signature:** Name, title, and contact details.

## Main Concept 2: Précis Writing and Executive Summaries

### Subtopic 1: Précis Writing Mechanics

A **précis** is a concise, clear summary of an original text, capturing its essential thesis, main arguments, and conclusion in approximately **one-third** of the original word count.

- **Core Précis Rules:**
  - Written entirely in the summary writer's **own words** (avoid direct quotes).
  - Retains the **original tone, order, and proportion** of arguments.
  - Contains **no personal opinions, interpretations, or outside additions**.
  - Written in continuous prose using third-person perspective.

### Subtopic 2: Functional Report Layouts

Formal reports present data and recommendations logically using structured sections:
- *Title Page & Executive Summary*
- *Introduction & Objective*
- *Methodology / Findings*
- *Conclusions & Actionable Recommendations*

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Functional Writing Type | Target Audience | Primary Goal | Register / Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formal Business Email | Clients / Colleagues | Convey information / Request | Professional, direct, polite |
| Cover Letter | Hiring Manager | Demonstrate fit for a role | Persuasive, formal, academic |
| Précis | General / Academic | Condense text to $\sim \frac{1}{3}$ length | Objective, concise, third-person |
| Executive Summary | Decision Makers | Synthesize key report findings | Data-driven, action-oriented |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Précis Target Length Ratio:

$$\text{Target Précis Word Count} \approx \frac{\text{Original Text Word Count}}{3}$$

Salutation Punctuation Rule:

$$\text{Formal Business Letter} \longrightarrow \text{Salutation} + \mathbf{\text{Colon (:)}}$$

$$\text{Informal Personal Letter} \longrightarrow \text{Salutation} + \mathbf{\text{Comma (,)}}$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Inserting Personal Opinion in a Précis:** Adding personal critiques or outside knowledge into a summary précis (e.g., writing *I agree with the author that...*).
- **Vague Email Subject Lines:** Using generic subject lines like *Question* or *Important* instead of specific descriptors (*Request for Extension: Project Alpha*).
- **Overly Casual Salutations in Formal Register:** Opening formal job applications with informal greetings like *Hey* or *Hi there*.
