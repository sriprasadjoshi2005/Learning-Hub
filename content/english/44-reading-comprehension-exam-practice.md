Reading Comprehension (RC) in entrance examinations tests high-speed passage analysis, main idea identification, precise line-reference inference, authorial tone evaluation, and distractor elimination under strict timing.

## Main Concept 1: Question Taxonomy and Targeted Strategies

Reading comprehension items fall into distinct functional categories, each requiring a specific analytical approach.

- **Definitions and terminology:**
  - *Global Questions:* Test macro-understanding of the passage as a whole (Main Idea, Author's Purpose, Primary Organization).
  - *Detail Questions:* Test retrieval of explicitly stated information.
  - *Inference Questions:* Test logical extensions of stated facts (Requires reading between the lines).
  - *Tone / Attitude Questions:* Evaluate the author's perspective or emotional stance.
  - *Function / Line-Reference Questions:* Explain *why* an author included a specific sentence, phrase, or paragraph.

## Main Concept 2: Distractor Anatomy and Systematic Elimination

### Subtopic 1: Deconstructing Wrong Answer Choices

Examiners design incorrect options using predictable structural flaws:

```text
                     RC Distractor Classification
                                  │
         ┌────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
         ▼                        ▼                        ▼
  Out of Scope              Extreme / Absolute        Distorted Detail
  - Introduces unstated     - Uses *always, never,    - Reverses cause/effect
    external facts            completely, impossible*   or mixes parameters
```

### Subtopic 2: Step-by-Step RC Execution Blueprint

1. **Passage Sweep (60–90 seconds):** Read for thesis, paragraph topic sentences, and structural transitions (do not try to memorize details).
2. **Read Question Stem Carefully:** Identify question type and note line references.
3. **Return to Passage (Re-anchor):** Read 2 lines above and 2 lines below the reference line to establish full context.
4. **Formulate an Independent Answer:** Predict the answer in your own words *before* reading the options.
5. **Match and Eliminate:** Select the option matching your prediction; eliminate distractors using the POE framework.

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Question Type | Keyword Markers | Re-Anchoring Strategy | Core Trap to Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main Idea | *primary purpose, central thesis* | Synthesize Intro & Conclusion | Choosing a single paragraph detail |
| Direct Detail | *according to line X, stated in* | Match line text directly | Choosing an inverted/reversed detail |
| Inference | *implies, suggests, inferred* | Combine explicit facts logically | Making unstated wild assumptions |
| Function | *in order to, serves primarily to* | Determine structural purpose | Stating *what* it says instead of *why* |
| Tone | *attitude, tone, stance* | Evaluate connotative word choices | Confusing author tone with topic mood |

Inference Validation Equation:

$$\text{Textual Premise } A + \text{Textual Premise } B \implies \mathbf{\text{Direct Logical Consequence } C} \quad [\text{NO external assumptions!}]$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **The "Right Words, Wrong Logic" Trap:** Selecting an option that repeats exact phrases from the passage but arranges them to express an incorrect relationship.
- **Answering Global Questions Using Local Details:** Choosing a main idea option that accurately describes only one paragraph rather than the passage as a whole.
- **Relying on Personal Expertise:** Selecting an answer because it matches real-world facts you know, even though it is not supported by the passage text.

## Quiz

1. Consider a passage that details the historical rise of a policy, outlines its initial successes, and concludes by highlighting severe unforeseen economic drawbacks. Which option best captures the author's primary purpose?
  - [ ] To advocate for the immediate global adoption of the policy
  - [x] To provide a balanced overview of the policy's implementation and subsequent challenges
  - [ ] To prove that the policy was designed with malicious intent
  - [ ] To compare the policy to ancient historical trade agreements
   > The passage reviews both the historical rise/successes and subsequent challenges, making "balanced overview" the correct macro-purpose.

2. An inference question asks what can be deduced about a company based on the text: "Company X allocated 40% of its budget to R&D in 2021, higher than any competitor." Which inference is strictly valid?
  - [ ] Company X produced the most profitable patents in 2021.
  - [ ] Company X will dominate the market for the next decade.
  - [x] In 2021, no competitor allocated a larger percentage of its budget to R&D than Company X.
  - [ ] Company X spent more total money on R&D than any other firm in history.
   > The text explicitly states Company X's percentage was "higher than any competitor", making Option C a direct, unassailable restatement of that fact.

3. When answering a "Function" question asking why an author introduced a specific anecdote in Paragraph 3, what should your primary focus be?
  - [ ] Summarizing the plot of the anecdote in detail
  - [x] Explaining how the anecdote serves to illustrate or support the paragraph's broader argument
  - [ ] Checking whether the anecdote contains historical spelling errors
  - [ ] Finding a synonym for every word in the anecdote
   > Function questions ask *why* the author included an element—requiring you to identify its structural support role within the argument.
