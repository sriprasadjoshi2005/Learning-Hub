# English syllabus plan (50 lessons, foundation to university entrance-exam level)

Phase 1 : Foundations 1–12: Introduction to English structure, parts of speech (nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, interjections), articles and determiners, basic sentence formation, present and past simple/continuous tenses, subject–verb agreement, basic vocabulary development, punctuation, capitalization, and basic reading/writing principles.

Phase 2 : Intermediate grammar and writing 13–26: Advanced tense usage, modal verbs, active and passive voice, direct and indirect speech, conditional sentences, non-finite verbs, phrases and clauses, sentence transformation, advanced prepositions and conjunctions, vocabulary expansion and word formation, idioms and phrasal verbs, reading comprehension, paragraph and essay writing, and practical/functional writing.

Phase 3 : Advanced language and analysis 27–40: Advanced grammar and sentence structures, subject–verb agreement exceptions, complex clause analysis, advanced modifiers and grammatical precision, advanced vocabulary and morphology, synonyms, antonyms and nuances of meaning, advanced idiomatic and figurative language, critical reading and text analysis, literary appreciation and interpretation, advanced academic reading, academic and analytical writing, advanced editing and error correction, listening and spoken English, and advanced communication and presentation.

Phase 4 : Examination mastery and mocks 41–50: University entrance English examination overview, advanced grammar for competitive examinations, vocabulary for competitive examinations, reading comprehension exam practice, sentence arrangement and logical coherence, cloze tests and contextual grammar, verbal ability and critical reasoning, advanced English examination techniques, full-length English mock examination I, and full-length English mock examination II with performance analysis.

The sequence begins with fundamental English grammatical concepts and builds toward problems commonly encountered in board, state-level entrance and competitive university entrance examinations (such as SAT, ACT, CUET, and GRE). Check the latest official syllabus for the examination being attempted; this learning plan is a broad study pathway, not an official examination syllabus.
