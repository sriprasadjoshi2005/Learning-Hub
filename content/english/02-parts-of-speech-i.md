Every English word belongs to a functional grammatical category known as a part of speech. Nouns and pronouns serve as the primary building blocks of nomenclature and reference within sentences.

## Main Concept 1: Nouns

A **noun** is a word used to identify a person, place, thing, or abstract idea. Nouns function as subjects, direct objects, indirect objects, subject complements, and objects of prepositions.

- **Definitions and terminology:** Nouns are categorized by tangible status (concrete vs. abstract) and specificity (common vs. proper).
- **Important principles and rules:** Proper nouns must always be capitalized regardless of their placement in a sentence.
- **Classifications and subcategories:**
  - *Proper Nouns:* Specific names of people, places, or organizations (*London, Shakespeare, UNESCO*).
  - *Common Nouns:* Generic names for categories of people, places, or things (*city, playwright, organization*).
  - *Abstract Nouns:* Non-physical concepts, qualities, or states (*freedom, integrity, knowledge*).
  - *Collective Nouns:* Singular nouns representing groups of individuals (*committee, flock, jury*).
  - *Countable vs. Uncountable Nouns:* Countable nouns accept plural forms (*book/books*); uncountable nouns do not accept standard plurals or direct numerical counts (*information, water, advice*).

## Main Concept 2: Pronouns

A **pronoun** is a functional word that replaces a noun or noun phrase (known as its *antecedent*) to prevent repetitive phrasing.

### Subtopic 1: Personal and Possessive Pronouns

- **Personal Pronouns:** Represent specific persons or things across grammatical cases:
  - *Subjective Case:* $I, you, he, she, it, we, they$
  - *Objective Case:* $me, you, him, her, it, us, them$
- **Possessive Pronouns:** Indicate ownership without needing a following noun ($mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs$). Contrast with possessive adjectives ($my, your, his, her, its, our, their$).

### Subtopic 2: Demonstrative, Reflexive, and Relative Pronouns

- **Demonstrative Pronouns:** Point to specific items in space or time ($this, that, these, those$).
- **Reflexive / Emphatic Pronouns:** End in *-self* or *-selves* ($myself, himself, themselves$). They act reflexively when the subject and object are identical, or emphatically for stress.
- **Relative Pronouns:** Introduce relative clauses modifying a preceding noun ($who, whom, whose, which, that$).

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Noun / Pronoun Category | Primary Sub-types | Example Words | Sentence Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proper Noun | Specific Names | *Paris, Dr. Watson* | *Dr. Watson* arrived early. |
| Uncountable Noun | Mass / Abstract | *Information, Furniture* | The *information* was vital. |
| Personal Pronoun | Subjective / Objective | *He, Him, They, Them* | *They* invited *him* to speak. |
| Possessive Pronoun | Absolute Ownership | *Mine, Hers, Theirs* | The final decision is *hers*. |
| Reflexive Pronoun | Self-referential | *Myself, Ourselves* | She taught *herself* code. |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

The relationship between a pronoun and its antecedent follows strict agreement rules:

$$\text{Antecedent (Gender, Number, Person)} \iff \text{Pronoun (Matching Gender, Number, Person)}$$

$$\text{Uncountable Noun} \longrightarrow \text{Singular Verb Agreement} \quad (\text{e.g., } \text{Furniture} + \text{is}, \text{NOT } \text{are})$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Confusing Possessive Pronouns and Contractions:** *Its* (possessive pronoun) never takes an apostrophe. *It's* is a contraction for *it is* or *it has*.
- **Uncountable Noun Pluralization Errors:** Adding *-s* to mass nouns like *advice*, *equipment*, or *information* is incorrect (e.g., write *pieces of advice*, not *advices*).
- **Subjective vs. Objective Pronoun Confusion:** Using objective pronouns in compound subjects (e.g., *Him and me went*) instead of subjective pronouns (*He and I went*).

## Quiz

1. Which sentence contains a correctly used uncountable noun?
  - [ ] The professor provided several helpful informations.
  - [ ] She bought new furnitures for the office space.
  - [x] High-quality equipment is necessary for the experiment.
  - [ ] He gave me three good advices before the interview.
   > "Equipment" is an uncountable mass noun that takes a singular verb ("is") and does not take a plural "-s".

2. Select the sentence that uses a possessive pronoun correctly.
  - [ ] The dog chased it's own tail across the yard.
  - [ ] The red notebook on the desk is her's.
  - [x] Their team completed the project, but ours won the award.
  - [ ] My car is parked here, where is your's?
   > "Ours" is a true possessive pronoun and requires no apostrophe. "It's", "her's", and "your's" incorrectly add apostrophes.

3. Identify the reflexive pronoun function in: "The director himself approved the final manuscript."
  - [ ] Direct Object
  - [ ] Subjective Pronoun
  - [x] Emphatic (Intensive) Pronoun
  - [ ] Relative Pronoun
   > "Himself" is used emphatically immediately following the subject "director" to add emphasis, rather than acting as a direct object.
