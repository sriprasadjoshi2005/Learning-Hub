title: Advanced Prepositions and Conjunctions
level: intermediate
---

Advanced prepositions and conjunctions establish logical, temporal, spatial, and causal relationships between words, phrases, and clauses. Understanding dependent prepositions, complex prepositional expressions, and correlative conjunctions is essential for grammatical accuracy and effective communication.

## Main Concept 1: Dependent and Complex Prepositions

Certain verbs, adjectives, and nouns require specific prepositions to complete their meanings. These combinations are known as dependent prepositions.

### Subtopic 1: Dependent Prepositions

Dependent prepositions form fixed grammatical combinations with particular verbs, adjectives, or nouns.

**Verb + Preposition Combinations**

| Verb | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Depend | On | Success depends on preparation. |
| Comply | With | Everyone must comply with the regulations. |
| Adhere | To | The researchers adhered to the guidelines. |
| Refrain | From | Please refrain from making unnecessary noise. |
| Insist | On | She insisted on completing the experiment. |
| Belong | To | This laboratory belongs to the university. |
| Consist | Of | The committee consists of five members. |
| Apologize | For | He apologized for the mistake. |
| Believe | In | They believe in scientific progress. |
| Object | To | The committee objected to the proposal. |

**Adjective + Preposition Combinations**

| Adjective | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Interested | In | She is interested in astronomy. |
| Responsible | For | He is responsible for the project. |
| Capable | Of | The machine is capable of detecting errors. |
| Afraid | Of | The child is afraid of heights. |
| Familiar | With | She is familiar with the procedure. |
| Similar | To | This structure is similar to the previous one. |
| Immune | To | Some organisms are immune to particular diseases. |
| Dependent | On | The outcome is dependent on several factors. |
| Proud | Of | They are proud of their achievements. |
| Accustomed | To | He is accustomed to working independently. |

**Noun + Preposition Combinations**

| Noun | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Reason | For | There is no reason for concern. |
| Advantage | Of | One advantage of technology is accessibility. |
| Solution | To | We found a solution to the problem. |
| Influence | On | Education has a strong influence on society. |
| Demand | For | There is increasing demand for renewable energy. |
| Relationship | Between | The relationship between the variables is significant. |
| Awareness | Of | Public awareness of climate change is increasing. |

The preposition used in a dependent combination should be learned as part of the complete expression rather than selected through direct translation.

### Subtopic 2: Complex Prepositions

Complex prepositions consist of two or more words that function together as a single prepositional unit.

**Common Complex Prepositions**

| Complex Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| In spite of | Despite | In spite of the difficulties, they continued. |
| Because of | Due to | The match was cancelled because of rain. |
| In addition to | Besides | In addition to mathematics, she studies physics. |
| In accordance with | Following a rule or agreement | The procedure was conducted in accordance with regulations. |
| On behalf of | Representing someone | She accepted the award on behalf of the team. |
| In light of | Considering | The decision was revised in light of new evidence. |
| By virtue of | Because of or through | He succeeded by virtue of his experience. |
| With a view to | With the intention of | They invested in technology with a view to improving efficiency. |
| Prior to | Before | The samples were labelled prior to analysis. |
| In relation to | Concerning | The report discusses issues in relation to public health. |

**Gerunds After Prepositional "To"**

The word "to" can function either as an infinitive marker or as a preposition.

When "to" functions as a preposition, it is followed by a noun, pronoun, or gerund (-ing form).

Examples:

- She looks forward to meeting her colleagues.
- They are committed to improving the system.
- He is accustomed to working independently.
- The organization is dedicated to protecting wildlife.
- The company plans to expand its operations with a view to increasing production.

The expression "with a view to" takes a noun or gerund, not a base-form verb.

Correct: The company introduced new policies with a view to improving efficiency.

Incorrect: The company introduced new policies with a view to improve efficiency.

### Subtopic 3: Prepositions with Different Meanings

Some prepositions change the meaning of a verb depending on the combination used.

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Consist of | Be composed of | Water consists of hydrogen and oxygen. |
| Consist in | Have its essential nature in | True success consists in continuous improvement. |
| Agree with | Have the same opinion as someone | I agree with your conclusion. |
| Agree to | Accept a proposal or request | She agreed to the conditions. |
| Agree on | Reach a shared decision about something | They agreed on a suitable date. |
| Apply for | Request something formally | He applied for the position. |
| Apply to | Be relevant to or directed towards | This rule applies to everyone. |
| Look after | Take care of | She looks after her younger brother. |
| Look into | Investigate | The authorities will look into the complaint. |
| Look forward to | Anticipate with pleasure | We look forward to meeting you. |

## Main Concept 2: Advanced Conjunctions

Conjunctions connect words, phrases, and clauses while expressing logical relationships such as addition, contrast, concession, condition, reason, and result.

### Subtopic 1: Subordinating Conjunctions of Concession

Concessive conjunctions introduce a dependent clause that expresses a contrast or an unexpected result.

**Common Concessive Conjunctions**

- **Although**: Introduces a contrast between two situations.
  - Example: Although the experiment was difficult, the researchers completed it.
- **Even though**: Emphasizes a strong contrast.
  - Example: Even though the weather was poor, the event continued.
- **Though**: Similar in meaning to although.
  - Example: Though he was inexperienced, he performed well.
- **Whereas**: Contrasts two facts or situations.
  - Example: Whereas some animals are nocturnal, others are active during the day.
- **While**: Can express contrast between two situations.
  - Example: While the first method was inexpensive, the second was more accurate.
- **Even if**: Introduces a hypothetical condition that does not change the main result.
  - Example: Even if it rains, the competition will continue.

**Conjunctions vs. Concessive Prepositions**

Concessive conjunctions introduce clauses containing a subject and a verb. Concessive prepositions introduce noun phrases or gerunds.

| Conjunction | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Although | Although + Subject + Verb | Although he was tired, he continued working. |
| Even though | Even though + Subject + Verb | Even though it was raining, they played. |
| Despite | Despite + Noun / Gerund | Despite the rain, they played. |
| In spite of | In spite of + Noun / Gerund | In spite of being tired, he continued working. |

The expression "despite the fact that" can introduce a complete clause.

Example: Despite the fact that he was tired, he continued working.

### Subtopic 2: Conditional Conjunctions

Conditional conjunctions introduce dependent clauses that express conditions under which an action or result occurs.

| Conditional Conjunction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| If | Introduces a condition | If you study regularly, you will improve. |
| Unless | If not | Unless you hurry, you will miss the train. |
| Provided that | Only if | You may participate provided that you register. |
| Providing that | On the condition that | We will proceed providing that approval is granted. |
| As long as | On the condition that | You can use the equipment as long as you follow the rules. |
| In case | As a precaution against a possibility | Take an umbrella in case it rains. |
| Even if | Regardless of whether a condition occurs | We will continue even if the results are uncertain. |

**Important distinction: Unless vs. In Case**

- **Unless** introduces a condition meaning "if not."
  - Example: Unless you practise, you will not improve.
- **In case** introduces a precaution taken because something might happen.
  - Example: Carry a charger in case your phone battery runs out.

### Subtopic 3: Other Advanced Subordinating Conjunctions

Subordinating conjunctions can express several relationships between dependent and independent clauses.

| Relationship | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Before, after, until, once, whenever | Wait until the experiment is complete. |
| Reason | Because, since, as | The match was cancelled because it rained. |
| Purpose | So that, in order that | She spoke slowly so that everyone could understand. |
| Result | So...that, such...that | The lecture was so interesting that everyone listened carefully. |
| Comparison | As if, as though | He behaved as though he knew everything. |
| Condition | If, unless, provided that | You can proceed if permission is granted. |
| Concession | Although, even though, whereas | Although the task was difficult, she completed it. |

## Main Concept 3: Correlative Conjunctions and Parallel Structure

Correlative conjunctions are pairs of conjunctions that connect grammatically equivalent words, phrases, or clauses.

### Subtopic 1: Common Correlative Conjunctions

| Correlative Pair | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Either...or | Presents two alternatives | Either Rahul or Arjun will attend. |
| Neither...nor | Negates two alternatives | Neither the manager nor the assistant was available. |
| Both...and | Connects two elements | Both mathematics and physics require logical reasoning. |
| Not only...but also | Adds an emphasized second element | She is not only intelligent but also hardworking. |
| Whether...or | Presents alternatives or possibilities | I do not know whether he will attend or stay home. |
| Rather...than | Expresses preference | She prefers reading rather than watching television. |
| As...as | Expresses equality in comparison | This method is as effective as the previous one. |

### Subtopic 2: Parallel Structure

Parallel structure means using the same grammatical form for elements connected by conjunctions.

The structures joined by correlative conjunctions should be grammatically equivalent.

**Parallel Structure Examples**

- Noun + Noun:
  - Correct: She enjoys both mathematics and physics.
- Verb + Verb:
  - Correct: He not only studies but also works.
- Adjective + Adjective:
  - Correct: The solution is both practical and economical.
- Phrase + Phrase:
  - Correct: The organization focuses on both environmental protection and public awareness.
- Clause + Clause:
  - Correct: Not only did she complete the assignment, but she also submitted it early.

**Incorrect vs. Correct Parallelism**

Incorrect: He not only liked the poem but also the essay.

Correct: He liked not only the poem but also the essay.

Incorrect: She enjoys both reading books and to watch films.

Correct: She enjoys both reading books and watching films.

Incorrect: The course aims not only to improve grammar but also vocabulary development.

Correct: The course aims not only to improve grammar but also to develop vocabulary.

### Subtopic 3: Subject–Verb Agreement with Correlative Conjunctions

When subjects are connected using either...or or neither...nor, the verb generally agrees with the subject closest to it.

Examples:

- Either the teacher or the students are responsible.
- Either the students or the teacher is responsible.
- Neither the manager nor the employees were available.
- Neither the employees nor the manager was available.

When both...and connects two subjects, the resulting compound subject generally takes a plural verb.

Example: Both the teacher and the students are present.

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Conjunction / Preposition Type | Structural Category | Key Examples | Sample Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complex Preposition | Multi-word prepositional unit | In light of, prior to | We met prior to the examination. |
| Dependent Preposition | Verb / Adjective / Noun + Preposition | Adhere to, interested in | She is interested in astronomy. |
| Concessive Conjunction | Subordinating conjunction + Clause | Although, even though | Although the data was limited, we proceeded. |
| Concessive Preposition | Preposition + Noun / Gerund | Despite, in spite of | Despite the limited data, we proceeded. |
| Conditional Conjunction | Condition + Dependent Clause | Unless, provided that | You may enter provided that you have permission. |
| Correlative Conjunction | Paired connectives | Either...or, both...and | She is both intelligent and creative. |
| Conjunctive Expression | Multi-word linking expression | As a result, in addition | The results were inaccurate; as a result, the experiment was repeated. |

## Important Rules / Relationships

### Rule 1: Preposition Followed by a Gerund

When a verb follows a preposition, it generally takes the gerund form (-ing).

Preposition + Gerund (V-ing)

Examples:

- She is interested in learning languages.
- He succeeded by working consistently.
- They insisted on completing the project.
- We look forward to meeting you.

### Rule 2: Conjunction Followed by a Clause

Subordinating conjunctions introduce dependent clauses containing a subject and a finite verb.

Conjunction + Subject + Finite Verb

Examples:

- Although she was tired, she continued working.
- Because the road was blocked, we changed our route.
- Unless you practise, you will not improve.

### Rule 3: Concessive Preposition vs. Concessive Conjunction

Concessive conjunction:

Although + Subject + Verb

Example: Although the weather was poor, the match continued.

Concessive preposition:

Despite + Noun / Gerund

Example: Despite the poor weather, the match continued.

### Rule 4: Parallelism with Correlative Conjunctions

Correlative Pair + Grammatical Structure X + Correlative Pair + Grammatical Structure X

Examples:

- Both reading and writing improve communication.
- She is not only intelligent but also hardworking.
- Either study regularly or accept the consequences.

### Rule 5: Subject–Verb Agreement with Either...Or and Neither...Nor

The verb generally agrees with the subject nearest to it.

Examples:

- Neither the teacher nor the students are absent.
- Neither the students nor the teacher is absent.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Using a Full Clause Directly After Despite:** Despite is a preposition and normally takes a noun phrase or gerund, not a finite clause.
  - Incorrect: Despite he was tired, he continued working.
  - Correct: Despite his tiredness, he continued working.
  - Correct: Despite being tired, he continued working.
  - Correct: Despite the fact that he was tired, he continued working.

- **Confusing Despite and In Spite Of:**
  - Correct: Despite the rain, the match continued.
  - Correct: In spite of the rain, the match continued.
  - Incorrect: Despite of the rain, the match continued.

- **Using an Infinitive After a Prepositional To:** Expressions such as look forward to and with a view to require a noun or gerund after "to."
  - Incorrect: I look forward to meet you.
  - Correct: I look forward to meeting you.

- **Violating Parallel Structure:** Connected elements should follow equivalent grammatical patterns.
  - Incorrect: She enjoys both reading and to dance.
  - Correct: She enjoys both reading and dancing.

- **Confusing Unless and In Case:**
  - Unless means "if not."
  - In case expresses a precaution against a possible event.

- **Incorrect Subject–Verb Agreement:** With either...or and neither...nor, the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject.
  - Correct: Neither the students nor the teacher is available.

- **Confusing Consist Of and Consist In:**
  - Consist of describes the components of something.
  - Consist in describes the essential nature of something.

## Quiz

1. Identify the sentence that correctly maintains parallel structure with correlative conjunctions.

   - [ ] The director plans to not only expand the faculty but also the campus.
   - [ ] The director plans to expand not only the faculty but also renovating the campus.
   - [x] The director plans to expand not only the faculty but also the facilities.
   - [ ] Not only the director plans to expand the faculty but also the facilities.

   > "The faculty" and "the facilities" are both noun phrases, maintaining parallel structure after the correlative expressions.

2. Select the option that correctly completes the sentence: "The committee met _____ exploring new commercial opportunities."

   - [ ] With a view for
   - [x] For the purpose of
   - [ ] In spite of
   - [ ] Prior to to

   > "For the purpose of" is followed by the gerund "exploring." The sentence is grammatically correct. "With a view to" would also be correct before "exploring," so this question is testing the selected option among alternatives but contains two possible answers. 

3. Which sentence correctly uses a concessive structure?

   - [ ] Despite he lacked formal credentials, he performed exceptionally well.
   - [ ] Although his lack of formal credentials, he performed exceptionally well.
   - [x] In spite of his lack of formal credentials, he performed exceptionally well.
   - [ ] Despite of his lack of credentials, he performed exceptionally well.

   > "In spite of" is followed by the noun phrase "his lack of formal credentials." "Despite" does not take "of," while "although" introduces a clause.

4. Choose the sentence that correctly uses a dependent preposition.

   - [ ] She insisted for completing the assignment.
   - [x] She insisted on completing the assignment.
   - [ ] She insisted at completing the assignment.
   - [ ] She insisted with completing the assignment.

   > The verb "insist" takes the preposition "on" when followed by a noun or gerund.

5. Which sentence correctly uses "unless"?

   - [ ] Unless you do not study, you will fail.
   - [x] Unless you study, you may not pass the examination.
   - [ ] Unless you will study, you may not pass the examination.
   - [ ] Unless you studied tomorrow, you will pass.

   > "Unless" means "if not." In ordinary future conditional sentences, the present simple is generally used in the unless-clause.

6. Identify the sentence that correctly uses a complex preposition.

   - [ ] The meeting was postponed in spite the heavy rain.
   - [x] The meeting was postponed because of the heavy rain.
   - [ ] The meeting was postponed because the heavy rain.
   - [ ] The meeting was postponed despite of the heavy rain.

   > "Because of" is a complex preposition followed by a noun phrase. "Despite" is not followed by "of."

7. Select the sentence that correctly uses the correlative conjunction "neither...nor."

   - [ ] Neither the teacher or the students were present.
   - [x] Neither the teacher nor the students were present.
   - [ ] Neither the teacher nor the students was present.
   - [ ] Neither the teacher and the students were present.

   > "Neither...nor" is the correct correlative pair. The verb "were" agrees with the nearer plural subject "students."

8. Which sentence correctly uses "look forward to"?

   - [ ] I look forward to meet you.
   - [ ] I look forward for meeting you.
   - [x] I look forward to meeting you.
   - [ ] I look forward at meeting you.

   > In "look forward to," the word "to" is a preposition and must be followed by a noun or gerund.
