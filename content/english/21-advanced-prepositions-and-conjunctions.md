Advanced prepositions and conjunctions establish subtle logical, temporal, spatial, and causal links between sentence components. Mastery over fixed prepositional idioms and paired conjunctions is essential for syntactic precision and academic fluency.

## Main Concept 1: Dependent and Complex Prepositions

Many verbs, adjectives, and nouns require specific prepositions to complete their meaning. These are known as **dependent prepositions**.

- **Definitions and terminology:** 
  - *Dependent Prepositions:* Fixed combinations where a specific verb, adjective, or noun pairs with a dedicated preposition (e.g., *comply with, adhere to, immune from/to*).
  - *Complex (Multi-word) Prepositions:* Two- or three-word phrases acting as a single prepositional unit (e.g., *in light of, prior to, with a view to, by virtue of, on behalf of*).
- **Important principles and rules:**
  - Complex prepositions like *with a view to* or *look forward to* take a **gerund ($V_4$)**, not a base infinitive (e.g., *with a view to **expanding** operations*, NOT *to expand*).
  - Distinguish between prepositional pairs with distinct meanings (e.g., *consist of* [comprise] vs. *consist in* [inhere/exist in]).

## Main Concept 2: Advanced Conjunctions and Parallelism

Conjunctions connect words, phrases, or clauses, signaling specific logical relationships such as concession, condition, addition, or contrast.

### Subtopic 1: Subordinating Conjunctions of Concession and Condition

- **Concessive Conjunctions:** *Although, even though, whereas, while, despite the fact that*. Contrast with prepositions like *despite* or *in spite of* (which take noun phrases or gerunds, not full finite clauses).
- **Conditional Conjunctions:** *Provided that, as long as, unless* (means *if... not*), *in case* (precautionary condition).

### Subtopic 2: Correlative Conjunctions and Parallel Structure

Correlative conjunctions work in matching pairs to join grammatically equivalent structures:
- **Pairs:** *either... or, neither... nor, not only... but also, both... and, whether... or*.
- **Parallel Structure Rule:** The grammatical element following the first conjunction must match the grammatical structure following the second conjunction.
  - *Non-Parallel:* *He **not only liked** the poem, **but also the essay**.* (Verb + Noun vs. Noun)
  - *Parallel:* *He liked **not only the poem** **but also the essay**.* (Noun vs. Noun)

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Conjunction / Preposition Type | Structural Category | Key Examples | Sample Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complex Preposition | Phrase + Gerund/Noun | *with a view to, prior to* | We met *with a view to resolving* the dispute. |
| Concessive Conjunction | Subordinating | *although, whereas, even if* | *Although* the data was limited, we proceeded. |
| Concessive Preposition | Preposition + Noun | *despite, in spite of* | *Despite* the limited data, we proceeded. |
| Correlative Pair | Paired Connectives | *not only... but also* | She is *not only* diligent *but also* creative. |
| Fixed Dependent Prep. | Verb/Adj + Prep | *adhere to, refrain from* | You must *adhere to* safety guidelines. |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Parallelism Equation for Correlative Conjunctions:

$$\text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \mathbf{\text{Correlative}_1} + [\text{Grammatical Structure } X] + \mathbf{\text{Correlative}_2} + [\text{Grammatical Structure } X]$$

Complex Preposition Gerund Rule:

$$\text{Phrase ending in preposition } (\textit{look forward to} / \textit{with a view to}) + \mathbf{\text{Gerund } (V_4)}$$

$$\text{Example}: \text{Look forward to } \mathbf{\text{meeting}} \quad [\text{NOT } \text{look forward to } \text{meet}]$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Using a Full Clause After Prepositions Like "Despite":** Inserting a subject-verb clause directly after *despite* without *the fact that* (e.g., writing *Despite he was tired...* instead of *Despite **his tiredness**...* or *Despite **the fact that he was tired**...*).
- **Violating Parallelism with Correlative Conjunctions:** Placing paired conjunctions unevenly around different parts of speech (e.g., *She both enjoyed the lecture and the workshop* instead of *She enjoyed **both** the lecture **and** the workshop*).
- **Base Infinitive Error After "With a View To":** Following *with a view to* with a base verb instead of a gerund (e.g., writing *with a view to establish* instead of *with a view to establishing*).

## Quiz

1. Identify the sentence that correctly maintains parallel structure with correlative conjunctions.
  - [ ] The director plan to not only expand the faculty but also the campus.
  - [ ] The director plans to expand not only the faculty but also renovated the campus.
  - [x] The director plans to expand not only the faculty but also the facilities.
  - [ ] Not only the director plans to expand the faculty but also the facilities.
   > "Not only the faculty" (Noun Phrase) matches "but also the facilities" (Noun Phrase) in parallel structure.

2. Select the option that correctly completes the sentence: "The committee met _____ exploring new commercial opportunities."
  - [ ] with a view to
  - [x] with a view to
  - [ ] in spite of
  - [ ] prior to to
   > "With a view to" is a complex preposition meaning "with the intention of" and is correctly followed by the gerund "exploring".

3. Which sentence correctly uses concessive structures?
  - [ ] Despite he lacked formal credentials, he performed exceptionally well.
  - [ ] Although his lack of formal credentials, he performed exceptionally well.
  - [x] In spite of his lack of formal credentials, he performed exceptionally well.
  - [ ] Despite of his lack of credentials, he performed exceptionally well.
   > "In spite of" is correctly followed by the noun phrase "his lack of formal credentials". "Despite" cannot take "of", and "although" requires a finite clause.
