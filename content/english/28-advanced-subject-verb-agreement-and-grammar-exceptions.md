Advanced subject–verb agreement explores complex syntax exceptions—including fraction and percentage quantification, inverted locative constructions, parenthetical additions, plural-form singular nouns, and collective noun variations across English dialects.

## Main Concept 1: Quantifiers, Fractions, and Percentages

When subjects involve fractions, percentages, or proportional quantifiers (*a majority of, a lot of, the remainder of*), agreement depends on the **object of the preposition**.

- **Definitions and terminology:** This rule is known as **Agreement by Attraction** or the **Proportional Majority Rule**.
- **The Rule:**
  - If the prepositional object is **uncountable**, the verb is **singular**.
  - If the prepositional object is **plural countable**, the verb is **plural**.
- **Examples:**
  - *Two-thirds of the **water** **has** evaporated.* (Uncountable $\rightarrow$ Singular)
  - *Two-thirds of the **manuscripts** **have** been reviewed.* (Plural Countable $\rightarrow$ Plural)
  - *A majority of the **voters** **are** in favor.* (Plural Countable $\rightarrow$ Plural)

## Main Concept 2: Parenthetical Additions and Noun Exceptions

### Subtopic 1: Parenthetical Connectives vs. Compound "And"

When a singular subject is accompanied by phrases introduced by *along with, as well as, together with, in addition to, accompanied by, including*, these phrases are **parenthetical**. They do **not** form a compound subject and do **not** change the number of the head subject.

- *Incorrect:* *The director, as well as his assistants, **were** praised.*
- *Correct:* *The **director** [as well as his assistants] **was** praised.*

### Subtopic 2: Tricky Noun Categories and Dialect Variations

- **Nouns Plural in Form but Singular in Meaning:** Disciplines (*physics, mathematics, economics*), diseases (*measles, mumps*), and news (*news*) take **singular verbs** (*Mathematics **is** fascinating*).
- **Nouns Plural in Form and Meaning:** Pair nouns (*scissors, trousers, binoculars, tongs*) take **plural verbs** (*The scissors **are** sharp*), unless preceded by *a pair of* (*A pair of scissors **is** on the table*).
- **"The Number of" vs. "A Number of":**
  - ***The** number of + Plural Noun $\rightarrow$ **Singular Verb*** (*The number of applicants **is** high.*).
  - ***A** number of + Plural Noun $\rightarrow$ **Plural Verb*** (*A number of applicants **are** waiting.*).

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Complex Subject Pattern | Governing Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Fraction / % + Uncountable | Singular Verb | $60\%$ of the land *is* urbanized. |
| Fraction / % + Plural Noun | Plural Verb | $60\%$ of the trees *were* cut. |
| Subject + *as well as* + Noun | Ignore *as well as*; match main Subject | The captain *as well as* the crew *was* lost. |
| *The number of* + Plural Noun | Singular Verb | The number of cases *has* dropped. |
| *A number of* + Plural Noun | Plural Verb | A number of cases *have* emerged. |
| Nouns of Discipline (*Physics*) | Singular Verb | Economics *is* taught in room 4. |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Proportional Quantification Formula:

$$\text{Fraction / Percentage / Majority} + \text{of the } + \mathbf{\text{Noun Object}} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{Uncountable Noun} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Singular Verb}} \\ \text{Plural Countable Noun} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Plural Verb}} \end{cases}$$

Parenthetical Expression Rule:

$$\mathbf{\text{Head Subject}} + [\text{as well as / along with / together with } + \text{Other Nouns}] + \mathbf{\text{Verb matching Head Subject}}$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Distraction by "As Well As" Phrases:** Treating *as well as* or *along with* like *and*, resulting in erroneous plural verbs (e.g., writing *The CEO along with her directors are attending* instead of *is attending*).
- **Confusing "The Number" with "A Number":** Misapplying singular verbs to *a number of* or plural verbs to *the number of*.
- **Pluralizing Academic Disciplines:** Using plural verbs for subjects ending in *-ics* when referring to academic disciplines (e.g., writing *Physics are difficult* instead of *Physics is difficult*).

  - [ ] Three-fourths of the ancient forest were destroyed by the wildfire.
  - [x] Three-fourths of the research budget has been allocated to testing.
  - [ ] Three-fourths of the research budget have been allocated to testing.
  - [ ] Three-fourths of the lab instruments was recalibrated.
   > "Budget" is an uncountable singular noun phrase; fractions modifying uncountable nouns require a singular verb ("has been allocated").

2. Select the option that correctly completes the sentence: "The lead investigator, along with three field assistants, _____ arriving on tonight's flight."
  - [ ] are
  - [x] is
  - [ ] were
  - [ ] have been
   > The phrase "along with three field assistants" is parenthetical. The head subject is singular ("The lead investigator"), requiring "is".

3. Complete the sentence: "_____ number of rare manuscript errors _____ been documented by the archivist."
  - [ ] The; have
  - [ ] A; has
  - [x] A; have
  - [ ] The; are
   > "A number of" functions as a plural quantifier requiring the plural verb "have".
