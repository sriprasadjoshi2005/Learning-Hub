title: Conditional Sentences
level: intermediate

Conditional sentences express hypothetical situations, cause-and-effect relationships, dependencies, and counterfactual possibilities. A conditional sentence consists of a dependent **condition clause** (the *if*-clause) and an independent **result clause** (the main clause).

## Main Concept 1: The Four Standard Conditional Types

Conditionals are categorized according to their degree of reality and time frame.

- **Definitions and terminology:** 
  - *Real Conditionals:* Express factual truths or real possibilities (Zero and First Conditionals).
  - *Unreal Conditionals:* Express hypothetical, improbable, or counterfactual conditions (Second and Third Conditionals).

### 1. Zero Conditional (General Truths & Scientific Laws)
Expresses absolute facts, scientific realities, or habitual consequences.
- **Formula:** $\text{If} + \text{Present Simple} \longrightarrow \text{Present Simple}$
- *Example:* *If you heat water to $100^\circ\text{C}$, it boils.*

### 2. First Conditional (Real Present / Future Possibility)
Expresses realistic future possibilities based on plausible present conditions.
- **Formula:** $\text{If} + \text{Present Simple} \longrightarrow \text{Will / Can / May} + V_1$
- *Example:* *If the team completes the report today, we will submit it tomorrow.*

### 3. Second Conditional (Unreal Present / Future Hypothesis)
Expresses hypothetical, imaginary, or highly improbable situations in the present or future.
- **Formula:** $\text{If} + \text{Past Simple} \longrightarrow \text{Would / Could / Might} + V_1$
- *Example:* *If I had the funding right now, I would launch the study.*
- *Subjunctive Note:* The verb *be* becomes **were** for all persons in formal register (*If I **were** you...*).

### 4. Third Conditional (Unreal Past Counterfactual)
Expresses completely counterfactual past conditions and their impossible past outcomes (regrets or alternate histories).
- **Formula:** $\text{If} + \text{Past Perfect } (\text{had } + V_3) \longrightarrow \text{Would / Could / Might have} + V_3$
- *Example:* *If we had secured the patent earlier, we would have prevented the lawsuit.*

## Main Concept 2: Mixed Conditionals and Inversion

### Subtopic 1: Mixed Conditionals

Combine different temporal frameworks between the condition and result clauses:
- **Past Condition $\rightarrow$ Present Result:** A past event or choice alters a present state.
  - *Formula:* $\text{If} + \text{Past Perfect } (\text{had } + V_3) \longrightarrow \text{Would} + V_1\ \text{(Present)}$
  - *Example:* *If I had accepted that job last year, I **would live** in Paris now.*
- **Present Condition $\rightarrow$ Past Result:** An ongoing character trait or condition caused a specific past action.
  - *Formula:* $\text{If} + \text{Past Simple} \longrightarrow \text{Would have} + V_3\ \text{(Past)}$
  - *Example:* *If she **were** not so stubborn, she **would have accepted** their help yesterday.*

### Subtopic 2: Inverted Conditional Structures (Formal / Advanced)

Formal register allows omitting the conjunction *if* by inverting the auxiliary verb and subject:
- **First Conditional Inversion:** $\text{Should you require assistance...} \quad (\text{If you should require...})$
- **Second Conditional Inversion:** $\text{Were I in his position...} \quad (\text{If I were in his position...})$
- **Third Conditional Inversion:** $\text{Had we known the risk...} \quad (\text{If we had known the risk...})$

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Conditional Type | Condition Clause (*if*-clause) | Main Result Clause | Function / Timeframe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero Conditional | Present Simple | Present Simple | Universal Fact / Always |
| First Conditional | Present Simple | $\text{Will} + V_1$ | Real Possibility / Future |
| Second Conditional | Past Simple (Subjunctive *were*) | $\text{Would} + V_1$ | Hypothetical / Present-Future |
| Third Conditional | Past Perfect ($\text{had} + V_3$) | $\text{Would have} + V_3$ | Counterfactual / Past |
| Mixed (Past/Pres) | Past Perfect ($\text{had} + V_3$) | $\text{Would} + V_1$ | Past Action $\rightarrow$ Present Outcome |
| Inverted Third | $\text{Had} + \text{Subject} + V_3$ | $\text{Would have} + V_3$ | Formal Counterfactual Past |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Core Structural Summary:

$$\text{Zero}: \text{If} + \text{Present} \rightarrow \text{Present}$$

$$\text{First}: \text{If} + \text{Present} \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{Will}} + V_1$$

$$\text{Second}: \text{If} + \text{Past Simple} \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{Would}} + V_1$$

$$\text{Third}: \text{If} + \mathbf{\text{had}} + V_3 \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{Would have}} + V_3$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Using "Will" or "Would" Inside the *If*-Clause:** Incorrectly putting modal auxiliaries inside the condition clause (e.g., writing *If it will rain, we will stay* instead of *If it rains, we will stay*).
- **Was vs. Were in Second Conditional Subjunctive:** Using *was* instead of *were* for singular subjects in formal test items (e.g., writing *If he was president* instead of *If he **were** president*).
- **Mismatched Auxiliaries in Inverted Third Conditionals:** Retaining *if* while using auxiliary inversion (e.g., writing *If had I known* instead of *Had I known* or *If I had known*).

## Quiz

1. Select the grammatically correct Third Conditional sentence.
  - [ ] If the team had worked faster, they would finish the building on time.
  - [ ] If the team worked faster, they would have finished the building on time.
  - [x] If the team had worked faster, they would have finished the building on time.
  - [ ] If the team would have worked faster, they had finished the building on time.
   > Third conditional requires Past Perfect ("had worked") in the if-clause and Modal Perfect ("would have finished") in the main result clause.

2. Identify the inverted conditional equivalent of: "If you had informed us earlier, we would have adjusted the schedule."
  - [ ] Should you inform us earlier, we would have adjusted the schedule.
  - [ ] Were you to inform us earlier, we would adjust the schedule.
  - [x] Had you informed us earlier, we would have adjusted the schedule.
  - [ ] Had you inform us earlier, we would have adjusted the schedule.
   > Inverting a Third Conditional requires removing "if" and moving "Had" to the front of the subject: "Had you informed...".

3. Complete the sentence: "If she _____ more cautious, she would not make these careless mistakes."
  - [ ] is
  - [ ] was
  - [x] were
  - [ ] has been
   > The Second Conditional expresses present hypothetical conditions using the subjunctive "were" for all subjects.
