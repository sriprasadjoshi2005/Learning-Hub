Competitive examination grammar items test precise structural rules rather than informal usage. High-yield exam topics include modifier alignment, subject-verb concord exceptions, parallelism, pronoun-antecedent agreement, and tense consistency.

## Main Concept 1: High-Yield Error Patterns

Examiners systematically target predictable grammatical structures.

- **Definitions and terminology:**
  - *Error Location:* Identifying the precise segment containing a syntactic defect.
  - *Sentence Correction:* Choosing the option that rectifies the error while preserving concise, formal register.

### Core High-Yield Rules

1. **Parallelism in Correlative Structures:** Elements connected by *not only... but also, either... or, neither... nor*, and *both... and* must match grammatically.
2. **Modifier Proximity:** Participial phrases must immediately precede or follow the noun they modify.
3. **Subjunctive Mood in Mandative Clauses:** Verbs like *demand, propose, insist, require* take base form verbs without *-s* or *-ed*.
4. **Subject-Verb Agreement with Intervening Modifiers:** Verbs must agree with the true head noun, ignoring intervening prepositional phrases (*along with, as well as, together with*).

## Main Concept 2: Tense Consistency and Sub-Clausal Logic

### Subtopic 1: Sequence of Tenses

- **Past Principal Clause:** If the main clause is in the past tense, subordinate clauses must take a past form, unless stating a universal scientific truth.
  - *Incorrect:* *The researcher proved that the compound **dissolves** in acid.* (If presented as a specific trial result rather than universal law).
  - *Correct:* *The researcher proved that the compound **dissolved** in acid.*
- **Subordinate Future Conditionals:** Never use *will* or *would* inside dependent *if/when* clauses.
  - *Incorrect:* *If the board **will approve** the budget, we will start.*
  - *Correct:* *If the board **approves** the budget, we will start.*

### Subtopic 2: Pronoun Case in Comparative Ellipsis

In comparative structures introduced by *than* or *as*, the pronoun case depends on the unstated verb in the implied clause:
- *He respects the director more than **I** [respect the director].* (Subjective case)
- *He respects the director more than **me**.* (Objective case: *He respects the director more than he respects me*).

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| High-Yield Exam Topic | Common Distractor Error | Standard Correct Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Modifier Alignment | Introductory phrase modifies wrong noun | $\text{Intro Phrase} + \mathbf{\text{True Actor Noun}} + \text{Verb}$ |
| Correlative Parallelism | Mismatched parts of speech after pairs | $\text{Not only} + \mathbf{\text{Noun}} + \text{but also} + \mathbf{\text{Noun}}$ |
| Mandative Subjunctive | Adding *-s* or *-ed* after *demand that* | $\text{Demand that} + \text{Subject} + \mathbf{V_1\ \text{(Base form)}}$ |
| Parenthetical Agreement | Plural verb used with *as well as* | $\text{Singular Subj} + [\text{as well as } X] + \mathbf{\text{Singular Verb}}$ |
| Conditional Tense | Using *will/would* inside *if*-clause | $\text{If} + \mathbf{\text{Present Simple}} \rightarrow \text{Will} + V_1$ |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Sentence Correction Audit Equation:

$$\text{Sentence Under Review} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{Check 1: Does Subject match Verb in number?} \\ \text{Check 2: Is the introductory modifier touching its target?} \\ \text{Check 3: Are items in lists/comparisons parallel?} \\ \text{Check 4: Are pronouns clear and in the correct case?} \end{cases}$$

Mandative Subjunctive Exam Rule:

$$\text{Insist / Demand / Order / Require} + \text{that} + \text{Subject} + \mathbf{\text{Base Verb } (V_1)} \quad [\text{No } \textit{-s}, \text{no } \textit{-ed}, \text{no } \textit{was}]$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Treating Parenthetical Phrases as Compound Subjects:** Counting *along with* or *as well as* as part of a plural subject (e.g., *The professor along with her assistants **are** presenting* $\rightarrow$ should be ***is** presenting*).
- **Misjudging Comparative Pronoun Cases:** Automatically choosing objective pronouns after *than* when a subjective case is required by elliptical logic (e.g., writing *She speaks faster than him* instead of *She speaks faster than **he** [does]* in formal exams).
- **Ignoring Dangling Participial Phrases:** Accepting introductory *-ing* phrases whose logical subject appears far away or is missing entirely.

## Quiz

1. Identify the grammatically correct sentence correction for: "Having completed the survey, the data was organized by the research assistant."
  - [ ] Having completed the survey, the organization of data was done by the research assistant.
  - [x] Having completed the survey, the research assistant organized the data.
  - [ ] Having completed the survey, data was organized by the research assistant.
  - [ ] Complete the survey, the research assistant organized the data.
   > The introductory phrase "Having completed the survey" must immediately precede the person who completed it ("the research assistant").

2. Which choice represents a correct implementation of the mandative subjunctive mood?
  - [ ] The director requested that every employee attends the health safety briefing.
  - [ ] The director requested that every employee attended the health safety briefing.
  - [x] The director requested that every employee attend the health safety briefing.
  - [ ] The director requested that every employee is attending the health safety briefing.
   > The mandative verb "requested that..." requires the bare base verb ("attend"), regardless of singular third-person subjects.

3. Complete the sentence according to formal competitive examination standards: "The lead designer, together with his associate architects, _____ preparing the final blueprints."
  - [ ] are
  - [x] is
  - [ ] were
  - [ ] have been
   > The phrase "together with his associate architects" is parenthetical. The head subject is singular ("The lead designer"), requiring the singular verb "is".
