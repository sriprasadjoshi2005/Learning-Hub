Tense is a grammatical category that locates a situation in time relative to the moment of speech. The **Present Simple** and **Past Simple** tenses form the basic temporal backbone of English communication.

## Main Concept 1: Present Simple Tense

The **Present Simple** (Simple Present) expresses general truths, habits, repeated actions, permanent states, and scheduled future events.

- **Definitions and terminology:** Expresses actions without emphasizing their continuous aspect or completion status.
- **Structural Formation:**
  - *Affirmative:* Base verb ($V_1$), adding *-s* or *-es* for third-person singular subjects ($he, she, it$).
  - *Negative:* $\text{Subject} + \text{do not / does not} + \text{Base Verb } (V_1)$.
  - *Interrogative:* $\text{Do / Does} + \text{Subject} + \text{Base Verb } (V_1)?$
- **Primary Uses:**
  1. *Habits and Routines:* *She reads academic journals every morning.*
  2. *General Truths and Laws of Nature:* *Water boils at $100^\circ\text{C}$.*
  3. *Permanent Situations:* *They live in Geneva.*
  4. *Stative Verb Conditions:* *I understand the theorem.* (Stative verbs rarely take continuous forms).

## Main Concept 2: Past Simple Tense

The **Past Simple** (Simple Past) expresses completed actions, states, or routines that occurred at a specific, finished time in the past.

### Subtopic 1: Structural Formation

- **Regular Verbs:** Formed by adding *-ed* to the base form ($V_2$) (*walk $\rightarrow$ walked, study $\rightarrow$ studied*).
- **Irregular Verbs:** Have distinct past forms requiring memorization (*go $\rightarrow$ went, write $\rightarrow$ wrote, bring $\rightarrow$ brought*).
- **Negative Structure:** $\text{Subject} + \text{did not (didn't)} + \text{Base Verb } (V_1)$.
- **Interrogative Structure:** $\text{Did} + \text{Subject} + \text{Base Verb } (V_1)?$

### Subtopic 2: Primary Uses and Time Markers

- *Completed Actions in the Past:* Expresses an action that started and finished at a definite past moment (*The conference ended an hour ago.*).
- *Past Routines:* Used alongside adverbs of frequency or structures like *used to* (*In university, he studied eight hours daily.*).
- *Signal Words / Time Markers:* *yesterday, last week, in 2010, two days ago, formerly*.

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Tense | Subject Category | Affirmative | Negative | Interrogative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | $I / You / We / They$ | Base Verb ($V_1$) | do not + $V_1$ | Do + Subj + $V_1$? |
| Present Simple | $He / She / It$ | $V_1 + \text{-s/-es}$ | does not + $V_1$ | Does + Subj + $V_1$? |
| Past Simple | All Subjects (Regular) | $V_1 + \text{-ed}$ ($V_2$) | did not + $V_1$ | Did + Subj + $V_1$? |
| Past Simple | All Subjects (Irregular) | Irregular $V_2$ | did not + $V_1$ | Did + Subj + $V_1$? |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Third-Person Singular Present Spelling Rules ($V_5$):

$$\text{Base Verb Ending} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{-s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z, -o} \longrightarrow \text{Add } \mathbf{\text{-es}} & (\text{e.g., } \text{pass} \rightarrow \text{passes}, \text{go} \rightarrow \text{goes}) \\ \text{Consonant} + \mathbf{\text{-y}} \longrightarrow \text{Change } \text{-y} \text{ to } \mathbf{\text{-ies}} & (\text{e.g., } \text{study} \rightarrow \text{studies}) \\ \text{Vowel} + \mathbf{\text{-y}} \longrightarrow \text{Add } \mathbf{\text{-s}} & (\text{e.g., } \text{play} \rightarrow \text{plays}) \end{cases}$$

Past Simple Negative and Interrogative Invariant Base Rule:

$$\text{Subject} + \mathbf{\text{did not}} + \mathbf{V_1\ \text{(Base Form)}} \quad [\text{NOT } V_2]$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Forgetting Third-Person "-s" in Present Simple:** Omitting the *-s* suffix for third-person singular subjects in positive statements (e.g., writing *He work every day* instead of *He works every day*).
- **Double Past Tense Marking:** Using $V_2$ after *did/didn't* in negative statements or questions (e.g., writing *She didn't wrote the email* instead of *She didn't write the email*).
- **Misusing Present Simple for Ongoing Actions:** Using Present Simple to describe actions occurring right now at the moment of speech (e.g., saying *Look! It rains* instead of *Look! It is raining*).

## Quiz

1. Choose the sentence with correct third-person singular Present Simple agreement.
  - [ ] The analyst monitor market trends daily.
  - [x] The analyst monitors market trends daily.
  - [ ] The analyst do monitor market trends daily.
  - [ ] The analyst monitoring market trends daily.
   > The third-person singular noun "The analyst" requires the verb form "monitors" ($V_1 + \text{-s}$).

2. Select the grammatically correct negative Past Simple construction.
  - [ ] They didn't completed the laboratory report on time.
  - [ ] They don't completed the laboratory report on time.
  - [x] They didn't complete the laboratory report on time.
  - [ ] They did not completing the laboratory report on time.
   > The auxiliary "did not" must be followed by the un-inflected base form of the main verb ("complete").

3. Which time marker specifically signals the use of the Past Simple tense rather than the Present Simple?
  - [ ] Every afternoon
  - [ ] Seldom
  - [ ] On Tuesdays
  - [x] Three weeks ago
   > "Three weeks ago" explicitly refers to a finished, specific time period in the past, triggering the Past Simple tense.
