Grammatical precision requires eliminating structural ambiguities caused by misplaced modifiers, dangling modifiers, squinting modifiers, faulty comparisons, and redundant phrasing.

## Main Concept 1: Dangling and Misplaced Modifiers

A modifier must be placed as close as possible to the specific noun or pronoun it is intended to modify.

- **Definitions and terminology:**
  - **Misplaced Modifier:** A modifier separated from the word it modifies, creating awkward or unintended meanings (*She served dinner to the guests on paper plates* $\rightarrow$ Were the guests on paper plates?).
  - **Dangling Modifier:** A modifier (often an introductory participial phrase) whose intended target noun is completely omitted from the sentence (*Walking to the lab, the rain started* $\rightarrow$ The rain was walking?).
  - **Squinting Modifier:** A modifier placed ambiguously between two words, leaving it unclear which word it modifies (*Cycling frequently improves health* $\rightarrow$ Does frequent cycling improve health, or does cycling improve health frequently?).

## Main Concept 2: Faulty Comparisons and Redundancy

### Subtopic 1: Faulty (Illogical) Comparisons

When comparing items, the items compared must be grammatically and logically parallel.
- **Illogical Comparison:** *Unlike Shakespeare's plays, Bacon was a philosopher.* (Compares plays to a person!).
- **Corrected Comparison:** *Unlike Shakespeare's **plays**, Bacon's **plays** were philosophical.* OR *Unlike **Shakespeare**, Bacon was a philosopher.*
- **Use of "That of / Those of":**
  - *Illogical:* *The climate of Rome is warmer than London.*
  - *Logical:* *The climate of Rome is warmer than **that of** London.*

### Subtopic 2: Tautology and Redundancy

Redundancy occurs when words with identical meanings are duplicated unnecessarily.
- *Redundant:* *revert back, repeat again, combined together, absolute essential, advance planning, past history*.
- *Concise:* *revert, repeat, combined, essential, planning, history*.

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Modifier / Style Fault | Definition / Mechanism | Faulty Example | Corrected Precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dangling Modifier | Target noun omitted from clause | *Having finished the test, the bell rang.* | *Having finished the test, I heard the bell.* |
| Misplaced Modifier | Modifier placed next to wrong noun | *He wore a hat on his head that was blue.* | *He wore a blue hat on his head.* |
| Faulty Comparison | Comparing non-equivalent entities | *Her salary is higher than a manager.* | *Her salary is higher than a manager's.* |
| Redundancy | Duplicating semantic meaning | *Revert back to original state.* | *Revert to original state.* |
| Squinting Modifier | Ambiguous middle placement | *Students who study often succeed.* | *Students who study frequently succeed.* |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Introductory Modifier Target Alignment Rule:

$$\mathbf{\text{Introductory Modifier Phrase}} + \mathbf{,}\ + \mathbf{\text{Target Subject (MUST perform modifier action!)}} + \text{Predicate}$$

Illogical Comparison Correction Formula:

$$\text{Item } A \text{ of Group } 1 \text{ vs. Item } B \text{ of Group } 2 \implies \text{Use } \mathbf{\text{that of (singular)}} / \mathbf{\text{those of (plural)}}$$

$$\text{Example}: \text{The feathers of birds are softer than } \mathbf{\text{those of}} \text{ reptiles.}$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Dangling Participial Introductions:** Starting a sentence with an *-ing* or *-ed* phrase without placing the logical actor immediately after the comma (e.g., writing *After evaluating the data, the conclusion was drawn* instead of *After evaluating the data, **the researcher** drew the conclusion*).
- **Comparing People to Attributes:** Comparing a person directly to a quality, work, or location of another person (e.g., writing *T.S. Eliot's poetry is more complex than Frost* instead of *than Frost's* or *than that of Frost*).
- **Redundant Prepositional Pairs:** Combining *revert* with *back* or *refer* with *back*.

## Quiz

1. Select the sentence that correctly resolves the dangling modifier error.
  - [ ] Exhausted after a long shift, the bed was welcomed by the physician.
  - [ ] Exhausted after a long shift, sleep came quickly to the physician.
  - [x] Exhausted after a long shift, the physician welcomed a good night's sleep.
  - [ ] Exhausted after a long shift, the night went by peacefully.
   > The introductory modifier "Exhausted after a long shift" must be followed immediately by the person who was exhausted ("the physician").

2. Identify the option that contains a logical, grammatically correct comparison.
  - [ ] The engine output of Model A is greater than Model B.
  - [x] The engine output of Model A is greater than that of Model B.
  - [ ] The engine output of Model A is greater than Model B's engines.
  - [ ] Unlike Model A, the output of Model B is low.
   > Option B uses "that of" to compare the singular attribute ("engine output") of Model A to the engine output of Model B.

3. Which phrase contains an ungrammatical redundancy (tautology)?
  - [ ] Evaluate the findings
  - [ ] Postpone until tomorrow
  - [x] Revert back to the former plan
  - [ ] Analyze the parameters
   > "Revert" inherently means to go back to a previous state; adding "back" is a redundant tautology.
