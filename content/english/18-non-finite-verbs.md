Non-finite verbs are verb forms that do not express tense, person, or number, and cannot function as the primary predicate of a clause. Non-finite verbs operate as nouns, adjectives, or adverbs within a sentence.

## Main Concept 1: The Three Types of Non-Finite Verbs

Non-finite verbs comprise **infinitives**, **gerunds**, and **participles**.

```text
                       Non-Finite Verbs
                              │
         ┌────────────────────┼────────────────────┐
         ▼                    ▼                    ▼
    Infinitives            Gerunds            Participles
  (to + V₁ / Bare)        (V₁ + -ing)      (Present / Past / Perfect)
  Acts as: N/Adj/Adv      Acts as: Noun    Acts as: Adjective / Clause
```

- **Definitions and terminology:**
  - *Infinitives:* Base verb preceded by *to* (*to write*) or bare (*write*). Functions as noun, adjective, or adverb.
  - *Gerunds:* Verb form ending in *-ing* ($V_4$) functioning exclusively as a **noun**.
  - *Participles:* Verb forms ($V_4$ or $V_3$) functioning as **adjectives** or forming participle clauses.

## Main Concept 2: Detailed Breakdown and Syntactic Roles

### Subtopic 1: Infinitives vs. Bare Infinitives

- **Full Infinitive ($to + V_1$):**
  - *Subject of Verb:* *To err is human.*
  - *Direct Object:* *She decided **to study** law.*
  - *Adjective:* *He has a plan **to execute**.*
- **Bare Infinitive ($V_1$ without *to*):** Used after modal verbs (*can, must*), let/make/help (*Let him **go***), and verbs of perception (*I saw her **cross** the street*).

### Subtopic 2: Gerunds and Participles

- **Gerund Syntactic Roles ($V_4$ as Noun):**
  - *Subject:* ***Reading*** *expands vocabulary.*
  - *Direct Object:* *She enjoys **swimming**.*
  - *Object of Preposition:* *He succeeded by **working** hard.* (Prepositions must always be followed by gerunds, not infinitives!).
- **Participle Categories:**
  - *Present Participle ($V_4$ as Adjective):* Describes an active quality (*a **rising** star, the **running** engine*).
  - *Past Participle ($V_3$ as Adjective):* Describes a completed passive state (*the **written** code, a **broken** vase*).
  - *Perfect Participle ($\text{Having} + V_3$):* Expresses an action completed prior to the main clause action (* **Having finished** the project, she went home*).

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Non-Finite Category | Morphological Form | Primary Syntactic Role | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full Infinitive | $to + V_1$ | Noun / Adj / Adv | She wants *to learn*. |
| Bare Infinitive | $V_1$ (No *to*) | Verb Complement | Make him *stand* up. |
| Gerund | $V_4$ (*-ing*) | Noun (Subj / Obj / Obj Prep) | *Writing* requires focus. |
| Present Participle | $V_4$ (*-ing*) | Adjective / Modifier | The *crying* child slept. |
| Past Participle | $V_3$ (*-ed/-en*) | Adjective / Modifier | The *repaired* car works. |
| Perfect Participle | $\text{Having} + V_3$ | Adverbial Clause Modifier | *Having left*, he called. |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Preposition + Gerund Rule:

$$\text{Preposition} + \mathbf{\text{Gerund } (V_4)} \quad [\text{NOT } \text{Infinitive } (to + V_1)]$$

$$\text{Example}: \text{Interested in } \mathbf{\text{learning}} \quad [\text{NOT } \text{interested in to learn}]$$

Participle Clause Modifier Subject Agreement Rule:

$$\mathbf{\text{Participle Phrase}} + \mathbf{,}\ + \mathbf{\text{Main Subject}} \implies \text{Main Subject MUST perform the participle action}$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Dangling Participles:** Failing to ensure the main clause subject performs the action of the participle phrase (e.g., writing *Walking down the street, the trees were beautiful* instead of *Walking down the street, **I** saw beautiful trees*).
- **Infinitive after Prepositions:** Using infinitives instead of gerunds following prepositions (e.g., writing *He is good at to swim* instead of *He is good at **swimming***).
- **Gerund vs. Present Continuous Confusion:** Misidentifying gerunds as main verbs (e.g., confusing *Swimming is fun* [gerund subject] with *He is swimming* [present continuous finite verb]).

## Quiz

1. Identify the dangling participle error in the choices below.
  - [ ] Having completed the survey, the researcher analyzed the data.
  - [x] Having completed the survey, the data was analyzed by the researcher.
  - [ ] After completing the survey, the researcher analyzed the data.
  - [ ] The researcher, having completed the survey, analyzed the data.
   > "Having completed the survey" modifies "the data" in the incorrect option, suggesting the data itself completed the survey (a classic dangling modifier).

2. Select the sentence that correctly uses a gerund as an object of a preposition.
  - [ ] She is responsible for to organize the international conference.
  - [x] She is responsible for organizing the international conference.
  - [ ] She is responsible for organize the international conference.
  - [ ] She is responsible to organizing the international conference.
   > Prepositions ("for") must take a gerund ("organizing") as their object.

3. What is the syntactic function of "To evaluate" in: "To evaluate the hypothesis, the scientist conducted three trials."
  - [ ] Gerund acting as subject
  - [ ] Present Participle modifying scientist
  - [x] Full Infinitive acting as an adverb of purpose
  - [ ] Bare Infinitive acting as object
   > "To evaluate..." is an infinitival phrase functioning adverbially to express the purpose of the action in the main clause.
