Voice describes the relationship between the action expressed by the verb and the participants identified by the sentence arguments. Active voice places the agent as the grammatical subject, while passive voice shifts focus to the patient or recipient of the action.

## Main Concept 1: Mechanics of Voice Transformation

Voice transformation shifts sentence emphasis without altering the underlying proposition.

- **Definitions and terminology:**
  - **Active Voice:** The subject performs the action expressed by the verb (*The engineer built the bridge*).
  - **Passive Voice:** The subject receives the action expressed by the verb (*The bridge was built by the engineer*).
  - **Agent:** The entity performing the action (introduced by the preposition *by* in passive constructions).
- **Core Rules for Transformation:**
  1. The active direct object becomes the passive subject.
  2. The main verb changes into a form of **be + Past Participle ($V_3$)**, matching the original tense.
  3. The active subject becomes the object of the preposition *by* (or is omitted entirely if unknown/irrelevant).

## Main Concept 2: Passive Voice Across Tenses and Complex Structures

### Subtopic 1: Tense-by-Tense Passive Transformations

Passive voice requires inflecting the auxiliary verb *be* while maintaining $V_3$ as the main verb:

- **Present Simple:** *Active:* She writes reports. $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* Reports **are written** by her.
- **Past Simple:** *Active:* She wrote the report. $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* The report **was written** by her.
- **Present Continuous:** *Active:* She is writing a report. $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* A report **is being written** by her.
- **Past Continuous:** *Active:* She was writing a report. $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* A report **was being written** by her.
- **Present Perfect:** *Active:* She has written a report. $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* A report **has been written** by her.
- **Past Perfect:** *Active:* She had written a report. $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* A report **had been written** by her.
- **Modals:** *Active:* She must write a report. $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* A report **must be written** by her.

### Subtopic 2: Intransitive Verbs, Ditransitive Verbs, and Agent Omission

- **Intransitive Verbs Exception:** Verbs that do not take a direct object (*arrive, die, sleep, fall*) **cannot** be transformed into passive voice.
- **Ditransitive Verbs (Two Objects):** Can yield two distinct passive sentences:
  - *Active:* *The manager gave **me** (IO) **the key** (DO).*
  - *Passive 1 (Indirect Object Subject):* *I was given the key by the manager.*
  - *Passive 2 (Direct Object Subject):* *The key was given to me by the manager.*
- **Agent Omission:** Omit *by + agent* when the agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant (*The vault was robbed last night*).

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Active Tense Structure | Passive Equivalent Formula | Active Example | Passive Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present Simple ($V_1 / V_5$) | $\text{am/is/are} + V_3$ | They *clean* the lab. | The lab *is cleaned*. |
| Past Simple ($V_2$) | $\text{was/were} + V_3$ | They *cleaned* the lab. | The lab *was cleaned*. |
| Continuous ($be + V_4$) | $\text{be} + \text{being} + V_3$ | They *are cleaning* the lab. | The lab *is being cleaned*. |
| Perfect ($\text{have/has/had} + V_3$) | $\text{have/has/had} + \text{been} + V_3$ | They *have cleaned* the lab. | The lab *has been cleaned*. |
| Modal ($\text{Modal} + V_1$) | $\text{Modal} + \text{be} + V_3$ | They *must clean* the lab. | The lab *must be cleaned*. |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

General Active to Passive Transformation Equation:

$$\text{Active Syntax}: \mathbf{\text{Subject (Agent)}} + \mathbf{\text{Verb}} + \mathbf{\text{Object (Recipient)}}$$

$$\Downarrow$$

$$\text{Passive Syntax}: \mathbf{\text{Object (Recipient)}} + \mathbf{\text{[Tense-Marked } \textit{be}\text{]}} + \mathbf{V_3\ \text{(Past Participle)}} + [\text{by } \mathbf{\text{Subject (Agent)}}]$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Attempting to Passive-ize Intransitive Verbs:** Trying to convert sentences with intransitive verbs into passive voice (e.g., attempting to transform *He arrived late* into *Late was arrived by him*, which is ungrammatical).
- **Tense Distortion During Transformation:** Changing the tense of the sentence during voice conversion (e.g., transforming present continuous *She is painting the wall* into past passive *The wall was painted* instead of *The wall is being painted*).
- **Misplacing Particles in Phrasal Verbs:** Dropping prepositions attached to phrasal verbs in passive transformations (e.g., writing *The offer was looked by the board* instead of *The offer was looked **into** by the board*).

## Quiz

1. Convert the active sentence into correct passive voice: "The research team has published the breakthrough findings."
  - [ ] The breakthrough findings were published by the research team.
  - [ ] The breakthrough findings are published by the research team.
  - [x] The breakthrough findings have been published by the research team.
  - [ ] The breakthrough findings had been published by the research team.
   > The original tense is Present Perfect ("has published"). Plural passive object "findings" requires Present Perfect Passive "have been published".

2. Why is the sentence "The tired traveler slept for eight hours" incapable of passive voice transformation?
  - [ ] It contains a temporal prepositional phrase.
  - [x] The verb "slept" is intransitive and takes no direct object.
  - [ ] The subject is an animate entity.
  - [ ] The sentence is in the past simple tense.
   > Intransitive verbs do not take direct objects; without an active object to promote to the subject position, a passive transformation is impossible.

3. Which passive sentence correctly handles the phrasal verb from: "The committee called off the meeting"?
  - [ ] The meeting was called by the committee off.
  - [x] The meeting was called off by the committee.
  - [ ] The meeting had been called off by the committee.
  - [ ] The meeting was calling off by the committee.
   > In passive transformations of phrasal verbs, the verb particle ("off") remains attached directly to the past participle verb ("called off").
