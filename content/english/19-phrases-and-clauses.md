Phrases and clauses are the structural building blocks of English sentences. Distinguishing between independent syntactic units, dependent modifiers, and relative clauses is essential for mastering complex syntax.

## Main Concept 1: Phrases vs. Clauses

The fundamental distinction lies in subject-verb structure:

- **Definitions and terminology:**
  - **Phrase:** A group of words lacking a subject-verb combination that functions as a single part of speech (*in the quiet library, running down the hall*).
  - **Clause:** A group of words containing both a subject and a finite verb predicate.
- **Types of Phrases:**
  - *Noun Phrase:* Head noun + modifiers (*The tall academic advisor*).
  - *Prepositional Phrase:* Preposition + object (*across the university campus*).
  - *Participial Phrase:* Participle + complements (*driven by curiosity*).
  - *Absolute Phrase:* Noun + participle, modifying an entire clause (*His thesis completed, he graduated*).

## Main Concept 2: Independent and Dependent Clauses

### Subtopic 1: Independent vs. Dependent Clauses

- **Independent Clause (Main Clause):** Contains a subject and verb, expresses a complete thought, and can stand alone as a simple sentence (*The experiment succeeded.*).
- **Dependent Clause (Subordinate Clause):** Contains a subject and verb, but begins with a subordinating conjunction or relative pronoun, making it unable to stand alone (*...because the experiment succeeded*).
  - *Noun Clauses:* Act as nouns (* **What you declared** is true*).
  - *Adverbial Clauses:* Modify verbs/adjectives/adverbs, expressing time, cause, condition, or concession (*We left **when the bell rang** *).
  - *Adjective (Relative) Clauses:* Modify nouns (*The student **who won the award** celebrated*).

### Subtopic 2: Restrictive vs. Non-Restrictive Relative Clauses

- **Restrictive (Essential) Relative Clauses:** Essential to identifying the noun modified. Uses **no commas**, and accepts *that* or *who*.
  - *Example:* *The laboratory **that burned down** was uninsured.* (Specifies *which* lab).
- **Non-Restrictive (Non-Essential) Relative Clauses:** Adds extra, non-essential information. Set off by **commas**, and uses *which* or *who* (never *that*).
  - *Example:* *The primary laboratory, **which was built in 2010**, was upgraded.*

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Syntactic Unit | Subject-Verb Pair? | Complete Thought? | Functional Role | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prepositional Phrase | NO | NO | Modifier | *on the desk* |
| Participial Phrase | NO | NO | Adjective | *broken by heat* |
| Dependent Clause | YES | NO | Noun / Adj / Adv | *because she studied* |
| Independent Clause | YES | YES | Full Sentence | *She passed the test.* |
| Restrictive Rel. Clause | YES | NO | Essential Noun Mod | *the car that won* |
| Non-Restrictive Rel. | YES | NO | Non-essential Mod | *the car, which was red,* |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Relative Pronoun Selection Rule:

$$\text{Modified Entity} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{Human Person} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{who / whom / whose}} \\ \text{Non-human Item (Restrictive)} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{that / which}} \\ \text{Non-human Item (Non-Restrictive)} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{which}} \quad [\text{Enclosed in Commas!}] \end{cases}$$

Subordinate Clause Structure:

$$\text{Subordinating Conjunction} + \mathbf{\text{Subject}} + \mathbf{\text{Verb}} = \text{Dependent Adverbial Clause}$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Using "That" in Non-Restrictive Clauses:** Using *that* inside commas for non-essential relative clauses (e.g., writing *My car, that is red, is fast* instead of *My car, **which** is red, is fast*).
- **Mistaking Dependent Clauses for Full Sentences:** Treating a subordinate clause as a complete sentence (Sentence Fragment: *Because the temperature dropped rapidly.*).
- **Misplacing Relative Clauses:** Placing relative clauses far away from their antecedent noun, creating ambiguous modification.

1. Which sentence contains a correctly punctuated non-restrictive relative clause?
  - [ ] The solar panels that were installed last month generate power.
  - [ ] The solar panels, that were installed last month, generate power.
  - [x] The primary solar array, which was installed last month, generates clean power.
  - [ ] The primary solar array which was installed last month, generates clean power.
   > Non-restrictive relative clauses provide non-essential information, require enclosing commas, and must use "which" (not "that") for non-human antecedents.

2. Identify the underlined clause type: "The committee approved **what the director suggested**."
  - [ ] Adjective Clause
  - [ ] Adverbial Clause
  - [x] Noun Clause acting as direct object
  - [ ] Independent Clause
   > "What the director suggested" has a subject and verb and functions as the direct object of the verb "approved", making it a Noun Clause.

3. Select the option that represents a complete independent clause.
  - [ ] Running through the data logs carefully
  - [ ] Although the lead scientist verified the equations
  - [x] The lead scientist verified the equations
  - [ ] Which verified the equations completely
   > "The lead scientist verified the equations" contains a subject and verb, lacks subordinating words, and expresses a complete thought.
