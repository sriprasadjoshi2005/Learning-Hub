Sentence formation governs how words, phrases, and clauses combine to form clear, grammatically sound expressions. Mastering sentence structures, predicate patterns, and functional types is essential for precise communication.

## Main Concept 1: Essential Sentence Components and Patterns

A sentence must contain a **subject** and a **predicate**. The subject is the noun/pronoun entity performing or undergoing the verb's action; the predicate contains the finite verb and all accompanying complements or modifiers.

- **Definitions and terminology:** A *complement* completes the meaning of a verb, subject, or object. An *adverbial* specifies circumstances like time, place, or manner.
- **Important principles and rules:** Basic English simple sentences revolve around five primary clause patterns.
- **Five Primary Predicate Patterns:**
  1. **Subject + Verb (SV):** *The student slept.* (Intransitive verb)
  2. **Subject + Verb + Object (SVO):** *She wrote the report.* (Transitive verb + Direct Object)
  3. **Subject + Verb + Complement (SVC):** *The result appears promising.* (Linking verb + Subject Complement)
  4. **Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object (SVOO):** *The professor gave us the syllabus.* (Ditransitive verb)
  5. **Subject + Verb + Object + Object Complement (SVOC):** *The committee elected her chairperson.* (Complex-transitive verb)

## Main Concept 2: Four Functional Sentence Types

Sentences are classified functionally according to the speaker's communicative intent:

### Subtopic 1: Declarative and Interrogative Sentences

- **Declarative Sentences:** Make statements or convey information. End with a period. Word order: $\text{Subject} + \text{Verb}$ (*The experiment yielded novel data.*).
- **Interrogative Sentences:** Ask questions. End with a question mark. Require subject-auxiliary inversion or interrogative words ($Wh\text{-words}$):
  - *Yes/No Questions:* $\text{Auxiliary Verb} + \text{Subject} + \text{Main Verb}$ (*Did you submit the form?*).
  - *Wh- Questions:* $Wh\text{-word} + \text{Auxiliary Verb} + \text{Subject} + \text{Main Verb}$ (*Why did the reaction occur?*).

### Subtopic 2: Imperative and Exclamatory Sentences

- **Imperative Sentences:** Issue commands, requests, or instructions. The subject (*you*) is implied and omitted. Base verb form starts the clause (*Submit the final draft by noon.*).
- **Exclamatory Sentences:** Express strong emotion. End with an exclamation point. Introduced by *What* or *How*:
  - *What* + $(\text{a/an}) + \text{Adjective} + \text{Noun} + \text{Subject} + \text{Verb}!$ (*What a remarkable discovery it was!*).
  - *How* + $\text{Adjective/Adverb} + \text{Subject} + \text{Verb}!$ (*How efficiently she works!*).

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Functional Type | Purpose | Punctuation | Standard Word Order | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Declarative | Makes a statement | Period (.) | $\text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \text{Object}$ | The panel reviewed the application. |
| Interrogative | Asks a question | Question Mark (?) | $\text{Auxiliary} + \text{Subject} + \text{Verb}$ | Did the panel review the application? |
| Imperative | Issues command/request | Period / Exclamation (!/.) | $(\text{Implied You}) + \text{Base Verb}$ | Review the application carefully. |
| Exclamatory | Conveys strong emotion | Exclamation Mark (!) | $\text{What/How} + \text{Structure} + \text{Subject} + \text{Verb}$ | What a thorough review they conducted! |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Subject-Auxiliary Inversion Rule for Questions:

$$\text{Declarative}: \mathbf{\text{Subject}} + \mathbf{\text{Auxiliary Verb}} + \text{Main Verb} \longrightarrow \text{Interrogative}: \mathbf{\text{Auxiliary Verb}} + \mathbf{\text{Subject}} + \text{Main Verb}?$$

Example: *She (S) can (Aux) swim (V)* $\longrightarrow$ *Can (Aux) she (S) swim (V)?*

When no auxiliary verb exists in simple present/past tenses, insert the dummy auxiliary **do / does / did**:

$$\text{He wrote the letter} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Did}} + \text{he} + \mathbf{\text{write}}\ (\text{base form}) + \text{the letter}?$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Forgetting Auxiliary Inversion in Interrogatives:** Keeping declarative word order in formal questions (e.g., writing *Why you left early?* instead of *Why did you leave early?*).
- **Double Auxiliary Use after "Do/Does/Did":** Failing to revert the main verb to its base form after adding a dummy auxiliary (e.g., writing *Did he wrote the paper?* instead of *Did he write the paper?*).
- **Confusing Subject and Object Complements:** Confounding subject complements (modifying the subject via linking verbs) with direct objects (receiving action from transitive verbs).

   > Inserting the dummy auxiliary "Did" requires changing the main verb back to its base form "find".
