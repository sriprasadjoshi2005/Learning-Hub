Idiomatic expressions, phrasal verbs, and collocations form the metaphorical texture of English discourse. Mastering these expressions enhances conversational naturalness, rhetorical subtlety, and reading comprehension in advanced prose.

## Main Concept 1: Phrasal Verbs

A **phrasal verb** is a multi-word verb formed by combining a base verb with a preposition, an adverb, or both (known as a *particle*). The resulting combination has a figurative meaning distinct from its individual components.

- **Definitions and terminology:**
  - *Base Verb + Particle:* *look up, carry out, break down, put up with*.
  - *Literal vs. Figurative:* *take off* (literal: remove clothing; figurative: launch or succeed rapidly).
- **Syntactic Classifications:**
  1. **Transitive vs. Intransitive:** Transitive phrasal verbs require a direct object (*They **canceled (called off) the meeting***); intransitive ones do not (*The plane **took off***).
  2. **Separable vs. Inseparable:**
     - *Separable:* The object can sit between the verb and particle (*call the meeting off* OR *call off the meeting*). **Crucial Rule:** If the object is a pronoun, it **must** sit between the verb and particle (*call it off*, NOT *call off it*).
     - *Inseparable:* The particle cannot be separated from the verb (*look after the children*, NOT *look the children after*).

## Main Concept 2: Idioms, Collocations, and Register

### Subtopic 1: Idioms and Figurative Expressions

An **idiom** is a fixed phrase whose figurative meaning cannot be deduced from the literal definitions of its individual words (e.g., *burn the midnight oil* = study late; *bite the bullet* = face a difficult situation bravely).

### Subtopic 2: Collocations and Register Calibration

- **Collocations:** Natural, high-frequency pairings of words that native speakers habitually use together:
  - *Make vs. Do:* *make a decision, make a mistake* vs. *do homework, do research, do business*.
  - *Adjective + Noun:* *heavy rain* (NOT *strong rain*); *high probability* (NOT *big probability*).
- **Register Calibration:** Selecting appropriate idioms and phrasal verbs for specific communication contexts:
  - *Informal / Spoken:* *put up with, set up, figure out*.
  - *Formal / Academic:* *tolerate, establish, determine*.

## Important Classifications / Structures / Processes

| Phrasal Verb / Expression | Type / Category | Meaning | Formal Equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Carry out* | Transitive Separable | Conduct / execute | *Execute / Perform* |
| *Bring about* | Transitive Separable | Cause to happen | *Cause / Produce* |
| *Put up with* | Three-word Inseparable | Endure without complaint | *Tolerate* |
| *Burn the midnight oil* | Idiom | Work late into the night | *Study late* |
| *Make a decision* | Fixed Collocation | Reach a conclusion | *Decide* |

## Important Formulas / Rules / Relationships

Separable Phrasal Verb Pronoun Rule:

$$\text{Verb} + \mathbf{\text{Pronoun Object}} + \mathbf{\text{Particle}} \quad [\text{MANDATORY SEPARATION!}]$$

$$\text{Correct}: \text{She called } \mathbf{\text{it}} \text{ off.} \quad \Big\vert \quad \text{Incorrect}: \text{She called off } \mathbf{\text{it}}.$$

Collocation Selection Pattern:

$$\text{Academic Context} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Formal Single Verb}} \quad (\text{e.g., } \text{investigate} \text{ instead of } \text{look into})$$

## Common Exam Traps

- **Unseparated Pronoun Objects:** Placing a personal pronoun after the particle of a separable phrasal verb (e.g., writing *Please turn on it* instead of *Please turn it on*).
- **Collocation Substitution Errors:** Substituting near-synonyms into fixed collocations (e.g., saying *do a decision* instead of *make a decision*, or *big rain* instead of *heavy rain*).
- **Overusing Informal Phrasal Verbs in Academic Writing:** Using informal phrasal verbs in research essays where single-word academic verbs are expected (e.g., *get into* instead of *examine*).

## Quiz

1. Select the sentence that adheres strictly to the pronoun placement rule for separable phrasal verbs.
  - [ ] The committee decided to put off it until next week.
  - [x] The committee decided to put it off until next week.
  - [ ] The committee decided to put off that until next week.
  - [ ] The committee decided to putting it off until next week.
   > When the object of a separable phrasal verb is a pronoun ("it"), it must be placed between the verb ("put") and the particle ("off").

2. Which single-word formal verb best replaces the phrasal verb "carry out" in the academic statement: "The lab will carry out a series of tests"?
  - [ ] Perform
  - [x] Conduct
  - [ ] Fabricate
  - [ ] Initiate
   > In formal academic contexts, "conduct" or "execute" serves as the precise equivalent for "carry out".

3. Identify the correct collocation pair.
  - [ ] High rain fell across the northern valley.
  - [ ] The team did a huge mistake during calculations.
  - [x] The team made a significant breakthrough in energy efficiency.
  - [ ] She performed a great decision regarding her career.
   > "Made a breakthrough" is a natural, high-frequency academic collocation. "Make a mistake" and "heavy rain" represent the correct alternatives for the incorrect choices.
