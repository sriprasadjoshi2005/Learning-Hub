title: Cell Organelles and Their Functions

---

Eukaryotic cells are compartmentalised by membrane-bound structures called **organelles**. This spatial segregation allows distinct biochemical reactions to take place simultaneously within specialised microenvironments, increasing cellular efficiency and functional organisation.

## Eukaryotic Organelles Overview

| Organelle | Membrane Layers | Primary Function | Distinctive Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nucleus** | Double | Storage of genetic information, site of transcription and ribosome assembly. | Contains nucleolus and chromatin network. |
| **Mitochondria** | Double | Site of aerobic respiration and $\mathrm{ATP}$ synthesis via oxidative phosphorylation. | Inner membrane forms **cristae**; contains matrix, circular $\mathrm{DNA}$, and $\mathrm{70S}$ ribosomes. |
| **Chloroplast** | Double | Site of photosynthesis (light-dependent and light-independent reactions). | Stroma contains **thylakoid** stacks (**grana**), circular $\mathrm{DNA}$, and $\mathrm{70S}$ ribosomes. |
| **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)** | Single | **RER**: Protein synthesis and folding. **SER**: Lipid/steroid synthesis and $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ storage. | RER is studded with ribosomes; SER lacks ribosomes. |
| **Golgi Apparatus** | Single | Modification, sorting, and packaging of proteins and lipids. | Consists of flattened sacs (**cisternae**) with distinct *cis* (receiving) and *trans* (shipping) faces. |
| **Lysosomes** | Single | Intracellular digestion and organelle turnover. | Rich in acid hydrolases (active at $\mathrm{pH} \approx 5.0$); known as "suicide bags". |
| **Peroxisomes** | Single | $\mathrm{H_2O_2}$ metabolism and fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation. | Contains catalase and oxidases. |
| **Vacuoles** | Single | Storage of water, cell sap, excretory products, and maintenance of turgor pressure. | Membrane is called the **tonoplast**; can occupy up to $90\%$ of plant cell volume. |

## Structure and Functions of Major Organelles

### 1. Nucleus

The **nucleus** is the control centre of the eukaryotic cell because it contains most of the cell's genetic material.

- **Nuclear Envelope**: Double membrane surrounding the nucleus, containing nuclear pores that regulate molecular exchange.
- **Nucleoplasm**: Fluid matrix present inside the nucleus.
- **Chromatin**: Network of DNA and proteins that condenses into chromosomes during cell division.
- **Nucleolus**: Dense region responsible for ribosomal RNA (rRNA) synthesis and ribosomal subunit assembly.

### 2. Mitochondria

Mitochondria are commonly called the **powerhouses of the cell** because they generate most of the ATP during aerobic respiration.

- **Outer Membrane**: Smooth outer boundary surrounding the organelle.
- **Inner Membrane**: Folded into structures called **cristae**, increasing the surface area available for oxidative phosphorylation.
- **Intermembrane Space**: Region between the inner and outer membranes where protons accumulate during electron transport.
- **Matrix**: Inner fluid-filled compartment containing enzymes, circular DNA, and 70S ribosomes.

### 3. Chloroplasts

Chloroplasts are photosynthetic organelles found in plants and algae.

- **Outer and Inner Membranes**: Form the double-membrane envelope.
- **Stroma**: Fluid-filled matrix containing enzymes, circular DNA, and 70S ribosomes.
- **Thylakoids**: Flattened membrane-bound sacs containing chlorophyll and other photosynthetic pigments.
- **Grana**: Stacks of thylakoids where light-dependent reactions take place.
- **Stroma Lamellae**: Membranous connections between different grana.

### 4. Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)

The endoplasmic reticulum is an interconnected network of membrane-bound tubules and flattened sacs involved in synthesis and intracellular transport.

| Feature | Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER) | Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (SER) |
|---|---|---|
| **Ribosomes** | Present on the outer surface. | Absent. |
| **Structure** | Mainly flattened sacs (cisternae). | Mainly tubular structures. |
| **Primary Function** | Protein synthesis and folding. | Lipid and steroid synthesis. |
| **Additional Functions** | Produces proteins for secretion and membranes. | Detoxification and calcium storage. |

### 5. Golgi Apparatus

The Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids received from the endoplasmic reticulum.

- **Cisternae**: Flattened membrane-bound sacs forming the structural units of the Golgi apparatus.
- **Cis Face**: Receiving surface that accepts transport vesicles from the ER.
- **Trans Face**: Maturing and shipping surface that dispatches processed materials to their destinations.
- **Function**: Protein modification, glycosylation, sorting, packaging, and formation of secretory vesicles and lysosomes.

### 6. Lysosomes

Lysosomes are membrane-bound organelles responsible for intracellular digestion.

- Contain **acid hydrolases**, which break down proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, and nucleic acids.
- Maintain an acidic internal environment of approximately $\mathrm{pH}\ 5.0$.
- Participate in **autophagy**, the breakdown and recycling of damaged cellular organelles.
- Also participate in the digestion of materials taken up through endocytosis.

### 7. Peroxisomes

Peroxisomes are single-membrane organelles involved in oxidative metabolic reactions.

- Contain oxidase enzymes that produce hydrogen peroxide ($\mathrm{H_2O_2}$) during oxidation reactions.
- Contain **catalase**, which converts toxic hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen.
- Participate in fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation and other metabolic processes.

$$
2\mathrm{H_2O_2} \xrightarrow{\text{Catalase}} 2\mathrm{H_2O} + \mathrm{O_2}
$$

### 8. Vacuoles

Vacuoles are membrane-bound compartments involved in storage and maintaining cellular balance.

- **Tonoplast**: Single membrane surrounding the vacuole.
- **Cell Sap**: Fluid containing water, sugars, salts, pigments, and other dissolved substances.
- **Turgor Pressure**: Pressure exerted by the cell contents against the cell wall, helping maintain plant cell rigidity.
- In mature plant cells, the central vacuole can occupy up to $90\%$ of the cell volume.

## Endomembrane System

The **endomembrane system** consists of organelles whose structures and functions are coordinated through membrane exchange and vesicular transport.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Rough ER}
&\xrightarrow{\text{Transport Vesicle}} \text{Golgi Apparatus} \\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Secretory Vesicle}} \text{Plasma Membrane / Lysosome}
\end{aligned}
$$

The major components include:

- **Endoplasmic Reticulum**: Synthesises proteins and lipids.
- **Golgi Apparatus**: Modifies, sorts, and packages cellular products.
- **Lysosomes**: Perform intracellular digestion.
- **Vacuoles**: Participate in storage and intracellular transport.

> **Important:** Mitochondria, chloroplasts, and peroxisomes are **not** considered part of the endomembrane system because their functions are not coordinated with the ER-Golgi network.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Ribosome Membrane Status**: Ribosomes are **non-membrane-bound** cellular structures present in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.
- **Organelle Genomes**: Mitochondria and chloroplasts are semi-autonomous organelles possessing their own circular $\mathrm{DNA}$ and $\mathrm{70S}$ ribosomes, supporting the **Endosymbiotic Theory**.
- **Cristae vs Grana**: Cristae are folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane, whereas grana are stacks of thylakoids inside chloroplasts.
- **RER vs SER**: RER contains ribosomes and is primarily involved in protein synthesis, whereas SER lacks ribosomes and is involved in lipid synthesis and detoxification.
- **Golgi Orientation**: The *cis* face receives materials from the ER, whereas the *trans* face dispatches processed materials.
- **Lysosomal pH**: Lysosomes maintain an acidic environment of approximately $\mathrm{pH}\ 5.0$, allowing acid hydrolases to function efficiently.
- **Peroxisomes vs Lysosomes**: Peroxisomes contain oxidases and catalase for oxidative reactions, whereas lysosomes contain hydrolytic enzymes for intracellular digestion.
- **Tonoplast**: The membrane surrounding a plant cell vacuole is called the tonoplast.
- **Endomembrane System**: Mitochondria, chloroplasts, and peroxisomes are excluded from the endomembrane system.

## Quiz

1. Biochemical analysis of an organelle isolated from animal tissue reveals a low internal $\mathrm{pH}$ ($\sim 5.0$) and high concentrations of hydrolytic enzymes, including lipases, proteases, and nucleases. Which organelle was isolated?

   - [ ] Peroxisome
   - [x] Lysosome
   - [ ] Golgi apparatus
   - [ ] Glyoxysome

   > Lysosomes contain acid hydrolases with optimal activity around $\mathrm{pH}\ 5.0$. These enzymes break down proteins, lipids, nucleic acids, and carbohydrates during intracellular digestion.

2. Which group of cellular organelles operates coordinately as part of the eukaryotic endomembrane system?

   - [ ] Mitochondria, Chloroplasts, and Peroxisomes
   - [x] Endoplasmic Reticulum, Golgi Apparatus, Lysosomes, and Vacuoles
   - [ ] Nucleus, Mitochondria, and Ribosomes
   - [ ] Chloroplasts, Glyoxysomes, and Centrosomes

   > The endomembrane system includes the Endoplasmic Reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, and vacuoles. Their functions are integrated in protein processing, packaging, and intracellular transport.

3. Which feature of mitochondria and chloroplasts supports the endosymbiotic theory of their evolutionary origin?

   - [ ] They are bounded by a single phospholipid bilayer.
   - [ ] They synthesise proteins using $\mathrm{80S}$ cytoplasmic ribosomes attached to their outer surface.
   - [x] They possess their own circular double-stranded $\mathrm{DNA}$ and $\mathrm{70S}$ ribosomes.
   - [ ] They originate via de novo assembly from cytosol components.

   > Both mitochondria and chloroplasts contain prokaryote-like circular DNA and $\mathrm{70S}$ ribosomes, supporting the theory that they evolved from engulfed ancestral prokaryotic endosymbionts.
