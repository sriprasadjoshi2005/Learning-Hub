In 1969, **R.H. Whittaker** proposed the **Five Kingdom Classification** system. It classifies living organisms into Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia based on five main criteria: cell structure, thallus organisation, mode of nutrition, reproduction, and phylogenetic relationships.

## The Five Kingdoms Overview

| Kingdom | Cell Type | Cell Wall | Nuclear Membrane | Mode of Nutrition | Example Organisms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Monera** | Prokaryotic | Non-cellulosic (polysaccharide + amino acid) | Absent | Autotrophic (photosynthetic / chemosynthetic) and heterotrophic (saprophytic / parasitic) | Bacteria, *Cyanobacteria*, Mycoplasma, *Archaea* |
| **Protista** | Eukaryotic | Present in some | Present | Autotrophic and heterotrophic | Diatoms, Dinoflagellates, Euglenoids, Amoeba, Paramecium |
| **Fungi** | Eukaryotic | Present (composed of **chitin**) | Present | Heterotrophic (saprophytic / parasitic / symbiotic) | Yeast, Mucor, Penicillium, Mushrooms |
| **Plantae** | Eukaryotic | Present (composed of **cellulose**) | Present | Autotrophic (photosynthetic) | Mosses, Ferns, Gymnosperms, Angiosperms |
| **Animalia** | Eukaryotic | Absent | Present | Heterotrophic (primarily holozoic) | Sponges, Insects, Birds, Mammals |

## Sub-Cellular and Non-Cellular Entities

Whittaker's classification system does not include certain acellular entities because they lack cellular organisation and cannot carry out independent metabolic activities outside a suitable host:

- **Viruses**: Acellular entities consisting of genetic material ($\mathrm{DNA}$ or $\mathrm{RNA}$) enclosed inside a protein coat called a **capsid**. They remain inert outside a living host cell.
- **Viroids**: Discovered by **T.O. Diener (1971)**, these are infectious RNA particles **lacking a protein coat**. They are smaller than viruses and can cause plant diseases such as potato spindle tuber disease.
- **Prions**: Infectious proteinaceous agents that cause neurodegenerative disorders (e.g., bovine spongiform encephalopathy or "mad cow disease").
- **Lichens**: Symbiotic associations between algae (**phycobiont**) and fungi (**mycobiont**). The algae prepare food, while the fungus provides shelter and absorbs mineral nutrients. Lichens are sensitive indicators of air pollution, particularly sulphur dioxide ($\mathrm{SO_2}$).

## Archaea

**Archaea** are ancient prokaryotic organisms that can survive in extreme habitats due to their unique cell wall composition and membrane lipid structures. Important groups include:

- **Halophiles**: Organisms that thrive in extremely salty environments.
- **Thermoacidophiles**: Organisms that survive in hot and acidic environments, such as hot springs.
- **Methanogens**: Organisms found in marshy areas and the guts of ruminants that produce methane ($\mathrm{CH_4}$), a major component of biogas.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Excluding Viruses from the Five Kingdoms**: Viruses, viroids, and prions are **not** placed in any of Whittaker's five kingdoms because they lack cellular organisation.
- **Cell wall composition confusion**: Fungal cell walls are made of **chitin**, whereas plant cell walls are made of **cellulose**.
- **Viroid structure**: Assuming viroids contain a capsid. Viroids consist only of free, low-molecular-weight infectious $\mathrm{RNA}$.
- **Archaea and extreme environments**: Halophiles thrive in highly saline environments, thermoacidophiles survive in hot acidic environments, and methanogens produce methane.
- **Lichen composition**: Remember that the algal partner is the **phycobiont**, while the fungal partner is the **mycobiont**.

## Quiz

1. Which of the following characteristics distinguishes Kingdom Fungi from Kingdom Plantae in Whittaker's classification?
  - [ ] Presence of a true nuclear membrane
  - [x] Cell wall composition of chitin instead of cellulose and heterotrophic nutrition
  - [ ] Eukaryotic cellular organisation
  - [ ] Unicellular vs multicellular body plan
   > Fungi and Plantae are both eukaryotic organisms with cell walls. However, fungi possess chitinous cell walls and heterotrophic nutrition, whereas plants have cellulosic cell walls and are primarily autotrophic.

2. A plant pathologist discovers an infectious agent that causes severe stunting in potato crops. Upon molecular analysis, the agent is found to consist solely of a low-molecular-weight single-stranded RNA molecule with no protein coat. How should this agent be classified?
  - [ ] Virus
  - [ ] Prion
  - [x] Viroid
  - [ ] Lichen
   > Viroids are sub-viral infectious agents discovered by **T.O. Diener**. They consist exclusively of small, infectious RNA molecules without a protective protein coat or capsid.

3. Which organisms are known to thrive in extreme environments such as hot springs and high-salinity brine pools due to their specialised membrane structures?
  - [x] Archaea
  - [ ] Slime moulds
  - [ ] Chrysophytes
  - [ ] True fungi
   > Archaea include thermoacidophiles and halophiles that survive in extreme conditions. Their unique membrane lipids and cell wall structures help them withstand high temperatures, acidity, and extreme salinity.
