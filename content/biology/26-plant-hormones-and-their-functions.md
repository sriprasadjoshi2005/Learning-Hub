---
title: Plant Hormones and Their Functions
level: intermediate
---

Plant hormones, also called **Plant Growth Regulators (PGRs)**, are small organic molecules that regulate plant growth, development, and responses to environmental conditions. They influence processes such as cell division, cell elongation, flowering, fruit ripening, seed dormancy, and leaf fall.

## Major Classes of Plant Growth Regulators

Plant growth regulators are traditionally classified into two broad groups:

- **Growth promoters**: Auxins, Gibberellins, and Cytokinins.
- **Growth inhibitors**: Abscisic Acid (ABA) and Ethylene.

This classification is useful for understanding their major functions, although individual hormones can have different effects depending on the plant tissue and developmental stage.

## Major Plant Hormones and Their Functions

| Hormone | Important Examples | Biosynthetic Precursor | Major Physiological Roles | Practical Applications |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Auxins** | IAA, NAA, 2,4-D | Primarily Tryptophan | Cell elongation, apical dominance, root initiation, tropic responses | Rooting of cuttings; 2,4-D used as a selective herbicide against broad-leaved weeds |
| **Gibberellins** | GA₃ | Terpenoid pathway | Stem elongation, bolting, breaking seed dormancy, promoting germination | Increasing stalk length in grapes; malting in the brewing industry |
| **Cytokinins** | Zeatin, Kinetin | Adenine-related compounds | Cell division, shoot initiation, delayed leaf senescence | Plant tissue culture and micropropagation |
| **Abscisic Acid (ABA)** | ABA | Carotenoids | Stomatal closure, seed dormancy, inhibition of growth under stress | Reducing water loss during drought; maintaining seed dormancy |
| **Ethylene** | C₂H₄ | Methionine | Fruit ripening, abscission, senescence, triple response | Artificial fruit ripening and synchronisation of flowering in pineapple using ethephon |

### 1. Auxins

Auxins are plant hormones primarily associated with cell elongation and the regulation of growth responses.

**Important examples:**
- **IAA (Indole-3-acetic acid)**: The major naturally occurring auxin.
- **NAA (Naphthaleneacetic acid)**: A synthetic auxin used in agriculture and horticulture.
- **2,4-D (2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid)**: A synthetic auxin used as a selective herbicide.

**Major functions:**

- Promote cell elongation, particularly in stems.
- Maintain apical dominance by suppressing the growth of lateral buds.
- Promote adventitious and lateral root initiation.
- Regulate phototropism and geotropism.
- Help prevent premature fruit and leaf drop in certain applications.
- Promote fruit development, including parthenocarpy in some plants.

**Practical applications:**

- Used in rooting powders to encourage root formation in stem cuttings.
- 2,4-D selectively controls many broad-leaved dicot weeds in cereal fields.
- Certain auxins can induce seedless fruit development.

### 2. Gibberellins

Gibberellins are plant hormones that promote stem elongation, seed germination, and several developmental processes.

**Important example:**
- **GA₃ (Gibberellic Acid)** is a widely studied gibberellin.

**Major functions:**

- Promote rapid stem and internode elongation.
- Induce **bolting** in rosette plants such as cabbage and beet.
- Break seed dormancy and promote germination.
- Stimulate the production of hydrolytic enzymes, such as α-amylase, in germinating cereal grains.
- Promote fruit growth and development.
- Can delay senescence in certain plant tissues.

**Practical applications:**

- Increase stalk length in grapes, improving bunch structure.
- Used in the brewing industry during malting to stimulate enzyme production.
- Promote elongation and growth in commercially cultivated plants.

### 3. Cytokinins

Cytokinins are plant hormones that primarily stimulate cell division and promote shoot development.

**Important examples:**
- **Zeatin**: A naturally occurring cytokinin.
- **Kinetin**: A cytokinin-like compound commonly used in plant tissue culture.

**Major functions:**

- Promote cell division (cytokinesis).
- Stimulate shoot initiation and lateral bud development.
- Counteract apical dominance.
- Delay leaf senescence through the **Richmond-Lang effect**.
- Promote nutrient mobilisation towards actively growing tissues.

**Practical applications:**

- Widely used in plant tissue culture and micropropagation.
- Promote shoot formation from callus tissue.
- Help maintain the freshness of harvested leafy vegetables and cut flowers.

### 4. Abscisic Acid (ABA)

Abscisic Acid is primarily associated with growth inhibition, seed dormancy, and plant responses to environmental stress.

It is commonly called the **Stress Hormone**.

**Major functions:**

- Promotes stomatal closure during water stress.
- Reduces transpiration and conserves water.
- Promotes seed dormancy.
- Inhibits seed germination under unfavourable conditions.
- Helps plants tolerate drought and other environmental stresses.
- Can inhibit growth and promote senescence in certain conditions.

**Mechanism of stomatal closure:**

1. Water deficit causes ABA levels to increase in plant tissues.
2. ABA signalling activates ion channels in guard cells.
3. Potassium ions and other solutes move out of guard cells.
4. Water leaves the guard cells by osmosis.
5. Guard-cell turgor pressure decreases, causing the stomata to close.

### 5. Ethylene

Ethylene is a simple gaseous plant hormone with the chemical formula **C₂H₄**. It regulates fruit ripening, senescence, and abscission.

It is the only naturally occurring gaseous plant growth regulator.

**Major functions:**

- Promotes fruit ripening.
- Stimulates leaf and flower senescence.
- Promotes abscission (shedding) of leaves, flowers, and fruits.
- Produces the triple response in seedlings.
- Promotes flowering in pineapple.
- Can promote female flower formation in certain cucurbits.

**The Triple Response:**

When a growing seedling encounters an obstacle, ethylene produces three characteristic responses:

1. Reduced stem elongation.
2. Increased lateral swelling of the stem.
3. Horizontal growth or exaggerated curvature of the stem.

These changes help the seedling navigate around obstacles during emergence.

**Practical applications:**

- **Ethephon** releases ethylene and is used to accelerate and synchronise fruit ripening.
- Used to induce flowering in pineapple.
- Helps synchronise fruit ripening for harvesting and commercial processing.

## Inter-Hormonal Interactions

Plant hormones rarely function independently. Their effects often depend on interactions with other hormones.

### 1. Apical Dominance

**Apical dominance** is the phenomenon in which the actively growing shoot apex suppresses the growth of lateral axillary buds.

- Auxin produced in the shoot apex contributes to the suppression of lateral bud growth.
- Removing the shoot tip through **decapitation** reduces apical dominance.
- Cytokinins promote lateral bud growth and can counteract the inhibitory influence of the shoot apex.

**Result:** Removing the apical bud or applying cytokinin can encourage lateral branching, producing a bushier plant.

### 2. Auxin-to-Cytokinin Ratio in Tissue Culture

The relative concentrations of auxins and cytokinins in a plant tissue culture medium influence the type of organ that develops from undifferentiated callus tissue.

| Hormonal Ratio | Developmental Response | Process |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| High Auxin : Low Cytokinin | Root formation | Rhizogenesis |
| Low Auxin : High Cytokinin | Shoot formation | Caulogenesis |
| Approximately Equal Auxin : Cytokinin | Callus proliferation | Undifferentiated cell growth |

**Key concept:** The balance between auxin and cytokinin is important in determining whether cultured plant cells develop roots, shoots, or continue proliferating as callus.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Gaseous hormone:** Ethylene is the only naturally occurring gaseous plant growth regulator.
- **Stress hormone:** Abscisic Acid (ABA) promotes stomatal closure and seed dormancy.
- **Selective herbicide:** 2,4-D selectively kills many broad-leaved dicot weeds while generally sparing monocot cereal crops when used appropriately.
- **Bolting:** Gibberellins promote rapid stem and internode elongation in rosette plants.
- **Richmond-Lang effect:** Cytokinins delay leaf senescence.
- **Fruit ripening:** Ethylene promotes fruit ripening; it is not an auxin or gibberellin function.
- **Apical dominance:** Auxin from the shoot apex contributes to lateral bud suppression, while cytokinins promote lateral bud growth.
- **Tissue culture:** High auxin relative to cytokinin generally favours root formation, whereas high cytokinin relative to auxin generally favours shoot formation.

## Quiz

### 1. A plant tissue culture scientist adjusts the nutrient medium to contain a high concentration of cytokinin relative to auxin. What type of organ development is generally induced from the callus?

- [ ] Root differentiation
- [x] Shoot differentiation
- [ ] Seed coat formation
- [ ] Fruit ripening

> A high cytokinin-to-auxin ratio generally promotes shoot initiation (caulogenesis), whereas a high auxin-to-cytokinin ratio favours root initiation (rhizogenesis).

### 2. During severe drought, which plant growth regulator accumulates in leaves and triggers protective stomatal closure to reduce water loss?

- [ ] Gibberellin (GA₃)
- [ ] Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA)
- [x] Abscisic Acid (ABA)
- [ ] Ethylene

> Abscisic Acid is known as the stress hormone. During water deficit, ABA signalling causes guard cells to lose turgor, resulting in stomatal closure and reduced transpiration.

### 3. The phenomenon of bolting, involving rapid stem and internode elongation before flowering in rosette plants such as cabbage and beet, is promoted by:

- [ ] Auxin
- [x] Gibberellin
- [ ] Cytokinin
- [ ] Abscisic Acid

> Gibberellins stimulate internodal elongation and can induce bolting in rosette plants before flowering.

### 4. Which plant growth regulator is responsible for delaying leaf senescence through the Richmond-Lang effect?

- [ ] Ethylene
- [ ] Abscisic Acid
- [x] Cytokinin
- [ ] Gibberellin

> Cytokinins delay leaf senescence by maintaining metabolic activity and promoting nutrient mobilisation in leaves. This phenomenon is known as the Richmond-Lang effect.

### 5. Which synthetic plant growth regulator is commonly used as a selective herbicide to control broad-leaved weeds in cereal crops?

- [ ] IAA
- [ ] GA₃
- [x] 2,4-D
- [ ] Zeatin

> 2,4-D is a synthetic auxin that, when used at suitable concentrations, selectively controls many broad-leaved dicot weeds while generally sparing monocot cereal crops.

### 6. Which of the following is the only naturally occurring gaseous plant growth regulator?

- [ ] Auxin
- [ ] Gibberellin
- [ ] Cytokinin
- [x] Ethylene

> Ethylene (C₂H₄) is the only naturally occurring gaseous plant hormone. It plays important roles in fruit ripening, abscission, senescence, and the triple response.
