**Genetics** is the branch of biology that studies **heredity** and **variation**. Heredity refers to the transmission of traits from parents to offspring, while variation refers to the differences in structural, physiological, or genetic characteristics among individuals of the same species.

## Key Terminology in Genetics

Understanding basic genetic terminology is essential for studying inheritance patterns.

| Term | Definition | Example |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Gene** | The basic functional unit of inheritance; a segment of DNA that produces a functional RNA or, in many cases, a polypeptide | Gene controlling flower colour |
| **Allele** | An alternative form of a gene occupying the same locus on homologous chromosomes | $T$ and $t$ |
| **Genotype** | The genetic constitution of an organism for a particular trait or set of traits | $TT$, $Tt$, or $tt$ |
| **Phenotype** | The observable characteristics resulting from the interaction of genotype and environment | Tall or dwarf plant |
| **Homozygous** | Having two identical alleles for a particular gene | $TT$ or $tt$ |
| **Heterozygous** | Having two different alleles for a particular gene | $Tt$ |
| **Dominant Allele** | An allele expressed in the phenotype of a heterozygote in a complete dominance relationship | $T$ |
| **Recessive Allele** | An allele whose effect is masked by a dominant allele in a heterozygote and is expressed in the homozygous condition | $tt$ |

### Important Genetic Concepts

- **Locus:** The specific physical position of a gene on a chromosome.
- **Dominance:** The relationship in which one allele determines the phenotype of a heterozygote.
- **Recessiveness:** The condition in which an allele's effect is masked by another allele in a heterozygote.

For example, in pea plants:

- $TT$ = Homozygous dominant (Tall)
- $Tt$ = Heterozygous (Tall)
- $tt$ = Homozygous recessive (Dwarf)

Therefore, a dominant phenotype does not necessarily reveal the exact genotype.

## Gregor Mendel and the Foundation of Genetics

**Gregor Johann Mendel**, widely known as the *Father of Genetics*, established the fundamental principles of inheritance through experiments on the garden pea plant (*Pisum sativum*).

He conducted his hybridisation experiments between 1856 and 1863, studying how contrasting traits were transmitted across generations.

### Why Did Mendel Choose *Pisum sativum*?

Mendel selected garden pea plants because they possessed several experimental advantages:

1. **Distinct contrasting traits:** Pea plants exhibit easily distinguishable characteristics, such as tall or dwarf stems and yellow or green seeds.
2. **Short generation time:** They complete their life cycle relatively quickly.
3. **Numerous offspring:** They produce a large number of seeds, allowing statistical analysis of inheritance patterns.
4. **Natural self-pollination:** This makes it possible to maintain pure-breeding lines.
5. **Controlled cross-pollination:** Their flowers can be manually cross-pollinated, allowing Mendel to control the parents involved in a cross.

### Seven Pairs of Contrasting Characters Studied by Mendel

Mendel studied seven pairs of contrasting traits in garden pea plants.

| Character | Dominant Trait | Recessive Trait |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Stem Height** | Tall | Dwarf |
| **Seed Shape** | Round | Wrinkled |
| **Seed Colour** | Yellow | Green |
| **Flower Colour** | Violet | White |
| **Pod Shape** | Inflated | Constricted |
| **Pod Colour** | Green | Yellow |
| **Flower Position** | Axial | Terminal |

## Basic Mendelian Inheritance: A Monohybrid Cross

A **monohybrid cross** is a genetic cross involving a single pair of contrasting traits.

Consider a cross between a pure-breeding tall pea plant and a pure-breeding dwarf pea plant.

### Parental Generation (P)

$$
TT \times tt
$$

- Tall parent produces only $T$ gametes.
- Dwarf parent produces only $t$ gametes.

### First Filial Generation (F₁)

All offspring receive one allele from each parent.

$$
T \times t \longrightarrow Tt
$$

Therefore, all F₁ offspring are heterozygous tall plants.

### Second Filial Generation (F₂)

When F₁ plants are self-pollinated:

$$
Tt \times Tt
$$

The possible combinations are:

|  | $T$ (Parent 2) | $t$ (Parent 2) |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **$T$ (Parent 1)** | $TT$ | $Tt$ |
| **$t$ (Parent 1)** | $Tt$ | $tt$ |

The resulting genotypic ratio is:

$$
1TT:2Tt:1tt
$$

The resulting phenotypic ratio is:

$$
3\text{ Tall}:1\text{ Dwarf}
$$

Thus, approximately 75% of the F₂ generation will be tall and 25% will be dwarf.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Seed Colour vs. Pod Colour:** Yellow is the dominant seed colour, whereas green is the dominant pod colour.
- **Phenotype Does Not Determine Genotype:** A tall pea plant can have either $TT$ or $Tt$ genotype.
- **Homozygous vs. Heterozygous:** $TT$ and $tt$ are homozygous, whereas $Tt$ is heterozygous.
- **Dominant Does Not Mean More Common:** Dominance describes how an allele is expressed in a heterozygote, not how frequently it occurs in a population.
- **Flower Colour:** Violet is dominant over white in Mendel's pea plants.
- **Flower Position:** Axial is dominant over terminal.
- **Monohybrid F₂ Ratios:** Under complete dominance with normal segregation, the expected genotypic ratio is $1:2:1$, while the phenotypic ratio is $3:1$.

## Quiz

1. A garden pea plant with violet flowers is crossed with a pea plant with white flowers. All resulting F₁ offspring exhibit violet flowers. When an F₁ plant is self-pollinated, what proportion of the F₂ generation is expected to possess white flowers?

- [ ] $0\%$
- [x] $25\%$
- [ ] $50\%$
- [ ] $75\%$

> Violet flower colour is dominant ($V$) over white ($v$). The F₁ plants are heterozygous ($Vv$). Self-pollination produces the genotypic ratio $1VV:2Vv:1vv$, giving a phenotypic ratio of $3$ violet : $1$ white. Therefore, 25% of the F₂ generation is expected to have white flowers ($vv$).

2. Which term describes alternative forms of a single gene located at the same locus on homologous chromosomes?

- [ ] Cistrons
- [x] Alleles
- [ ] Transposons
- [ ] Mutons

> Alleles are alternative forms of a gene occupying the same locus on homologous chromosomes. They may differ in their DNA sequences and can produce different versions of a particular trait.

3. In garden pea plants (*Pisum sativum*), which pair of contrasting character states represents a dominant trait followed by its corresponding recessive state?

- [ ] Green seed colour (Dominant) / Yellow seed colour (Recessive)
- [x] Axial flower position (Dominant) / Terminal flower position (Recessive)
- [ ] Constricted pod shape (Dominant) / Inflated pod shape (Recessive)
- [ ] Yellow pod colour (Dominant) / Green pod colour (Recessive)

> In Mendel's pea experiments, axial flower position is dominant over terminal flower position. Yellow seed colour is dominant over green, while green pod colour is dominant over yellow.
