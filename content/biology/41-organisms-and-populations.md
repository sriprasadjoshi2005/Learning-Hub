Ecology is the scientific study of interactions among organisms and between organisms and their **abiotic environment**. These interactions influence the distribution, abundance, survival, and reproduction of organisms.

## Abiotic Factors and Adaptations

Organisms experience changes in four major abiotic factors: **temperature, water, light, and soil**. They survive under different environmental conditions through physiological, anatomical, and behavioural adaptations.

### Major Abiotic Factors

| Abiotic Factor | Ecological Importance |
|---|---|
| **Temperature** | Influences enzyme activity, metabolism, growth, and geographical distribution. |
| **Water** | Essential for metabolic reactions, transport, and maintaining cellular structure. |
| **Light** | Determines photosynthetic activity and influences flowering and other biological rhythms. |
| **Soil** | Its texture, composition, pH, and mineral content influence plant distribution and growth. |

### Responses to Abiotic Conditions

Organisms respond to environmental changes through different strategies:

- **Regulators:** Maintain a relatively constant internal environment despite changes in external conditions. Birds and mammals are examples of organisms that regulate body temperature.
- **Conformers:** Their internal conditions change with the external environment. Most animals and nearly all plants are conformers for several physiological parameters.
- **Migration:** Some organisms move temporarily to more favourable habitats when environmental conditions become unsuitable. For example, certain birds migrate to warmer regions during winter.
- **Suspension:** Some organisms reduce or suspend metabolic activities during unfavourable conditions.

Examples of suspension include:

- **Hibernation:** Winter dormancy in animals such as bears and rodents.
- **Aestivation:** Summer dormancy in animals such as certain snails and fishes.
- **Diapause:** A stage of suspended development, particularly in insects and zooplankton.

### Temperature Adaptations

Temperature influences the survival and distribution of organisms.

- **Eurythermal organisms:** Can tolerate a wide range of temperatures.
- **Stenothermal organisms:** Can tolerate only a narrow range of temperatures.

**Allen's Rule:** Mammals from colder climates generally have shorter ears and limbs than their counterparts in warmer climates. This reduces exposed surface area and helps minimise heat loss.

**Bergmann's Rule:** In many endothermic animals, populations or species in colder climates tend to have larger body sizes. Larger bodies have a lower surface-area-to-volume ratio, reducing heat loss.

## Population Attributes

A **population** is a group of individuals of the same species living in a particular geographical area at a given time.

Populations have characteristics that are not applicable to individual organisms.

### Important Population Attributes

| Attribute | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Population Density (N)** | Number of individuals per unit area or volume. |
| **Natality** | Birth rate within a population. |
| **Mortality** | Death rate within a population. |
| **Sex Ratio** | Proportion of males and females in a population. |
| **Age Distribution** | Proportion of individuals belonging to different age groups. |

### Age Pyramids

Age pyramids represent the distribution of individuals across three age groups:

- **Pre-reproductive:** Individuals that have not yet reached reproductive maturity.
- **Reproductive:** Individuals capable of reproduction.
- **Post-reproductive:** Individuals that have passed the reproductive stage.

Three major age-pyramid patterns are:

1. **Expanding population:** A broad base indicates a large proportion of young individuals. The population is likely to increase.
2. **Stable population:** The pre-reproductive and reproductive groups are relatively similar in size. Population size tends to remain stable.
3. **Declining population:** A narrow base indicates fewer young individuals. The population is likely to decrease.

## Population Growth Models

Population growth depends on natality, mortality, immigration, and emigration.

The population density at a given time is influenced by:

\[
N_{t+1}=N_t+(B+I)-(D+E)
\]

Where:

- \(N_t\): Population density at time \(t\).
- \(B\): Number of births.
- \(I\): Number of individuals immigrating into the population.
- \(D\): Number of deaths.
- \(E\): Number of individuals emigrating from the population.

Two important mathematical models describe population growth.

### 1. Exponential Growth

Exponential growth occurs when resources are unlimited and environmental conditions are favourable.

\[
\frac{dN}{dt}=rN
\]

Where:

- \(N\): Population size or density.
- \(r\): Intrinsic rate of natural increase.
- \(\frac{dN}{dt}\): Rate of change in population size.

The solution is:

\[
N_t=N_0e^{rt}
\]

Where:

- \(N_0\): Initial population size.
- \(N_t\): Population size after time \(t\).
- \(e\): Base of the natural logarithm.

**Characteristics:**
- Population increases rapidly when resources are unlimited.
- Growth rate is proportional to population size.
- Produces a J-shaped growth curve.
- Rarely continues indefinitely under natural conditions.

### 2. Logistic Growth

In natural environments, resources such as food, water, and space are limited. Population growth is therefore restricted by environmental resistance.

The logistic growth equation is:

\[
\frac{dN}{dt}=rN\left(\frac{K-N}{K}\right)
\]

Where:

- \(N\): Population size or density.
- \(r\): Intrinsic rate of natural increase.
- \(K\): Carrying capacity of the environment.

**Carrying Capacity (K):** The maximum population size that an environment can sustain over a long period with its available resources.

**Characteristics:**
- Growth is initially rapid.
- Growth slows as the population approaches carrying capacity.
- Net population growth becomes zero when \(N=K\).
- Produces an S-shaped or sigmoid growth curve.

### Exponential vs Logistic Growth

| Feature | Exponential Growth | Logistic Growth |
|---|---|---|
| Resource availability | Unlimited | Limited |
| Growth pattern | J-shaped | S-shaped |
| Carrying capacity | Not considered | Considered |
| Growth equation | \(\frac{dN}{dt}=rN\) | \(\frac{dN}{dt}=rN\left(\frac{K-N}{K}\right)\) |
| Natural occurrence | Under ideal conditions | More realistic for populations with limited resources |

### Logistic Growth Curve

    Population (N)
          ^
       K -|---------------------- Carrying capacity (K)
          |                 ______
          |             ___/
          |          __/
          |       __/
          |    __/
          |___/
          +--------------------------> Time (t)

The logistic curve initially rises rapidly, then slows as resources become increasingly limited, eventually approaching carrying capacity.

### Important Mathematical Result

The maximum population growth rate in the logistic model occurs at:

\[
N=\frac{K}{2}
\]

At this population size:

\[
\left(\frac{dN}{dt}\right)_{\max}=\frac{rK}{4}
\]

This is because the product \(N(K-N)\) reaches its maximum when \(N=K/2\).

## Population Interactions

Organisms in a community interact with individuals of the same or different species. These interactions influence survival, reproduction, and population regulation.

The effect of an interaction is represented using:

- **(+)**: Benefits the organism.
- **(-)**: Harms the organism.
- **(0)**: Neither benefits nor harms the organism.

### Major Types of Population Interactions

| Interaction | Species A | Species B | Meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Mutualism** | + | + | Both species benefit. |
| **Competition** | - | - | Both species are negatively affected. |
| **Predation** | + | - | One species benefits by consuming another. |
| **Parasitism** | + | - | Parasite benefits while the host is harmed. |
| **Commensalism** | + | 0 | One species benefits while the other is unaffected. |
| **Amensalism** | - | 0 | One species is harmed while the other is unaffected. |

### 1. Mutualism (+/+)

Mutualism is an interaction in which both species benefit from their association.

Examples:

- **Lichens:** A symbiotic association between an alga or cyanobacterium and a fungus. The photosynthetic partner provides organic nutrients, while the fungus provides shelter and helps absorb water and minerals.
- **Mycorrhizae:** An association between fungi and plant roots. Fungi improve the absorption of water and minerals, especially phosphorus, while receiving carbohydrates from the plant.
- **Fig and fig wasp:** The wasp pollinates the fig while using its flowers for reproduction.
- **Plant-pollinator relationships:** Insects obtain nectar or pollen while transferring pollen between flowers.

### 2. Competition (-/-)

Competition occurs when two or more organisms require the same limited resource, such as food, water, light, or space.

It negatively affects both competing organisms because each reduces the other's access to resources.

**Types of Competition:**

- **Intraspecific competition:** Competition between individuals of the same species.
- **Interspecific competition:** Competition between individuals of different species.

**Examples:**

- Goats and Galapagos tortoises competing for vegetation.
- Different plant species competing for sunlight and soil nutrients.
- Animals competing for limited food and territory.

**Gause's Competitive Exclusion Principle:** Two closely related species competing for exactly the same limited resources cannot coexist indefinitely. The competitively inferior species may eventually be eliminated unless they occupy different niches or resource partitioning occurs.

**Resource Partitioning:** Competing species reduce competition by using different resources, occupying different habitats, or feeding at different times.

### 3. Predation (+/-)

Predation is an interaction in which one organism, called the **predator**, captures and consumes another organism, called the **prey**.

Examples:

- Tiger feeding on deer.
- Snake feeding on rodents.
- Ladybird beetles feeding on aphids.

**Ecological Importance of Predation:**

- Controls prey populations.
- Maintains community diversity.
- Transfers energy between trophic levels.
- Helps prevent a single species from becoming excessively dominant.

**Keystone Predators:** Some predators have a disproportionately large influence on community structure.

Example: The sea star *Pisaster* controls populations of mussels along rocky shores. When *Pisaster* is removed, mussels can dominate the habitat and reduce species diversity.

**Plant Defences Against Herbivores:**

Plants have evolved several adaptations to reduce herbivory:

- **Mechanical defences:** Thorns and spines, as seen in plants such as Acacia and cactus.
- **Chemical defences:** Toxic or unpalatable substances such as nicotine, caffeine, quinine, and cardiac glycosides.

### 4. Parasitism (+/-)

Parasitism is an interaction in which one organism, the **parasite**, obtains nutrients or other benefits from another organism, the **host**, causing harm to the host.

Examples:

- *Cuscuta* growing on hedge plants and absorbing nutrients.
- Ticks feeding on the blood of dogs.
- *Plasmodium* living inside humans and causing malaria.
- Tapeworms living in the human intestine.

**Adaptations of Parasites:**

- Loss or reduction of unnecessary organs.
- Development of specialised attachment structures.
- High reproductive capacity.
- Complex life cycles involving one or more hosts in some parasites.

### 5. Commensalism (+/0)

Commensalism occurs when one species benefits while the other remains unaffected.

Examples:

- **Orchid and mango tree:** The orchid grows on the mango tree for structural support and better access to sunlight without harming it.
- **Cattle egret and grazing cattle:** Egrets feed on insects disturbed by grazing cattle.
- **Barnacles and whales:** Barnacles obtain transport and access to food-rich waters while the whale is generally unaffected.

### 6. Amensalism (-/0)

Amensalism occurs when one species is harmed while the other remains unaffected.

Examples:

- *Penicillium* produces penicillin, which inhibits the growth of certain bacteria.
- Some plants release allelochemicals that inhibit the germination or growth of nearby plants.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Regulators vs Conformers:** Regulators maintain relatively constant internal conditions, whereas conformers allow internal conditions to change with the environment.
- **Allen's Rule vs Bergmann's Rule:** Allen's rule concerns the length of external appendages; Bergmann's rule concerns body size.
- **Eurythermal vs Stenothermal:** Eurythermal organisms tolerate a wide temperature range; stenothermal organisms tolerate a narrow range.
- **Exponential vs Logistic Growth:** Exponential growth produces a J-shaped curve under unlimited resources; logistic growth produces an S-shaped curve under limited resources.
- **Carrying Capacity:** When \(N=K\), the net population growth rate is zero, not maximum.
- **Maximum Logistic Growth:** The maximum growth rate occurs at \(N=K/2\), not at \(N=K\).
- **Population Growth:** Births and immigration increase population size, whereas deaths and emigration decrease it.
- **Competition:** Both competing species are negatively affected, even if one is a stronger competitor.
- **Predation vs Parasitism:** Predators generally kill and consume prey, whereas parasites typically obtain resources from a living host over an extended period.
- **Mutualism vs Commensalism:** Mutualism benefits both species (+/+); commensalism benefits one while leaving the other unaffected (+/0).
- **Gause's Principle:** Competitive exclusion applies when species compete for the same limiting resources; resource partitioning can allow coexistence.
- **Amensalism:** The harmed species is represented by (-), while the unaffected species is represented by (0).

## Quiz

1. A population of rodents growing in an isolated island ecosystem exhibits logistic growth. What happens to the net population growth rate \(\frac{dN}{dt}\) when its population size \(N\) reaches the carrying capacity \(K\)?

- [ ] Growth rate reaches maximum exponential velocity (\(rN\)).
- [x] Growth rate drops to zero (\(\frac{dN}{dt}=0\)).
- [ ] Carrying capacity doubles automatically.
- [ ] Mortality drops to zero while birth rate surges.

> In logistic growth, \(\frac{dN}{dt}=rN\left(\frac{K-N}{K}\right)\). When \(N=K\), the term \(\frac{K-N}{K}\) becomes zero, making the net population growth rate zero.

2. Mammals adapted to cold sub-polar regions typically possess shorter external extremities, such as ears and limbs, than their warm-climate counterparts. Which ecological principle describes this adaptation?

- [ ] Gause's Principle
- [x] Allen's Rule
- [ ] Bergmann's Rule
- [ ] Lindeman's Efficiency Law

> Allen's rule states that mammals from colder climates generally have shorter appendages, reducing exposed surface area and helping minimise heat loss.

3. An epiphytic orchid grows attached to a sturdy branch of a mango tree. The orchid gains structural support and access to sunlight, while the mango tree experiences neither benefit nor harm. What type of ecological interaction is shown?

- [ ] Mutualism
- [ ] Amensalism
- [x] Commensalism
- [ ] Parasitism

> Commensalism is an interaction in which one species benefits (+) while the other remains unaffected (0).

4. In a logistic growth model, at what population size does the population growth rate reach its maximum value?

- [ ] \(N=K\)
- [ ] \(N=0\)
- [x] \(N=\frac{K}{2}\)
- [ ] \(N=2K\)

> The logistic growth rate is maximum when \(N(K-N)\) is maximum, which occurs at \(N=K/2\). The maximum growth rate is \(rK/4\).

5. Which of the following is an example of a population interaction in which both species are negatively affected?

- [ ] Mutualism
- [x] Competition
- [ ] Commensalism
- [ ] Predation

> Competition is represented by (-/-) because both competing organisms experience reduced access to limited resources.

6. A species of bird migrates to another geographical region during winter when temperatures become unfavourable. Which survival strategy does this represent?

- [ ] Aestivation
- [ ] Hibernation
- [x] Migration
- [ ] Diapause

> Migration involves the temporary movement of organisms to more favourable habitats when environmental conditions become unsuitable.

7. Which statement correctly distinguishes Allen's Rule from Bergmann's Rule?

- [ ] Both rules describe changes in reproductive rates.
- [x] Allen's Rule concerns appendage length, while Bergmann's Rule concerns body size.
- [ ] Allen's Rule concerns body size, while Bergmann's Rule concerns appendage length.
- [ ] Both rules state that cold-climate mammals have smaller bodies and longer limbs.

> Allen's rule describes shorter external appendages in colder climates, whereas Bergmann's rule describes the tendency for endotherms to have larger body sizes in colder climates.

8. *Penicillium* produces a chemical substance that inhibits the growth of certain bacteria, while the fungus itself is unaffected. Which interaction does this represent?

- [ ] Mutualism
- [ ] Parasitism
- [ ] Competition
- [x] Amensalism

> Amensalism is represented by (-/0). One organism is harmed, while the other remains unaffected. Penicillin produced by *Penicillium* inhibits susceptible bacteria.

9. Two closely related species compete for exactly the same limited resources in an ecosystem. According to Gause's Competitive Exclusion Principle, what is most likely to happen if neither species changes its resource use?

- [ ] Both species will necessarily increase indefinitely.
- [ ] Both species will become mutualistic.
- [x] The competitively inferior species may eventually be eliminated.
- [ ] Competition will automatically become commensalism.

> Gause's principle states that two species competing for the same limiting resources cannot coexist indefinitely under identical ecological conditions. The inferior competitor may be eliminated.

10. Which of the following population changes would decrease population density?

- [ ] Increased natality
- [ ] Increased immigration
- [x] Increased mortality
- [ ] Increased birth rate

> Mortality represents deaths within a population. An increase in mortality decreases population density, whereas natality and immigration generally increase it.
