Nucleic acids, namely **DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)** and **RNA (Ribonucleic Acid)**, are the primary informational macromolecules of life. DNA serves as the genetic material in most organisms, storing and transmitting hereditary information. Its double-helical structure allows genetic information to be accurately copied and passed on during cell division.

## DNA Double Helix Structure

The **double helix model of DNA** was proposed by James Watson and Francis Crick in 1953. Their model was based on X-ray diffraction data, particularly the work of Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins, and **Chargaff's Rules**.

### Chargaff's Rules

Chargaff's rules describe the relative proportions of nitrogenous bases in double-stranded DNA:

$$
\%A = \%T
$$

$$
\%G = \%C
$$

Therefore:

$$
A + G = T + C
$$

- **Purines**: Adenine (A) and Guanine (G).
- **Pyrimidines**: Cytosine (C) and Thymine (T).

The total number of purines equals the total number of pyrimidines in double-stranded DNA.

### Structural Properties of B-DNA

| Feature | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| Structure | Double-helical |
| Number of strands | Two polynucleotide chains |
| Orientation | Antiparallel ($5' \rightarrow 3'$ and $3' \rightarrow 5'$) |
| Sugar | Deoxyribose |
| Backbone | Alternating sugar and phosphate groups |
| Base pairing | Adenine pairs with Thymine; Guanine pairs with Cytosine |
| A–T hydrogen bonds | 2 |
| G–C hydrogen bonds | 3 |
| Helical pitch | $3.4\text{ nm}$ ($34\text{ Å}$) |
| Base pairs per turn | Approximately 10 |
| Distance between adjacent base pairs | $0.34\text{ nm}$ |

### Complementary Base Pairing

Nitrogenous bases pair through hydrogen bonds inside the DNA double helix.

- **Adenine (A)** pairs with **Thymine (T)** through 2 hydrogen bonds.
- **Guanine (G)** pairs with **Cytosine (C)** through 3 hydrogen bonds.

$$
A = T \qquad (2\text{ hydrogen bonds})
$$

$$
G \equiv C \qquad (3\text{ hydrogen bonds})
$$

The complementary base-pairing arrangement ensures that each DNA strand can serve as a template for synthesising a new strand.

## Central Dogma of Molecular Biology

Proposed by Francis Crick, the **Central Dogma of Molecular Biology** describes the general flow of genetic information from DNA to RNA to proteins.

### Flow of Genetic Information

$$
\text{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Replication}} \text{DNA}
$$

$$
\text{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Transcription}} \text{mRNA}
$$

$$
\text{mRNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Translation}} \text{Protein}
$$

| Process | Function | Main Enzyme or Machinery |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Replication | Produces a new DNA molecule from an existing DNA molecule. | DNA polymerase |
| Transcription | Produces RNA using DNA as a template. | RNA polymerase |
| Translation | Uses mRNA information to synthesise a polypeptide. | Ribosome |

### Reverse Transcription

Some viruses, known as **retroviruses**, use RNA as their genetic material and produce DNA from an RNA template.

- The enzyme **Reverse Transcriptase** synthesises DNA using RNA as a template.
- The newly formed DNA can integrate into the host genome.
- Example: Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV).

$$
\text{RNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Reverse Transcriptase}} \text{DNA}
$$

## DNA Replication

**DNA replication** is the process by which a DNA molecule produces an identical copy of itself before cell division.

### Semi-Conservative Replication

The semi-conservative model states that each newly formed DNA molecule contains:

- One original (parental) DNA strand.
- One newly synthesised DNA strand.

This mechanism was experimentally demonstrated by **Matthew Meselson and Franklin Stahl in 1958**, using the bacterium *Escherichia coli* and nitrogen isotopes.

### Meselson–Stahl Experiment

The experiment used two nitrogen isotopes:

- **Nitrogen-15 ($^{15}N$)**: Heavy isotope.
- **Nitrogen-14 ($^{14}N$)**: Light isotope.

**Experimental procedure:**

1. *E. coli* bacteria were grown for several generations in a medium containing $^{15}N$, allowing their DNA to become heavy.
2. The bacteria were transferred to a medium containing $^{14}N$.
3. DNA samples were collected after successive generations.
4. DNA was separated using density gradient centrifugation.

### Experimental Results

| Generation | DNA Composition | Result |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Before transfer | $100\%$ heavy ($^{15}N/^{15}N$) | All DNA is heavy |
| First generation | $100\%$ hybrid ($^{15}N/^{14}N$) | All DNA is hybrid |
| Second generation | $50\%$ hybrid and $50\%$ light ($^{14}N/^{14}N$) | Two DNA populations |
| Third generation | $25\%$ hybrid and $75\%$ light | Hybrid proportion decreases |

These results supported the semi-conservative model of DNA replication.

## Enzymes Involved in DNA Replication

DNA replication requires several enzymes that perform different functions.

| Enzyme | Function |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Helicase** | Unwinds the DNA double helix by breaking hydrogen bonds between complementary bases. |
| **DNA Polymerase III** | Synthesises new DNA strands by adding nucleotides in the $5' \rightarrow 3'$ direction in *E. coli*. |
| **Primase** | Synthesises short RNA primers that provide a free $3'-OH$ group for DNA polymerase. |
| **DNA Ligase** | Joins Okazaki fragments by forming phosphodiester bonds in the lagging strand. |
| **Topoisomerase** | Relieves twisting strain and supercoiling ahead of the replication fork. |

### Leading and Lagging Strands

Because DNA polymerases can synthesise DNA only in the $5' \rightarrow 3'$ direction, the two new strands are produced differently.

- **Leading strand**: Synthesised continuously towards the replication fork.
- **Lagging strand**: Synthesised discontinuously away from the replication fork in short segments called **Okazaki fragments**.
- **DNA ligase** joins the Okazaki fragments to form a continuous DNA strand.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Direction of Synthesis**: Both DNA and RNA polymerases synthesise new nucleic acid strands exclusively in the **$5' \rightarrow 3'$ direction**. They read the template strand in the $3' \rightarrow 5'$ direction.
- **Chargaff's Rules**: These rules apply to **double-stranded DNA**, not necessarily to single-stranded DNA or RNA.
- **Hydrogen Bonds**: Adenine–Thymine pairs have 2 hydrogen bonds, whereas Guanine–Cytosine pairs have 3.
- **DNA Dimensions**: B-DNA has approximately 10 base pairs per turn, a pitch of $3.4\text{ nm}$ and a distance of $0.34\text{ nm}$ between adjacent base pairs.
- **Semi-Conservative Replication**: After the first generation in $^{14}N$ medium, all DNA molecules are hybrid. After the second generation, $50\%$ are hybrid and $50\%$ are light.
- **DNA Polymerase III**: In *E. coli*, DNA polymerase III is the primary enzyme responsible for synthesising new DNA strands. DNA polymerases cannot initiate synthesis *de novo* and require a primer.
- **Okazaki Fragments**: These are formed on the discontinuously synthesised lagging strand, not the leading strand.

## Quiz

1. A double-stranded DNA sample is analysed and found to contain $28\%$ Cytosine residues. According to Chargaff's rules, what is the percentage of Adenine in this DNA molecule?

- [ ] $28\%$
- [x] $22\%$
- [ ] $44\%$
- [ ] $56\%$

> In double-stranded DNA, the percentage of Cytosine equals the percentage of Guanine. Therefore, $C + G = 28\% + 28\% = 56\%$. The remaining $44\%$ is divided equally between Adenine and Thymine. Hence, Adenine constitutes $22\%$.

2. During DNA replication in *E. coli*, which enzyme synthesises short RNA sequences that provide a free $3'-OH$ group for DNA polymerase?

- [ ] DNA Ligase
- [ ] Topoisomerase
- [x] Primase
- [ ] Helicase

> DNA polymerases cannot initiate DNA synthesis independently. Primase synthesises short RNA primers that provide the free $3'-OH$ group required for DNA polymerase to begin adding nucleotides.

3. *E. coli* cells grown exclusively in $^{15}N$ medium for many generations are transferred to standard $^{14}N$ medium. After two complete rounds of DNA replication, what fraction of the double-stranded DNA molecules will be hybrid ($^{15}N/^{14}N$)?

- [ ] $0\%$
- [ ] $25\%$
- [x] $50\%$
- [ ] $100\%$

> After the first generation, all DNA molecules are hybrid ($^{15}N/^{14}N$). During the second generation, each hybrid molecule produces one hybrid molecule and one light molecule ($^{14}N/^{14}N$). Therefore, $50\%$ of the DNA molecules remain hybrid.

4. Which type of bond holds complementary nitrogenous bases together in the DNA double helix?

- [ ] Phosphodiester bonds
- [ ] Glycosidic bonds
- [x] Hydrogen bonds
- [ ] Peptide bonds

> Complementary nitrogenous bases are held together by hydrogen bonds. Adenine and Thymine form 2 hydrogen bonds, while Guanine and Cytosine form 3.

5. Which enzyme joins the Okazaki fragments during DNA replication?

- [ ] Helicase
- [ ] Primase
- [ ] DNA Polymerase III
- [x] DNA Ligase

> DNA ligase joins adjacent Okazaki fragments by forming phosphodiester bonds, producing a continuous DNA strand on the lagging strand.

6. Which enzyme enables retroviruses such as HIV to synthesise DNA from an RNA template?

- [ ] DNA Ligase
- [x] Reverse Transcriptase
- [ ] RNA Polymerase
- [ ] Helicase

> Reverse Transcriptase is an enzyme that synthesises DNA using RNA as a template. This process is known as reverse transcription.
