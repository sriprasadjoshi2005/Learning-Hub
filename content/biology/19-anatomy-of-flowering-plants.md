## title: Anatomy of Flowering Plants
level: intermediate

Plant anatomy examines the internal cellular structures of plant organs. Plant tissues are classified into **Meristematic** (dividing) and **Permanent** (specialized) tissues.

## Classification of Plant Tissues

```
                               Plant Tissues
                                     |
         -----------------------------------------------------------
         |                                                         |
 Meristematic Tissues                                      Permanent Tissues
(Apical, Intercalary, Lateral)                                     |
                                           ---------------------------------
                                           |                               |
                                    Simple Permanent               Complex Permanent
                                (Parenchyma, Collenchyma,         (Xylem, Phloem)
                                 Sclerenchyma)
```

## Simple Permanent Tissues

1. **Parenchyma**: Thin primary cell walls (cellulose); living cells functioning in photosynthesis, storage, and secretion.
2. **Collenchyma**: Living cells with localized wall thickenings of **cellulose, hemicellulose, and pectin** at corners; provides mechanical flexibility to young stems.
3. **Sclerenchyma**: Dead at maturity with thick **lignified** secondary cell walls; provides structural support (fibres and sclereids).

## Complex Permanent Tissues

* **Xylem**: Transport of water and minerals (Tracheids, Vessels, Xylem Fibres, Xylem Parenchyma). *Note: Vessels are absent in Gymnosperms*.
* **Phloem**: Transport of organic solutes/food (Sieve Tube Elements, Companion Cells, Phloem Parenchyma, Phloem Fibres). *Note: Companion cells are absent in Gymnosperms*.

## Dicot vs Monocot Anatomical Differences

| Parameter | Dicot Stem | Monocot Stem | Dicot Root | Monocot Root |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Vascular Bundles** | Arranged in a ring; open (cambium present) | Scattered; closed (no cambium) | Radial; diarch to hexarch | Radial; polyarch ($>6$) |
| **Hypodermis** | Collenchymatous | Sclerenchymatous | Absent | Absent |
| **Pith** | Well-developed | Absent or reduced | Small / inconspicuous | Large and well-developed |
| **Secondary Growth** | Present | Absent | Present | Absent |

## Common Exam Traps

* **Living vs Dead Tissues**: Collenchyma is a **living** mechanical tissue (pectin thickenings), while Sclerenchyma is a **dead** mechanical tissue (lignified walls).
* **Gymnosperm Conductive Elements**: Gymnosperms lack true **Xylem Vessels** (possess tracheids only) and lack **Companion Cells** (possess albuminous cells instead).

## Quiz

1. Transverse section of a plant stem shows sclerenchymatous hypodermis, scattered vascular bundles enclosed by bundle sheaths, and absence of secondary growth. How should this stem be identified?

* \[ \] Dicot stem
* \[x\] Monocot stem
* \[ \] Dicot root
* \[ \] Monocot root

> Monocot stems are characterized by a sclerenchymatous hypodermis, scattered vascular bundles surrounded by bundle sheaths, closed vascular bundles, and a lack of secondary growth.

2. Which mechanical simple permanent tissue consists of living cells with localized cell wall thickenings made of cellulose, hemicellulose, and pectin at their corners?

* \[ \] Sclerenchyma
* \[x\] Collenchyma
* \[ \] Parenchyma
* \[ \] Xylem tracheids

> Collenchyma is a living simple tissue characterized by unevenly thickened cell walls containing pectin, cellulose, and hemicellulose, providing flexibility to growing stems.

3. Which cellular components of phloem tissue are completely absent in the vascular bundles of gymnosperms?

* \[ \] Tracheids and Xylem Fibres
* \[x\] Sieve tube elements and Companion cells
* \[ \] Albuminous cells and Sieve cells
* \[ \] Parenchyma cells and Fibres

> Gymnosperms lack true sieve tube elements and companion cells in their phloem; instead, they possess primitive sieve cells and albuminous cells.
