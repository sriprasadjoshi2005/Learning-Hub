Plant anatomy examines the internal structure of plant organs, including roots, stems and leaves. Plant tissues are classified into two major groups: **meristematic tissues**, which actively divide, and **permanent tissues**, which become specialized to perform specific functions.

## Classification of Plant Tissues

Plant tissues are broadly classified into two groups:

    Plant Tissues
          |
    -----------------------------
    |                           |
    Meristematic Tissues        Permanent Tissues
    |                           |
    |                           -----------------------------
    |                           |                           |
    Apical                      Simple Permanent             Complex Permanent
    Intercalary                 |                           |
    Lateral                     Parenchyma                  Xylem
                                Collenchyma                 Phloem
                                Sclerenchyma

- **Meristematic tissues** consist of actively dividing cells responsible for plant growth.
- **Permanent tissues** consist of mature, specialized cells that generally have lost the ability to divide.

## Simple Permanent Tissues

Simple permanent tissues consist of only one type of cell performing similar functions.

| Tissue | Cell Nature | Cell Wall Characteristics | Main Functions |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Parenchyma** | Living | Thin primary cellulose walls | Photosynthesis, storage and secretion |
| **Collenchyma** | Living | Unevenly thickened corners containing cellulose, hemicellulose and pectin | Mechanical support and flexibility |
| **Sclerenchyma** | Dead at maturity | Thick, lignified secondary walls | Mechanical strength and structural support |

### 1. Parenchyma

- Consists of living cells with thin primary cell walls.
- Cells are usually loosely packed, with intercellular spaces.
- Functions include storage, photosynthesis and secretion.
- **Chlorenchyma** is parenchyma containing chloroplasts and performing photosynthesis.
- **Aerenchyma** contains large air spaces that help aquatic plants with buoyancy and gaseous exchange.

### 2. Collenchyma

- Consists of living, elongated cells.
- Cell walls are unevenly thickened at the corners due to deposits of cellulose, hemicellulose and pectin.
- Provides mechanical support and flexibility to growing plant parts.
- Commonly found beneath the epidermis of young dicot stems and in leaf stalks.

### 3. Sclerenchyma

- Consists of cells that are usually dead at maturity.
- Possesses thick, lignified secondary cell walls.
- Provides mechanical strength and rigidity to plant organs.
- Two major forms are:
  - **Fibres:** Long, narrow cells with tapering ends.
  - **Sclereids:** Shorter, irregularly shaped cells found in seed coats and the gritty texture of pear fruits.

## Complex Permanent Tissues

Complex permanent tissues consist of different types of cells working together to perform a common function. The two major complex tissues are **xylem** and **phloem**.

### 1. Xylem

Xylem transports water and dissolved minerals from the roots to other parts of the plant. It also provides mechanical support.

It consists of four elements:

| Element | Nature | Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Tracheids** | Dead | Water conduction and support |
| **Vessels** | Dead | Efficient water conduction |
| **Xylem Fibres** | Dead | Mechanical strength |
| **Xylem Parenchyma** | Living | Storage and lateral conduction |

**Important:** Most gymnosperms lack xylem vessels and conduct water mainly through tracheids. Exceptions include certain groups such as Gnetales.

### 2. Phloem

Phloem transports organic food materials, particularly sugars, from source regions to sink regions of the plant.

It consists of four elements:

| Element | Nature | Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Sieve Tube Elements** | Living, enucleate at maturity | Transport of organic food |
| **Companion Cells** | Living | Regulate and support sieve tube elements |
| **Phloem Parenchyma** | Living | Storage and transport |
| **Phloem Fibres** | Dead | Mechanical support |

**Important:** Gymnosperms generally lack true sieve tube elements and companion cells. Instead, they possess sieve cells and albuminous cells, respectively.

## Dicot vs Monocot Anatomical Differences

Dicot and monocot stems and roots differ in their vascular bundle arrangement, hypodermis, pith and potential for secondary growth.

### Stem Anatomy

| Feature | Dicot Stem | Monocot Stem |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Vascular Bundles** | Arranged in a ring | Scattered throughout the ground tissue |
| **Vascular Bundle Type** | Open, with cambium | Closed, without cambium |
| **Hypodermis** | Collenchymatous | Sclerenchymatous |
| **Ground Tissue** | Differentiated into cortex, endodermis, pericycle and pith | Generally undifferentiated |
| **Pith** | Well-developed | Not distinctly differentiated |
| **Secondary Growth** | Usually present | Generally absent |

### Root Anatomy

| Feature | Dicot Root | Monocot Root |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Vascular Arrangement** | Radial | Radial |
| **Xylem Condition** | Usually diarch to hexarch | Usually polyarch |
| **Pith** | Small or inconspicuous | Large and well-developed |
| **Secondary Growth** | Usually present | Generally absent |

- **Radial vascular bundles:** Xylem and phloem occur on alternate radii.
- **Polyarch:** A vascular arrangement with more than six xylem poles.
- **Open vascular bundle:** Contains cambium between xylem and phloem.
- **Closed vascular bundle:** Lacks cambium between xylem and phloem.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Living vs Dead Tissues:** Collenchyma is a living mechanical tissue with uneven wall thickening, while sclerenchyma is generally dead at maturity with lignified walls.
- **Parenchyma:** It is a living tissue with thin primary walls and may perform storage, photosynthesis or gaseous exchange.
- **Gymnosperm Xylem:** Most gymnosperms lack vessels and primarily use tracheids for water conduction.
- **Gymnosperm Phloem:** Gymnosperms lack true sieve tube elements and companion cells. They possess sieve cells and albuminous cells instead.
- **Dicot vs Monocot Stems:** Dicot stems generally have ring-arranged, open vascular bundles, whereas monocot stems have scattered, closed vascular bundles.
- **Dicot vs Monocot Roots:** Dicot roots usually have fewer xylem poles and a small pith, whereas monocot roots are generally polyarch with a large pith.
- **Secondary Growth:** It is generally present in dicot stems and roots but absent in monocots.

## Quiz

1. A transverse section of a plant stem shows a sclerenchymatous hypodermis, scattered vascular bundles enclosed by bundle sheaths and an absence of secondary growth. How should this stem be identified?

- [ ] Dicot stem
- [x] Monocot stem
- [ ] Dicot root
- [ ] Monocot root

> Monocot stems characteristically possess a sclerenchymatous hypodermis, scattered and closed vascular bundles, bundle sheaths and generally lack secondary growth.

2. Which simple permanent tissue consists of living cells with localized cell wall thickenings containing cellulose, hemicellulose and pectin?

- [ ] Sclerenchyma
- [x] Collenchyma
- [ ] Parenchyma
- [ ] Xylem tracheids

> Collenchyma consists of living cells with unevenly thickened corners. These thickenings provide mechanical support and flexibility to growing plant parts.

3. Which conducting elements are absent from the phloem of gymnosperms?

- [ ] Tracheids and xylem fibres
- [x] Sieve tube elements and companion cells
- [ ] Albuminous cells and sieve cells
- [ ] Phloem parenchyma and phloem fibres

> Gymnosperms generally possess sieve cells and albuminous cells instead of true sieve tube elements and companion cells.

4. Which of the following is a dead mechanical tissue at maturity with thick, lignified secondary cell walls?

- [ ] Parenchyma
- [ ] Collenchyma
- [x] Sclerenchyma
- [ ] Phloem parenchyma

> Sclerenchyma provides mechanical strength and rigidity. Its cells generally become dead at maturity due to the development of thick, lignified secondary walls.

5. Which tissue is responsible for transporting water and dissolved minerals from the roots to the aerial parts of a plant?

- [x] Xylem
- [ ] Phloem
- [ ] Collenchyma
- [ ] Epidermis

> Xylem conducts water and dissolved minerals from the roots towards the stem and leaves. It also provides mechanical support.

6. Which anatomical feature is generally characteristic of a monocot root?

- [ ] Diarch xylem and small pith
- [ ] Secondary growth and ring-arranged vascular bundles
- [x] Polyarch xylem and large pith
- [ ] Open vascular bundles and cambium

> Monocot roots generally possess polyarch xylem and a large, well-developed pith. They usually lack secondary growth.
