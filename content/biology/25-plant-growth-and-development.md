## title: Plant Growth and Development
level: intermediate → advanced

Plant growth is an irreversible permanent increase in the size of an organ or cell, accompanied by metabolic processes. Development encompasses all changes an organism undergoes during its life cycle.

## Phases of Plant Growth

1. **Meristematic Phase**: Constantly dividing cells at root and shoot apices; rich in protoplasm with thin cellulosic walls.
2. **Elongation Phase**: Cells behind the apex increase in size, develop vacuoles, and expand their cell walls.
3. **Maturation Phase**: Cells attain maximum wall thickening and undergo differentiation into specialized functional tissues.

## Arithmetic vs Geometric Growth Rates

### Arithmetic Growth
One daughter cell continues to divide while the other differentiates (e.g., root elongation at constant rate).

$$
L_t = L_0 + rt
$$

### Geometric Growth
Both daughter cells retain the capacity to divide, leading to an exponential growth phase (e.g., initial embryo development).

$$
W_1 = W_0 e^{rt}
$$

* **Sigmoid Growth Curve**: Plotted as an S-curve featuring **Lag phase** $\rightarrow$ **Log (Exponential) phase** $\rightarrow$ **Stationary phase**.

```
    Size/Weight
        ^
        |              /--- Stationary Phase
        |             /
        |            /  <-- Log (Exponential) Phase
        |           /
        |     -----   <-- Lag Phase
        +-----------------------------------> Time
```

## Cellular Differentiation Concepts

* **Differentiation**: Cells derive from meristems and mature to perform specific structural functions (e.g., loss of protoplasm to form xylem vessels).
* **Dedifferentiation**: Living permanent cells regain the capacity to divide under specific conditions (e.g., parenchyma cells forming **interfascicular cambium**).
* **Redifferentiation**: Cells produced by dedifferentiated meristems lose their division capacity once again to mature into permanent tissues (e.g., secondary xylem/phloem formed by vascular cambium).

## Photoperiodism and Vernalization

* **Photoperiodism**: Response of plants to the relative duration of day and night light exposure, mediated by the pigment protein **Phytochrome**.
  * *Short-Day Plants (SDP)*: Require dark periods exceeding a critical duration to flower (e.g., Chrysanthemum).
  * *Long-Day Plants (LDP)*: Require photoperiods exceeding a critical day length (e.g., Wheat, Radish).
* **Vernalization**: Quantitative or qualitative dependence of flowering on low-temperature exposure ($1^\circ\mathrm{C}-5^\circ\mathrm{C}$).

## Common Exam Traps

* **Critical Period Focus**: Short-Day Plants are actually **Long-Night Plants**; interrupting their continuous dark period with a flash of light prevents flowering.
* **Dedifferentiation Example**: Formation of **cork cambium** and **interfascicular cambium** from mature parenchyma cells are classic examples of dedifferentiation.

* \[ \] Linear arithmetic growth
* \[x\] Exponential geometric growth in a limited environment
* \[ \] Constant logarithmic growth without carrying capacity

> Geometric growth in a finite environment produces a Sigmoid curve characterized by an initial lag phase, an exponential log phase, and a plateau stationary phase.
