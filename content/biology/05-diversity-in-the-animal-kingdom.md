Kingdom **Animalia** encompasses multicellular, eukaryotic, heterotrophic organisms that lack cell walls. Animals are classified into major phyla based on fundamental characteristics such as body plans, levels of organisation, germ layers, body cavities, symmetry, and developmental patterns.

## Levels of Organisation and Body Symmetry

Animals are broadly classified according to their structural complexity, tissue organisation, and body symmetry.

### 1. Levels of Organisation

Animals exhibit three major levels of structural organisation:

- **Cellular Level**: Cells are loosely aggregated with minimal division of labour, and true tissues are absent (e.g., Phylum Porifera).
- **Tissue Level**: Similar cells are organised into tissues that perform specific functions (e.g., Phylum Cnidaria and Ctenophora).
- **Organ / Organ-system Level**: Different tissues combine to form specialised organs and functional organ systems (e.g., Platyhelminthes to Chordata).

### 2. Germ Layers

During embryonic development, animals form germ layers that give rise to different tissues and organs:

- **Diploblastic**: Animals possess two embryonic germ layers: the outer **ectoderm** and inner **endoderm**, separated by a non-cellular layer called **mesoglea** (e.g., Cnidaria and Ctenophora).
- **Triploblastic**: Animals possess three embryonic germ layers: **ectoderm**, **mesoderm**, and **endoderm** (e.g., Platyhelminthes to Chordata).

### 3. Body Cavity (Coelom)

The **coelom** is the body cavity present between the body wall and the gut wall, lined by mesoderm. Based on its presence and development, animals are classified into three groups:

$$
\text{Body Cavity Classification} =
\begin{cases}
\text{Acoelomate} & \text{No body cavity (Platyhelminthes)} \\
\text{Pseudocoelomate} & \text{Body cavity not completely lined by mesoderm (Aschelminthes)} \\
\text{Coelomate / Eucoelomate} & \text{True coelom completely lined by mesoderm (Annelida to Chordata)}
\end{cases}
$$

- **Acoelomates**: Animals without a body cavity between the body wall and gut (e.g., Platyhelminthes).
- **Pseudocoelomates**: Animals possessing a body cavity that is not completely lined by mesoderm (e.g., Aschelminthes).
- **Coelomates**: Animals possessing a true coelom completely lined by mesoderm (e.g., Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Hemichordata, and Chordata).

## Overview of Major Animal Phyla

| Phylum | Symmetry | Coelom Status | Distinctive Features | Representative Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Porifera** | Mostly asymmetrical | No true coelom | Ostia and osculum pore system, choanocytes (collar cells), spicules | *Sycon*, *Spongilla*, *Euspongia* |
| **Cnidaria (Coelenterata)** | Radial | No true coelom | Cnidocytes (stinging cells), polyp and medusa body forms | *Hydra*, *Aurelia*, *Physalia* |
| **Ctenophora** | Biradial | No true coelom | Eight rows of ciliated comb plates, bioluminescence | *Pleurobrachia*, *Ctenoplana* |
| **Platyhelminthes** | Bilateral | Acoelomate | Dorso-ventrally flattened body, flame cells for excretion | *Taenia*, *Fasciola*, *Planaria* |
| **Aschelminthes (Nematoda)** | Bilateral | Pseudocoelomate | Cylindrical body, complete digestive tract | *Ascaris*, *Wuchereria*, *Ancylostoma* |
| **Annelida** | Bilateral | Coelomate | Metameric segmentation, nephridia for excretion | *Nereis*, *Pheretima* (Earthworm), *Hirudinaria* |
| **Arthropoda** | Bilateral | Coelomate (haemocoel present) | Chitinous exoskeleton, jointed appendages, open circulatory system | *Apis*, *Bombyx*, *Anopheles*, *Periplaneta* |
| **Mollusca** | Bilateral | Coelomate | Soft body, calcareous shell, muscular foot, mantle cavity, radula | *Pila*, *Pinctada*, *Octopus* |
| **Echinodermata** | Radial (Adult) / Bilateral (Larva) | Coelomate | Water vascular system, tube feet, spiny skin, calcareous endoskeleton | *Asterias* (Starfish), *Echinus* |
| **Hemichordata** | Bilateral | Coelomate | Worm-like body divided into proboscis, collar, and trunk; stomochord | *Balanoglossus*, *Saccoglossus* |
| **Chordata** | Bilateral | Coelomate | Notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, paired pharyngeal gill slits, post-anal tail | *Ascidia*, *Branchiostoma*, *Labeo*, *Rana*, *Panthera* |

## Common Exam Traps

- **Symmetry Shift in Echinoderms**: Adult echinoderms exhibit **radial symmetry**, whereas their free-swimming larvae display **bilateral symmetry**.
- **Pseudocoelomate distinction**: Aschelminthes (roundworms) are pseudocoelomate because their body cavity is not completely lined by mesoderm. Platyhelminthes, in contrast, are acoelomate.
- **Nerve Cord Position**: Chordates possess a **dorsal, hollow, single** nerve cord, whereas most non-chordates possess a **ventral, solid, usually paired** nerve cord.
- **Levels of Organisation**: Porifera exhibit cellular-level organisation, Cnidaria and Ctenophora exhibit tissue-level organisation, and higher animal phyla exhibit organ or organ-system-level organisation.
- **Diploblastic vs Triploblastic**: Cnidaria and Ctenophora are diploblastic, whereas Platyhelminthes onwards are triploblastic.
- **Coelom vs Haemocoel**: Arthropods are classified as coelomates, but their main body cavity is a haemocoel associated with an open circulatory system.

## Quiz

1. An organism collected from marine waters displays radial symmetry as an adult, possesses an endoskeleton of calcareous ossicles, and moves using a specialised water vascular system. How should its larval stage be characterised regarding symmetry?
  - [ ] Asymmetrical
  - [ ] Radial
  - [x] Bilateral
  - [ ] Biradial
   > Echinoderms exhibit a unique developmental pattern in which adults show secondary radial symmetry, whereas their free-swimming larvae are bilaterally symmetrical.

2. Which of the following diagnostic features correctly distinguishes Phylum Aschelminthes from Phylum Platyhelminthes?
  - [ ] Presence of triploblastic germ layers
  - [ ] Presence of bilateral symmetry
  - [x] Presence of a pseudocoelom and complete digestive tract
  - [ ] Absence of a respiratory system
   > Platyhelminthes are acoelomate and generally possess an incomplete digestive tract (blind-sac plan), whereas Aschelminthes are pseudocoelomate and possess a complete digestive system (tube-within-a-tube plan). Both phyla are triploblastic and bilaterally symmetrical.

3. During an examination of an unknown invertebrate, a biologist notes metameric segmentation, a true coelom lined by mesoderm, paired nephridia, and a closed circulatory system. To which phylum does this animal belong?
  - [ ] Arthropoda
  - [x] Annelida
  - [ ] Mollusca
  - [ ] Hemichordata
   > Annelids are characterised by true metameric segmentation, a coelom, nephridia for excretion and osmoregulation, and a closed circulatory system. Arthropods and most molluscs, in contrast, possess open circulatory systems.
