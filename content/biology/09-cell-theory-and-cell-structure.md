Cells are the structural and functional units of all living organisms. **Cytology** and cell biology focus on understanding how prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell architectures support essential metabolic processes.

## History and Postulates of Cell Theory

The development of cell theory established the fundamental understanding that all living organisms are composed of cells.

| Scientist | Year | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Matthias Schleiden** | 1838 | Concluded that all plants are composed of cells. |
| **Theodor Schwann** | 1839 | Concluded that all animals are made of cells and identified the thin outer boundary (plasma membrane). |
| **Rudolf Virchow** | 1855 | Expanded cell theory by proposing that all living cells arise from pre-existing cells. |

### Modern Cell Theory Postulates

1. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells and cell products.
2. The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells through cell division.

**Omnis cellula e cellula** is the principle proposed by Rudolf Virchow, meaning that every cell originates from a pre-existing cell.

## Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cell Comparison

Living organisms exhibit two fundamental cellular organisation patterns: prokaryotic and eukaryotic.

| Feature | Prokaryotic Cell | Eukaryotic Cell |
|---|---|---|
| **Nuclear Structure** | No true nucleus; genetic material resides in an unenclosed **nucleoid** region. | True nucleus surrounded by a double-membrane **nuclear envelope**. |
| **Genetic Material** | Usually circular, double-stranded $\mathrm{DNA}$; lacking typical eukaryotic histones. | Linear, double-stranded $\mathrm{DNA}$ associated with **histone proteins**. |
| **Membrane-bound Organelles** | Absent (no mitochondria, ER, Golgi apparatus, etc.). | Present (mitochondria, ER, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, etc.). |
| **Ribosomes** | $\mathrm{70S}$ (Subunits: $\mathrm{50S} + \mathrm{30S}$). | $\mathrm{80S}$ cytoplasmic ribosomes (Subunits: $\mathrm{60S} + \mathrm{40S}$). |
| **Cell Wall** | Present in most; composed of **peptidoglycan** (murein) in bacteria. | Present in plants (cellulose) and fungi (chitin); absent in animals. |
| **Cell Size** | Generally smaller, approximately $0.1$–$5\,\mu m$. | Generally larger, approximately $10$–$100\,\mu m$. |
| **Cell Division** | Usually through binary fission. | Through mitosis or meiosis. |

## Bacterial Cell Envelope Modifications

Prokaryotic cells, particularly bacteria, often possess a complex cell envelope consisting of three major layers.

$$
\text{Glycocalyx} \longrightarrow \text{Cell Wall} \longrightarrow \text{Plasma Membrane}
$$

### Components of the Bacterial Cell Envelope

- **Glycocalyx**: Outermost protective covering that may occur as a thick, well-organised capsule or a loose slime layer.
  - **Capsule**: Thick and firmly attached to the cell surface; provides protection against desiccation and host immune defences.
  - **Slime Layer**: Thin, loose, and easily removable covering that helps in surface attachment.
- **Cell Wall**: Rigid layer primarily composed of peptidoglycan in bacteria. It maintains cell shape and protects against osmotic pressure.
- **Plasma Membrane**: Selectively permeable phospholipid bilayer that regulates the movement of substances into and out of the cell.

### Mesosomes

**Mesosomes** were historically described as infoldings of the prokaryotic plasma membrane and were attributed functions such as:

- Cellular respiration.
- Cell wall formation.
- $\mathrm{DNA}$ replication and distribution.

**Important:** Mesosomes were historically described in bacterial cell structure textbooks, but modern research indicates that they are generally preparation artefacts produced during chemical fixation for electron microscopy. They are not recognised as genuine functional organelles in living bacteria.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Histone Association**: Prokaryotic genomic $\mathrm{DNA}$ is traditionally described as *naked*, meaning it is not associated with typical eukaryotic histones. However, bacterial DNA is organised by nucleoid-associated proteins and other architectural proteins.
- **Exceptions to Cell Theory**: Viruses and viroids are acellular entities and do not conform to the conventional cellular organisation of life. Mature mammalian red blood cells lack nuclei but remain cells, having originated from precursor cells.
- **Ribosomal Differences**: Prokaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes are $\mathrm{70S}$, whereas eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes are $\mathrm{80S}$.
- **Ribosomal Subunits**: $\mathrm{70S} = \mathrm{50S} + \mathrm{30S}$ and $\mathrm{80S} = \mathrm{60S} + \mathrm{40S}$. The Svedberg unit is a measure of sedimentation rate, so the values are not arithmetically additive.
- **Cell Wall Composition**: Bacterial cell walls contain peptidoglycan, plant cell walls contain cellulose, and fungal cell walls contain chitin.
- **Mesosomes**: Although historically associated with respiration and other cellular functions, mesosomes are now considered artefacts of conventional chemical fixation.

## Quiz

1. Who modified the original cell theory formulated by Schleiden and Schwann by proposing that all new cells arise from pre-existing cells (*Omnis cellula e cellula*)?

   - [ ] Robert Hooke
   - [ ] Anton van Leeuwenhoek
   - [x] Rudolf Virchow
   - [ ] Robert Brown

   > Rudolf Virchow (1855) refined cell theory by proposing that cells do not form spontaneously; rather, they originate through the division of pre-existing cells (*Omnis cellula e cellula*).

2. A micro-analytical dye test reveals that a newly isolated unicellular microorganism contains double-stranded circular $\mathrm{DNA}$ without typical eukaryotic histones, $\mathrm{70S}$ ribosomes, and mesosomal membrane invaginations. How should this cell be classified?

   - [x] Prokaryote
   - [ ] Eukaryotic Alga
   - [ ] Fungal Spore
   - [ ] Protozoan

   > Circular $\mathrm{DNA}$ lacking typical eukaryotic histones and the presence of $\mathrm{70S}$ ribosomes are characteristic features of prokaryotic organisms, such as bacteria. Mesosomes are a historically described feature but are not considered genuine structures in living bacteria.

3. Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria differ primarily in the structure and composition of which cellular component?

   - [ ] $\mathrm{80S}$ ribosomal subunit assembly
   - [ ] Nuclear membrane permeability
   - [x] Cell wall peptidoglycan thickness and presence of an outer membrane
   - [ ] Cytoplasmic phospholipid arrangement

   > Gram-positive bacteria possess a thick peptidoglycan layer that retains the crystal violet stain. Gram-negative bacteria have a thinner peptidoglycan layer surrounded by an outer membrane containing lipopolysaccharide (LPS), causing them to lose crystal violet during decolourisation and take up the safranin counterstain.
