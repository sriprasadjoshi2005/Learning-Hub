## title: Biotechnology — Applications
level: advanced

Biotechnology applies genetic principles to healthcare, agriculture, industry, and environmental remediation to improve human quality of life.

## Applications in Agriculture

* **Bt Cotton**: Genetically modified crop engineered to resist lepidopteran pests (cotton bollworms).
  * Contains **$cry$ genes** isolated from soil bacterium *Bacillus thuringiensis* ($cryIAc$ and $cryIIAb$ control bollworms; $cryIAb$ controls corn borer).
  * Produces inactive **protoxin** crystals that convert into active toxin in the **alkaline $\text{pH}$** of the insect midgut, solubilizing crystals and creating pores that cause cell lysis.
* **RNA Interference (RNAi)**: Post-transcriptional gene silencing method used to protect tobacco plants against the root-knot nematode *Meloidogyne incognita*. Double-stranded $\mathrm{RNA}$ ($\mathrm{dsRNA}$) triggers degradation of specific nematode $\mathrm{mRNA}$.

## Applications in Medicine

* **Recombinant Human Insulin (Humulin)**: Developed by Eli Lilly (1983).
  * Human insulin consists of two short polypeptide chains: **Chain A** ($21$ amino acids) and **Chain B** ($30$ amino acids) linked by **disulfide bonds**.
  * Synthesized by cloning Chain A and Chain B genes separately into *E. coli* plasmids, producing chains independently and joining them *in vitro* via disulfide linkages.

```
 Pro-insulin: [Chain A] --- [C-Peptide] --- [Chain B]  (Inactive precursor)
                                 |
                                 v Maturation (Removal of C-peptide)
 Active Insulin: [Chain A] =========== [Chain B]  (Joined by disulfide bonds)
```

* **Gene Therapy**: First clinical gene therapy performed in 1990 on a 4-year-old girl with **Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) deficiency** causing $\mathrm{SCID}$ (Severe Combined Immunodeficiency). Functional $\text{ADA cDNA}$ introduced into lymphocytes using a retroviral vector.
* **Molecular Diagnosis**: Early disease detection using $\mathrm{PCR}$, $\mathrm{ELISA}$ (based on antigen-antibody interaction), and single-stranded nucleic acid probes.

## Common Exam Traps

* **Protoxin Activation Condition**: $Bt$ toxin is harmless to the bacterium because it exists as an inactive **protoxin**. It turns toxic only in the **alkaline $\text{pH}$** of the insect gut.
* **C-Peptide Absence**: Mature active human insulin lacks the **C-peptide** present in precursor pro-insulin.

## Quiz

1. Why does the crystalline endotoxin protein produced by *Bacillus thuringiensis* ($Bt$) not kill the bacterium itself, yet effectively kills cotton bollworms upon ingestion?

* \[ \] The bacterium possesses immunity enzymes that neutralize the toxin.
* \[x\] The toxin is produced as an inactive protoxin that requires the alkaline $\text{pH}$ of the insect midgut for activation.
* \[ \] The toxin functions only in cold environmental temperatures.
* \[ \] Insect saliva converts the protein into an antibiotic.

> $Bt$ toxin exists as an inactive protoxin in the bacterium. Ingesting it exposes it to the alkaline $\text{pH}$ of the insect gut, solubilizing and activating the protein to perforate gut epithelial cells.

2. In 1983, Eli Lilly successfully produced recombinant human insulin ("Humulin") in *E. coli*. How did they bypass the problem of processing precursor pro-insulin?

* \[ \] Expressed intact pro-insulin and cleaved C-peptide using human stomach pepsin.
* \[x\] Synthesized $\text{DNA}$ sequences for Chain A and Chain B separately, expressed them in *E. coli*, and joined them using disulfide bonds *in vitro*.
* \[ \] Extracted natural mature insulin directly from transgenic cow milk.
* \[ \] Used retroviruses to assemble the C-peptide directly inside pancreatic cells.

> Eli Lilly synthesized separate gene sequences for insulin chains A and B, expressed them independently in *E. coli*, and combined them *in vitro* with disulfide bonds, avoiding C-peptide processing.
