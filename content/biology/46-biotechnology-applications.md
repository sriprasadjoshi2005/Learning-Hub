Biotechnology applies genetic engineering and molecular biology to healthcare, agriculture, industry, and environmental management. Its applications include the development of pest-resistant crops, production of therapeutic proteins, gene therapy, and advanced molecular diagnostic techniques.

## Applications in Agriculture

### 1. Genetically Modified Crops

Genetically modified (GM) crops contain introduced genes that provide desirable characteristics such as pest resistance, improved nutritional quality, or tolerance to environmental stress.

**Bt Cotton**

Bt cotton is a genetically modified crop engineered to resist certain insect pests, particularly cotton bollworms.

- It contains **cry genes** obtained from the soil bacterium *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt).
- Different cry genes produce toxins effective against different insect groups.
- Important examples include:
  - **cryIAc and cryIIAb**: Provide resistance against cotton bollworms.
  - **cryIAb**: Effective against certain corn borers.

**Mechanism of Bt Toxin Action:**

1. *Bacillus thuringiensis* produces crystalline insecticidal proteins called **Cry proteins**.
2. These proteins accumulate as inactive protoxins in the bacterial cells.
3. When an insect consumes Bt-containing plant tissue, the crystals enter its digestive tract.
4. The alkaline pH of the insect midgut solubilizes the crystals.
5. Gut proteases convert the protoxin into its active toxin form.
6. The active toxin binds to specific receptors on the midgut epithelial cells.
7. It creates pores in the cell membrane, causing cell swelling, lysis, and ultimately insect death.

**Important:** Bt toxins are insect-specific because their activity depends on suitable gut conditions and the presence of compatible receptors.

### 2. RNA Interference (RNAi)

RNA interference is a natural biological mechanism that regulates gene expression by silencing specific genes.

- It is a form of **post-transcriptional gene silencing**.
- Double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) triggers the degradation of complementary messenger RNA (mRNA).
- As a result, the corresponding protein is not produced.

**Application against Root-Knot Nematodes:**

The nematode *Meloidogyne incognita* infects the roots of tobacco plants and reduces agricultural productivity.

Scientists developed a method to protect tobacco plants using RNA interference.

**Mechanism:**

1. A DNA sequence corresponding to an essential nematode gene is introduced into the plant using an appropriate vector.
2. The introduced sequence is arranged so that both sense and antisense RNA strands are produced.
3. These complementary strands pair to form double-stranded RNA (dsRNA).
4. When the nematode feeds on the transgenic plant, it takes up the dsRNA.
5. The dsRNA triggers degradation of the complementary nematode mRNA.
6. The target gene is silenced, preventing the production of its required protein and protecting the plant.

## Applications in Medicine

### 1. Recombinant Human Insulin

Insulin is a peptide hormone produced by pancreatic beta cells that regulates blood glucose levels.

**Structure of Human Insulin:**

Mature human insulin consists of two polypeptide chains:

| Chain | Number of Amino Acids |
|---|---:|
| Chain A | 21 |
| Chain B | 30 |
| **Total** | **51** |

The two chains are connected by **disulfide bonds**.

**Pro-insulin vs Mature Insulin:**

- Insulin is initially synthesized as **preproinsulin**, which is processed into pro-insulin.
- Pro-insulin contains Chain A, Chain B, and an additional connecting segment called the **C-peptide**.
- During maturation, the C-peptide is removed.
- Mature insulin consists of Chains A and B joined by disulfide bonds.

**Production of Recombinant Human Insulin:**

In 1983, Eli Lilly produced recombinant human insulin, marketed as **Humulin**.

The challenge was that ordinary *E. coli* could not naturally process human pro-insulin into mature insulin by removing the C-peptide.

**Production method:**

1. DNA sequences coding for insulin Chain A and Chain B were prepared separately.
2. Each sequence was inserted into a suitable *E. coli* plasmid vector.
3. The recombinant bacteria independently produced Chain A and Chain B.
4. The two polypeptide chains were isolated and purified.
5. They were joined *in vitro* through the formation of disulfide bonds.
6. Functional recombinant human insulin was obtained.

**Important:** The two-chain production method avoided the need for bacterial processing of the C-peptide.

### 2. Gene Therapy

Gene therapy involves introducing, replacing, or modifying genetic material in a patient's cells to treat or prevent a disease.

**First Clinical Gene Therapy:**

- Performed in **1990**.
- Used to treat a four-year-old girl suffering from **Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) deficiency**.
- ADA deficiency can cause **Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID)**.
- The condition severely impairs immune-system function.

**ADA Deficiency:**

- ADA is an enzyme involved in purine metabolism.
- Its deficiency leads to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, particularly deoxyadenosine-derived compounds.
- These metabolites damage developing lymphocytes and impair immune function.

**Gene Therapy Procedure:**

1. Lymphocytes are isolated from the patient's blood.
2. A functional ADA cDNA sequence is introduced into these lymphocytes using a retroviral vector.
3. Genetically modified lymphocytes are cultured.
4. The modified cells are returned to the patient's bloodstream.
5. These cells produce functional ADA enzyme and improve immune function.

**Limitation:**

- Mature lymphocytes have a limited lifespan.
- Therefore, repeated administration of genetically modified lymphocytes may be necessary.
- Introducing a functional ADA gene into suitable bone marrow stem cells could provide a more permanent correction.

### 3. Molecular Diagnosis

Molecular diagnosis uses biological molecules such as DNA, RNA, and proteins to detect diseases accurately, often before noticeable symptoms appear.

Important techniques include:

#### A. Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)

- Amplifies specific DNA sequences from a very small sample.
- Helps detect pathogens and genetic mutations.
- Can identify infections even when the pathogen is present in very small quantities.

#### B. Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)

- Based on specific antigen-antibody interactions.
- Uses enzyme-linked antibodies or antigens to generate a detectable signal.
- Commonly used to detect specific antigens or antibodies in biological samples.

#### C. Nucleic Acid Probes

- Short, labelled, single-stranded DNA or RNA sequences.
- Bind to complementary nucleic acid sequences through hybridization.
- Help identify specific genes, mutations, or pathogen-associated genetic material.

**Molecular Diagnosis Comparison:**

| Technique | Basic Principle | Main Application |
|---|---|---|
| PCR | DNA amplification | Detection of specific DNA sequences |
| ELISA | Antigen-antibody interaction | Detection of antigens or antibodies |
| Nucleic acid probes | Complementary base pairing | Identification of specific DNA or RNA sequences |

## Other Applications of Biotechnology

### 1. Industrial Biotechnology

Biotechnology is used in industries to produce useful substances through microorganisms, enzymes, and genetically engineered organisms.

Examples include:

- Production of antibiotics.
- Manufacture of industrial enzymes.
- Production of fermented foods and beverages.
- Development of biofuels and biodegradable materials.

### 2. Environmental Biotechnology

Biotechnology contributes to environmental protection through biological processes.

Examples include:

- **Bioremediation:** Use of microorganisms to degrade or remove environmental pollutants.
- **Wastewater treatment:** Microorganisms break down organic matter in sewage.
- **Biodegradation:** Biological decomposition of organic waste.
- **Biofertilizers:** Microorganisms improve nutrient availability in soil and support plant growth.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Bt Protoxin Activation**: Bt proteins are produced as inactive protoxins. They become active after crystal solubilization and proteolytic processing in the alkaline insect midgut.
- **Bt Gene Specificity**: Different cry genes target different insect groups. cryIAc and cryIIAb are associated with cotton bollworm resistance, whereas cryIAb targets certain corn borers.
- **Bt Toxin Mechanism**: The active toxin binds to receptors on midgut epithelial cells and forms pores, causing cell lysis.
- **RNAi**: RNA interference is post-transcriptional gene silencing. It causes degradation of complementary mRNA rather than directly destroying the target gene's DNA.
- **RNAi Example**: The tobacco plant was genetically modified to produce dsRNA against the root-knot nematode *Meloidogyne incognita*.
- **Insulin Chain Length**: Chain A contains 21 amino acids, and Chain B contains 30 amino acids. Mature insulin contains 51 amino acids in total.
- **C-Peptide**: Present in pro-insulin but absent from mature insulin.
- **Recombinant Insulin Production**: Eli Lilly produced insulin by expressing Chains A and B separately in *E. coli* and joining them *in vitro* through disulfide bonds.
- **Gene Therapy for ADA Deficiency**: The first clinical gene therapy was performed in 1990 using a functional ADA cDNA sequence introduced into lymphocytes with a retroviral vector.
- **ADA Deficiency and SCID**: ADA deficiency causes an inherited form of severe combined immunodeficiency.
- **PCR vs ELISA**: PCR detects specific nucleic acid sequences through amplification, whereas ELISA detects antigens or antibodies through antigen-antibody interactions.
- **Nucleic Acid Probes**: They identify complementary DNA or RNA sequences through hybridization; they do not amplify DNA like PCR.
