## title: Chromosomal Basis of Inheritance
level: intermediate → advanced

The **Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance**, proposed by Walter Sutton and Theodor Boveri (1902) and experimentally verified by Thomas Hunt Morgan using *Drosophila melanogaster*, states that genes are located at specific loci on chromosomes, and chromosome segregation during meiosis accounts for Mendelian inheritance patterns.

## Linkage and Genetic Recombination

* **Linkage**: The physical association of two or more genes located on the same chromosome. Linked genes tend to be inherited together and do not assort independently.
* **Recombination**: The generation of non-parental gene combinations caused by crossing over during pachytene of Prophase I.
* **Gene Mapping**: Alfred Sturtevant showed that the recombination frequency ($\mathrm{RF}$) between two linked genes is directly proportional to the physical distance separating them on a chromosome.

$$
1\%\ \text{Recombination Frequency} = 1\ \text{map unit } (\text{mu}) = 1\ \text{centimorgan } (\text{cM})
$$

## Sex Determination Systems

| System | Female Genotype | Male Genotype | Organism Examples |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **$\mathrm{XX - XY}$** | Homogametic ($\mathrm{XX}$) | Heterogametic ($\mathrm{XY}$) | Humans, *Drosophila*, most mammals |
| **$\mathrm{XX - XO}$** | Homogametic ($\mathrm{XX}$) | Heterogametic ($\mathrm{XO}$) | Grasshoppers, insects |
| **$\mathrm{ZZ - ZW}$** | Heterogametic ($\mathrm{ZW}$) | Homogametic ($\mathrm{ZZ}$) | Birds, reptiles, butterflies |
| **Haplodiploidy** | Diploid ($2n$) | Haploid ($n$) | Honeybees, ants, wasps |

## Human Pedigree Analysis Symbols and Inheritance Patterns

Pedigree charts trace specific phenotypic traits across generations:

* **Autosomal Dominant**: Trait appears in every generation; affected individuals have at least one affected parent (e.g., Myotonic dystrophy).
* **Autosomal Recessive**: Trait often skips generations; affected individuals can be born to unaffected carrier parents (e.g., Sickle cell anemia, Cystic fibrosis).
* **X-Linked Recessive**: Affects males much more frequently than females; passed from carrier mothers to sons via criss-cross inheritance (e.g., Hemophilia, Red-green color blindness).

## Common Exam Traps

* **Maximum Recombination Frequency**: The maximum observable recombination frequency between two linked genes cannot exceed **$50\%$**, which matches the independent assortment ratio of unlinked genes.
* **Heterogametic Sex Differences**: In humans, **males** are heterogametic ($\mathrm{XY}$), whereas in birds, **females** are heterogametic ($\mathrm{ZW}$).

## Quiz

1. Two genes, $A$ and $B$, are located on the same chromosome. A dihybrid test cross yields $84\%$ parental-type offspring and $16\%$ recombinant-type offspring. What is the map distance between gene $A$ and gene $B$?

* \[ \] $84\ \text{cM}$
* \[ \] $42\ \text{cM}$
* \[x\] $16\ \text{cM}$
* \[ \] $8\ \text{cM}$

> Recombination frequency ($\text{RF}$) directly converts to genetic map distance: $1\%\ \text{recombination} = 1\ \text{centimorgan (cM)}$. An $\text{RF}$ of $16\%$ equals a distance of $16\ \text{cM}$.

2. A human male with red-green color blindness ($X^c Y$) marries a female with normal vision whose father was color-blind ($X^C X^c$). What is the probability that their first biological son will be color-blind?

* \[ \] $0\%$
* \[ \] $25\%$
* \[x\] $50\%$
* \[ \] $100\%$

> The carrier mother ($X^C X^c$) passes either the normal $X^C$ allele or the color-blind $X^c$ allele to her male offspring with equal probability ($50\%$). The father contributes his $Y$ chromosome to sons, so the chance of a son inheriting color blindness is $50\%$.

3. In domestic poultry (birds), which chromosome pair composition determines female biological sex?

* \[ \] $\text{XX}$
* \[ \] $\text{XY}$
* \[x\] $\text{ZW}$
* \[ \] $\text{ZZ}$

> Birds follow the $\text{ZZ-ZW}$ sex determination system, where females are heterogametic ($\text{ZW}$) and males are homogametic ($\text{ZZ}$).
