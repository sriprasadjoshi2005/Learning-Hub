## title: Reproductive Health
level: intermediate → advanced

Reproductive health refers to total well-being in all aspects of reproduction—physical, emotional, behavioral, and social. 

## Contraceptive Methods

Contraceptive methods prevent unwanted pregnancies and are categorized by mechanism:

| Method Category | Mechanism of Action | Examples |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Natural / Traditional** | Periodic abstinence, coitus interruptus, lactational amenorrhea | Avoidance of coitus during fertility window (Days 10-17) |
| **Barrier Methods** | Prevent physical contact of sperm and ovum | Condoms, Diaphragms, Cervical caps |
| **Intrauterine Devices (IUDs)** | **Non-medicated**: Increase phagocytosis of sperm.<br>**Copper-releasing**: $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ suppresses sperm motility.<br>**Hormone-releasing**: Makes cervix hostile to sperm | Non-medicated: Lippes loop.<br>Copper: $\text{CuT}$, $\text{Cu7}$, Multiload 375.<br>Hormone: Progestasert, $\text{LNG-20}$ |
| **Oral Contraceptives** | Inhibit ovulation and implantation; alter cervical mucus | Combined pills (Estrogen + Progestogen), "Saheli" (Non-steroidal, once-a-week pill) |
| **Surgical (Sterilization)** | Block gamete transport; permanent method | Vasectomy (Males: cut/tie vas deferens), Tubectomy (Females: cut/tie Fallopian tubes) |

## Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART)

Used to assist infertile couples when medical treatments are insufficient:

* **In-Vitro Fertilisation (IVF) & Test Tube Baby Programme**:
  * **ZIFT (Zygote Intra-Fallopian Transfer)**: Zygote or early embryo up to $8\text{ blastomeres}$ transferred into Fallopian tube.
  * **IUT (Intra-Uterine Transfer)**: Embryo with more than $8\text{ blastomeres}$ transferred directly into the uterus.
* **GIFT (Gamete Intra-Fallopian Transfer)**: Transfer of an unfertilized ovum collected from a donor into the Fallopian tube of another female.
* **ICSI (Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection)**: Single sperm directly injected into the cytoplasm of an ovum *in vitro*.
* **IUI (Intrauterine Insemination)**: Semen collected from husband/donor artificially introduced into the vagina or uterus.

## Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

Diseases transmitted through sexual intercourse (e.g., Gonorrhea, Syphilis, Genital herpes, Chlamydiasis, Hepatitis-B, $\mathrm{HIV}$).
> *Note*: **Hepatitis-B**, **Genital Herpes**, and **$\mathrm{HIV}$ infections** are completely non-curable even if detected early, whereas others are curable if diagnosed promptly.

## Common Exam Traps

* **ZIFT vs IUT Threshold**: Embryos up to $8\text{ blastomeres}$ are transferred via **ZIFT** (Fallopian tube); embryos with $>8\text{ blastomeres}$ are transferred via **IUT** (Uterus).
* **"Saheli" Composition**: Saheli is a unique **non-steroidal** oral contraceptive developed by CDRI (Central Drug Research Institute, Lucknow) taken once a week with minimal side effects.

## Quiz

1. A couple undergoing assisted reproduction requires embryo transfer. If an *in vitro* fertilized embryo has developed to the 16-blastomere stage, into which anatomical site should it be transferred for successful implantation?

* \[ \] Fallopian tube via ZIFT
* \[x\] Uterus via IUT
* \[ \] Cervical canal
* \[ \] Ovary via GIFT

> Embryos up to 8 blastomeres are transferred into the Fallopian tube (ZIFT). Embryos beyond 8 blastomeres (such as 16-blastomere morulae) are transferred into the uterus via Intra-Uterine Transfer (IUT).

2. Which mechanism explains how copper-releasing Intrauterine Devices (IUDs) like CuT prevent conception inside the female reproductive tract?

* \[ \] Direct inhibition of gonadotropin secretion from the anterior pituitary
* \[x\] Release of free $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions that suppress sperm motility and fertilizing capacity
* \[ \] Surgical blockage of ovum release from Graafian follicles
* \[ \] Destruction of the uterine endometrial lining

> Copper ions ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$) released from CuT suppress sperm motility, reducing their swimming speed and fertilizing capacity before reaching the ampulla.

3. Which of the following Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) remains incurable even when detected early during clinical diagnosis?

* \[ \] Syphilis
* \[ \] Gonorrhea
* \[x\] Genital Herpes
* \[ \] Chlamydiasis

> While most STIs caused by bacteria (Syphilis, Gonorrhea, Chlamydia) are completely curable with antibiotics, viral STIs like Genital Herpes, Hepatitis-B, and HIV are incurable.
