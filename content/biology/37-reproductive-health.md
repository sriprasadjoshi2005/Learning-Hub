Reproductive health refers to total well-being in all aspects of reproduction, including **physical, emotional, behavioral, and social well-being**. It involves responsible reproductive behavior, prevention of sexually transmitted infections, access to contraception, and the ability to have safe and healthy reproductive lives.

## Contraceptive Methods

Contraceptive methods prevent unwanted pregnancies. They are classified according to their mechanisms of action.

### 1. Natural and Traditional Methods

These methods avoid the use of medicines or devices and depend on behavioral practices.

| Method | Mechanism |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Periodic Abstinence** | Avoiding sexual intercourse during the fertile period of the menstrual cycle, approximately Days 10–17 in a typical 28-day cycle. |
| **Coitus Interruptus** | Withdrawal of the penis from the vagina before ejaculation. |
| **Lactational Amenorrhea** | Suppression of ovulation during intense breastfeeding, particularly during the first six months after childbirth when menstruation has not resumed. |

**Important:** Natural methods have comparatively higher failure rates because they depend heavily on correct timing and consistent practice.

### 2. Barrier Methods

Barrier methods physically prevent sperm from reaching the ovum.

| Method | Mechanism / Function |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Condoms** | Thin rubber or latex coverings used over the penis or inside the vagina to prevent sperm entry. |
| **Diaphragms** | Flexible devices placed over the cervix to block sperm entry. |
| **Cervical Caps** | Small devices that cover the cervix and prevent sperm from entering the uterus. |
| **Vaginal Vaults** | Devices placed inside the vagina to cover the cervix. |

- Condoms also provide significant protection against **sexually transmitted infections (STIs)**, including HIV.
- Diaphragms, cervical caps, and vaults are often used with spermicidal creams, jellies, or foams.

### 3. Intrauterine Devices (IUDs)

IUDs are contraceptive devices inserted into the uterus by trained healthcare professionals.

| IUD Category | Mechanism of Action | Examples |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Non-medicated IUDs** | Increase phagocytosis of sperms within the uterus. | Lippes loop |
| **Copper-releasing IUDs** | Release copper ions that suppress sperm motility and fertilizing capacity. | CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375 |
| **Hormone-releasing IUDs** | Make the uterus unsuitable for implantation and the cervix hostile to sperm. | Progestasert, LNG-20 |

**Key distinctions:**
- Non-medicated IUDs primarily increase sperm phagocytosis.
- Copper-releasing IUDs reduce sperm motility and fertilizing capacity.
- Hormone-releasing IUDs alter the uterine environment and cervical mucus.

### 4. Oral Contraceptive Pills

Oral contraceptives contain hormones or other compounds that prevent pregnancy.

**Combined Oral Pills:**
- Contain combinations of **estrogen and progestogen**.
- Suppress ovulation by inhibiting the release of pituitary gonadotropins.
- Alter cervical mucus, making sperm penetration more difficult.
- Make the endometrium less suitable for implantation.

**Saheli:**
- A non-steroidal oral contraceptive developed by the Central Drug Research Institute (CDRI), Lucknow.
- Contains **centchroman (ormeloxifene)**.
- Taken once a week after an initial dosing schedule.
- Has comparatively fewer side effects than many conventional hormonal pills.

### 5. Emergency Contraception

Emergency contraceptive methods are used after unprotected intercourse or contraceptive failure.

- Emergency contraceptive pills can reduce the possibility of pregnancy when taken within the recommended time window.
- A copper-releasing IUD can also be used as emergency contraception when inserted within the appropriate time window.
- These methods should not be treated as substitutes for regular contraception.

### 6. Surgical Methods (Sterilization)

Sterilization procedures are intended as permanent contraceptive methods. They prevent the transport of gametes.

| Procedure | Performed In | Mechanism |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Vasectomy** | Males | A small portion of each vas deferens is cut and tied or sealed, preventing sperm from entering the semen. |
| **Tubectomy** | Females | A small portion of each Fallopian tube is cut and tied or sealed, preventing the ovum and sperm from meeting. |

**Important:** Vasectomy does not stop testosterone production or ejaculation. Tubectomy does not stop ovulation or menstruation.

## Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART)

Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART) are medical techniques used to help individuals or couples experiencing infertility achieve pregnancy.

### 1. In-Vitro Fertilisation (IVF)

- Fertilization occurs outside the female body under laboratory conditions.
- The resulting embryo is cultured for an appropriate period before being transferred into the female reproductive tract.
- IVF is commonly called the **test-tube baby technique**.

### 2. ZIFT and IUT

The site of embryo transfer depends on the developmental stage of the embryo.

| Technique | Full Form | Developmental Stage | Transfer Site |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **ZIFT** | Zygote Intra-Fallopian Transfer | Zygote or early embryo up to 8 blastomeres | Fallopian tube |
| **IUT** | Intra-Uterine Transfer | Embryo with more than 8 blastomeres | Uterus |

**Developmental sequence:**

    Fertilization
         |
       Zygote
         |
     Cleavage
         |
    Early Embryo
    (Up to 8 blastomeres)
         |
        ZIFT
         |
    Fallopian Tube

    Embryo with more than 8 blastomeres
         |
        IUT
         |
        Uterus

### 3. GIFT (Gamete Intra-Fallopian Transfer)

- An ovum collected from a donor is transferred into the Fallopian tube of another female who cannot produce ova but can provide a suitable environment for fertilization and development.
- Fertilization occurs inside the female reproductive tract.

### 4. ICSI (Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection)

- A single sperm is directly injected into the cytoplasm of an ovum under laboratory conditions.
- It is particularly useful in cases of certain forms of male-factor infertility.
- Fertilization and subsequent embryo development occur under controlled laboratory conditions.

### 5. IUI (Intrauterine Insemination)

- Semen collected from the husband or a donor is introduced artificially into the uterus using appropriate medical techniques.
- It helps overcome certain problems associated with sperm delivery.
- Insemination into the vagina is also possible in some artificial insemination procedures, but it is not technically the same as intrauterine insemination.

### Comparison of ART Techniques

| Technique | What Is Transferred or Injected? | Where Does It Occur? |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **IVF** | Fertilization outside the body | Laboratory |
| **ZIFT** | Zygote or embryo up to 8 blastomeres | Fallopian tube |
| **IUT** | Embryo beyond 8 blastomeres | Uterus |
| **GIFT** | Unfertilized ovum | Fallopian tube |
| **ICSI** | Single sperm injected into an ovum | Laboratory |
| **IUI** | Semen | Uterus |

## Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

Sexually Transmitted Infections are infections primarily transmitted through sexual contact. Some can also spread through infected blood, shared needles, or from an infected mother to her child.

### Common STIs

| Infection | Causative Organism | Type |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Gonorrhea** | *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* | Bacterial |
| **Syphilis** | *Treponema pallidum* | Bacterial |
| **Chlamydiasis** | *Chlamydia trachomatis* | Bacterial |
| **Genital Herpes** | Herpes simplex virus (HSV) | Viral |
| **Hepatitis-B** | Hepatitis-B virus (HBV) | Viral |
| **HIV/AIDS** | Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) | Viral |
| **Genital Warts** | Human papillomavirus (HPV) | Viral |
| **Trichomoniasis** | *Trichomonas vaginalis* | Protozoan |

### Symptoms and Complications

Common symptoms may include:

- Genital itching, irritation, or abnormal discharge.
- Pain or burning sensation during urination.
- Genital sores, blisters, or ulcers.
- Pain in the lower abdomen.
- Swelling or discomfort in the genital region.

**Important:** Many STIs may remain asymptomatic, particularly during their early stages. Therefore, absence of symptoms does not necessarily indicate absence of infection.

Untreated STIs can lead to:
- Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID).
- Infertility and ectopic pregnancy.
- Pregnancy and childbirth complications.
- Increased susceptibility to other infections.

### Curability of STIs

| Category | Examples | Treatment |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Generally Curable Bacterial STIs** | Gonorrhea, Syphilis, Chlamydiasis | Appropriate antibiotics |
| **Viral STIs** | HIV, Genital Herpes, Hepatitis-B, HPV | Treatment can control infection or complications, but does not generally eliminate the virus completely. |

**Important distinctions:**
- HIV can be controlled effectively using antiretroviral therapy (ART), but there is currently no generally available cure.
- Genital herpes can be managed using antiviral medicines, but the virus remains in the body.
- Chronic Hepatitis-B can be controlled with antiviral treatment, although complete viral eradication is uncommon.
- HPV infections often clear naturally, but some persistent infections can cause genital warts or cancers.

### Prevention of STIs

- Use condoms consistently and correctly.
- Avoid multiple or unprotected sexual partners.
- Seek early medical diagnosis and treatment.
- Avoid sharing needles or contaminated injection equipment.
- Get appropriate vaccinations, particularly against **Hepatitis-B and HPV**.
- Ensure sexual partners are tested and treated when necessary.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Natural Methods:** Periodic abstinence generally involves avoiding intercourse during the fertile period, approximately Days 10–17 in a typical 28-day cycle. This is not a universally fixed fertility window.
- **Lactational Amenorrhea:** Its contraceptive effect is most reliable during the first six months after childbirth when the mother is exclusively or nearly exclusively breastfeeding and menstruation has not resumed.
- **Copper IUDs:** Copper ions suppress sperm motility and fertilizing capacity; they do not primarily prevent ovulation.
- **Hormonal IUDs:** They alter cervical mucus and the uterine environment.
- **Saheli:** It is a non-steroidal contraceptive containing centchroman (ormeloxifene), not a conventional estrogen-progestogen combination.
- **ZIFT vs IUT:** Embryos up to 8 blastomeres are transferred into the Fallopian tube through ZIFT; embryos with more than 8 blastomeres are transferred into the uterus through IUT.
- **GIFT:** Transfers an unfertilized ovum, not a fertilized embryo.
- **ICSI:** Involves direct injection of a single sperm into the ovum's cytoplasm.
- **Vasectomy:** Blocks sperm transport but does not stop testosterone secretion or ejaculation.
- **Tubectomy:** Blocks the Fallopian tubes but does not stop ovarian hormone production or menstruation.
- **STIs:** Many bacterial STIs are curable with appropriate antibiotics, whereas viral infections such as HIV and genital herpes are generally not completely curable.
- **Asymptomatic Infections:** An infected individual may transmit an STI without showing obvious symptoms.

## Quiz

1. Which of the following contraceptive methods primarily prevents pregnancy by increasing the phagocytosis of sperms within the uterus?

- [ ] CuT
- [ ] LNG-20
- [x] Lippes loop
- [ ] Saheli

> Lippes loop is a non-medicated IUD that increases sperm phagocytosis in the uterus. Copper-releasing IUDs suppress sperm motility, while hormone-releasing IUDs alter the cervical mucus and uterine environment.

2. A couple undergoing assisted reproduction has an embryo that has developed to the 16-blastomere stage following in-vitro fertilization. Which technique and transfer site are appropriate?

- [ ] ZIFT into the Fallopian tube
- [x] IUT into the uterus
- [ ] GIFT into the Fallopian tube
- [ ] ICSI into the uterus

> Embryos with more than 8 blastomeres are transferred directly into the uterus through IUT. ZIFT is used for a zygote or an early embryo containing up to 8 blastomeres.

3. Which statement correctly describes the contraceptive action of a copper-releasing IUD such as CuT?

- [ ] It permanently inhibits the secretion of FSH and LH.
- [x] It releases copper ions that suppress sperm motility and fertilizing capacity.
- [ ] It prevents the release of ova from the ovaries.
- [ ] It surgically blocks the Fallopian tubes.

> Copper-releasing IUDs release Cu²⁺ ions that reduce sperm motility and fertilizing capacity, thereby decreasing the possibility of fertilization.

4. Which of the following assisted reproductive techniques involves transferring an unfertilized ovum from a donor into the Fallopian tube of another female?

- [ ] ZIFT
- [ ] IUT
- [x] GIFT
- [ ] ICSI

> GIFT stands for Gamete Intra-Fallopian Transfer. It involves transferring an unfertilized ovum into the Fallopian tube, where fertilization can occur naturally inside the female reproductive tract.

5. Which of the following statements about sexually transmitted infections is correct?

- [ ] All STIs are completely curable with antibiotics.
- [ ] HIV can be completely eliminated using antiretroviral therapy.
- [x] Many bacterial STIs are curable, whereas viral STIs such as genital herpes and HIV generally cannot be completely cured.
- [ ] An individual without STI symptoms cannot transmit an infection.

> Bacterial infections such as gonorrhea, syphilis, and chlamydiasis are generally curable with appropriate antibiotics. Viral infections such as HIV and genital herpes can be managed with treatment but are not generally eliminated completely. Some STIs can also be transmitted by individuals who have no symptoms.
