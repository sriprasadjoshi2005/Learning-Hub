**Environmental pollution** refers to undesirable changes in the physical, chemical, or biological characteristics of air, water, or land that negatively affect living organisms and ecosystems.

**Sustainability** involves managing natural resources responsibly to meet present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

## Air Pollution and Control Measures

Air pollution occurs when harmful gases, particulate matter, and other pollutants accumulate in the atmosphere at concentrations that adversely affect living organisms and the environment.

### Major Air Pollutants

| Pollutant | Major Sources | Harmful Effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbon Monoxide (CO)** | Incomplete combustion of fuels | Reduces oxygen transport by binding to haemoglobin. |
| **Sulphur Dioxide (SO₂)** | Burning coal and petroleum | Causes respiratory problems and contributes to acid rain. |
| **Nitrogen Oxides (NOₓ)** | Automobile exhaust and power plants | Contribute to photochemical smog and acid rain. |
| **Particulate Matter (PM)** | Industrial emissions, vehicles, and construction | Causes respiratory and cardiovascular problems. |
| **Hydrocarbons** | Incomplete combustion and fuel evaporation | Some contribute to photochemical smog and are carcinogenic. |

### Air Pollution Control Devices

#### 1. Electrostatic Precipitator

An electrostatic precipitator removes particulate matter, such as dust and smoke particles, from industrial exhaust.

**Working Principle:**

- Exhaust gases pass through a region containing high-voltage electrode wires.
- Dust particles acquire an electrical charge.
- Charged particles are attracted towards oppositely charged collecting plates.
- Accumulated particles are removed from the plates.

**Efficiency:** Electrostatic precipitators can remove over 99% of particulate matter under suitable operating conditions.

#### 2. Scrubber

A scrubber removes harmful gaseous pollutants from industrial exhaust.

**Working Principle:**

- Polluted exhaust gases pass through a spray of water or a suitable chemical solution.
- Soluble gases and other pollutants are absorbed or chemically neutralised.
- The treated gases are released with a reduced pollutant concentration.

**Example:** Lime or limestone-based scrubbers are used to remove sulphur dioxide (SO₂) from industrial emissions.

#### 3. Catalytic Converter

Catalytic converters are fitted into automobile exhaust systems to reduce harmful emissions.

They contain expensive catalytic metals such as:

- **Platinum (Pt)**
- **Palladium (Pd)**
- **Rhodium (Rh)**

These catalysts promote chemical reactions that convert harmful exhaust gases into less harmful substances.

**Major Reactions:**

- Carbon monoxide is oxidised into carbon dioxide:

\[
2CO+O_2\rightarrow 2CO_2
\]

- Unburnt hydrocarbons are oxidised into carbon dioxide and water:

\[
C_xH_y+\left(x+\frac{y}{4}\right)O_2
\rightarrow xCO_2+\frac{y}{2}H_2O
\]

- Nitrogen oxides are reduced to nitrogen:

\[
2NO+2CO\rightarrow N_2+2CO_2
\]

**Important:** Vehicles equipped with catalytic converters must use **unleaded petrol** because lead can poison and deactivate the catalytic metals.

## Water Pollution and Control

Water pollution occurs when harmful substances enter water bodies and alter their physical, chemical, or biological properties.

Major sources include:

- Domestic sewage.
- Industrial effluents.
- Agricultural runoff containing fertilisers and pesticides.
- Oil spills and chemical waste.
- Thermal discharges from industries and power plants.

### Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)

**Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)** is the amount of dissolved oxygen required by aerobic microorganisms to decompose biodegradable organic matter present in a water sample under specified conditions.

BOD is commonly measured over five days at \(20^\circ\mathrm{C}\), expressed as \(\mathrm{mg/L}\) of oxygen.

**Interpretation:**

- **High BOD:** Indicates a high concentration of biodegradable organic matter and generally greater organic pollution.
- **Low BOD:** Indicates a lower concentration of biodegradable organic matter.

When untreated sewage enters a water body, microorganisms multiply and consume dissolved oxygen while decomposing organic matter.

**Important:** High BOD generally corresponds to low dissolved oxygen (DO), potentially causing the death of aquatic organisms.

### Eutrophication

Eutrophication is the enrichment of a water body with nutrients, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus compounds.

It promotes excessive growth of algae and aquatic plants.

**Types:**

- **Natural Eutrophication:** A gradual natural process through which lakes accumulate nutrients and sediments over long periods.
- **Cultural Eutrophication:** Accelerated eutrophication caused by human activities, particularly sewage discharge and agricultural fertiliser runoff.

**Process of Cultural Eutrophication:**

1. Fertilisers and sewage introduce excess nitrogen and phosphorus into water.
2. Rapid algal growth produces an algal bloom.
3. Algae eventually die and become organic matter.
4. Decomposers break down the dead organic matter and consume dissolved oxygen.
5. Dissolved oxygen levels decrease, potentially causing fish mortality.

**Algal Bloom:** Rapid and excessive growth of algae in nutrient-rich water.

**Dead Zones:** Areas of water with extremely low dissolved oxygen, making survival difficult for many aquatic organisms.

### Biomagnification

Biomagnification is the progressive increase in the concentration of non-biodegradable toxic substances at successive trophic levels of a food chain.

These substances accumulate in organisms because they are not easily broken down or eliminated.

Examples include:

- DDT (a persistent pesticide).
- Mercury and certain mercury compounds.

**Example: DDT in an Aquatic Food Chain**

The concentration of DDT generally increases at higher trophic levels, with top consumers experiencing the greatest accumulation.

**Important:** Biomagnification is different from bioaccumulation.

- **Bioaccumulation:** Build-up of a substance within an individual organism over time.
- **Biomagnification:** Increase in the concentration of a substance across successive trophic levels.

## Global Environmental Phenomena

Human activities have altered atmospheric composition and contributed to major global environmental problems.

### 1. Greenhouse Effect and Global Warming

The **greenhouse effect** is the natural process through which certain atmospheric gases absorb and re-emit outgoing infrared radiation, warming the Earth's surface and lower atmosphere.

Major greenhouse gases include:

- Carbon dioxide (\(\mathrm{CO_2}\)).
- Methane (\(\mathrm{CH_4}\)).
- Nitrous oxide (\(\mathrm{N_2O}\)).
- Water vapour (\(\mathrm{H_2O}\)).
- Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and other industrial gases.

**Global Warming:** The long-term increase in Earth's average surface temperature, primarily driven by the enhanced greenhouse effect caused by human activities.

Major human activities contributing to global warming include:

- Burning fossil fuels.
- Deforestation.
- Industrial activities.
- Agriculture and livestock production.
- Release of industrial greenhouse gases.

**Consequences of Global Warming:**

- Melting glaciers and polar ice.
- Rising sea levels.
- Changes in rainfall patterns.
- Increased frequency and intensity of certain extreme weather events.
- Shifts in species distribution and ecosystem functioning.

### 2. Ozone Layer Depletion

The **ozone layer** is a region of the stratosphere containing relatively high concentrations of ozone (\(\mathrm{O_3}\)).

It protects living organisms by absorbing most harmful ultraviolet radiation, particularly UV-B.

**Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** are major ozone-depleting substances.

**Mechanism of Ozone Depletion:**

1. CFCs rise into the stratosphere.
2. Ultraviolet radiation breaks down CFC molecules.
3. Reactive chlorine atoms are released.
4. Chlorine atoms catalytically destroy ozone molecules.

Simplified reactions:

\[
CF_2Cl_2\xrightarrow{UV}CF_2Cl^\bullet+Cl^\bullet
\]

\[
Cl^\bullet+O_3\rightarrow ClO^\bullet+O_2
\]

\[
ClO^\bullet+O\rightarrow Cl^\bullet+O_2
\]

**Net Reaction:**

\[
O_3+O\rightarrow 2O_2
\]

The chlorine atom is regenerated and can participate in further ozone-destruction cycles.

### Ozone Hole

The term **ozone hole** refers to a severe seasonal thinning of the stratospheric ozone layer, particularly over Antarctica.

Polar stratospheric clouds and returning sunlight during Antarctic spring contribute to rapid ozone destruction through chlorine-based reactions.

### Dobson Unit (DU)

The thickness or total column amount of atmospheric ozone is commonly expressed in **Dobson Units (DU)**.

One Dobson Unit corresponds to a layer of pure ozone approximately \(0.01\text{ mm}\) thick at standard temperature and pressure.

### Montreal Protocol (1987)

The **Montreal Protocol** was adopted in 1987 to control and phase out the production and consumption of ozone-depleting substances, including CFCs.

It is an important international agreement for protecting the stratospheric ozone layer.

## Sustainable Development and Environmental Management

Sustainable development aims to balance economic growth, social well-being, and environmental protection.

### Major Environmental Management Practices

- **Reduce:** Minimise the consumption of resources and production of waste.
- **Reuse:** Use products multiple times instead of discarding them.
- **Recycle:** Convert waste materials into useful products.
- **Afforestation:** Establish forests by planting trees in areas that previously lacked forest cover.
- **Conservation:** Protect natural resources and biodiversity.
- **Renewable Energy:** Use energy sources such as solar, wind, and hydropower to reduce dependence on fossil fuels.

### Integrated Wastewater Treatment

Wastewater treatment reduces pollutants before sewage is released into natural water bodies.

Major stages include:

1. **Primary Treatment:** Physical removal of floating and suspended solids through filtration, screening, and sedimentation.
2. **Secondary Treatment:** Biological decomposition of organic matter by microorganisms, reducing BOD.
3. **Tertiary Treatment:** Additional treatment to remove nutrients, pathogens, and remaining contaminants when required.

**Activated Sludge Process:**

- Primary-treated sewage enters an aeration tank.
- Air is pumped into the tank to support aerobic microbial growth.
- Microorganisms form flocs and consume organic matter.
- The mixture enters a settling tank.
- Activated sludge settles at the bottom.
- A portion is returned to the aeration tank as inoculum, while the remaining sludge undergoes further treatment.

**Important:** Proper wastewater treatment reduces BOD and protects aquatic ecosystems from oxygen depletion.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Electrostatic Precipitator:** Removes particulate matter, not gaseous pollutants such as SO₂.
- **Scrubber:** Removes gaseous pollutants such as SO₂ using water or suitable chemical solutions.
- **Catalytic Converter:** Converts CO, unburnt hydrocarbons, and nitrogen oxides into less harmful substances.
- **Unleaded Petrol:** Essential for catalytic converters because lead poisons the catalytic metals.
- **BOD:** High BOD indicates greater biodegradable organic pollution, not better water quality.
- **BOD vs DO:** High organic pollution generally increases BOD and decreases dissolved oxygen.
- **Eutrophication:** Caused by excessive nutrient enrichment, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus.
- **Biomagnification:** Concentration of persistent toxic substances increases at successive trophic levels.
- **Bioaccumulation vs Biomagnification:** Bioaccumulation occurs within an organism; biomagnification occurs across trophic levels.
- **Good vs Bad Ozone:** Stratospheric ozone is protective, while tropospheric ozone is an air pollutant.
- **Ozone Depletion:** CFCs release reactive chlorine atoms that catalytically destroy stratospheric ozone.
- **Dobson Unit:** Used to measure total column atmospheric ozone.
- **Montreal Protocol:** Adopted in 1987 to control ozone-depleting substances; it is not the Kyoto Protocol.
- **Wastewater Treatment:** Secondary treatment is primarily biological and significantly reduces BOD.
