Evolutionary biology studies the origin, diversity, and historical development of living organisms. Earth formed approximately **4.5 billion years ago (bya)**, while the earliest evidence of life dates back to approximately 3.5–3.8 billion years ago. The origin of life and the subsequent evolution of organisms explain how the diversity of modern life developed over geological time.

## Theories of Origin of Life

The origin of life has been explained through several historical theories and scientific hypotheses.

### 1. Theory of Special Creation

- Proposes that all living organisms were created by a supernatural power.
- It is a historical and religious explanation rather than a scientifically testable evolutionary theory.

### 2. Theory of Panspermia

- Suggests that life, or its precursor forms, may have reached Earth from outer space.
- It proposes an extraterrestrial origin for the material associated with life but does not explain how life originally arose.

### 3. Chemical Evolution (Oparin-Haldane Hypothesis)

The Oparin-Haldane hypothesis proposes that life originated from non-living organic molecules through a gradual process of chemical evolution.

**Major Propositions:**

- Early Earth had a hot environment with intense volcanic activity and frequent electrical storms.
- The primitive atmosphere was considered reducing, containing gases such as methane (CH₄), ammonia (NH₃), hydrogen (H₂), and water vapour (H₂O).
- Free molecular oxygen (O₂) was absent or extremely limited.
- Energy from ultraviolet radiation, lightning, and volcanic activity facilitated chemical reactions.
- Simple inorganic substances gradually formed organic molecules.
- Organic molecules accumulated and underwent further chemical interactions, eventually leading to primitive self-replicating systems and early cellular life.

**Proposed sequence:**

    Primitive Earth
          |
    Reducing Atmosphere
          |
    Simple Inorganic Molecules
          |
    Simple Organic Molecules
          |
    Complex Organic Molecules
          |
    Primitive Molecular Systems
          |
    First Cellular Life Forms

**Important:** Chemical evolution proposes that organic molecules arose from non-living substances before the emergence of the first living organisms. This is different from biogenesis, which states that existing living organisms arise from pre-existing life.

## Miller-Urey Experiment (1953)

Stanley Miller and Harold Urey experimentally tested aspects of the chemical evolution hypothesis in 1953.

### Experimental Setup

- A closed laboratory apparatus was constructed to simulate certain proposed conditions of primitive Earth.
- It contained methane (CH₄), ammonia (NH₃), hydrogen (H₂), and water vapour.
- Water was heated to produce vapour, representing the primitive ocean.
- Electric sparks simulated lightning as an energy source.
- A condenser cooled the gases, allowing water and reaction products to circulate through the apparatus.

### Major Findings

- Organic compounds were produced from simple inorganic starting materials.
- The products included amino acids such as glycine and alanine.
- The experiment demonstrated that biologically important organic molecules could form abiotically under certain simulated conditions.

**Limitations:**
- The experiment did not produce living organisms.
- It did not demonstrate the complete origin of life.
- Its atmospheric composition represented one proposed model of primitive Earth, and the exact composition of the early atmosphere remains scientifically debated.

## Evidence for Biological Evolution

Evolution is supported by evidence from comparative anatomy, embryology, fossils, molecular biology, and biogeography.

## 1. Anatomical Evidence: Homologous and Analogous Structures

Comparative anatomy reveals similarities and differences in the body structures of organisms.

### Homologous Structures

Homologous structures have a common evolutionary origin and similar basic anatomical organization but perform different functions.

**Examples:**
- Forelimbs of humans, cheetahs, whales, and bats.
- Thorn of *Bougainvillea* and tendril of *Cucurbita*.

**Evolutionary Significance:**
- Indicate common ancestry.
- Demonstrate **divergent evolution**, in which related organisms develop different adaptations from a common ancestral structure.

### Analogous Structures

Analogous structures have different evolutionary origins and anatomical arrangements but perform similar functions.

**Examples:**
- Wings of butterflies and birds.
- Sweet potato (modified root) and Irish potato (modified stem).

**Evolutionary Significance:**
- Do not necessarily indicate close common ancestry.
- Demonstrate **convergent evolution**, in which unrelated organisms independently develop similar functional adaptations under similar selective pressures.

### Comparison

| Feature | Homologous Structures | Analogous Structures |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Origin | Same evolutionary origin | Different evolutionary origins |
| Basic Anatomy | Similar underlying structure | Different underlying structure |
| Function | May differ | Usually similar |
| Evolutionary Pattern | Divergent evolution | Convergent evolution |
| Example | Human arm and whale flipper | Bird wing and butterfly wing |

## 2. Adaptive Radiation

Adaptive radiation is the evolutionary process in which organisms originating from a common ancestral group diversify into different species adapted to different ecological niches.

### Darwin's Finches

- Charles Darwin observed several species of finches in the Galapagos Islands.
- These finches are believed to have evolved from a common ancestral species.
- Different populations developed distinct beak shapes and sizes.
- Their beaks became adapted to different food sources, such as seeds, insects, and other available resources.

### Australian Marsupials

- Australia contains a remarkable diversity of marsupials.
- Different marsupial species evolved from common ancestral stock.
- They adapted to different ecological roles and habitats.
- Examples include kangaroos, koalas, wombats, and marsupial moles.

### Adaptive Radiation vs Convergent Evolution

Adaptive radiation involves diversification from a common ancestor into different ecological niches.

Convergent evolution involves unrelated lineages independently developing similar adaptations.

**Important:** Adaptive radiation is a special example of divergent evolution.

## 3. Fossil Evidence

Fossils are preserved remains, impressions, or traces of organisms from the geological past.

They provide evidence about:
- Organisms that existed in earlier geological periods.
- Changes in body structures over evolutionary time.
- Extinction and the appearance of new species.
- Transitional forms connecting different evolutionary groups.

### Geological Time and Fossil Records

- Older fossils are generally found in deeper sedimentary rock layers when geological layers have not been disturbed.
- Fossil sequences reveal changes in the diversity and complexity of life through geological time.
- Radiometric dating can help estimate the ages of rocks and fossils.

**Example:** Archaeopteryx possesses both reptilian and bird-like characteristics and is an important fossil in understanding the evolutionary history of birds.

## 4. Embryological Evidence

Comparative embryology examines similarities and differences in the development of embryos across different organisms.

- Early vertebrate embryos show certain similarities in their developmental structures.
- These similarities reflect aspects of shared evolutionary ancestry.
- Developmental similarities must be interpreted carefully because embryos do not simply pass through the adult stages of other organisms.

### Haeckel's Biogenetic Law

Ernst Haeckel proposed the statement:

**Ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny.**

- Ontogeny refers to the development of an individual organism.
- Phylogeny refers to the evolutionary history of a species or group.

Haeckel suggested that embryonic development repeats the evolutionary history of an organism.

However, this idea is not accepted as a literal description of embryonic development.

### Karl Ernst von Baer

- Karl Ernst von Baer emphasized that embryos of different vertebrates show similarities in early developmental stages.
- General characteristics appear earlier than specialized characteristics.
- Embryos do not pass through the adult stages of other animals during development.

**Important:** Embryological similarities provide evidence of common ancestry, but Haeckel's literal recapitulation theory is scientifically rejected.

## 5. Molecular Evidence

Molecular biology provides strong evidence for evolutionary relationships through comparisons of DNA, RNA, and proteins.

### Major Evidence

- Organisms use DNA as their primary hereditary material.
- The genetic code is nearly universal across living organisms.
- Many essential metabolic pathways are shared.
- Similarities in DNA and protein sequences can indicate evolutionary relationships.

### Molecular Homology

- Closely related organisms generally share greater similarity in their genetic sequences.
- Mutations accumulate over generations and contribute to differences between species.
- Molecular comparisons help reconstruct evolutionary relationships.

**Example:** Similarities in cytochrome c and other conserved proteins can be used to compare evolutionary relationships among organisms.

## 6. Biogeographical Evidence

Biogeography studies the geographical distribution of organisms across Earth.

- Organisms in geographically isolated regions may evolve independently.
- Island species often resemble organisms from nearby mainland regions.
- Geographic isolation can promote the formation of new species.
- Continental drift and historical land connections help explain present-day distributions.

**Example:** Australian marsupials demonstrate how geographical isolation can influence evolutionary diversification.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Origin of Earth vs Origin of Life:** Earth formed approximately 4.5 bya, while the earliest evidence of life dates back to approximately 3.5–3.8 bya.
- **Chemical Evolution:** Oparin and Haldane proposed that life originated through gradual chemical changes involving non-living substances.
- **Primitive Atmosphere:** The classical Oparin-Haldane model describes a reducing atmosphere with no free molecular oxygen.
- **Miller-Urey Experiment:** Conducted by Stanley Miller and Harold Urey in 1953; it produced organic molecules, including amino acids, but did not create life.
- **Homology:** Same evolutionary origin and basic structural plan, potentially different functions; indicates divergent evolution.
- **Analogy:** Different evolutionary origins but similar functions; indicates convergent evolution.
- **Sweet Potato vs Irish Potato:** Sweet potato is a modified root, whereas Irish potato is a modified stem. Their storage functions are similar, making them analogous structures.
- **Adaptive Radiation:** Diversification of organisms from a common ancestor into different ecological niches.
- **Darwin's Finches:** A classic example of adaptive radiation in the Galapagos Islands.
- **Haeckel's Biogenetic Law:** The statement "Ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny" is not accepted as a literal description of embryonic development.
- **Von Baer's Observations:** General embryonic characteristics appear before specialized characteristics.
- **Molecular Evidence:** Similarities in DNA and protein sequences provide evidence of evolutionary relationships.
- **Fossils:** Fossils provide direct physical evidence of organisms that lived in the geological past.

## Quiz

1. Stanley Miller and Harold Urey conducted an experiment in 1953 using methane, ammonia, hydrogen, and water vapour with electric sparks. Which major organic compounds were produced?

- [ ] DNA and RNA
- [x] Amino acids
- [ ] Living bacterial cells
- [ ] Complex multicellular organisms

> The Miller-Urey experiment produced amino acids such as glycine and alanine from simple inorganic starting materials. It demonstrated the possibility of abiotic organic synthesis but did not produce living organisms.

2. The forelimbs of humans, whales, bats, and cheetahs have a similar underlying bone arrangement but perform different functions. What evolutionary phenomenon does this demonstrate?

- [x] Homology resulting from divergent evolution
- [ ] Analogy resulting from convergent evolution
- [ ] Adaptive radiation without common ancestry
- [ ] Artificial selection

> Homologous structures share a common evolutionary origin and basic anatomical organization. Their different functions developed through divergent evolution from a common ancestral structure.

3. Sweet potato and Irish potato both function as underground food-storage organs. However, sweet potato is a modified root, whereas Irish potato is a modified stem. How are these structures classified?

- [ ] Homologous structures
- [x] Analogous structures resulting from convergent evolution
- [ ] Vestigial structures
- [ ] Atavistic structures

> Sweet potato and Irish potato have different anatomical origins but perform the same storage function. Therefore, they are analogous structures.

4. Darwin's finches in the Galapagos Islands evolved different beak shapes adapted to different food sources from a common ancestral species. Which evolutionary process is demonstrated?

- [ ] Genetic drift alone
- [ ] Artificial selection
- [x] Adaptive radiation
- [ ] Convergent evolution

> Adaptive radiation occurs when organisms from a common ancestral group diversify into different species adapted to different ecological niches. Darwin's finches are a classic example.

5. Which statement correctly describes Karl Ernst von Baer's observations regarding embryonic development?

- [ ] Embryos pass through the adult stages of other animal groups.
- [x] General embryonic characteristics appear before specialized characteristics.
- [ ] Every embryo follows an identical developmental pathway.
- [ ] Embryonic development has no relationship to evolutionary ancestry.

> Von Baer observed that general characteristics appear earlier during embryonic development, while specialized characteristics develop later. His observations contradicted the literal interpretation of Haeckel's biogenetic law.

6. Which of the following provides molecular evidence supporting the evolutionary relationship between organisms?

- [ ] Similar geographical climates alone
- [ ] Similar habitats without genetic similarities
- [x] Similarities in DNA and protein sequences
- [ ] Identical adult body sizes

> DNA and protein sequence similarities can reveal shared ancestry. Molecular comparisons are widely used to reconstruct evolutionary relationships among organisms.
