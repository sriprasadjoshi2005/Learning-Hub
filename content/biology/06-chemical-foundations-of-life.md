All biological systems are governed by the laws of **chemistry and physics**. Life depends on a specific set of chemical elements, molecular bonding properties, and the unique physical and chemical characteristics of water to maintain cellular structure, metabolic activities, and homeostasis.

## Essential Elements of Life

Out of the 92 naturally occurring elements, approximately **25 elements are essential for life**. Four major elements constitute about **96% of living matter**:

- **Bulk Elements**: Carbon ($\mathrm{C}$), Hydrogen ($\mathrm{H}$), Oxygen ($\mathrm{O}$), and Nitrogen ($\mathrm{N}$). These form the fundamental backbone of biological molecules.
- **Macro-minerals**: Calcium ($\mathrm{Ca}$), Phosphorus ($\mathrm{P}$), Potassium ($\mathrm{K}$), Sulfur ($\mathrm{S}$), Sodium ($\mathrm{Na}$), Chlorine ($\mathrm{Cl}$), and Magnesium ($\mathrm{Mg}$). These are required in relatively large quantities for structural and physiological functions.
- **Trace Elements**: Elements required in very small quantities (generally less than $0.01\%$ of body mass), such as Iron ($\mathrm{Fe}$), Iodine ($\mathrm{I}$), Zinc ($\mathrm{Zn}$), and Copper ($\mathrm{Cu}$).

## Chemical Bonds in Biological Systems

Chemical bonds and intermolecular interactions stabilise biomolecules and determine the structural organisation of cells. The major types include:

| Bond Type | Nature of Interaction | Relative Strength in Water | Biological Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Covalent Bond** | Sharing of electron pairs between atoms. | Strongest | Forms the backbone of DNA, proteins, and carbohydrates. |
| **Ionic Bond** | Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. | Weakened by water molecules | Contributes to protein structure through ionic interactions and salt bridges. |
| **Hydrogen Bond** | Attraction between hydrogen bonded to an electronegative atom ($\mathrm{O}$ or $\mathrm{N}$) and another electronegative atom. | Weak individually; significant collectively | DNA base pairing, water cohesion, and protein secondary structures ($\alpha$-helix and $\beta$-sheets). |
| **Van der Waals Forces** | Weak transient electrostatic interactions caused by fluctuating electron distributions. | Very weak individually | Contributes to molecular packing, protein folding, and membrane organisation. |

## Unique Properties of Water and pH

Water ($\mathrm{H_2O}$) is a **polar molecule** capable of forming up to four hydrogen bonds with neighbouring water molecules. Its unusual physical and chemical properties make it essential for life.

1. **High Specific Heat Capacity**: Water absorbs or releases large amounts of heat with relatively small temperature changes. This protects organisms and cellular environments against rapid temperature fluctuations.
2. **High Heat of Vaporisation**: A considerable amount of energy is required to convert liquid water into vapour because intermolecular hydrogen bonds must be disrupted. This enables evaporative cooling through sweating in mammals and transpiration in plants.
3. **Cohesion and Adhesion**: Cohesion between water molecules produces high surface tension, while adhesion between water and other surfaces contributes to capillary action. Together, these properties assist the ascent of sap in plants.
4. **Universal Solvent**: Water dissolves many polar and ionic substances by surrounding dissolved ions with hydration shells, facilitating biochemical reactions and transport.

### pH and Biological Buffers

The **pH scale** measures the hydrogen ion concentration of a solution and indicates whether it is acidic, neutral, or alkaline.

$$
\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\mathrm{H^+}]
$$

- **Acidic Solution**: pH below 7.
- **Neutral Solution**: pH equal to 7 at approximately $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
- **Alkaline Solution**: pH above 7.

Organisms maintain their internal pH within narrow limits using **buffer systems**, which resist sudden changes in hydrogen ion concentration.

An important example is the **carbonic acid-bicarbonate buffer system** in human blood:

$$
\mathrm{CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 \rightleftharpoons HCO_3^- + H^+}
$$

This reversible reaction helps regulate blood pH by balancing carbon dioxide, carbonic acid, bicarbonate ions, and hydrogen ions.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Bond Strength Context**: Ionic bonds can be very strong in dry conditions or vacuum, but in aqueous cellular environments, **covalent bonds** are generally much stronger than ionic interactions.
- **pH Scale Logarithm**: A change of one pH unit represents a **10-fold change** in hydrogen ion concentration ($[\mathrm{H^+}]$), not a linear change.
- **Hydrophobic Interactions**: Nonpolar molecules do not chemically repel water. Instead, water molecules tend to exclude nonpolar substances to maximise their own hydrogen-bonding interactions.
- **Hydrogen Bonds vs Covalent Bonds in Water**: Evaporation requires disrupting intermolecular hydrogen bonds between water molecules, not breaking the covalent $\mathrm{O-H}$ bonds within individual water molecules.
- **DNA Bonding**: Hydrogen bonds connect complementary nitrogenous bases between the two DNA strands, whereas phosphodiester covalent bonds form the sugar-phosphate backbone of each strand.
- **Water as a Temperature Buffer**: High specific heat capacity helps stabilise temperature, while high heat of vaporisation enables evaporative cooling. These are two distinct properties.

## Quiz

1. Human blood maintains a tight pH baseline near $7.40$. If metabolic acidosis causes the blood pH to drop from $7.40$ to $6.40$, how has the free hydrogen ion concentration $[\mathrm{H^+}]$ changed?
  - [ ] Decreased by a factor of 10
  - [ ] Increased by a factor of 2
  - [x] Increased by a factor of 10
  - [ ] Increased by a factor of 100
   > The pH scale is logarithmic ($\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\mathrm{H^+}]$). A decrease of $1.0$ pH unit corresponds to a $10^1 = 10$-fold increase in free hydrogen ion concentration.

2. Which chemical bond or interaction is primarily responsible for holding together the two antiparallel strands of a double-stranded DNA molecule?
  - [ ] Phosphodiester covalent bonds
  - [ ] Ionic bonds between phosphate groups
  - [x] Hydrogen bonds between complementary nitrogenous bases
  - [ ] Disulfide linkages between deoxyribose sugars
   > Phosphodiester covalent bonds form the sugar-phosphate backbone of each DNA strand, whereas hydrogen bonds between complementary nitrogenous bases (Adenine-Thymine and Guanine-Cytosine) hold the two opposing strands together.

3. Water's high heat of vaporisation is crucial for thermal regulation in terrestrial organisms. Which physical phenomenon directly explains why liquid water requires substantial thermal energy to transition into gas?
  - [ ] Breaking of covalent $\mathrm{O-H}$ bonds within individual water molecules
  - [x] Disruption of extensive intermolecular hydrogen-bonding networks between adjacent water molecules
  - [ ] Formation of hydration shells around nonpolar solutes
  - [ ] High ionisation tendency into $\mathrm{H^+}$ and $\mathrm{OH^-}$ ions at room temperature
   > Evaporating water requires disrupting the extensive network of intermolecular hydrogen bonds holding liquid water molecules together. The covalent bonds within individual water molecules remain intact during this process.
