title: Human Digestive System and Nutrition
level: advanced

The human digestive system comprises the **alimentary canal** and associated digestive glands (salivary glands, liver, and pancreas). Together, they break down complex food macromolecules into simpler, absorbable units through mechanical and enzymatic digestion.

## Anatomy of the Alimentary Canal

The alimentary canal is a continuous muscular tube extending from the mouth to the anus.

$$
\text{Mouth} \rightarrow \text{Pharynx} \rightarrow \text{Oesophagus} \rightarrow \text{Stomach} \rightarrow \text{Small Intestine} \rightarrow \text{Large Intestine} \rightarrow \text{Anus}
$$

The **small intestine** is divided into three regions:

1. **Duodenum**: First and shortest region; receives bile and pancreatic juice.
2. **Jejunum**: Middle region; an important site of nutrient absorption.
3. **Ileum**: Final region; completes digestion and absorption before opening into the large intestine.

### Layers of the Gut Wall

From the outermost to the innermost layer, the gut wall consists of four major layers:

1. **Serosa**: Outermost protective layer, consisting of connective tissue covered by a thin mesothelium.
2. **Muscularis**: Smooth muscle layer responsible for peristalsis and mixing movements. It generally contains an outer longitudinal and an inner circular muscle layer.
3. **Submucosa**: Loose connective tissue containing blood vessels, lymphatic vessels, nerves, and, in the duodenum, Brunner's glands.
4. **Mucosa**: Innermost layer responsible for secretion, digestion, and absorption. In the small intestine, it forms folds, villi, and microvilli that greatly increase the absorptive surface area.

## Digestive Glands and Their Secretions

The major digestive glands produce secretions that facilitate the breakdown of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats.

| Digestive Gland | Secretion | Major Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Salivary glands | Saliva | Begins starch digestion and lubricates food |
| Gastric glands | Gastric juice | Provides an acidic environment and begins protein digestion |
| Liver | Bile | Emulsifies fats and helps neutralize acidic chyme |
| Pancreas | Pancreatic juice | Supplies enzymes for carbohydrate, protein, and fat digestion |
| Intestinal glands | Succus entericus | Completes the digestion of food into absorbable units |

## Enzymatic Digestion Breakdown

Digestion involves the enzymatic hydrolysis of complex food molecules into simpler substances that can be absorbed through the intestinal wall.

### Carbohydrate Digestion

| Site | Enzyme | Substrate → Product |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Oral cavity | Salivary amylase (Ptyalin) | Starch → Maltose and smaller carbohydrates |
| Duodenum | Pancreatic amylase | Starch → Maltose and smaller carbohydrates |
| Small intestine | Maltase | Maltose → Glucose |
| Small intestine | Sucrase | Sucrose → Glucose + Fructose |
| Small intestine | Lactase | Lactose → Glucose + Galactose |

Salivary amylase begins starch digestion in the mouth. Its activity decreases in the highly acidic environment of the stomach.

### Protein Digestion

| Site | Enzyme | Substrate → Product |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Stomach | Pepsin | Proteins → Proteoses and peptones |
| Stomach (infants) | Rennin (Chymosin) | Milk casein → Coagulated casein |
| Duodenum | Trypsin and chymotrypsin | Proteins and polypeptides → Smaller peptides |
| Small intestine | Carboxypeptidases | Peptides → Smaller peptides and amino acids |
| Small intestine | Aminopeptidases and dipeptidases | Peptides and dipeptides → Amino acids |

**Pepsin activation:** Gastric chief cells release inactive pepsinogen, which is converted into active pepsin in the acidic environment created by hydrochloric acid (HCl). Pepsin can also activate additional pepsinogen molecules.

### Fat Digestion

| Site | Enzyme / Substance | Substrate → Product |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Duodenum | Bile salts | Large fat globules → Small emulsified droplets |
| Duodenum | Pancreatic lipase | Triglycerides → Monoglycerides and fatty acids |
| Small intestine | Intestinal lipases | Further breakdown of lipids |

**Important:** Bile salts emulsify fats but do not chemically digest them. Pancreatic lipase performs the major enzymatic digestion of dietary triglycerides.

## Role of Bile in Digestion

Bile is synthesized by the **liver** and stored and concentrated in the **gallbladder** before being released into the duodenum.

Its major components include:

- **Bile salts**: Sodium glycocholate and sodium taurocholate, which facilitate fat emulsification.
- **Bile pigments**: Bilirubin and biliverdin, formed during the breakdown of haemoglobin.
- **Phospholipids**: Help stabilize emulsified fat droplets.
- **Bicarbonate and electrolytes**: Contribute to neutralizing acidic chyme.

### Major Functions of Bile

1. **Fat emulsification**: Breaks large fat globules into smaller droplets, increasing the surface area available to lipase.
2. **Acid neutralization**: Helps create a suitable pH for pancreatic and intestinal enzymes.
3. **Micelle formation**: Bile salts help form micelles that facilitate the transport of lipid digestion products to the intestinal absorptive surface.

**Bile contains no digestive enzymes.** Its role is essential for efficient lipid digestion and absorption, but the actual chemical breakdown of fats is performed by lipases.

## Absorption of Nutrients

The small intestine is the principal site of nutrient absorption. Its inner surface contains numerous **villi**, each covered with epithelial cells bearing microscopic projections called **microvilli**.

Each villus contains:

- **Blood capillaries**: Absorb water-soluble nutrients such as glucose and amino acids.
- **Lacteal**: A lymphatic capillary responsible for absorbing most dietary lipids in the form of chylomicrons.

### Absorption of Different Nutrients

| Nutrient | Absorption Mechanism | Destination |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Glucose and galactose | Sodium-dependent secondary active transport | Blood capillaries |
| Fructose | Facilitated diffusion | Blood capillaries |
| Amino acids | Mainly sodium-dependent transport | Blood capillaries |
| Fatty acids and monoglycerides | Enter intestinal cells and are re-esterified into triglycerides | Mainly lacteals as chylomicrons |
| Water and electrolytes | Osmosis and specific transport mechanisms | Blood |

### Lipid Absorption and Chylomicron Formation

Dietary fats follow a distinct absorption pathway:

1. Bile salts emulsify fats and help form micelles containing lipid digestion products.
2. Fatty acids and monoglycerides enter intestinal epithelial cells (enterocytes).
3. Inside enterocytes, they are re-esterified to form triglycerides.
4. Triglycerides combine with cholesterol, phospholipids, and proteins to form **chylomicrons**.
5. Chylomicrons enter the lymphatic capillaries called **lacteals**.
6. They travel through the lymphatic system and eventually enter the bloodstream.

Most long-chain dietary fats therefore reach the bloodstream through lymph rather than directly through intestinal blood capillaries.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Bile has no digestive enzymes**: It facilitates lipid digestion through physical emulsification and helps neutralize acidic chyme.
- **Rennin vs Renin**: **Rennin (chymosin)** is a milk-coagulating digestive enzyme found in the stomach of infants. **Renin** is an enzyme released by kidney juxtaglomerular cells that participates in blood pressure regulation.
- **Pepsinogen vs Pepsin**: Pepsinogen is the inactive precursor secreted by gastric chief cells; pepsin is its active protein-digesting form.
- **Bile vs Lipase**: Bile salts emulsify fats, whereas pancreatic lipase chemically hydrolyses triglycerides.
- **Villi vs Lacteals**: Villi are finger-like projections of the intestinal mucosa, while lacteals are lymphatic capillaries located inside individual villi.
- **Blood vs Lymphatic absorption**: Glucose and amino acids enter blood capillaries, whereas most long-chain dietary lipids enter lacteals as chylomicrons.
- **Small intestine vs Large intestine**: The small intestine is the principal site of enzymatic digestion and nutrient absorption. The large intestine primarily absorbs water and electrolytes and helps form faeces.

## Quiz

1. Fatty acids and glycerol-derived lipid products are packaged into chylomicrons inside intestinal epithelial cells. Into which specialized structures inside intestinal villi are these particles primarily absorbed?

- [ ] Hepatic portal veins
- [x] Lacteals (lymphatic capillaries)
- [ ] Crypts of Lieberkühn
- [ ] Brunner's glands

> Chylomicrons are lipoprotein particles formed inside enterocytes. Because of their size, they enter the lymphatic capillaries called lacteals and eventually reach the bloodstream through the lymphatic circulation.

2. What is the physiological mechanism responsible for converting inactive pepsinogen into active pepsin in the stomach?

- [ ] Cleavage by enterokinase
- [ ] Emulsification by bile salts
- [x] Exposure to hydrochloric acid (HCl) in an acidic environment
- [ ] Activation by pancreatic lipase

> Gastric chief cells secrete inactive pepsinogen. Hydrochloric acid secreted by parietal cells creates a highly acidic environment (approximately pH 1.5–2.0), facilitating its conversion into active pepsin.

3. Which digestive secretion enters the duodenum, contains no digestive enzymes, and plays an essential role in fat emulsification?

- [ ] Pancreatic juice
- [ ] Succus entericus
- [x] Bile
- [ ] Gastric juice

> Bile contains bile salts and phospholipids but no digestive enzymes. Bile salts emulsify large fat globules into smaller droplets, increasing the surface area available for pancreatic lipase.

4. Which layer of the alimentary canal contains loose connective tissue, blood vessels, lymphatic vessels, and nerves?

- [ ] Serosa
- [ ] Muscularis
- [x] Submucosa
- [ ] Mucosa

> The submucosa is a connective tissue layer containing blood vessels, lymphatic vessels, and nerves. Brunner's glands are also present in the submucosa of the duodenum.

5. Which enzyme is primarily responsible for the conversion of triglycerides into monoglycerides and fatty acids in the small intestine?

- [ ] Salivary amylase
- [ ] Pepsin
- [x] Pancreatic lipase
- [ ] Maltase

> Pancreatic lipase is the principal enzyme responsible for triglyceride digestion in the small intestine. Bile salts assist this process by emulsifying fats but do not themselves hydrolyse triglycerides.
