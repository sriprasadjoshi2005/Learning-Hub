---
title: Reproduction in Flowering Plants
level: advanced
---

Sexual reproduction in **angiosperms** involves the formation of male and female gametophytes, pollination, double fertilization, and seed development. The process includes **microsporogenesis**, **megasporogenesis**, and the transformation of floral structures into seeds and fruits after fertilization.

## Male and Female Gametophytes

### 1. Male Gametophyte: Microsporogenesis

**Microsporogenesis** is the process of formation of haploid microspores from diploid microspore mother cells (MMCs) through meiosis. It occurs inside the **microsporangia** of the anther.

**Process:**

$$
\text{Microspore Mother Cell } (2n)
\xrightarrow{\text{Meiosis}}
4 \text{ Microspores } (n)
$$

- Each microspore is haploid and develops into a pollen grain.
- The pollen grain represents the male gametophyte.
- A young pollen grain generally contains two cells:

  - **Vegetative Cell**: Larger cell containing abundant food reserves. It forms the pollen tube during germination.
  - **Generative Cell**: Smaller cell that divides mitotically to produce two male gametes.

**Mature Pollen Grain:**

- In most angiosperms, pollen grains are released at the two-celled stage.
- In some species, pollen grains are released at the three-celled stage, already containing two male gametes.

### 2. Female Gametophyte: Megasporogenesis

**Megasporogenesis** is the process of formation of haploid megaspores from a diploid megaspore mother cell (MMC) through meiosis. It occurs inside the **ovule**.

**Process:**

$$
\text{Megaspore Mother Cell } (2n)
\xrightarrow{\text{Meiosis}}
4 \text{ Megaspores } (n)
$$

- The four haploid megaspores are generally arranged in a linear tetrad.
- Usually, three megaspores degenerate.
- The remaining functional megaspore develops into the female gametophyte.

### 3. Polygonum-Type Embryo Sac

The **Polygonum-type embryo sac** is the most common type of female gametophyte in angiosperms. It develops from a single functional megaspore and is typically **7-celled and 8-nucleate**.

**Structure of the Mature Embryo Sac:**

| Component | Number | Location / Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Egg Cell | 1 | Located at the micropylar end; fuses with a male gamete during syngamy |
| Synergids | 2 | Located beside the egg cell; guide the pollen tube through the filiform apparatus |
| Antipodal Cells | 3 | Located at the chalazal end; generally involved in nutritive functions |
| Central Cell | 1 | Contains two polar nuclei and participates in triple fusion |

**Important structural features:**

- **Micropylar End**: Contains one egg cell and two synergids, collectively forming the egg apparatus.
- **Synergids**: Possess a specialised structure called the **filiform apparatus**, which helps guide the pollen tube into the embryo sac.
- **Central Cell**: Contains two haploid polar nuclei, which may fuse to form a diploid secondary nucleus.
- **Chalazal End**: Contains three antipodal cells.

**Key concept:** The mature Polygonum-type embryo sac has seven cells but eight nuclei because the central cell contains two polar nuclei.

## Double Fertilization

**Double fertilization** is a characteristic feature of angiosperms. It involves two fusion events within the embryo sac: **syngamy** and **triple fusion**.

### 1. Syngamy

Syngamy is the fusion of one haploid male gamete with the haploid egg cell to form a diploid zygote.

$$
\text{Male Gamete } (n) + \text{Egg Cell } (n)
\longrightarrow \text{Zygote } (2n)
$$

- The diploid zygote develops into the embryo.
- The embryo eventually forms the new plant.

### 2. Triple Fusion

Triple fusion is the fusion of the second haploid male gamete with the two haploid polar nuclei present in the central cell.

$$
\text{Male Gamete } (n) + 2 \text{ Polar Nuclei } (n+n)
\longrightarrow \text{Primary Endosperm Nucleus } (3n)
$$

- The resulting Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN) is typically triploid.
- It develops into the endosperm, which provides nourishment to the developing embryo.

### Significance of Double Fertilization

| Fusion Event | Gametes / Nuclei Involved | Product | Ploidy |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Syngamy | Male gamete + Egg cell | Zygote | Diploid (2n) |
| Triple Fusion | Male gamete + Two polar nuclei | Primary Endosperm Nucleus | Triploid (3n) |

**Development after fertilization:**

$$
\text{Zygote } (2n) \longrightarrow \text{Embryo}
$$

$$
\text{Primary Endosperm Nucleus } (3n)
\longrightarrow \text{Endosperm}
$$

**Key concept:** The two fertilization events produce different structures: the zygote develops into the embryo, while the primary endosperm nucleus develops into the nutritive endosperm.

## Post-Fertilization Structures

After successful fertilization, several floral structures undergo changes to form the seed and fruit.

| Pre-Fertilization Structure | Post-Fertilization Product |
| ----- | ----- |
| Ovule | Seed |
| Ovary | Fruit |
| Ovary Wall | Pericarp |
| Outer Integument | Testa (Outer Seed Coat) |
| Inner Integument | Tegmen (Inner Seed Coat) |
| Primary Endosperm Nucleus | Endosperm (Typically 3n) |
| Zygote | Embryo |

**Important points:**

- The ovule develops into the seed.
- The ovary develops into the fruit.
- The ovary wall forms the pericarp, which surrounds the fruit.
- The outer integument generally forms the testa.
- The inner integument generally forms the tegmen.
- The zygote develops into the embryo.
- The primary endosperm nucleus develops into the endosperm.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Microsporogenesis:** A diploid microspore mother cell undergoes meiosis to produce four haploid microspores.
- **Megasporogenesis:** A diploid megaspore mother cell produces four haploid megaspores, of which usually only one remains functional.
- **Embryo sac structure:** The typical Polygonum-type embryo sac is 7-celled and 8-nucleate, not 8-celled and 8-nucleate.
- **Synergids:** The filiform apparatus is present in synergids and helps guide the pollen tube into the embryo sac.
- **Syngamy:** Fusion of one male gamete with the egg cell produces a diploid zygote (2n).
- **Triple fusion:** Fusion of one male gamete with two polar nuclei produces a typically triploid primary endosperm nucleus (3n).
- **Double fertilization:** It involves both syngamy and triple fusion and is characteristic of angiosperms.
- **Endosperm ploidy:** Angiosperm endosperm is typically triploid (3n), whereas gymnosperm nutritive tissue is haploid (n) and develops from the female gametophyte before fertilization.
- **Seed coat:** The outer integument generally forms the testa, while the inner integument generally forms the tegmen.
- **Fruit formation:** The ovary develops into the fruit, while the ovule develops into the seed.
