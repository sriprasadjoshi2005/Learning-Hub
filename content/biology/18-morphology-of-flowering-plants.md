## title: Morphology of Flowering Plants
level: intermediate

Morphology deals with the external structure and form of plant organs. Angiosperms feature a root system and a shoot system (stem, leaves, flowers, fruits, and seeds).

## Root System and Modifications

* **Tap Root System**: Originates from the radicle (e.g., Dicots like Mustard).
* **Fibrous Root System**: Radicle dies early; replaced by roots from stem base (e.g., Monocots like Wheat).
* **Adventitious Roots**: Roots arising from plant parts other than the radicle (e.g., *Monstera*, Banyan).

| Root Function | Modification Type | Description / Example |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Storage | Fusiform / Napiform | Carrot (conical), Radish (fusiform), Turnip (napiform) |
| Support | Prop Roots | Hanging structures supporting heavy branches (Banyan tree) |
| Support | Stilt Roots | Supporting roots coming out of lower stem nodes (Maize, Sugarcane) |
| Respiration | Pneumatophores | Vertical roots growing upward for oxygen exchange in mangles (*Rhizophora*) |

## Stem and Leaf Morphology

* **Stem Modifications**:
  * *Storage*: Rhizome (Ginger), Tuber (Potato), Corm (*Colocasia*).
  * *Defense*: Thorns (modified axillary buds in *Bougainvillea*).
  * *Photosynthetic*: Phylloclade (flattened green stems performing photosynthesis in succulents like *Opuntia*).
* **Leaf Modifications**:
  * *Phyllode*: Petiole becomes green, expanded, and photosynthetic (Australian *Acacia*).
  * *Venation*: Reticulate (Dicots) vs Parallel (Monocots).

## Flower Structure and Inflorescence

$$
\text{Flower Whorls} \longrightarrow \text{Calyx (Sepals)} + \text{Corolla (Petals)} + \text{Androecium (Stamens)} + \text{Gynoecium (Carpels)}
$$

* **Aestivation**: Mode of arrangement of sepals/petals (Valvate, Twisted, Imbricate, Vexillary).
* **Placentation**: Arrangement of ovules inside the ovary:
  * *Marginal* (Pea), *Axile* (Tomato, Citrus), *Parietal* (Mustard), *Free-central* (*Dianthus*), *Basal* (Sunflower).

## Common Exam Traps

* **Phylloclade vs Phyllode**: A **Phylloclade** is a modified photosynthetic **stem** (*Opuntia*), whereas a **Phyllode** is a modified photosynthetic **petiole/leaf** (*Acacia*).
* **Thorns vs Spines**: Thorns are modified **axillary stems** (*Citrus*); spines are modified **leaves** (Cacti).

## Quiz

1. Mangrove plants growing in saline swampy areas develop upright, negatively geotropic roots that project above the mud surface. What are these structural adaptations called?

* \[ \] Prop roots
* \[ \] Stilt roots
* \[x\] Pneumatophores
* \[ \] Haustorial roots

> Pneumatophores are specialized respiratory roots found in halophytic mangroves (*Rhizophora*) that grow upward to obtain atmospheric oxygen in waterlogged soils.

2. In an Australian *Acacia* plant, the normal leaf lamina drops off early, and the petiole becomes expanded, green, and performs photosynthesis. What is this structural modification?

* \[ \] Cladode
* \[ \] Phylloclade
* \[x\] Phyllode
* \[ \] Tendril

> A phyllode is a modified petiole or leaf stalk that flattens out, turns green, and functions photosynthetically when the main leaf lamina is reduced or shed.

3. What type of placentation is characterized by ovules arranged on a central axis without any internal dividing septa within a unilocular ovary, as observed in *Dianthus* and Primrose?

* \[ \] Axile placentation
* \[ \] Parietal placentation
* \[x\] Free-central placentation
* \[ \] Marginal placentation

> In free-central placentation, ovules are borne on a central axis in a single-chambered (unilocular) ovary where septa are completely absent.
