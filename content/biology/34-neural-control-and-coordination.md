## title: Neural Control and Coordination
level: intermediate → advanced

The nervous system controls and coordinates rapid cellular communication through electrical impulses (action potentials) and chemical transmission across synapses.

## Structure of a Neuron

A neuron consists of a **Cell Body (Soma)** (containing cytoplasm, organelles, and **Nissl's granules**), **Dendrites** (receptive inputs), and a long **Axon** (transmits impulses away).

* **Myelinated Axons**: Encased in Schwann cell **Myelin Sheaths** with gaps called **Nodes of Ranvier**; enables fast **Saltatory Conduction**.

## Nerve Impulse Generation and Conduction

```
 1. Resting State (-70 mV)      2. Depolarization (+30 mV)     3. Repolarization (-70 mV)
 High K⁺ inside, Na⁺ outside  ---> Voltage Na⁺ channels open ---> Voltage K⁺ channels open
 (Maintained by Na⁺/K⁺ pump)      Na⁺ rushes IN                  K⁺ rushes OUT
```

1. **Resting Membrane Potential**: Resting membrane is polarized ($\approx -70\text{ mV}$ inside). $\mathrm{Na^+/K^+}$ pump maintains high $[\mathrm{K^+}]$ inside and high $[\mathrm{Na^+}]$ outside.
2. **Action Potential (Depolarization)**: Stimulus reaches threshold ($\approx -55\text{ mV}$), voltage-gated $\mathrm{Na^+}$ channels open $\rightarrow$ rapid inflow of $\mathrm{Na^+}$ reverses polarity to $+30\text{ mV}$.
3. **Repolarization**: $\mathrm{Na^+}$ channels close, voltage-gated $\mathrm{K^+}$ channels open $\rightarrow \mathrm{K^+}$ flows out, restoring negative resting potential.

## Synaptic Transmission

* **Chemical Synapse**: Action potential reaches presynaptic terminal $\rightarrow$ Opens voltage-gated $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ channels $\rightarrow \mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ triggers exocytosis of neurotransmitters (e.g., **Acetylcholine**) into the **Synaptic Cleft** $\rightarrow$ Binds to postsynaptic receptors opening ion channels.

## Human Brain Division

* **Forebrain**: **Cerebrum** (sensory/motor processing, intelligence), **Thalamus** (relay station), **Hypothalamus** (thermoregulation, hunger, thirst, pituitary regulation).
* **Midbrain**: Corpora quadrigemina (visual and auditory reflexes).
* **Hindbrain**: **Cerebellum** (posture and muscular coordination), **Pons** (fiber tracts connecting brain regions), **Medulla Oblongata** (cardiovascular, respiratory, and gastric control centers).

## Common Exam Traps

* **Nissl's Granules Location**: Nissl's granules (dense clusters of RER and free ribosomes) are present in the **Cell Body (Soma)** and **Dendrites**, but are completely **absent in the Axon**.
* **Unidirectional Synaptic Flow**: Chemical synaptic transmission is strictly **one-way** because neurotransmitters are stored only in presynaptic vesicles and receptors exist only on postsynaptic membranes.

> The resting potential is maintained by high membrane permeability to $\text{K}^+$, low permeability to $\text{Na}^+$, and the electrogenic $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$ pump ($3\text{ Na}^+$ out / $2\text{ K}^+$ in).
