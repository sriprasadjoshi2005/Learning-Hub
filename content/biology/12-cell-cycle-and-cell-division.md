The **cell cycle** is an ordered sequence of events through which a cell grows, replicates its genetic material, and divides into daughter cells. It ensures the accurate duplication and distribution of genetic material during cell division.

## Phases of the Cell Cycle

The cell cycle consists of two major phases:

1. **Interphase:** The preparatory phase, during which the cell grows and replicates its DNA. It occupies approximately 95% of the total cell cycle duration in many actively dividing cells.
2. **M-Phase (Mitotic Phase):** The division phase, during which the nucleus and cytoplasm divide to produce daughter cells.

The overall sequence is:

$$
\text{Interphase }(\mathrm{G_1}\rightarrow\mathrm{S}\rightarrow\mathrm{G_2})
\rightarrow
\text{M-Phase }(\text{Karyokinesis}\rightarrow\text{Cytokinesis})
$$

### Interphase

Interphase is the period between two successive cell divisions. It consists of three stages:

#### 1. G₁ Phase (Gap 1)

- The cell undergoes active growth.
- RNA, proteins, and cellular organelles are synthesised.
- Normal metabolic activities take place.
- DNA content is $2\mathrm{C}$, and the chromosome number is $2n$.

#### 2. S Phase (Synthesis)

- Replication of nuclear DNA takes place.
- Each chromosome produces two sister chromatids joined at the centromere.
- The centrosome is also duplicated in animal cells.
- DNA content increases from $2\mathrm{C}$ to $4\mathrm{C}$.
- Chromosome number remains $2n$ because sister chromatids are still joined.

#### 3. G₂ Phase (Gap 2)

- The cell continues to grow.
- Proteins required for cell division, including tubulin, are synthesised.
- The cell prepares for mitosis and spindle apparatus formation.
- DNA content remains $4\mathrm{C}$.

### G₀ Phase (Quiescent Stage)

Some cells exit the active cell cycle from the G₁ phase and enter a non-dividing state called **G₀**.

- Cells remain metabolically active but do not normally proceed toward division.
- Some cells can re-enter the cell cycle under suitable conditions.
- Mature neurons and many cardiac muscle cells generally remain in G₀ for long periods.

### Changes in DNA Content and Chromosome Number

For a typical diploid cell with $2n$ chromosomes:

| Cell Cycle Stage | Chromosome Number | DNA Content | Chromatid Condition |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| G₁ | $2n$ | $2\mathrm{C}$ | One chromatid per chromosome |
| S | $2n$ | $2\mathrm{C}\rightarrow4\mathrm{C}$ | DNA replication occurs |
| G₂ | $2n$ | $4\mathrm{C}$ | Two sister chromatids per chromosome |
| After mitosis and cytokinesis | $2n$ per daughter cell | $2\mathrm{C}$ per daughter cell | One chromatid per chromosome |

**Important:** DNA content and chromosome number are different measurements. Chromosome number is conventionally counted by the number of centromeres.

## Mitosis and Meiosis

Cell division occurs mainly through two processes: **mitosis** and **meiosis**.

### Comparison Between Mitosis and Meiosis

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Number of Divisions** | One nuclear division | Two successive nuclear divisions |
| **Daughter Cells** | Two | Usually four |
| **Chromosome Number** | Maintained ($2n\rightarrow2n$) | Halved ($2n\rightarrow n$) |
| **Genetic Similarity** | Daughter cells are generally genetically identical | Daughter cells are genetically diverse |
| **Synapsis** | Absent | Occurs during Prophase I |
| **Crossing Over** | Normally absent | Occurs during Prophase I |
| **Biological Role** | Growth, repair, and tissue regeneration | Production of haploid cells for sexual reproduction and generation of genetic variation |

### Mitosis

**Mitosis** is a type of cell division in which one parent nucleus divides to produce two genetically similar daughter nuclei with the same chromosome number as the parent nucleus.

It is important for:

- Growth and development.
- Replacement of damaged or dead cells.
- Tissue repair and regeneration.
- Asexual reproduction in many organisms.

The stages of mitosis are:

1. **Prophase:** Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes, the spindle begins to form, and the nucleolus disappears.
2. **Metaphase:** Chromosomes align at the equatorial plane of the cell.
3. **Anaphase:** Centromeres separate, and sister chromatids move toward opposite poles.
4. **Telophase:** Chromosomes reach the poles, decondense, and new nuclear envelopes form.

After nuclear division, **cytokinesis** divides the cytoplasm, producing two daughter cells.

### Meiosis

**Meiosis** is a specialised type of cell division involving two successive nuclear divisions that reduce the chromosome number by half.

- Meiosis I is the reductional division, separating homologous chromosomes.
- Meiosis II is the equational division, separating sister chromatids.
- Genetic variation is generated through crossing over and independent assortment.

The process generally produces four haploid cells from one diploid parent cell.

## Stages of Meiotic Prophase I

**Prophase I** is the longest and most complex stage of meiosis. It is divided into five sub-stages, during which homologous chromosomes pair and exchange genetic material.

### 1. Leptotene

- Chromatin begins to condense into visible chromosomes.
- Chromosomes appear as thin, thread-like structures.
- Each chromosome has already replicated, although its sister chromatids may not be clearly distinguishable.

### 2. Zygotene

- Homologous chromosomes pair with each other.
- This pairing is called **synapsis**.
- A protein structure called the **synaptonemal complex** develops between homologous chromosomes.
- The paired homologous chromosomes form a **bivalent**, containing four chromatids in total, also called a tetrad.

### 3. Pachytene

- Homologous chromosomes become closely paired.
- **Crossing over** occurs between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes.
- Genetic material is exchanged, producing new combinations of alleles.
- The process involves recombination proteins and enzyme complexes, including recombinases.

### 4. Diplotene

- The synaptonemal complex dissolves.
- Homologous chromosomes begin to separate but remain attached at crossover sites.
- These visible attachment points are called **chiasmata** (singular: chiasma).

### 5. Diakinesis

- Chromosomes become highly condensed.
- Chiasmata move toward the ends of chromosomes, a process called **terminalisation**.
- The nucleolus disappears, and the nuclear envelope breaks down.
- The spindle apparatus becomes organised for the next stage.

### Summary of Prophase I

| Sub-stage | Major Event |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Leptotene** | Chromosomes become visible through condensation |
| **Zygotene** | Synapsis and synaptonemal complex formation |
| **Pachytene** | Crossing over between non-sister chromatids |
| **Diplotene** | Synaptonemal complex dissolves; chiasmata become visible |
| **Diakinesis** | Terminalisation of chiasmata and breakdown of nuclear envelope |

## Meiosis I and Meiosis II

The two meiotic divisions differ in the structures they separate.

### Meiosis I: Reductional Division

- Homologous chromosomes pair during Prophase I.
- Homologous pairs align at the equator during Metaphase I.
- Homologous chromosomes separate during Anaphase I.
- Chromosome number is reduced from diploid ($2n$) to haploid ($n$).

### Meiosis II: Equational Division

- Chromosomes align individually at the equator during Metaphase II.
- Sister chromatids separate during Anaphase II.
- The resulting cells contain haploid chromosome sets.

### Anaphase I vs. Anaphase II

| Feature | Anaphase I | Anaphase II |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **What Separates?** | Homologous chromosomes | Sister chromatids |
| **Centromeres** | Do not divide | Divide |
| **Type of Division** | Reductional | Equational |
| **Chromosome Number** | Reduced from $2n$ to $n$ across the division | Remains haploid |

## Common Exam Traps

- **Chromosome Number vs. DNA Content:** During S phase, DNA content doubles from $2\mathrm{C}$ to $4\mathrm{C}$, but chromosome number remains $2n$ because sister chromatids remain joined at their centromeres.
- **G₁ vs. G₂:** DNA content is $2\mathrm{C}$ in G₁ and $4\mathrm{C}$ in G₂.
- **Crossing Over:** Crossing over occurs during **Pachytene**, not Zygotene or Diplotene.
- **Synapsis:** Pairing of homologous chromosomes occurs during **Zygotene**.
- **Chiasmata:** These become clearly visible during **Diplotene** after the synaptonemal complex dissolves.
- **Anaphase I vs. Anaphase II:** Homologous chromosomes separate during Anaphase I, whereas sister chromatids separate during Anaphase II.
- **Mitosis vs. Meiosis:** Mitosis normally produces two genetically similar daughter cells, whereas meiosis generally produces four genetically diverse haploid cells.
- **G₀ Phase:** Cells in G₀ are metabolically active but have temporarily or permanently exited the active cell cycle.

## Quiz

1. A mammalian cell with a diploid chromosome number of $2n=16$ and a DNA content of $2\mathrm{C}$ completes the S phase of interphase. What are its chromosome number and total DNA content at the beginning of G₂?

- [ ] 32 chromosomes; $4\mathrm{C}$ DNA
- [x] 16 chromosomes; $4\mathrm{C}$ DNA
- [ ] 16 chromosomes; $2\mathrm{C}$ DNA
- [ ] 8 chromosomes; $4\mathrm{C}$ DNA

> During S phase, DNA replication doubles the DNA content from $2\mathrm{C}$ to $4\mathrm{C}$. However, the sister chromatids remain joined at their centromeres, so the chromosome number remains unchanged at $2n=16$.

2. Crossing over, the exchange of genetic material between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes, occurs during which sub-stage of meiotic Prophase I?

- [ ] Leptotene
- [ ] Zygotene
- [x] Pachytene
- [ ] Diplotene

> Crossing over occurs during Pachytene of Prophase I. It produces new combinations of genetic material and contributes to genetic variation.

3. During which stage of mitosis do sister chromatids separate at their centromeres and move toward opposite poles of the cell?

- [ ] Prophase
- [ ] Metaphase
- [x] Anaphase
- [ ] Telophase

> During mitotic Anaphase, centromeres divide, allowing sister chromatids to separate. Each chromatid becomes an individual daughter chromosome and moves toward an opposite spindle pole.
