## Classic Experimental Setups

1. **Meselson and Stahl Experiment**: Used $^{15}\mathrm{N}$ and $^{14}\mathrm{N}$ density gradient centrifugation in $\mathrm{CsCl}$ to prove semi-conservative $\mathrm{DNA}$ replication.
2. **Hershey and Chase Experiment**: Used radioactive $^{35}\mathrm{S}$ (protein coat) and $^{32}\mathrm{P}$ ($\mathrm{DNA}$) in bacteriophages to prove $\mathrm{DNA}$ is the genetic material.
3. **Englemann's Prism Experiment**: Placed *Cladophora* alga in a light spectrum with aerobic bacteria to demonstrate the action spectrum of photosynthesis.

## Common Exam Traps

* **Isotope Target Matching**: Remember that $^{35}\mathrm{S}$ labels **proteins** (sulfur in cysteine/methionine) but not $\mathrm{DNA}$; $^{32}\mathrm{P}$ labels **$\mathrm{DNA}$** (phosphate backbone) but not proteins.
* **Vector Insertion Inactivation**: Inserting a foreign gene into the $\mathrm{BamHI}$ site of plasmid $\mathrm{pBR322}$ disrupts the **$tet^R$ gene**, causing the recombinant host to lose tetracycline resistance while retaining ampicillin resistance.

## Quiz

1. A laboratory technician inserts a foreign target gene into the unique $\text{BamHI}$ restriction endonuclease site of plasmid vector $\text{pBR322}$ and transforms *E. coli* host cells. What antibiotic resistance phenotype will successful recombinant transformants display?

* \[ \] Resistant to both ampicillin and tetracycline
* \[x\] Resistant to ampicillin, but sensitive to tetracycline
* \[ \] Sensitive to ampicillin, but resistant to tetracycline
* \[ \] Sensitive to both ampicillin and tetracycline

> The $\text{BamHI}$ site lies within the tetracycline resistance gene ($tet^R$). Inserting foreign $\text{DNA}$ inactivates $tet^R$ (insertional inactivation), leaving recombinant cells resistant only to ampicillin ($amp^R$).

2. In the Hershey-Chase blender experiment, why did bacterial cells infected with bacteriophages cultured in $^{32}\text{P}$-containing medium show high intracellular radioactivity following centrifugation?

* \[ \] Phosphorus $^{32}\text{P}$ incorporated into the outer protein capsid was injected into the host cytoplasm.
* \[x\] Phosphorus $^{32}\text{P}$ incorporated into phage $\text{DNA}$ entered the bacterial cell during infection.
* \[ \] $^{32}\text{P}$ bound to the outer bacterial lipopolysaccharide layer.
* \[ \] $^{32}\text{P}$ was actively transported across the membrane via primary ion pumps.

> Radioisotope $^{32}\text{P}$ selectively labels nucleic acids ($\text{DNA}$). The presence of intracellular $^{32}\text{P}$ inside host bacteria proved that viral $\text{DNA}$ enters the host cell to direct viral replication.

3. Which specific band shift pattern was observed by Meselson and Stahl in $\text{CsCl}$ density gradients after transferring *E. coli* from heavy $^{15}\text{N}$ medium to light $^{14}\text{N}$ medium for exactly one generation ($20\text{ minutes}$)?

* \[ \] $100\%$ Heavy $\text{DNA}$ band
* \[ \] $50\%$ Heavy $\text{DNA}$ band and $50\%$ Light $\text{DNA}$ band
* \[x\] $100\%$ Hybrid ($^{15}\text{N}/^{14}\text{N}$) intermediate density $\text{DNA}$ band
* \[ \] $50\%$ Light $\text{DNA}$ band and $50\%$ Hybrid $\text{DNA}$ band

> After Generation 1 in $^{14}\text{N}$, all double-stranded $\text{DNA}$ molecules consist of one heavy strand ($^{15}\text{N}$) and one light strand ($^{14}\text{N}$), forming a single intermediate hybrid band.
