High-stakes entrance examinations frequently test the ability to interpret biological diagrams, identify labelled structures, analyse experimental setups, and connect laboratory observations with underlying biological principles. Mastery of these visual and experimental concepts is essential for solving application-based questions.

## Essential Biological Diagrams and Key Features

### 1. Chloroplast and Mitochondrion

These organelles are central to energy conversion and contain specialized membrane systems.

| Feature | Chloroplast | Mitochondrion |
|---|---|---|
| Outer membrane | Present | Present |
| Inner membrane | Present | Present |
| Internal structure | Thylakoids arranged into grana | Inner membrane forms cristae |
| Internal fluid | Stroma | Matrix |
| Main function | Photosynthesis | Aerobic respiration |
| ATP production | Photophosphorylation | Oxidative phosphorylation |

**Important diagram identifiers:**

- **Stroma:** Fluid-filled region surrounding the thylakoids.
- **Thylakoid lumen:** Internal space enclosed by the thylakoid membrane; protons accumulate here during the light reactions.
- **Grana:** Stacks of thylakoids.
- **Cristae:** Folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane that increase surface area.
- **Matrix:** Inner mitochondrial compartment containing enzymes, mitochondrial DNA, and ribosomes.

**Exam focus:** During photosynthesis, proton accumulation occurs inside the thylakoid lumen. During mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, protons accumulate in the intermembrane space.

### 2. Plant Stem and Root Anatomy

Plant anatomical diagrams are frequently used to distinguish monocot and dicot vascular arrangements.

**Root anatomy:**

| Structure | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| Epiblema | Outermost layer; may bear root hairs |
| Cortex | Parenchymatous region |
| Endodermis | Innermost cortical layer |
| Casparian strips | Suberin deposits in endodermal walls |
| Pericycle | Gives rise to lateral roots |
| Vascular tissue | Xylem and phloem arranged radially |
| Pith | Central region; generally small in dicot roots and large in monocot roots |

**Stem anatomy:**

| Feature | Dicot Stem | Monocot Stem |
|---|---|---|
| Vascular bundles | Arranged in a ring | Scattered |
| Bundle arrangement | Conjoint, collateral, open | Conjoint, collateral, closed |
| Cambium | Present between xylem and phloem | Generally absent |
| Secondary growth | Usually present | Generally absent |
| Hypodermis | Usually collenchymatous | Usually sclerenchymatous |

**Important distinctions:**

- **Radial vascular bundles:** Xylem and phloem occur on alternate radii, as in roots.
- **Conjoint vascular bundles:** Xylem and phloem occur together in the same vascular bundle, as in stems.
- **Casparian strips:** Control the movement of water and dissolved substances through the endodermis by restricting apoplastic movement.

### 4. Nephron Micro-Anatomy

**Major components:**

### 5. Plasmodium Life Cycle

*Plasmodium* is the protozoan parasite responsible for malaria. Its life cycle involves two hosts:

- Human: Intermediate host, where asexual reproduction occurs.
- Female *Anopheles* mosquito: Definitive host, where sexual reproduction occurs.

**Major stages:**

| Stage | Location | Important Event |
|---|---|---|
| Sporozoite | Mosquito salivary glands | Infective stage transmitted to humans |
| Liver stage | Human liver cells | Asexual multiplication |
| Merozoite | Bloodstream | Invades red blood cells |
| Trophozoite | Red blood cells | Feeding and growth |
| Schizont | Liver cells and RBCs | Multiple nuclear divisions |
| Gametocytes | Human blood | Taken up by mosquito |
| Gametes | Mosquito gut | Fertilization |
| Zygote / Ookinete | Mosquito gut | Formation of motile stage |
| Oocyst | Mosquito gut wall | Produces sporozoites |

**Important exam identifiers:**

- Sporozoites are the infective stage for humans.
- Gametocytes are the stages taken up by mosquitoes.
- Fertilization occurs in the mosquito gut.
- Sporozoites eventually migrate to the mosquito salivary glands.

### 6. pBR322 Cloning Vector

pBR322 is a commonly studied plasmid cloning vector used in recombinant DNA technology.

**Important components:**

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| ori | Origin of replication |
| rop | Helps regulate plasmid copy number |
| ampR | Ampicillin resistance gene |
| tetR | Tetracycline resistance gene |
| Restriction sites | Sites where restriction enzymes cut DNA |

**Important restriction sites:**

- **PstI:** Located within the ampicillin-resistance gene.
- **BamHI:** Located within the tetracycline-resistance gene.

**Insertional inactivation:**

| Insertion Site | Gene Disrupted | Recombinant Phenotype |
|---|---|---|
| PstI | ampR | Ampicillin-sensitive, tetracycline-resistant |
| BamHI | tetR | Ampicillin-resistant, tetracycline-sensitive |

**Exam focus:** Insertion of foreign DNA into a resistance gene can inactivate that gene, allowing recombinant colonies to be distinguished from non-recombinant colonies.

## Classic Experimental Setups

### 1. Meselson and Stahl Experiment (1958)

The Meselson and Stahl experiment demonstrated that DNA replication is **semi-conservative**.

**Experimental principle:**

- *Escherichia coli* was initially grown in a medium containing heavy nitrogen isotope \(^{15}N\).
- The bacteria were then transferred to a medium containing lighter nitrogen isotope \(^{14}N\).
- DNA samples were extracted after successive generations.
- DNA was separated using density gradient centrifugation in cesium chloride (CsCl).

**Observations:**

| Generation | DNA Composition | Band Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Before transfer | \(^{15}N/^{15}N\) | One heavy band |
| Generation 1 | \(^{15}N/^{14}N\) | One intermediate hybrid band |
| Generation 2 | 50% hybrid, 50% light | One intermediate and one light band |
| Generation 3 | 25% hybrid, 75% light | Intermediate and light bands |

**Interpretation:**

- After one generation, every DNA molecule contained one parental heavy strand and one newly synthesized light strand.
- After two generations, half the DNA molecules were hybrid and half were entirely light.
- These observations supported semi-conservative replication.

**Three proposed DNA replication models:**

| Model | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Conservative | Original DNA remains together; an entirely new DNA molecule is produced |
| Semi-conservative | Each daughter DNA molecule contains one parental and one newly synthesized strand |
| Dispersive | Each daughter strand contains interspersed segments of old and new DNA |

### 2. Hershey and Chase Experiment (1952)

The Hershey and Chase experiment demonstrated that **DNA is the genetic material** in bacteriophages.

They used bacteriophages that infect *Escherichia coli*.

**Radioactive labelling:**

| Isotope | Labelled Component | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| \(^{35}S\) | Protein coat | Sulfur occurs in cysteine and methionine |
| \(^{32}P\) | DNA | Phosphorus occurs in the DNA phosphate backbone |

**Experimental procedure:**

1. One batch of bacteriophages was grown in a medium containing radioactive \(^{35}S\).
2. Another batch was grown in a medium containing radioactive \(^{32}P\).
3. The labelled phages were allowed to infect bacterial cells.
4. A blender was used to detach phage protein coats from bacterial surfaces.
5. The mixture was centrifuged to separate bacterial cells from the detached phage coats.
6. Radioactivity was examined in the pellet and supernatant.

**Observations:**

| Label | Pellet | Supernatant |
|---|---|---|
| \(^{35}S\) | Little or no radioactivity | Most radioactivity |
| \(^{32}P\) | Most radioactivity | Little or no radioactivity |

**Conclusion:**

- DNA entered bacterial cells during infection.
- Most protein coats remained outside the bacterial cells.
- Therefore, DNA, rather than protein, directed the production of new bacteriophages.

### 3. Engelmann's Prism Experiment

The Engelmann experiment demonstrated the **action spectrum of photosynthesis**.

**Experimental setup:**

- A filamentous green alga, *Cladophora*, was exposed to different wavelengths of light.
- A prism separated white light into its component colours.
- Aerobic bacteria were placed around the algal filament.
- The distribution of bacteria was observed.

**Observation:**

- Aerobic bacteria accumulated mainly in the blue-violet and red regions.
- Fewer bacteria accumulated in the green region.

**Explanation:**

- Photosynthesis releases oxygen.
- Aerobic bacteria accumulate in regions with higher oxygen concentration.
- Therefore, photosynthetic activity is greatest in blue-violet and red light and relatively low in green light.

**Conclusion:** The experiment demonstrated that the action spectrum of photosynthesis broadly corresponds to the absorption spectrum of photosynthetic pigments.

## Other Important Biological Experiments

### 1. Griffith's Transformation Experiment (1928)

Frederick Griffith demonstrated bacterial transformation using two strains of *Streptococcus pneumoniae*.

| Bacterial Strain | Characteristics | Effect in Mice |
|---|---|---|
| S strain | Smooth, capsulated, virulent | Mice died |
| R strain | Rough, non-capsulated, non-virulent | Mice survived |
| Heat-killed S strain | Dead virulent bacteria | Mice survived |
| Live R + heat-killed S | Non-virulent bacteria transformed | Mice died |

**Conclusion:** A transforming principle from dead S bacteria converted live R bacteria into virulent forms.

Griffith did not identify the transforming principle as DNA.

### 2. Avery, MacLeod and McCarty Experiment (1944)

These scientists identified DNA as the transforming principle.

**Experimental approach:**

- Extracts from heat-killed S bacteria were treated with different enzymes.
- Protease destroyed proteins.
- RNase destroyed RNA.
- DNase destroyed DNA.

**Observation:**

Transformation continued after protein or RNA destruction but stopped when DNA was destroyed.

**Conclusion:** DNA was the transforming principle responsible for bacterial transformation.

### 3. Beadle and Tatum Experiment (1941)

Used the fungus *Neurospora crassa* to investigate the relationship between genes and enzymes.

**Major findings:**

- Mutations in specific genes caused defects in particular metabolic reactions.
- This led to the **one gene–one enzyme hypothesis**.
- It was later refined to the one gene–one polypeptide hypothesis.

### 4. Taylor, Woods and Hughes Experiment (1957)

Used radioactive thymidine to study DNA replication in chromosomes of *Vicia faba* root-tip cells.

**Important observation:**

- After one replication in radioactive medium, both sister chromatids were labelled.
- After a subsequent replication in non-radioactive medium, only one chromatid of each chromosome retained the radioactive label.

**Conclusion:** The observations supported semi-conservative DNA replication at the chromosomal level in eukaryotic cells.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Meselson-Stahl Isotopes**: \(^{15}N\) labels DNA with heavy nitrogen, while \(^{14}N\) produces lighter DNA.
- **Generation 1**: After one generation in \(^{14}N\) medium, all DNA is hybrid \(^{15}N/^{14}N\), producing one intermediate-density band.
- **Generation 2**: Half the DNA is hybrid and half is light. There is no heavy-only DNA band.
- **Hershey-Chase Isotopes**: \(^{35}S\) labels protein coats, whereas \(^{32}P\) labels DNA.
- **Hershey-Chase Pellet**: Most \(^{32}P\) radioactivity appears in the bacterial pellet because phage DNA enters the bacterial cells.
- **Griffith vs Avery**: Griffith discovered transformation but did not identify DNA as the transforming principle. Avery and colleagues identified DNA as the transforming principle.
- **Engelmann's Experiment**: Aerobic bacteria accumulate in regions of high oxygen production, particularly under blue-violet and red light.
- **Action Spectrum vs Absorption Spectrum**: An action spectrum shows the effectiveness of different wavelengths in driving photosynthesis; an absorption spectrum shows the wavelengths absorbed by pigments.
- **pBR322 BamHI Site**: Insertion disrupts tetR, producing ampicillin-resistant but tetracycline-sensitive recombinant cells.
- **pBR322 PstI Site**: Insertion disrupts ampR, producing tetracycline-resistant but ampicillin-sensitive recombinant cells.
- **Plasmodium Infective Stage**: Sporozoites infect humans, whereas gametocytes are taken up by mosquitoes.
- **Vasa Recta vs Loop of Henle**: The Loop of Henle establishes the medullary osmotic gradient; the vasa recta helps maintain it.
- **Cardiac ECG**: P wave represents atrial depolarization, QRS represents ventricular depolarization, and T wave represents ventricular repolarization.

## Quiz

1. In a cross-section of a typical dicot root, how are the xylem and phloem tissues arranged?

- [ ] Conjoint and collateral
- [x] Radial, with xylem and phloem on alternate radii
- [ ] Concentric, with phloem completely surrounding xylem
- [ ] Scattered throughout the cortex

> In roots, vascular tissues are arranged radially. Xylem and phloem occur on alternate radii, unlike the conjoint vascular bundles commonly found in stems.

2. During oxidative phosphorylation in mitochondria, where do protons accumulate as they are pumped by the electron transport chain?

- [ ] Mitochondrial matrix
- [x] Intermembrane space
- [ ] Outer membrane surface only
- [ ] Cytoplasm

> The electron transport chain pumps protons from the mitochondrial matrix into the intermembrane space, establishing the electrochemical gradient used by ATP synthase.

3. A foreign DNA fragment is inserted into the BamHI restriction site of pBR322. Which antibiotic resistance phenotype is expected in successful recombinant transformants?

- [ ] Ampicillin-sensitive and tetracycline-resistant
- [x] Ampicillin-resistant and tetracycline-sensitive
- [ ] Resistant to both antibiotics
- [ ] Sensitive to both antibiotics

> The BamHI site lies within the tetR gene. Insertion of foreign DNA disrupts tetracycline resistance, while ampR remains functional.

4. In the Hershey-Chase experiment, which radioactive isotope was used to label the protein coat of bacteriophages?

- [ ] \(^{14}C\)
- [x] \(^{35}S\)
- [ ] \(^{32}P\)
- [ ] \(^{15}N\)

> Sulfur is present in the amino acids cysteine and methionine but is absent from DNA. Therefore, \(^{35}S\) selectively labelled phage protein coats.

5. What was the DNA band pattern observed by Meselson and Stahl after one generation of *E. coli* growth in \(^{14}N\) medium following growth in \(^{15}N\) medium?

- [ ] One heavy-density band
- [ ] Separate heavy and light bands
- [x] One intermediate-density hybrid band
- [ ] One light-density band

> After one generation, every DNA molecule contains one parental \(^{15}N\)-labelled strand and one newly synthesized \(^{14}N\)-labelled strand. All DNA therefore appears as a single intermediate-density band.

6. Which experimental observation distinguished DNA as the transforming principle in the Avery, MacLeod and McCarty experiment?

- [ ] Transformation stopped after protease treatment.
- [ ] Transformation stopped after RNase treatment.
- [x] Transformation stopped after DNase treatment.
- [ ] Transformation continued after DNase treatment.

> DNase selectively destroys DNA. When DNA was destroyed, bacterial transformation stopped, demonstrating that DNA was the transforming principle.

7. In Engelmann's prism experiment, where did aerobic bacteria accumulate most strongly around the *Cladophora* filament?

- [ ] Green and yellow light regions
- [ ] Only the infrared region
- [x] Blue-violet and red light regions
- [ ] All wavelength regions equally

> Photosynthesis is most effective under blue-violet and red wavelengths. Greater oxygen production in these regions attracts aerobic bacteria.

8. Which stage of the *Plasmodium* life cycle is transmitted from an infected female *Anopheles* mosquito to a human?

- [ ] Merozoite
- [ ] Gametocyte
- [x] Sporozoite
- [ ] Ookinete

> Sporozoites are present in the salivary glands of infected female *Anopheles* mosquitoes and enter human blood during a mosquito bite.

9. Which structure establishes the medullary osmotic gradient in the kidney, while the vasa recta helps maintain it?

- [ ] Proximal convoluted tubule
- [ ] Distal convoluted tubule
- [x] Loop of Henle
- [ ] Bowman's capsule

> The Loop of Henle establishes the medullary osmotic gradient through counter-current multiplication. The vasa recta maintains this gradient through counter-current exchange.

10. In a normal electrocardiogram, which event is represented by the QRS complex?

- [ ] Atrial depolarization
- [x] Ventricular depolarization
- [ ] Ventricular repolarization
- [ ] Atrial contraction without electrical activity

> The QRS complex represents ventricular depolarization. Atrial repolarization occurs at approximately the same time but is generally hidden within the QRS complex.
