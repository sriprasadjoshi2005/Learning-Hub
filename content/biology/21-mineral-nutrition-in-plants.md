title: Mineral Nutrition in Plants
level: intermediate
---

Plants require essential inorganic mineral elements absorbed mainly by their roots from the soil. These elements are necessary for normal growth, metabolism, enzyme activity and cellular structure. The study of mineral requirements and their functions in plants is called **mineral nutrition**.

## Essential Mineral Elements

An element is considered **essential** for plant growth if it satisfies all three criteria:

1. The plant cannot complete its life cycle and produce viable seeds in the absence of the element.
2. Its function cannot be replaced by another element.
3. It is directly involved in plant metabolism or is required for an essential metabolic process.

## Classification of Mineral Elements

Essential mineral elements are classified into two major groups based on the quantities required by plants.

### Macronutrients

**Macronutrients** are required in relatively large quantities, generally at concentrations greater than $10$ mmole per kg of dry matter.

| Element | Symbol | Major Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Carbon | $\mathrm{C}$ | Structural component of organic molecules |
| Hydrogen | $\mathrm{H}$ | Component of water and organic compounds |
| Oxygen | $\mathrm{O}$ | Component of water and organic compounds |
| Nitrogen | $\mathrm{N}$ | Constituent of amino acids, proteins and nucleic acids |
| Phosphorus | $\mathrm{P}$ | Component of ATP, nucleic acids and phospholipids |
| Sulfur | $\mathrm{S}$ | Component of certain amino acids and coenzymes |
| Potassium | $\mathrm{K}$ | Osmotic regulation and enzyme activation |
| Calcium | $\mathrm{Ca}$ | Cell wall formation and cell division |
| Magnesium | $\mathrm{Mg}$ | Central atom of chlorophyll and enzyme activation |

### Micronutrients

**Micronutrients** are required in very small or trace quantities, generally at concentrations below $10$ mmole per kg of dry matter.

| Element | Symbol | Major Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Iron | $\mathrm{Fe}$ | Electron transport and chlorophyll synthesis |
| Manganese | $\mathrm{Mn}$ | Enzyme activation and photosynthetic water splitting |
| Copper | $\mathrm{Cu}$ | Electron transport and enzyme activity |
| Molybdenum | $\mathrm{Mo}$ | Component of nitrogenase and nitrate reductase |
| Zinc | $\mathrm{Zn}$ | Enzyme activation and auxin synthesis |
| Boron | $\mathrm{B}$ | Cell wall formation and pollen germination |
| Chlorine | $\mathrm{Cl}$ | Osmotic balance and photosynthetic water splitting |
| Nickel | $\mathrm{Ni}$ | Component of the enzyme urease |

## Physiological Roles and Deficiency Symptoms

Mineral elements perform specific functions in plants. Their deficiency produces characteristic symptoms, which depend partly on their mobility within the plant.

| Element | Physiological Function | Deficiency Symptoms |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Nitrogen ($\mathrm{N}$)** | Constituent of amino acids, proteins, nucleic acids and chlorophyll | General chlorosis, usually beginning in older leaves |
| **Potassium ($\mathrm{K}$)** | Osmotic regulation, stomatal movement and enzyme activation | Marginal chlorosis followed by necrosis of older leaves |
| **Calcium ($\mathrm{Ca}$)** | Formation of calcium pectate in the middle lamella and cell division | Stunted growth and damage to young meristems |
| **Magnesium ($\mathrm{Mg}$)** | Central atom of chlorophyll and activator of photosynthetic and respiratory enzymes | Interveinal chlorosis, especially in older leaves |
| **Boron ($\mathrm{B}$)** | Cell wall formation, pollen germination and carbohydrate transport | Death of shoot and root tips; brown heart in certain crops |
| **Molybdenum ($\mathrm{Mo}$)** | Component of nitrate reductase and nitrogenase | Whiptail disease in cauliflower |

### Mobility of Mineral Elements

The mobility of an element determines where deficiency symptoms appear first.

- **Mobile elements:** Can be transported from older tissues to younger growing regions. Their deficiency symptoms generally appear first in older leaves.
- **Immobile or poorly mobile elements:** Cannot be readily redistributed to younger tissues. Their deficiency symptoms generally appear first in young leaves and growing regions.

| Mobile Elements | Typical First Appearance of Deficiency |
| ----- | ----- |
| Nitrogen ($\mathrm{N}$) | Older leaves |
| Potassium ($\mathrm{K}$) | Older leaves |
| Magnesium ($\mathrm{Mg}$) | Older leaves |
| Phosphorus ($\mathrm{P}$) | Older leaves |

| Immobile or Poorly Mobile Elements | Typical First Appearance of Deficiency |
| ----- | ----- |
| Calcium ($\mathrm{Ca}$) | Young leaves and growing points |
| Sulfur ($\mathrm{S}$) | Young leaves |
| Iron ($\mathrm{Fe}$) | Young leaves |

## Biological Nitrogen Fixation

Atmospheric nitrogen ($\mathrm{N_2}$) is abundant, but most plants cannot directly utilize it. Certain microorganisms convert atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia ($\mathrm{NH_3}$), which plants can subsequently assimilate.

This process is called **biological nitrogen fixation**.

### Nitrogen-Fixing Microorganisms

Important nitrogen-fixing microorganisms include:

- **Rhizobium:** Symbiotic bacteria present in the root nodules of leguminous plants.
- **Azotobacter:** Free-living nitrogen-fixing bacteria.
- **Anabaena:** Nitrogen-fixing cyanobacterium, commonly associated with the aquatic fern *Azolla*.
- **Nostoc:** Nitrogen-fixing cyanobacterium found in aquatic and terrestrial environments.

### Nitrogenase Enzyme Complex

The conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia is catalyzed by the **nitrogenase enzyme complex**.

The overall reaction is:

$$
\mathrm{N_2 + 8H^+ + 8e^- + 16ATP}
$$

$$
\mathrm{\xrightarrow{Nitrogenase} 2NH_3 + H_2 + 16ADP + 16P_i}
$$

Important characteristics:

- Nitrogenase contains iron and molybdenum-containing protein components.
- It catalyzes the reduction of atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia.
- It requires a large amount of ATP.
- It is highly sensitive to oxygen and functions under low-oxygen conditions.

### Role of Leghemoglobin

**Leghemoglobin** is a pink-coloured oxygen-binding pigment found in the root nodules of leguminous plants.

Its main functions are:

1. Binds free oxygen within the root nodule.
2. Maintains a low concentration of free oxygen to protect nitrogenase.
3. Helps supply controlled amounts of oxygen for the respiration of nitrogen-fixing bacteria.

This allows nitrogen fixation to occur while supporting the energy requirements of the bacteria.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Essentiality Criteria:** An element is essential only if its absence prevents completion of the plant life cycle, its function cannot be replaced and it directly participates in metabolism.
- **Macronutrients vs Micronutrients:** The classification depends on the quantity required, not the relative importance of the element.
- **Magnesium vs Iron:** Magnesium is the central atom of chlorophyll, whereas iron is important in electron transport and chlorophyll synthesis.
- **Mobile Element Deficiency:** Deficiencies of nitrogen, potassium and magnesium generally appear first in older leaves.
- **Immobile Element Deficiency:** Calcium and iron deficiencies generally appear first in young tissues.
- **Molybdenum:** It is a component of both nitrogenase and nitrate reductase. Its deficiency can cause whiptail disease in cauliflower.
- **Nitrogenase Sensitivity:** Nitrogenase is oxygen-sensitive and requires a low-oxygen environment for nitrogen fixation.
- **Leghemoglobin:** It binds oxygen and maintains low free oxygen concentrations inside legume root nodules. It does not directly fix nitrogen.
- **Nitrogen Fixation:** Biological nitrogen fixation converts atmospheric $\mathrm{N_2}$ into ammonia, not directly into nitrate.

## Quiz

1. A farmer observes interveinal chlorosis appearing first in the older leaves of a crop plant. Which mineral deficiency is most likely responsible?

- [ ] Calcium
- [x] Magnesium
- [ ] Boron
- [ ] Iron

> Magnesium is the central atom of chlorophyll and is mobile within plants. During deficiency, it can be transported from older leaves to younger tissues, causing chlorosis to appear first in older leaves.

2. Which enzyme complex catalyzes the reduction of atmospheric nitrogen gas ($\mathrm{N_2}$) into ammonia ($\mathrm{NH_3}$) during biological nitrogen fixation?

- [ ] Nitrate reductase
- [ ] Nitrite reductase
- [x] Nitrogenase
- [ ] Glutamine synthetase

> The nitrogenase enzyme complex, containing iron and molybdenum-containing components, catalyzes the energy-intensive reduction of atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia.

3. What is the primary physiological function of leghemoglobin in the root nodules of leguminous plants?

- [ ] Direct transport of sucrose from leaves to bacteria
- [ ] Catalysis of ATP hydrolysis during nitrogen reduction
- [x] Binding oxygen to maintain low free oxygen levels for nitrogenase
- [ ] Storage of synthesized amino acids in root tissues

> Leghemoglobin binds oxygen and maintains a low concentration of free oxygen inside root nodules. This protects the oxygen-sensitive nitrogenase enzyme while allowing controlled oxygen supply for bacterial respiration.

4. Which of the following is a micronutrient required by plants in trace quantities and forms a component of the enzyme urease?

- [ ] Magnesium
- [ ] Calcium
- [x] Nickel
- [ ] Potassium

> Nickel is an essential micronutrient and a component of urease, an enzyme involved in the breakdown of urea.

5. Which mineral deficiency is associated with whiptail disease in cauliflower?

- [ ] Zinc
- [ ] Iron
- [x] Molybdenum
- [ ] Boron

> Molybdenum is required for enzymes such as nitrate reductase. Its deficiency can cause whiptail disease in cauliflower, characterized by abnormal leaf development.

6. Why is nitrogenase protected from high oxygen concentrations inside leguminous root nodules?

- [ ] Nitrogenase requires oxygen to reduce nitrogen.
- [x] Nitrogenase is highly sensitive to oxygen and becomes inactive at high oxygen concentrations.
- [ ] Oxygen directly converts nitrogen into ammonia.
- [ ] Nitrogenase functions only in the presence of atmospheric oxygen.

> Nitrogenase is an oxygen-sensitive enzyme complex. Leghemoglobin binds oxygen and maintains low free oxygen levels inside root nodules, protecting nitrogenase while supporting bacterial respiration.
