Multicellular animals require specialized circulatory systems to transport oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and metabolic wastes throughout the body. The human cardiovascular system consists of **blood, blood vessels, and a four-chambered myogenic heart**. It maintains internal homeostasis by ensuring continuous circulation between tissues and organs.

## Blood Composition and Formed Elements

Blood is a **fluid connective tissue** consisting of approximately 55% plasma and 45% formed elements.

### Plasma

**Plasma** is the liquid component of blood, consisting mainly of water (approximately 90–92%), along with proteins, electrolytes, nutrients, hormones, and metabolic wastes.

Major plasma proteins include:

- **Albumins**: Maintain the osmotic balance of blood.
- **Globulins**: Include antibodies and transport proteins.
- **Fibrinogen**: Essential for blood clotting.

### Formed Elements

The formed elements of blood include erythrocytes, leukocytes, and thrombocytes.

| Formed Element | Characteristics | Major Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Erythrocytes (RBCs)** | Biconcave, non-nucleated cells containing haemoglobin; lifespan approximately 120 days | Transport oxygen and some carbon dioxide |
| **Leukocytes (WBCs)** | Nucleated cells involved in immune defence | Protect against pathogens and foreign substances |
| **Thrombocytes (Platelets)** | Cell fragments produced from megakaryocytes in bone marrow | Participate in blood clotting |

### Types of Leukocytes

Leukocytes are classified into two major groups based on the presence or absence of cytoplasmic granules.

#### Granulocytes

| Type | Approximate Percentage | Major Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Neutrophils** | 60–65% | Phagocytosis of bacteria and foreign particles |
| **Eosinophils** | 2–3% | Involved in allergic responses and defence against parasites |
| **Basophils** | 0.5–1% | Release histamine, heparin, and other inflammatory mediators |

#### Agranulocytes

| Type | Approximate Percentage | Major Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Lymphocytes** | 20–25% | Responsible for adaptive immunity through B cells and T cells |
| **Monocytes** | 6–8% | Differentiate into macrophages and other phagocytic cells in tissues |

### Platelets and Blood Clotting

**Platelets (thrombocytes)** are small, non-nucleated cell fragments released from large bone marrow cells called **megakaryocytes**.

They play an essential role in haemostasis:

1. Platelets adhere to the damaged blood vessel wall.
2. They become activated and release chemical signals.
3. Additional platelets aggregate to form a temporary platelet plug.
4. Coagulation reactions produce fibrin, which stabilizes the clot.

## Human Heart: Structure and Conducting System

The human heart is a muscular, four-chambered organ located in the thoracic cavity, between the lungs. It is enclosed by a double-walled protective membrane called the **pericardium**.

The four chambers are:

- **Right atrium**: Receives deoxygenated blood from the body through the venae cavae.
- **Right ventricle**: Pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs through the pulmonary artery.
- **Left atrium**: Receives oxygenated blood from the lungs through the pulmonary veins.
- **Left ventricle**: Pumps oxygenated blood into the aorta for distribution throughout the body.

### Heart Valves

Valves ensure unidirectional blood flow through the heart.

| Valve | Location | Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Tricuspid valve** | Between right atrium and right ventricle | Prevents backflow into the right atrium |
| **Bicuspid (Mitral) valve** | Between left atrium and left ventricle | Prevents backflow into the left atrium |
| **Pulmonary semilunar valve** | Between right ventricle and pulmonary artery | Prevents backflow into the right ventricle |
| **Aortic semilunar valve** | Between left ventricle and aorta | Prevents backflow into the left ventricle |

### Conducting System of the Heart

The human heart is **myogenic**, meaning its rhythmic contractions originate within the cardiac muscle itself rather than requiring an external nerve impulse to initiate each heartbeat.

The electrical conduction pathway is:

$$
\text{SA Node} \rightarrow \text{AV Node} \rightarrow \text{Bundle of His} \rightarrow \text{Purkinje Fibres}
$$

1. **Sinoatrial (SA) Node**: Located in the right atrium; acts as the natural pacemaker by generating spontaneous electrical impulses.
2. **Atrioventricular (AV) Node**: Receives impulses from the SA node and briefly delays their transmission, allowing the ventricles to fill.
3. **Bundle of His**: Conducts impulses from the AV node into the interventricular septum.
4. **Purkinje Fibres**: Distribute impulses throughout the ventricular walls, coordinating ventricular contraction.

The SA node normally generates impulses at approximately 70–75 beats per minute under resting conditions.

## Human Cardiac Cycle

The **cardiac cycle** refers to the sequence of events involving contraction (systole) and relaxation (diastole) of the heart chambers during one complete heartbeat.

At a heart rate of approximately 75 beats per minute, one cardiac cycle lasts about 0.8 seconds.

### Phases of the Cardiac Cycle

1. **Joint Diastole (0.4 s)**:
   - Both atria and ventricles are relaxed.
   - Atrioventricular valves remain open.
   - Blood flows from the atria into the ventricles.

2. **Atrial Systole (0.1 s)**:
   - Both atria contract.
   - Blood is pushed into the ventricles.
   - Atrial contraction completes ventricular filling.

3. **Ventricular Systole (0.3 s)**:
   - Ventricles contract, increasing ventricular pressure.
   - Atrioventricular valves close, producing the first heart sound (**LUB**).
   - When ventricular pressure exceeds pressure in the aorta and pulmonary artery, semilunar valves open.
   - Blood is ejected into the aorta and pulmonary artery.

4. **Ventricular Diastole**:
   - Ventricles relax and their pressure decreases.
   - Semilunar valves close, producing the second heart sound (**DUB**).
   - When ventricular pressure falls below atrial pressure, the atrioventricular valves open and ventricular filling begins again.

### Heart Sounds

| Heart Sound | Valve Closure | Timing |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **First Sound (LUB)** | Tricuspid and bicuspid (mitral) valves | Beginning of ventricular systole |
| **Second Sound (DUB)** | Aortic and pulmonary semilunar valves | Beginning of ventricular diastole |

## Cardiac Output and Stroke Volume

**Stroke volume** is the volume of blood pumped out by one ventricle during a single heartbeat.

**Cardiac output** is the volume of blood pumped by each ventricle per minute.

\[
\text{Cardiac Output} = \text{Stroke Volume} \times \text{Heart Rate}
\]

For an average resting adult:

- Stroke Volume = 70 mL
- Heart Rate = 72 beats/min

\[
\begin{aligned}
CO &= 70 \times 72 \\
&= 5040 \text{ mL/min} \\
&\approx 5 \text{ L/min}
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore, the average resting cardiac output is approximately **5 litres per minute**.

## Electrocardiogram (ECG)

An **electrocardiogram (ECG)** is a graphical recording of the electrical activity of the heart during a cardiac cycle.

A standard ECG contains three major waves:

| ECG Wave | Electrical Event | Associated Mechanical Event |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **P-wave** | Depolarization of the atria | Initiates atrial contraction |
| **QRS-complex** | Depolarization of the ventricles | Initiates ventricular contraction |
| **T-wave** | Repolarization of the ventricles | Associated with ventricular relaxation |

### Important ECG Concepts

- **P-wave** represents atrial depolarization.
- **QRS-complex** represents ventricular depolarization. Atrial repolarization occurs simultaneously and is normally hidden by this complex.
- **T-wave** represents ventricular repolarization.
- The number of QRS complexes recorded per minute can be used to determine heart rate.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Myogenic vs Neurogenic Heart**: The human heart is myogenic because the heartbeat originates within specialized cardiac tissue, particularly the SA node.
- **SA Node vs AV Node**: The SA node is the natural pacemaker, while the AV node delays electrical impulses before they reach the ventricles.
- **LUB vs DUB**: LUB results from closure of atrioventricular valves; DUB results from closure of semilunar valves.
- **QRS vs T-wave**: QRS represents ventricular depolarization, whereas T-wave represents ventricular repolarization.
- **Platelets vs Megakaryocytes**: Platelets are cell fragments derived from megakaryocytes; they are not complete nucleated cells.
- **Right vs Left Ventricle**: The right ventricle pumps blood to the lungs, while the left ventricle pumps blood to the entire body.
- **Cardiac Output vs Stroke Volume**: Stroke volume is blood pumped per beat, whereas cardiac output is blood pumped per minute.
- **ECG and Heart Rate**: Counting QRS complexes over a known time interval provides the heart rate.
- **Pulmonary Artery vs Pulmonary Vein**: The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs, while pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood to the left atrium.

## Quiz

1. On a standard clinical electrocardiogram (ECG), which deflection represents the electrical depolarization of the ventricles immediately before ventricular contraction?

- [ ] P-wave
- [x] QRS-complex
- [ ] T-wave
- [ ] U-wave

> The QRS-complex represents ventricular depolarization, which initiates ventricular contraction (systole). Atrial repolarization occurs simultaneously but is generally hidden by the QRS-complex.

2. During ventricular contraction, the sudden closure of which heart valves produces the characteristic first heart sound (LUB)?

- [x] Atrioventricular valves (tricuspid and bicuspid/mitral)
- [ ] Semilunar valves of the aorta and pulmonary artery
- [ ] Eustachian valve of the inferior vena cava
- [ ] Thebesian valve of the coronary sinus

> Closure of the atrioventricular valves at the beginning of ventricular systole produces the first heart sound (LUB), preventing blood from flowing backward into the atria.

3. An athlete has a resting heart rate of 60 beats per minute and a stroke volume of 80 mL per contraction. What is the individual's cardiac output?

- [ ] 4200 mL/min
- [x] 4800 mL/min
- [ ] 5000 mL/min
- [ ] 5600 mL/min

> Cardiac output is calculated by multiplying stroke volume by heart rate:
>
> \[
> CO = 80 \times 60 = 4800 \text{ mL/min}
> \]

4. Which component of blood is responsible for releasing histamine and heparin during inflammatory and allergic responses?

- [ ] Neutrophils
- [ ] Eosinophils
- [x] Basophils
- [ ] Lymphocytes

> Basophils are granulocytes that release histamine and heparin. Histamine contributes to inflammatory and allergic responses, while heparin acts as an anticoagulant.

5. Which structure of the cardiac conduction system briefly delays the electrical impulse before it reaches the ventricles?

- [ ] SA node
- [x] AV node
- [ ] Bundle of His
- [ ] Purkinje fibres

> The atrioventricular (AV) node briefly delays the electrical impulse received from the SA node. This delay allows the atria to complete ventricular filling before ventricular contraction begins.
