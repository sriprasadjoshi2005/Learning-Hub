## title: Biomolecules and Their Functions
level: foundation

All living organisms are composed of organic macromolecules built from smaller monomeric units. The four major classes of biomolecules are **carbohydrates**, **proteins**, **lipids**, and **nucleic acids**.

## Major Biomolecules Summary

| Biomolecule | Monomer / Building Block | Linkage / Bond Type | Key Functions | Diagnostic Tests |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Carbohydrates** | Monosaccharides (e.g., glucose) | Glycosidic bond | Primary energy source ($\mathrm{4\ kcal/g}$), structural components (cellulose, chitin) | Iodine test (starch), Benedict's test (reducing sugars) |
| **Proteins** | Amino acids ($20$ standard types) | Peptide bond | Enzymatic catalysis, structural support, transport, immunity | Biuret test (violet color for peptide bonds) |
| **Lipids** | Fatty acids + glycerol | Ester bond | Long-term energy storage ($\mathrm{9\ kcal/g}$), cell membrane backbone | Sudan III / Emulsion test |
| **Nucleic Acids** | Nucleotides (sugar + base + phosphate) | Phosphodiester bond | Storage and expression of genetic information ($\mathrm{DNA}$, $\mathrm{RNA}$) | Dische diphenylamine test |

## Structure and Function of Enzymes

Enzymes are biological catalysts (mostly proteins, except ribozymes) that accelerate chemical reactions by lower the **activation energy** ($\mathrm{E_a}$) without being consumed in the process.

$$
\text{Substrate (S)} + \text{Enzyme (E)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Enzyme-Substrate Complex (ES)} \longrightarrow \text{Enzyme-Product Complex (EP)} \longrightarrow \text{Enzyme (E)} + \text{Product (P)}
$$

### Enzyme Inhibition Patterns
1. **Competitive Inhibition**: Inhibitor closely resembles the substrate and binds to the **active site**. $\mathrm{V_{max}}$ remains unchanged, but $\mathrm{K_m}$ increases. (Can be overcome by increasing substrate concentration).
2. **Non-Competitive Inhibition**: Inhibitor binds to an **allosteric site** (site other than active site). $\mathrm{V_{max}}$ decreases, while $\mathrm{K_m}$ remains unchanged.

## Common Exam Traps

* **$\mathrm{K_m}$ (Michaelis Constant) Relationship**: High $\mathrm{K_m}$ indicates **low affinity** of enzyme for substrate; low $\mathrm{K_m}$ indicates **high affinity**.
* **Lipids are not true polymers**: Unlike proteins, nucleic acids, and polysaccharides, lipids are not macromolecular polymers because they are not formed by repeating monomer units linked end-to-end.

## Quiz

1. An inhibitor is added to an enzymatic reaction mixture. Kinetic studies reveal that adding a large excess of natural substrate completely restores the maximum reaction velocity ($\mathrm{V_{max}}$) to control levels. What type of inhibition is occurring, and how is the Michaelis constant ($\mathrm{K_m}$) affected?

* \[ \] Non-competitive inhibition; $\mathrm{K_m}$ decreases
* \[ \] Competitive inhibition; $\mathrm{K_m}$ decreases
* \[x\] Competitive inhibition; $\mathrm{K_m}$ increases
* \[ \] Uncompetitive inhibition; $\mathrm{K_m}$ remains unchanged

> In competitive inhibition, the inhibitor binds reversibly to the active site. High substrate concentrations outcompete the inhibitor, restoring $\mathrm{V_{max}}$. However, more substrate is required to reach half-maximal velocity, which increases the numerical value of $\mathrm{K_m}$.

2. Which biomolecule acts as a structural component in plant cell walls and consists of an unbranched polymer of $\mathrm{D}$-glucose units connected by $\beta\mathrm{-(1\rightarrow 4)}$ glycosidic linkages?

* \[ \] Amylose
* \[ \] Glycogen
* \[x\] Cellulose
* \[ \] Chitin

> Cellulose is a linear polysaccharide of glucose units linked by $\beta\mathrm{-(1\rightarrow 4)}$ glycosidic bonds, giving it high mechanical strength. Amylose has $\alpha\mathrm{-(1\rightarrow 4)}$ bonds, glycogen is branched with $\alpha\mathrm{-(1\rightarrow 6)}$ linkages, and chitin is made of N-acetylglucosamine subunits.

3. Amino acids are amphoteric molecules containing both an amino group ($\mathrm{-NH_2}$) and a carboxyl group ($\mathrm{-COOH}$). At a specific $\mathrm{pH}$ known as the isoelectric point ($\mathrm{pI}$), an amino acid exists as a dipolar ion with no net electrical charge. What is this form called?

* \[ \] Anion
* \[ \] Cation
* \[x\] Zwitterion
* \[ \] Radical

> At its isoelectric point ($\mathrm{pI}$), an amino acid forms a Zwitterion where the carboxyl group carries a negative charge ($\mathrm{-COO^-}$) and the amino group carries a positive charge ($\mathrm{-NH_3^+}$), yielding a net neutral charge.
