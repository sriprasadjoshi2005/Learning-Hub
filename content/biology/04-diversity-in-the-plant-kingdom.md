title: Diversity in the Plant Kingdom
level: foundation
---

Kingdom **Plantae** includes multicellular, photosynthetic eukaryotic organisms. Plants exhibit diverse morphological and anatomical adaptations and are classified into five major groups: **Algae**, **Bryophytes**, **Pteridophytes**, **Gymnosperms**, and **Angiosperms**.

## Major Plant Groups Overview

| Group | Vascular System | Dominant Phase | Reproduction / Seed Status | Key Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Algae** | Absent (Thalloid) | Gametophyte ($n$) in most | Spores; no embryo formation | *Spirogyra*, *Volvox*, *Fucus* |
| **Bryophytes** | Absent (Avascular) | Gametophyte ($n$) | Spores; requires $\mathrm{H_2O}$ for fertilisation | *Funaria*, *Marchantia*, *Sphagnum* |
| **Pteridophytes** | Present (Vascular) | Sporophyte ($2n$) | Spores; seedless | *Dryopteris*, *Equisetum*, *Selaginella* |
| **Gymnosperms** | Present | Sporophyte ($2n$) | Naked seeds (ovules not enclosed by an ovary) | *Pinus*, *Cycas*, *Ginkgo* |
| **Angiosperms** | Present | Sporophyte ($2n$) | Enclosed seeds inside fruits | Monocots and Dicots |

## Alternation of Generations and Life Cycles

Plants exhibit **alternation of generations**, in which two multicellular or specialised phases alternate during their life cycle:

- **Gametophyte ($n$)**: Haploid phase that produces gametes through mitosis.
- **Sporophyte ($2n$)**: Diploid phase that produces haploid spores through meiosis.

The general life cycle follows this sequence:

$$
\text{Gametophyte } (n) \xrightarrow{\text{Mitosis}} \text{Gametes } (n) \xrightarrow{\text{Syngamy}} \text{Zygote } (2n) \xrightarrow{\text{Mitosis}} \text{Sporophyte } (2n) \xrightarrow{\text{Meiosis}} \text{Spores } (n)
$$

The three major types of plant life cycles are:

- **Haplontic Life Cycle**: The gametophytic phase is dominant, free-living, and photosynthetic. The sporophytic phase is represented only by the single-celled zygote, which undergoes meiosis to produce haploid spores (e.g., *Volvox*, *Spirogyra*).
- **Diplontic Life Cycle**: The sporophyte is the dominant, photosynthetic, and independent phase. The gametophyte is highly reduced (e.g., Gymnosperms, Angiosperms, and *Fucus*).
- **Haplo-diplontic Life Cycle**: Both gametophytic and sporophytic phases are multicellular and independent or partially dependent on each other (e.g., Bryophytes, Pteridophytes, *Ectocarpus*, and Kelps).

## Key Evolutionary Milestones

1. **Bryophytes – Amphibians of the Plant Kingdom**: Bryophytes are called the amphibians of the plant kingdom because they live on land but require liquid water for the movement of motile, flagellated male gametes (**antherozoids**) towards the female reproductive organ (**archegonium**).
2. **Pteridophytes – First Terrestrial Vascular Plants**: Pteridophytes possess well-developed vascular tissues, namely **xylem** and **phloem**, for the transport of water, minerals, and food. Primitive forms generally lack true vessels in xylem and companion cells in phloem.
3. **Origin of Seed Habit**: **Heterospory**, the production of two types of spores (microspores and megaspores), was an important evolutionary development. Retention of the megaspore within the megasporangium, as seen in *Selaginella*, represents an evolutionary precursor to the seed habit found in Gymnosperms and Angiosperms.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Confusing dominant phases**: The **gametophyte ($n$)** is dominant in Bryophytes, whereas the **sporophyte ($2n$)** is dominant in Pteridophytes, Gymnosperms, and Angiosperms.
- **Naked vs enclosed seeds**: Gymnosperms produce naked seeds because their ovules are **not enclosed within an ovary**. Angiosperms produce seeds enclosed within fruits.
- **Bryophyte water dependency**: Although bryophytes are terrestrial plants, they require water for fertilisation because their male gametes are motile and flagellated.
- **Exceptions in life cycles**: Most algae are haplontic, but *Fucus* is **diplontic**, while *Ectocarpus* and *Polysiphonia* exhibit **haplo-diplontic** life cycles.
- **Heterospory and seed evolution**: Heterospory in plants such as *Selaginella* is an important evolutionary precursor to the origin of seeds.

## Quiz

1. A botanist discovers a non-vascular terrestrial plant where the sporophyte is physically attached to and nutritionally dependent on the multicellular haploid gametophyte. To which group does this plant belong?
  - [ ] Gymnosperms
  - [x] Bryophytes
  - [ ] Pteridophytes
  - [ ] Angiosperms
   > In Bryophytes, the haploid gametophyte is the dominant and independent phase, while the diploid sporophyte remains attached to and nutritionally dependent on the gametophyte.

2. Heterospory (production of microspores and megaspores) in species like *Selaginella* is considered a significant evolutionary step because it led to:
  - [ ] Evolution of motile flagellated sperm
  - [ ] Development of non-vascular transport
  - [x] Origin of the seed habit
  - [ ] Dominance of the haplontic life cycle
   > Heterospory and the retention of the female gametophyte within the megasporangium in heterosporous pteridophytes laid the essential evolutionary foundation for the development of the seed habit.

3. Which of the following algae exhibits a diplontic life cycle despite belonging to a predominantly haplontic group?
  - [ ] *Spirogyra*
  - [ ] *Volvox*
  - [x] *Fucus*
  - [ ] *Chlamydomonas*
   > While most algae follow haplontic or haplo-diplontic life cycles, the brown alga *Fucus* exhibits a diplontic life cycle in which the diploid sporophyte is the dominant phase.
