## title: Diversity in the Plant Kingdom
level: foundation

Kingdom Plantae includes multicellular, photosynthetic eukaryotic organisms. Plants show diverse morphological and anatomical adaptations, categorized into five major groups: **Algae**, **Bryophytes**, **Pteridophytes**, **Gymnosperms**, and **Angiosperms**.

## Major Plant Groups Overview

| Group | Vascular System | Dominant Phase | Reproduction / Seed Status | Key Examples |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Algae** | Absent (Thalloid) | Gametophyte ($n$) | Spores; non-embryonic | *Spirogyra*, *Volvox*, *Fucus* |
| **Bryophytes** | Absent (Avascular) | Gametophyte ($n$) | Spores; depends on $\mathrm{H_2O}$ for fertilisation | *Funaria*, *Marchantia*, *Sphagnum* |
| **Pteridophytes** | Present (Vascular) | Sporophyte ($2n$) | Spores; seedless | *Dryopteris*, *Equisetum*, *Selaginella* |
| **Gymnosperms** | Present | Sporophyte ($2n$) | Naked seeds (no ovary wall) | *Pinus*, *Cycas*, *Ginkgo* |
| **Angiosperms** | Present | Sporophyte ($2n$) | Enclosed seeds inside fruits | *Monocots* and *Dicots* |

## Alternation of Generations and Life Cycles

Plants alternate between a haploid gamete-producing phase (**gametophyte**) and a diploid spore-producing phase (**sporophyte**):

$$\text{Gametophyte } (n) \xrightarrow{\text{Mitosis}} \text{Gametes } (n) \xrightarrow{\text{Syngamy}} \text{Zygote } (2n) \xrightarrow{\text{Mitosis}} \text{Sporophyte } (2n) \xrightarrow{\text{Meiosis}} \text{Spore } (n)$$

* **Haplontic Life Cycle**: Gametophytic phase is dominant, free-living, and photosynthetic. Sporophytic phase is represented only by the single-celled zygote (e.g., *Volvox*, *Spirogyra*).
* **Diplontic Life Cycle**: Sporophyte is dominant, photosynthetic, and independent. Gametophyte is reduced (e.g., Gymnosperms, Angiosperms, *Fucus*).
* **Haplo-diplontic Life Cycle**: Both phases are multicellular and intermediate (e.g., Bryophytes, Pteridophytes, *Ectocarpus*, Kelps).

## Key Evolutionary Milestones

1. **Amphibians of Plant Kingdom**: Bryophytes require liquid water for motile flagellated antherozoids (male gametes) to swim to female archegonia.
2. **First Terrestrial Vascular Plants**: Pteridophytes possess true xylem and phloem (though xylem lacks true vessels and phloem lacks companion cells in primitive forms).
3. **Origin of Seed Habit**: Retaining the megaspore inside the megasporangium (seen precursor-wise in heterosporous pteridophytes like *Selaginella*) paved the way for seed evolution in Gymnosperms and Angiosperms.

## Common Exam Traps

* **Confusing dominant phases**: Gametophyte ($n$) is dominant in Bryophytes; Sporophyte ($2n$) is dominant in Pteridophytes, Gymnosperms, and Angiosperms.
* **Naked vs enclosed seeds**: Gymnosperms form seeds, but they lack fruits because ovules are **not** enclosed by an ovary wall.
* **Exceptions in life cycles**: Most algae are haplontic, but *Fucus* (a brown alga) is **diplontic**, and *Ectocarpus* / *Polysiphonia* are **haplo-diplontic**.

## Quiz

1. A botanist discovers a non-vascular terrestrial plant where the sporophyte is physically attached to and nutritionally dependent on the multicellular haploid gametophyte. To which group does this plant belong?

* \[ \] Gymnosperms
* \[x\] Bryophytes
* \[ \] Pteridophytes
* \[ \] Angiosperms

> In Bryophytes, the haploid gametophyte is the dominant, independent phase, while the diploid sporophyte remains attached to and dependent on the gametophyte for nutrition.

2. Heterospory (production of microspores and megaspores) in species like *Selaginella* is considered a significant evolutionary step because it led to:

* \[ \] Evolution of motile flagellated sperm
* \[ \] Development of non-vascular transport
* \[x\] Origin of the seed habit
* \[ \] Dominance of the haplontic life cycle

> Heterospory and the retention of the female gametophyte inside the megasporangium in heterosporous pteridophytes laid the essential foundation for seed habit evolution.

3. Which of the following algae exhibits a diplontic life cycle despite belonging to a predominantly haplontic group?

* \[ \] *Spirogyra*
* \[ \] *Volvox*
* \[x\] *Fucus*
* \[ \] *Chlamydomonas*

> While most green and brown algae follow a haplontic or haplo-diplontic life cycle, the brown alga *Fucus* exhibits a diplontic life cycle where the diploid sporophyte is the dominant phase.
