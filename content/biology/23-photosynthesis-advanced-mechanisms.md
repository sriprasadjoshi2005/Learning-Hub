Photosynthesis proceeds through two interconnected phases: the **Light-Dependent Reactions**, which convert light energy into chemical energy stored in ATP and NADPH, and the **Carbon Fixation Reactions**, which use ATP and NADPH to assimilate carbon dioxide into carbohydrates.

## Light-Dependent Reactions and the Z-Scheme

The light-dependent reactions occur in the **thylakoid membranes** of chloroplasts. They involve two photosystems:

- **Photosystem II (PS II)**: Contains the reaction-centre chlorophyll *a*, called $\mathrm{P_{680}}$, which absorbs light most effectively at approximately $680\text{ nm}$.
- **Photosystem I (PS I)**: Contains the reaction-centre chlorophyll *a*, called $\mathrm{P_{700}}$, which absorbs light most effectively at approximately $700\text{ nm}$.

Both photosystems work together to transfer electrons and generate ATP and NADPH.

### Non-Cyclic Photophosphorylation (Z-Scheme)

Non-cyclic photophosphorylation involves the movement of electrons from water to NADP$^+$ through both photosystems.

### Electron Flow

1. **Photolysis of water**: Light energy drives the splitting of water molecules at PS II, releasing electrons, protons, and oxygen.
2. **Photosystem II**: Electrons replace those lost by the $\mathrm{P_{680}}$ reaction centre after light absorption.
3. **Electron Transport Chain (ETC)**: Electrons move through carriers, including plastoquinone, the cytochrome $b_6f$ complex, and plastocyanin.
4. **Photosystem I**: Electrons reach PS I and are re-energized by another photon of light.
5. **NADPH formation**: Electrons pass through ferredoxin to NADP$^+$ reductase, which reduces NADP$^+$ to NADPH.

The photolysis of water is represented as:

$$
\mathrm{2H_2O \rightarrow 4H^+ + 4e^- + O_2}
$$

**Products of non-cyclic photophosphorylation:**

- ATP
- NADPH
- Oxygen ($\mathrm{O_2}$)

### Cyclic Photophosphorylation

Cyclic photophosphorylation involves only **Photosystem I**. The excited electrons return to the electron transport chain instead of reducing NADP$^+$.

- Electrons from PS I pass through ferredoxin and return to the cytochrome $b_6f$ complex through an electron transport pathway.
- Their transport contributes to proton movement into the thylakoid lumen.
- The resulting proton gradient drives ATP synthesis.

**Characteristics:**

- Involves only PS I.
- Commonly associated with stroma lamellae.
- Produces ATP only.
- Does not produce NADPH or release oxygen.
- Helps provide additional ATP when the Calvin cycle requires more ATP relative to NADPH.

## Chemiosmotic Hypothesis and ATP Synthesis

The chemiosmotic hypothesis, proposed by **Peter Mitchell**, explains how ATP is synthesized using a proton gradient across a membrane.

In chloroplasts, ATP synthesis is driven by the electrochemical proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane.

### Formation of the Proton Gradient

Protons ($\mathrm{H^+}$) accumulate inside the **thylakoid lumen** through three major mechanisms:

1. **Photolysis of water**: Splitting water releases protons directly into the thylakoid lumen.
2. **Electron transport**: The cytochrome $b_6f$ complex contributes to proton accumulation in the lumen.
3. **Reduction of NADP$^+$**: Protons are consumed from the stroma during NADPH formation, helping maintain the proton gradient.

### ATP Synthase Mechanism

The ATP synthase complex consists of two major components:

| Component | Location | Function |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **CF$_0$** | Embedded in the thylakoid membrane | Forms the proton channel through which $\mathrm{H^+}$ flows. |
| **CF$_1$** | Projects into the stroma | Catalyses ATP synthesis from ADP and inorganic phosphate ($\mathrm{P_i}$). |

As protons move from the thylakoid lumen into the stroma through ATP synthase, the released energy drives:

$$
\mathrm{ADP + P_i \rightarrow ATP}
$$

**Key concept**: ATP synthesis is powered by the movement of protons down their electrochemical gradient, not directly by light.

## Carbon Fixation Pathways

The carbon fixation reactions use ATP and NADPH produced during the light-dependent reactions to convert inorganic carbon into organic compounds.

Three major pathways are:

- **C$_3$ pathway**: Calvin cycle.
- **C$_4$ pathway**: Hatch-Slack pathway.
- **CAM pathway**: Crassulacean Acid Metabolism.

### 1. C$_3$ Pathway (Calvin Cycle)

The Calvin cycle occurs in the **stroma of chloroplasts** and is the primary carbon fixation pathway in most plants.

It is called the C$_3$ pathway because its first stable product is a three-carbon compound.

### Main Components

| Feature | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Primary CO$_2$ acceptor** | Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate ($\mathrm{RuBP}$), a five-carbon compound. |
| **Key enzyme** | RuBisCO (Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase). |
| **First stable product** | 3-phosphoglycerate ($\mathrm{3-PGA}$), a three-carbon compound. |
| **Location** | Chloroplast stroma. |
| **Energy requirement per glucose** | 18 ATP and 12 NADPH. |

### Three Stages of the Calvin Cycle

**1. Carboxylation**

- Carbon dioxide combines with RuBP.
- The reaction is catalysed by RuBisCO.
- The unstable six-carbon intermediate immediately splits into two molecules of 3-PGA.

$$
\mathrm{RuBP + CO_2 \rightarrow 2(3\text{-}PGA)}
$$

**2. Reduction**

- 3-PGA is phosphorylated using ATP.
- It is subsequently reduced using NADPH.
- The resulting product is glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P), a three-carbon sugar.

**3. Regeneration**

- Most G3P molecules are used to regenerate RuBP.
- ATP is required for this regeneration.
- A small portion of G3P leaves the cycle and contributes to carbohydrate synthesis.

### Energy Requirement

For the fixation of one carbon dioxide molecule:

$$
\mathrm{3\ ATP + 2\ NADPH}
$$

For the synthesis of one glucose molecule, six carbon dioxide molecules must be fixed:

$$
\mathrm{6 \times 3 = 18\ ATP}
$$

$$
\mathrm{6 \times 2 = 12\ NADPH}
$$

Therefore, the total requirement is:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{18\ ATP + 12\ NADPH}}
$$

### 2. C$_4$ Pathway (Hatch-Slack Pathway)

The C$_4$ pathway is an adaptation found in plants such as **maize, sugarcane, and sorghum**. It helps reduce photorespiration by concentrating carbon dioxide around RuBisCO.

### Kranz Anatomy

C$_4$ plants possess a specialized leaf structure called **Kranz anatomy**.

- Vascular bundles are surrounded by large bundle sheath cells.
- Bundle sheath cells are surrounded by mesophyll cells.
- Bundle sheath cells contain numerous chloroplasts and carry out the Calvin cycle.
- Mesophyll cells initially fix carbon dioxide using PEP carboxylase.

### C$_4$ Carbon Fixation Mechanism

**Step 1: Initial CO$_2$ fixation in mesophyll cells**

- Carbon dioxide combines with phosphoenolpyruvate ($\mathrm{PEP}$), a three-carbon compound.
- The reaction is catalysed by PEP carboxylase.
- The first stable product is oxaloacetic acid ($\mathrm{OAA}$), a four-carbon compound.

$$
\mathrm{PEP\ (3C) + CO_2 \rightarrow OAA\ (4C)}
$$

**Step 2: Transport to bundle sheath cells**

- OAA is commonly converted into malate or aspartate.
- These four-carbon compounds are transported to bundle sheath cells.

**Step 3: CO$_2$ release**

- Malate is decarboxylated, releasing carbon dioxide.
- The released carbon dioxide becomes concentrated around RuBisCO.
- Pyruvate is produced as a three-carbon compound.

**Step 4: Calvin cycle**

- RuBisCO fixes the released carbon dioxide through the Calvin cycle.
- Pyruvate returns to mesophyll cells, where it is used to regenerate PEP using ATP.

### C$_3$ vs C$_4$ Plants

| Feature | C$_3$ Plants | C$_4$ Plants |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| First stable product | 3-PGA (3C) | OAA (4C) |
| Primary CO$_2$ acceptor | RuBP | PEP |
| Initial carboxylating enzyme | RuBisCO | PEP carboxylase |
| Initial carbon fixation site | Mesophyll cells | Mesophyll cells |
| Calvin cycle location | Mesophyll chloroplasts | Bundle sheath chloroplasts |
| Kranz anatomy | Absent | Present |
| Photorespiration | More significant under hot conditions | Very low under normal conditions |
| Examples | Wheat, rice, potato | Maize, sugarcane, sorghum |

**Advantage of C$_4$ plants**: Their carbon-concentrating mechanism increases photosynthetic efficiency under high temperatures, intense light, and conditions where stomatal closure reduces internal carbon dioxide availability.

### 3. Photorespiration (C$_2$ Cycle)

Photorespiration occurs when RuBisCO acts as an **oxygenase** instead of a carboxylase.

- At high oxygen concentrations and low carbon dioxide availability, RuBisCO binds oxygen instead of carbon dioxide.
- RuBP reacts with oxygen to produce one molecule of 3-PGA and one molecule of 2-phosphoglycolate.
- The 2-phosphoglycolate is recycled through a pathway involving chloroplasts, peroxisomes, and mitochondria.

**Consequences:**

- Carbon is lost as carbon dioxide.
- Energy is consumed during the recycling process.
- No ATP or NADPH is produced by photorespiration.
- It reduces the net photosynthetic efficiency of C$_3$ plants.

C$_4$ plants minimize photorespiration by maintaining a high carbon dioxide concentration around RuBisCO in bundle sheath cells.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Photosystem order**: Electrons flow through PS II first and then PS I during non-cyclic photophosphorylation.
- **Reaction centre wavelengths**: PS II contains P$_{680}$, while PS I contains P$_{700}$.
- **Non-cyclic photophosphorylation**: Produces ATP, NADPH, and oxygen.
- **Cyclic photophosphorylation**: Involves only PS I and produces ATP, without NADPH or oxygen.
- **Proton accumulation**: Protons accumulate inside the thylakoid lumen, not the stroma.
- **ATP synthase orientation**: CF$_1$ projects into the stroma, where ATP is synthesized.
- **Calvin cycle location**: Occurs in the chloroplast stroma, not in the thylakoid membranes.
- **C$_3$ pathway**: RuBP is the primary carbon dioxide acceptor, and 3-PGA is the first stable product.
- **C$_4$ pathway**: PEP is the initial carbon dioxide acceptor, and OAA is the first stable product.
- **C$_4$ enzymes**: PEP carboxylase operates in mesophyll cells, while RuBisCO operates in bundle sheath cells.
- **Energy requirement**: One glucose molecule requires 18 ATP and 12 NADPH in the Calvin cycle.
- **Photorespiration**: It consumes energy and releases carbon dioxide, reducing photosynthetic efficiency.

## Quiz

1. How many total molecules of ATP and NADPH are consumed in the stroma of a C$_3$ plant to synthesize one molecule of glucose through the Calvin cycle?

- [ ] 12 ATP and 12 NADPH
- [x] 18 ATP and 12 NADPH
- [ ] 30 ATP and 12 NADPH
- [ ] 18 ATP and 18 NADPH

> Fixing one CO$_2$ molecule requires 3 ATP and 2 NADPH. One glucose molecule requires six CO$_2$ molecules, so the total requirement is 18 ATP and 12 NADPH.

2. Tropical plants such as maize and sugarcane reduce photorespiration by concentrating CO$_2$ around RuBisCO in specialized bundle sheath cells. What structural feature characterizes this adaptation?

- [ ] Aerenchyma
- [x] Kranz anatomy
- [ ] Velamen tissue
- [ ] Bulliform cells

> Kranz anatomy consists of bundle sheath cells arranged around vascular bundles and surrounded by mesophyll cells. This arrangement supports the C$_4$ carbon-concentrating mechanism.

3. During non-cyclic photophosphorylation, where do protons ($\mathrm{H^+}$) accumulate to build the electrochemical gradient required for ATP synthesis?

- [ ] Chloroplast stroma
- [x] Thylakoid lumen
- [ ] Intermembrane space
- [ ] Peroxisomal matrix

> Water splitting and electron transport contribute to proton accumulation inside the thylakoid lumen, creating a gradient that drives ATP synthesis as protons flow back into the stroma.

4. Which photosystem contains the reaction-centre chlorophyll called P$_{700}$?

- [ ] Photosystem II
- [x] Photosystem I
- [ ] Both photosystems
- [ ] Neither photosystem

> Photosystem I contains P$_{700}$, which absorbs light most effectively at approximately 700 nm. Photosystem II contains P$_{680}$.

5. Which enzyme catalyses the initial fixation of carbon dioxide in the mesophyll cells of C$_4$ plants?

- [ ] RuBisCO
- [x] PEP carboxylase
- [ ] ATP synthase
- [ ] NADP$^+$ reductase

> PEP carboxylase combines carbon dioxide with phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) to form oxaloacetic acid (OAA), the first stable four-carbon product of the C$_4$ pathway.

6. What is the main reason C$_4$ plants experience significantly less photorespiration than C$_3$ plants?

- [ ] They do not contain chlorophyll.
- [ ] They do not require the Calvin cycle.
- [x] They concentrate carbon dioxide around RuBisCO in bundle sheath cells.
- [ ] They use oxygen instead of carbon dioxide during carbon fixation.

> C$_4$ plants use a carbon-concentrating mechanism to maintain high CO$_2$ levels around RuBisCO. This favours its carboxylase activity over its oxygenase activity and suppresses photorespiration.
