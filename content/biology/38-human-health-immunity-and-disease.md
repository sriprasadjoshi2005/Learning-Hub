Health is a state of complete **physical, mental, and social well-being**, not merely the absence of disease. Human health is influenced by nutrition, lifestyle, environmental conditions, and infectious agents. The immune system protects the body against pathogens and other foreign substances through specialized defense mechanisms.

## Types of Immunity

Immunity is the ability of the body to resist infections and defend itself against disease-causing organisms and foreign substances.

The immune system is broadly divided into **Innate Immunity** and **Acquired Immunity**.

### 1. Innate Immunity

Innate immunity is the non-specific defense mechanism present from birth. It provides immediate protection against a wide range of pathogens.

It consists of four major types of barriers:

| Barrier Type | Mechanism | Examples |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Physical Barriers** | Prevent the entry of pathogens. | Skin, mucous membranes |
| **Physiological Barriers** | Chemical conditions or secretions inhibit pathogens. | HCl in the stomach, lysozyme in tears and saliva |
| **Cellular Barriers** | Immune cells engulf and destroy pathogens. | Neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, natural killer cells |
| **Cytokine Barriers** | Proteins interfere with viral replication and coordinate immune responses. | Interferons released by virus-infected cells |

### 2. Acquired Immunity

Acquired immunity develops after exposure to specific antigens. It is highly specific and possesses **immunological memory**, allowing the body to respond more effectively during subsequent exposure.

It has two major components:

| Type | Main Cells | Mechanism |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Humoral Immunity** | B-lymphocytes | B-cells differentiate into plasma cells that produce antibodies against specific antigens. |
| **Cell-Mediated Immunity** | T-lymphocytes | T-cells recognize antigen-bearing cells and coordinate immune responses or destroy infected and abnormal cells. |

### Primary and Secondary Immune Responses

| Feature | Primary Response | Secondary Response |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Exposure | First exposure to an antigen | Subsequent exposure to the same antigen |
| Response Speed | Relatively slow | Rapid |
| Antibody Production | Lower | Higher |
| Duration | Usually shorter | Usually longer |
| Main Reason | Initial activation of naive lymphocytes | Activation of memory cells |

**Memory cells** are responsible for the faster and stronger secondary immune response.

## Antibodies and Their Structure

Antibodies, also called **immunoglobulins**, are specialized proteins produced by plasma cells in response to specific antigens.

### Antibody Structure (H₂L₂)

An antibody molecule typically consists of four polypeptide chains:

- Two identical **heavy chains (H)**.
- Two identical **light chains (L)**.
- Chains are held together by disulfide bonds.
- The molecule has a characteristic Y-shaped structure.
- Variable regions at the tips recognize and bind specific antigens.
- Constant regions determine the antibody class and participate in immune functions.

### Major Classes of Immunoglobulins

| Antibody | Major Function |
| ----- | ----- |
| **IgG** | Most abundant antibody in blood; can cross the placenta and provide passive immunity to the fetus. |
| **IgA** | Found in secretions such as saliva, tears, mucus, and colostrum; protects mucosal surfaces. |
| **IgM** | First antibody produced during the primary immune response; also highly effective at activating complement. |
| **IgE** | Involved in allergic reactions; binds to mast cells and basophils and promotes histamine release. |
| **IgD** | Primarily functions as an antigen receptor on the surface of naive B-lymphocytes. |

**Important:** IgG is the major antibody transferred across the placenta, whereas IgA is abundant in colostrum and breast milk.

## Active and Passive Immunity

Immunity can also be classified according to how antibodies or immune protection are acquired.

### 1. Active Immunity

- Develops when the body's own immune system responds to an antigen.
- Antibodies and memory cells are produced.
- Protection generally develops more slowly but can be long-lasting.

**Examples:**
- Immunity developed after recovering from an infection.
- Immunity developed after vaccination.

### 2. Passive Immunity

- Develops when ready-made antibodies are transferred from another source.
- Protection is immediate but temporary.
- The recipient does not develop immunological memory from the transferred antibodies alone.

**Examples:**
- Maternal IgG transferred through the placenta.
- Maternal IgA transferred through colostrum.
- Administration of antibody-containing antiserum or immunoglobulin preparations.

### Comparison

| Feature | Active Immunity | Passive Immunity |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Source | Body produces its own immune response | Ready-made antibodies are received |
| Onset | Relatively slow | Immediate |
| Duration | Usually longer | Temporary |
| Memory Cells | Formed | Not formed by the transferred antibodies |
| Example | Vaccination | Maternal antibodies |

## Vaccination and Immunisation

Vaccination introduces an antigen or antigenic material into the body to stimulate an immune response without causing the intended full-blown disease.

- Vaccines stimulate the production of antibodies and memory cells.
- Subsequent exposure to the same pathogen triggers a faster secondary immune response.
- Vaccines may contain weakened pathogens, inactivated pathogens, purified antigens, or genetic instructions for producing specific antigens.

### Principles of Vaccination

    First Exposure to Vaccine
              |
       Antigen Recognition
              |
       Activation of B-cells
         and T-cells
              |
       Antibody Production
              |
         Memory Cells
              |
       Subsequent Exposure
              |
       Faster, Stronger
        Immune Response

**Important:** Vaccination generally produces active acquired immunity, while administration of preformed antibodies produces passive immunity.

## Major Human Pathogens and Diseases

Pathogens are disease-causing organisms or infectious agents. They include bacteria, viruses, protozoans, and fungi.

### 1. Bacterial Diseases

| Disease | Causative Organism | Transmission | Major Symptoms / Diagnostic Features |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Typhoid** | *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi (*Salmonella typhi*) | Contaminated food and water | Sustained high fever, weakness, abdominal pain; Widal test may support diagnosis. |
| **Pneumonia** | *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae* | Inhalation of infected respiratory droplets | Alveoli become inflamed and may fill with fluid; fever, cough, breathing difficulty. |
| **Common bacterial diarrhoeal infections** | Various bacteria, including pathogenic *Escherichia coli* | Contaminated food and water | Diarrhoea, abdominal cramps, and dehydration. |

### Typhoid

- Caused by *Salmonella Typhi*.
- Commonly transmitted through contaminated food and water.
- Symptoms include sustained fever, weakness, headache, and abdominal discomfort.
- Severe cases may lead to intestinal haemorrhage or perforation.
- The **Widal test** detects antibodies against specific Salmonella antigens, although culture-based testing is preferred for definitive diagnosis.

### Pneumonia

- Commonly caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Haemophilus influenzae*.
- Infection causes inflammation of the alveoli.
- Alveolar spaces may fill with fluid, reducing efficient gas exchange.
- Symptoms include fever, cough, chest discomfort, and difficulty breathing.
- Severe oxygen deficiency may cause bluish discoloration of the lips or fingernails (cyanosis).

### 2. Protozoan Diseases

| Disease | Causative Organism | Vector / Transmission | Major Symptoms |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Malaria** | *Plasmodium vivax*, *P. falciparum*, and other species | Bite of infected female *Anopheles* mosquito | Recurrent fever, chills, and sweating. |
| **Amoebiasis** | *Entamoeba histolytica* | Contaminated food and water; houseflies can mechanically carry cysts | Abdominal pain, constipation or diarrhoea, and stools containing mucus and blood. |

### Malaria

Malaria is caused by protozoan parasites of the genus *Plasmodium*.

**Life Cycle and Transmission:**
- An infected female *Anopheles* mosquito injects sporozoites into the human bloodstream.
- Sporozoites enter liver cells and multiply.
- Merozoites released from the liver infect red blood cells.
- Parasites multiply inside erythrocytes, eventually causing them to rupture.
- Rupture releases new merozoites and the toxic pigment **haemozoin**.

**Symptoms:**
- Recurrent episodes of chills and high fever.
- Sweating after the fever subsides.
- Anaemia due to destruction of red blood cells.

**Important:** Haemozoin released during the rupture of infected red blood cells contributes to the characteristic fever and chills of malaria.

### Amoebiasis

- Caused by *Entamoeba histolytica*.
- Infection occurs mainly through ingestion of cysts in contaminated food or water.
- Houseflies can act as mechanical carriers by transferring infective cysts to food.
- Symptoms include abdominal pain, constipation or diarrhoea, and stools containing excess mucus and blood.

### 3. Fungal Diseases

| Disease | Causative Organisms | Transmission | Major Symptoms |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Ringworm** | *Microsporum*, *Trichophyton*, *Epidermophyton* | Direct contact, contaminated clothing, towels, or soil | Circular, dry, scaly skin lesions with itching; may affect nails and scalp. |

### Ringworm

- Despite its name, ringworm is not caused by a worm.
- It is a fungal infection known as **dermatophytosis**.
- It commonly affects the skin, scalp, and nails.
- Warm and moist conditions favor fungal growth.
- Treatment generally involves appropriate antifungal medicines.

## Allergies and Autoimmunity

### 1. Allergies

An allergy is an exaggerated immune response to normally harmless substances called **allergens**.

Examples include pollen, dust mites, certain foods, and animal dander.

**Mechanism:**
- First exposure to an allergen can stimulate the production of IgE antibodies.
- IgE binds to mast cells and basophils.
- Subsequent exposure causes these cells to release histamine and other inflammatory mediators.
- These substances produce allergic symptoms.

**Common Symptoms:**
- Sneezing and runny nose.
- Itching and skin rashes.
- Swelling and inflammation.
- Difficulty breathing in severe reactions.

**Important:** Antihistamines are commonly used to relieve symptoms by blocking the effects of histamine. Severe anaphylaxis requires urgent medical treatment, typically including epinephrine.

### 2. Autoimmunity

Autoimmunity occurs when the immune system mistakenly recognizes the body's own cells or tissues as foreign and attacks them.

Examples:
- Rheumatoid arthritis.
- Type 1 diabetes mellitus.
- Systemic lupus erythematosus.

## AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome)

AIDS is a condition caused by infection with the **Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)**, a retrovirus that progressively weakens the immune system.

### HIV Structure and Nature

- HIV belongs to the family Retroviridae.
- It contains two copies of single-stranded RNA.
- It possesses the enzyme **reverse transcriptase**, which converts viral RNA into DNA.
- The viral DNA integrates into the host cell's genome.

### Mechanism of Infection

HIV primarily attacks **CD4⁺ helper T-lymphocytes**, which coordinate immune responses.

    HIV Enters the Body
             |
      Infects CD4⁺ Cells
             |
       Viral RNA Released
             |
      Reverse Transcriptase
       Converts RNA to DNA
             |
       Viral DNA Integration
             |
       New Viruses Produced
             |
      CD4⁺ T-cell Destruction
             |
      Weakened Immune System
             |
       Opportunistic Infections
             |
            AIDS

### Transmission of HIV

HIV can spread through:

- Unprotected sexual contact with an infected person.
- Transfusion of contaminated blood.
- Sharing contaminated needles and syringes.
- Transmission from an infected mother to her child during pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding.

**HIV is not transmitted through:**
- Handshakes or casual physical contact.
- Sharing food or utensils.
- Hugging.
- Mosquito bites.
- Sharing toilets or swimming pools.

### Diagnosis and Treatment

- **ELISA** has historically been used to screen for HIV antibodies; modern testing may use antigen-antibody combination assays.
- Confirmatory testing follows the recommended diagnostic algorithm.
- Antiretroviral therapy (ART) suppresses HIV replication and helps preserve immune function.
- ART can allow people with HIV to live long and healthy lives, but it does not currently eliminate the infection completely.

**Important:** HIV infection does not immediately mean that a person has AIDS. AIDS is the advanced stage of HIV infection characterized by severe immune suppression or specific opportunistic illnesses.

## Cancer

Cancer is a group of diseases characterized by uncontrolled cell division and the ability of abnormal cells to invade surrounding tissues or spread to distant parts of the body.

### Normal Cells vs Cancer Cells

Normal cells generally regulate their division through controlled growth signals and interactions with neighboring cells.

Cancer cells develop abnormalities that allow them to escape normal growth regulation.

### Contact Inhibition

- Normal cells generally stop dividing when they come into contact with neighboring cells.
- This property is called **contact inhibition**.
- Cancer cells often lose contact inhibition and continue proliferating, forming an abnormal mass of cells.

### Tumors

Tumors are abnormal masses of proliferating cells and may be classified into two major types.

| Feature | Benign Tumors | Malignant Tumors |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Growth | Usually slow | Often rapid |
| Local Invasion | Generally absent | Present |
| Metastasis | Absent | Can occur |
| Danger | Usually less dangerous | Potentially life-threatening |

### Metastasis

Metastasis is the spread of cancer cells from the original tumor to distant organs through blood or lymph.

- Cancer cells detach from the primary tumor.
- They invade surrounding tissues.
- They enter blood vessels or lymphatic vessels.
- They travel to distant locations and establish secondary tumors.

**Important:** Metastasis is a defining characteristic of malignant tumors and makes cancer particularly dangerous.

### Causes and Risk Factors of Cancer

| Factor | Examples |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Chemical Carcinogens** | Tobacco smoke and certain industrial chemicals |
| **Physical Carcinogens** | Ultraviolet radiation and ionizing radiation |
| **Biological Agents** | Certain oncogenic viruses, such as HPV and hepatitis viruses |
| **Genetic Factors** | Inherited mutations in cancer-associated genes |
| **Lifestyle Factors** | Tobacco use, excessive alcohol consumption, and certain dietary or behavioral factors |

### Cancer Detection and Treatment

**Diagnostic Methods:**
- **Biopsy:** Microscopic examination of a tissue sample.
- **Histopathology:** Examination of tissue architecture and cellular abnormalities.
- **Imaging:** X-rays, CT scans, MRI, and other imaging techniques.
- **Molecular Tests:** Detection of specific genetic or molecular changes.

**Treatment Methods:**
- **Surgery:** Physical removal of the tumor.
- **Radiotherapy:** Use of ionizing radiation to destroy cancer cells.
- **Chemotherapy:** Use of drugs that kill or inhibit rapidly dividing cells.
- **Immunotherapy:** Stimulates or modifies the immune system to attack cancer.
- **Targeted Therapy:** Acts on specific molecular pathways or abnormalities in cancer cells.

## Drugs and Substance Abuse

Drug and alcohol abuse can negatively affect physical health, mental health, behavior, and social relationships.

### Commonly Abused Substances

| Substance Category | Examples | Major Effects |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Opioids** | Heroin, morphine | Pain relief, sedation, respiratory depression, dependence |
| **Cannabinoids** | Marijuana, hashish | Altered perception, impaired coordination, changes in memory |
| **Coca Alkaloids** | Cocaine | Increased alertness, stimulation, elevated heart rate |
| **Alcohol** | Ethanol | Impaired judgment, coordination, and nervous system function |
| **Nicotine** | Tobacco products | Dependence, increased cardiovascular risk |

### Opioids

- Opioids act on opioid receptors in the central nervous system.
- They can produce pain relief and euphoria.
- Heroin is a highly addictive opioid derived from morphine.
- Excessive opioid consumption can cause severe respiratory depression and potentially fatal overdose.

### Cannabinoids

- Cannabinoids are obtained from the inflorescences of the *Cannabis sativa* plant.
- They affect cannabinoid receptors in the brain.
- They can alter perception, memory, coordination, and judgment.

### Cocaine

- Cocaine is obtained from the coca plant.
- It acts as a powerful central nervous system stimulant.
- It increases the activity of neurotransmitters such as dopamine by interfering with their reuptake.
- It can cause increased heart rate, elevated blood pressure, and dependence.

### Prevention and Control of Substance Abuse

- Avoid experimentation with addictive substances.
- Recognize peer pressure and develop healthy coping strategies.
- Seek professional support when substance dependence develops.
- Encourage awareness and education about the consequences of substance abuse.
- Maintain supportive family and social relationships.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Innate vs Acquired Immunity:** Innate immunity is non-specific and present from birth; acquired immunity is antigen-specific and develops immunological memory.
- **Humoral vs Cell-Mediated Immunity:** Humoral immunity is primarily mediated by B-lymphocytes and antibodies; cell-mediated immunity is mediated by T-lymphocytes.
- **IgG vs IgA:** IgG crosses the placenta, while IgA is abundant in colostrum, saliva, tears, and other secretions.
- **IgM:** It is generally the first antibody produced during a primary immune response.
- **Allergy:** IgE-mediated activation of mast cells causes histamine release and allergic symptoms.
- **Typhoid:** Caused by *Salmonella Typhi*; the Widal test detects antibodies against Salmonella antigens.
- **Pneumonia:** Infection causes inflammation and fluid accumulation in the alveoli.
- **Malaria Vector:** The disease is transmitted by infected female *Anopheles* mosquitoes, not male mosquitoes.
- **Malaria Fever:** Haemozoin released during rupture of infected erythrocytes contributes to recurring fever and chills.
- **Amoebiasis:** Caused by *Entamoeba histolytica*; houseflies act as mechanical carriers, not biological vectors.
- **Ringworm:** It is a fungal infection, not a parasitic worm infection.
- **HIV:** HIV primarily attacks CD4⁺ helper T-lymphocytes and is a retrovirus containing RNA and reverse transcriptase.
- **HIV vs AIDS:** HIV is the virus; AIDS is the advanced clinical condition caused by progressive immune deficiency.
- **HIV Diagnosis:** ELISA is a historically important screening method, but modern diagnosis uses antigen-antibody tests and confirmatory testing algorithms.
- **Cancer:** Loss of contact inhibition contributes to uncontrolled cell proliferation.
- **Metastasis:** It refers to the spread of malignant cells to distant organs.
- **Graft Rejection:** Primarily involves cell-mediated immunity through T-lymphocytes rather than antibody-mediated humoral immunity.
- **Vaccination:** Produces active acquired immunity by stimulating the body's own immune response.
- **Passive Immunity:** Provides immediate but temporary protection through transferred antibodies.

## Quiz

1. Which immunoglobulin class is most abundant in human colostrum and provides passive immunity to newborns through breast milk?

- [ ] IgG
- [x] IgA
- [ ] IgE
- [ ] IgM

> IgA is abundant in colostrum and other mucosal secretions. It provides passive protection to newborns by helping defend mucosal surfaces against pathogens.

2. A patient experiences rejection of a transplanted organ because the recipient's immune system recognizes the donor tissue as foreign. Which immune mechanism is primarily responsible?

- [ ] Humoral immunity mediated by B-lymphocytes
- [x] Cell-mediated immunity mediated by T-lymphocytes
- [ ] Physiological barriers involving gastric acid
- [ ] Cytokine barriers involving interferons alone

> T-lymphocytes recognize foreign antigens associated with transplanted tissue and initiate immune responses that contribute to graft rejection.

3. What substance released during the rupture of infected red blood cells contributes to the recurring fever and chills associated with malaria?

- [ ] Histamine
- [x] Haemozoin
- [ ] Interferon
- [ ] Lysozyme

> Haemozoin is a toxic pigment produced during the digestion of haemoglobin by malaria parasites. Its release during infected erythrocyte rupture contributes to the characteristic fever and chills.

4. Which statement correctly distinguishes HIV infection from AIDS?

- [ ] HIV and AIDS are two different names for the same virus.
- [ ] AIDS is the virus that infects red blood cells.
- [x] HIV is the virus, while AIDS is the advanced condition resulting from severe immune deficiency caused by HIV infection.
- [ ] HIV only affects B-lymphocytes and does not affect T-lymphocytes.

> HIV is a retrovirus that primarily attacks CD4⁺ helper T-lymphocytes. AIDS is the advanced stage of infection characterized by severe immune suppression and susceptibility to opportunistic infections.

5. Which of the following features most clearly distinguishes a malignant tumor from a benign tumor?

- [ ] Presence of normal cell division
- [ ] Complete absence of blood supply
- [x] Ability to invade surrounding tissues and undergo metastasis
- [ ] Permanent inability to divide

> Malignant tumors can invade neighboring tissues and spread to distant organs through blood or lymph. This spread is called metastasis and is a major characteristic of malignant cancer.

6. Which antibody is generally produced first during the primary immune response to a newly encountered antigen?

- [ ] IgA
- [ ] IgG
- [x] IgM
- [ ] IgE

> IgM is generally the first antibody produced during a primary immune response. IgG becomes particularly important in later stages and is the major antibody capable of crossing the placenta.

7. Which of the following diseases is caused by a protozoan parasite transmitted through the bite of an infected female *Anopheles* mosquito?

- [ ] Typhoid
- [ ] Amoebiasis
- [x] Malaria
- [ ] Ringworm

> Malaria is caused by *Plasmodium* species. The infected female *Anopheles* mosquito injects sporozoites into the human bloodstream during a bite.

8. Which antibody-mediated mechanism is primarily involved in immediate allergic reactions?

- [ ] IgM activation of red blood cells
- [x] IgE binding to mast cells and triggering histamine release
- [ ] IgG crossing the placenta
- [ ] IgA activation of macrophages

> IgE antibodies bind to mast cells and basophils. When an allergen cross-links these antibodies, the cells release histamine and other inflammatory mediators, producing allergic symptoms.
