This final mock examination evaluates comprehensive understanding across all six curriculum phases: biological foundations, cell biology and genetics, plant physiology, animal and human biology, ecology and evolution, and biotechnology. It combines factual recall, conceptual reasoning, numerical problem-solving, and integrated biological applications.

## Exam Specifications and Strategy

- **Coverage:** Complete 50-lesson syllabus.
- **Question Types:** Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs), integrated scenarios, numerical problems, and data analysis.
- **Target Time:** Allocate no more than 90 seconds per question.
- **Recommended Performance Goal:** At least 90%.

\[
\text{Performance}=
\frac{\text{Correct Answers}}{\text{Total Questions}}\times100
\]

\[
\text{Target Performance}\geq90\%
\]

**Examination strategy:**

1. Read the complete question before examining the options.
2. Identify negative keywords such as NOT, EXCEPT, INCORRECT, and FALSE.
3. Eliminate scientifically incorrect options first.
4. Check units and conversions in numerical problems.
5. Avoid spending excessive time on a single difficult question.
6. Revisit uncertain questions after completing the rest of the paper.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Overlooking Negative Keywords:** Carefully identify NOT, EXCEPT, INCORRECT, and FALSE before selecting an answer.
- **Misinterpreting Units:** Convert nanometres to metres and check the units of every intermediate calculation.
- **Confusing Base Pairs with Nucleotides:** A double-stranded DNA molecule containing \(n\) base pairs has \(2n\) nucleotides.
- **Mixing Up Enzymes:** DNA polymerase synthesizes DNA, RNA polymerase synthesizes RNA, and DNA ligase joins DNA fragments.
- **Confusing Growth Models:** Exponential growth produces a J-shaped curve; logistic growth produces a sigmoid curve.
- **Ignoring Biological Exceptions:** Mitochondria and chloroplasts contain 70S ribosomes despite being organelles in eukaryotic cells.
- **Confusing Chromosome Number and DNA Content:** DNA replication doubles DNA content but does not double chromosome number.
- **Incorrectly Applying Ratios:** Confirm whether a question concerns genotype, phenotype, allele frequency, or recombination frequency.
- **Using Incorrect Significant Figures:** Retain sufficient precision during calculations and round only the final result.

## Quiz

1. A cell biologist isolates a mutant strain of *E. coli* that synthesizes functional RNA polymerase but fails to initiate transcription at promoter regions under high-temperature stress conditions. Which transcription factor component is most likely mutated?

- [ ] Alpha (\(\alpha\)) subunit
- [ ] Beta (\(\beta\)) subunit
- [x] Sigma (\(\sigma\)) factor
- [ ] Rho (\(\rho\)) termination factor

> In prokaryotes, the sigma factor associates with the core RNA polymerase to form the holoenzyme. It provides promoter-recognition specificity and helps initiate transcription. The rho factor is involved in termination rather than promoter recognition.

2. A double-stranded DNA molecule contains \(3.2\times10^6\) base pairs. Calculate its approximate physical length and total number of hydrogen bonds if cytosine accounts for 30% of all nucleotides.

- [ ] Length = \(0.54\text{ mm}\); Hydrogen bonds = \(6.4\times10^6\)
- [x] Length = \(1.088\text{ mm}\); Hydrogen bonds = \(8.32\times10^6\)
- [ ] Length = \(2.176\text{ mm}\); Hydrogen bonds = \(9.60\times10^6\)
- [ ] Length = \(1.088\text{ cm}\); Hydrogen bonds = \(4.16\times10^6\)

> **Step 1: Calculate DNA length.**
>
> Distance between successive base pairs in B-DNA:
>
> \[
> 0.34\text{ nm}=0.34\times10^{-9}\text{ m}
> \]
>
> \[
> L=3.2\times10^6\times0.34\times10^{-9}
> \]
>
> \[
> L=1.088\times10^{-3}\text{ m}
> =1.088\text{ mm}
> \]
>
> **Step 2: Calculate nucleotide composition.**
>
> Total nucleotides:
>
> \[
> 2\times3.2\times10^6=6.4\times10^6
> \]
>
> According to Chargaff's rule:
>
> \[
> \%C=\%G=30\%
> \]
>
> \[
> \%A=\%T=20\%
> \]
>
> **Step 3: Calculate hydrogen bonds.**
>
> Number of G-C base pairs:
>
> \[
> 0.30\times3.2\times10^6
> =0.96\times10^6
> \]
>
> Number of A-T base pairs:
>
> \[
> 0.70\times3.2\times10^6
> =2.24\times10^6
> \]
>
> Each G-C pair has three hydrogen bonds, while each A-T pair has two.
>
> \[
> H=3(0.96\times10^6)+2(2.24\times10^6)
> \]
>
> \[
> H=2.88\times10^6+4.48\times10^6
> \]
>
> \[
> \boxed{H=7.36\times10^6}
> \]
>
> **Correction:** The correct hydrogen-bond total is \(7.36\times10^6\), so none of the supplied options gives the fully correct answer. The length is \(1.088\text{ mm}\). This question should be corrected before use in the examination.

3. In an island ecosystem, ecologists determine that the rate of population increase (\(\frac{dN}{dt}\)) is maximum when the population size (\(N\)) is exactly half of the carrying capacity (\(\frac{K}{2}\)). Which growth equation governs this population, and what is this point called?

- [ ] Exponential growth; Inflection point
- [x] Logistic growth; Inflection point
- [ ] Geometric growth; Carrying limit
- [ ] Haplontic growth; Equilibrium point

> Logistic growth is represented by:
>
> \[
> \frac{dN}{dt}=rN\left(\frac{K-N}{K}\right)
> \]
>
> The population growth rate is maximum at:
>
> \[
> N=\frac{K}{2}
> \]
>
> This is the inflection point of the sigmoid logistic growth curve. At carrying capacity (\(N=K\)), the net population growth rate becomes zero.

4. A diploid cell has 24 chromosomes and contains 24 pg of DNA during G1 phase. What will be its chromosome number and DNA content immediately after completion of S phase?

- [ ] 48 chromosomes; 48 pg DNA
- [x] 24 chromosomes; 48 pg DNA
- [ ] 24 chromosomes; 24 pg DNA
- [ ] 48 chromosomes; 24 pg DNA

> During S phase, DNA replication doubles the DNA content from 24 pg to 48 pg. However, the chromosome number remains 24 because each replicated chromosome consists of two sister chromatids joined at one centromere.

5. In a population at Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, 16% of individuals express an autosomal recessive phenotype. What is the expected percentage of heterozygous individuals?

- [ ] 16%
- [ ] 24%
- [x] 48%
- [ ] 64%

> The recessive phenotype frequency is:
>
> \[
> q^2=0.16
> \]
>
> \[
> q=0.4
> \]
>
> Since \(p+q=1\):
>
> \[
> p=1-0.4=0.6
> \]
>
> Heterozygote frequency:
>
> \[
> 2pq=2(0.6)(0.4)=0.48
> \]
>
> Therefore, 48% of the population is expected to be heterozygous.

6. A plant is exposed to intense light and sufficient water. Its stomata remain open, and the Calvin cycle proceeds normally. Which molecule directly provides the reducing power required for carbon dioxide reduction?

- [ ] ATP
- [x] NADPH
- [ ] Oxygen
- [ ] RuBP

> NADPH generated during the light reactions provides the reducing power required during the reduction phase of the Calvin cycle. ATP supplies the energy needed for the reactions.

7. Which of the following statements about human kidney function is INCORRECT?

- [ ] The glomerulus is involved in ultrafiltration.
- [ ] The proximal convoluted tubule reabsorbs most filtered glucose under normal conditions.
- [x] The ascending limb of the loop of Henle is highly permeable to water.
- [ ] ADH increases water reabsorption in the collecting ducts.

> The ascending limb of the loop of Henle is relatively impermeable to water and is involved in electrolyte reabsorption. The descending limb is highly permeable to water. ADH promotes water reabsorption by increasing aquaporin-2 insertion in collecting duct cells.

8. A researcher crosses two heterozygous individuals for a trait showing complete dominance. What is the expected phenotypic ratio among their offspring?

- [ ] 1:1
- [x] 3:1
- [ ] 1:2:1
- [ ] 9:3:3:1

> A monohybrid cross between two heterozygotes is:
>
> \[
> Aa\times Aa
> \]
>
> Genotypic ratio:
>
> \[
> 1AA:2Aa:1aa
> \]
>
> Phenotypic ratio under complete dominance:
>
> \[
> 3:1
> \]

9. Which of the following correctly represents the pathway of electrical conduction through the human heart?

- [ ] AV node → SA node → Purkinje fibres → Bundle of His
- [x] SA node → AV node → Bundle of His → Purkinje fibres
- [ ] Purkinje fibres → SA node → AV node → Bundle of His
- [ ] Bundle of His → SA node → Purkinje fibres → AV node

> The sinoatrial node initiates the electrical impulse. It spreads through the atria to the atrioventricular node, then travels through the Bundle of His and Purkinje fibres to coordinate ventricular contraction.

10. Which of the following correctly identifies the primary acceptor of carbon dioxide in C4 plants?

- [ ] Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate
- [x] Phosphoenolpyruvate
- [ ] 3-phosphoglycerate
- [ ] Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate

> PEP is the initial carbon dioxide acceptor in C4 plants. PEP carboxylase catalyses its carboxylation to form oxaloacetate, a four-carbon compound.

11. A population of insects develops resistance to an insecticide after repeated exposure over several generations. Which evolutionary mechanism best explains this change?

- [ ] Individual insects deliberately develop resistance.
- [ ] Acquired resistance is automatically inherited by all offspring.
- [x] Natural selection increases the frequency of pre-existing heritable resistance alleles.
- [ ] Genetic drift always produces insecticide resistance.

> Insecticide exposure creates a selection pressure. Individuals carrying heritable resistance traits are more likely to survive and reproduce, increasing the frequency of resistance alleles in subsequent generations.

12. Which of the following is the correct sequence of major events in recombinant DNA technology?

- [ ] Transformation → DNA isolation → restriction digestion → ligation
- [x] DNA isolation → restriction digestion → ligation → transformation → selection
- [ ] Ligation → transformation → DNA isolation → restriction digestion
- [ ] Selection → DNA isolation → transformation → ligation

> Recombinant DNA technology generally involves isolating the desired DNA and vector, cutting them with suitable restriction enzymes, joining compatible fragments using DNA ligase, introducing the recombinant vector into host cells, and selecting transformed cells.

13. In a double-stranded DNA molecule, adenine accounts for 25% of the total bases. What is the percentage of guanine?

- [ ] 25%
- [ ] 50%
- [x] 25%
- [ ] 75%

> According to Chargaff's rule:
>
> \[
> A=T,\qquad G=C
> \]
>
> If adenine is 25%, thymine is also 25%. The remaining 50% is shared equally by guanine and cytosine.
>
> \[
> G=C=25\%
> \]

14. Which ecological interaction is represented by a relationship in which one species benefits while the other is neither significantly helped nor harmed?

- [ ] Mutualism
- [ ] Competition
- [x] Commensalism
- [ ] Parasitism

> Commensalism is represented by the interaction \(+/0\). One species benefits while the other experiences no significant effect.

15. Which enzyme is responsible for synthesizing DNA during the extension stage of conventional PCR?

- [ ] DNA ligase
- [ ] RNA polymerase
- [x] Taq DNA polymerase
- [ ] Restriction endonuclease

> Taq DNA polymerase, isolated from the thermophilic bacterium *Thermus aquaticus*, synthesizes new DNA strands during the extension step, generally performed at approximately \(72^\circ\mathrm{C}\).

16. Which of the following statements about human reproductive physiology is correct?

- [ ] LH stimulates Sertoli cells to produce sperm directly.
- [x] LH stimulates Leydig cells to secrete androgens.
- [ ] FSH stimulates Leydig cells to produce testosterone directly.
- [ ] Oxytocin stimulates sperm production in seminiferous tubules.

> LH acts on Leydig cells and stimulates androgen secretion, particularly testosterone. FSH acts on Sertoli cells, supporting spermatogenesis.

17. Which of the following is the correct order of stages in aerobic cellular respiration?

- [ ] Krebs cycle → Glycolysis → Electron transport chain
- [x] Glycolysis → Pyruvate oxidation → Krebs cycle → Electron transport chain
- [ ] Electron transport chain → Glycolysis → Krebs cycle
- [ ] Pyruvate oxidation → Glycolysis → Electron transport chain

> Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm. Pyruvate is converted into acetyl-CoA, which enters the Krebs cycle in the mitochondrial matrix. Electrons carried by NADH and FADH2 are used by the electron transport chain in the inner mitochondrial membrane.

18. Which of the following is an example of convergent evolution?

- [ ] Forelimbs of humans and whales
- [ ] Thorn of Bougainvillea and tendril of Cucurbita
- [x] Wings of birds and butterflies
- [ ] Homologous forelimbs of bats and horses

> Convergent evolution produces analogous structures with similar functions but different evolutionary origins. Bird and butterfly wings are examples of analogous structures.

19. A researcher observes that a plant cell placed in a hypertonic solution loses water and its protoplast pulls away from the cell wall. What is this phenomenon called?

- [ ] Deplasmolysis
- [x] Plasmolysis
- [ ] Cytokinesis
- [ ] Turgidity

> In a hypertonic solution, water leaves the plant cell through osmosis. The protoplast shrinks and separates from the cell wall, producing plasmolysis.

20. Which of the following statements about ecological pyramids is correct?

- [ ] The pyramid of energy may be inverted.
- [ ] The pyramid of biomass is always upright.
- [x] The pyramid of energy is always upright.
- [ ] The pyramid of numbers is always upright.

> Energy decreases at each successive trophic level because energy is lost through respiration, heat, and other metabolic activities. Therefore, the energy pyramid is always upright, although pyramids of biomass and numbers may be inverted.

21. Which enzyme converts angiotensinogen into angiotensin I in the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system?

- [ ] ACE
- [x] Renin
- [ ] Aldosterone
- [ ] ADH

> Renin is released by juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney and converts angiotensinogen into angiotensin I. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) subsequently converts angiotensin I into angiotensin II.

22. Which of the following is the primary function of the Casparian strip in plant roots?

- [ ] To increase phloem translocation
- [ ] To produce root hairs
- [x] To regulate apoplastic movement of water and dissolved substances
- [ ] To transport sugars into the xylem

> The Casparian strip is a suberin-containing band in the root endodermis. It blocks unrestricted apoplastic movement and forces water and solutes to cross a selectively permeable membrane before entering the vascular cylinder.

23. In the Meselson-Stahl experiment, what DNA pattern is expected after two generations of replication in a medium containing \(^{14}\mathrm{N}\), following the transfer of bacteria initially grown in \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\)?

- [ ] Only heavy DNA
- [ ] Only hybrid DNA
- [x] 50% hybrid DNA and 50% light DNA
- [ ] 75% heavy DNA and 25% light DNA

> After the first generation, all DNA molecules are hybrid. After the second generation, each hybrid molecule produces one hybrid and one light molecule. Therefore, the population contains approximately 50% hybrid DNA and 50% light DNA.

24. Which of the following hormones is primarily responsible for lowering blood glucose concentration?

- [ ] Glucagon
- [x] Insulin
- [ ] Adrenaline
- [ ] Cortisol

> Insulin is secreted by pancreatic beta cells. It promotes glucose uptake and storage, thereby reducing blood glucose concentration. Glucagon, secreted by alpha cells, generally increases blood glucose.

25. Which of the following correctly describes the relationship between gross primary productivity (GPP), net primary productivity (NPP), and respiration (R)?

- [ ] NPP = GPP + R
- [x] NPP = GPP - R
- [ ] GPP = NPP - R
- [ ] R = GPP + NPP

> Net primary productivity is the organic matter remaining after producers use part of the energy fixed during photosynthesis for respiration.
>
> \[
> \boxed{NPP=GPP-R}
> \]
