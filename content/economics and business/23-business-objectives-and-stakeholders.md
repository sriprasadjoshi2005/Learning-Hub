Every business operates with objectives that guide its decisions, activities, and long-term direction. These objectives may involve profitability, growth, customer satisfaction, employee welfare, innovation, and social responsibility.

Businesses also interact with different groups known as stakeholders. Stakeholders influence business decisions or are affected by business activities.

## 1. Meaning of Business Objectives

Business objectives are specific results that an organisation aims to achieve through its activities.

They provide direction and help businesses evaluate performance.

Examples include:

- Increasing profitability.
- Expanding market share.
- Improving product quality.
- Increasing customer satisfaction.
- Reducing production costs.
- Improving employee productivity.
- Expanding into new markets.
- Achieving environmental sustainability.

## 2. Importance of Business Objectives

Clear objectives help organisations:

- Establish priorities.
- Coordinate departments.
- Allocate resources effectively.
- Measure performance.
- Motivate employees.
- Evaluate business strategies.
- Identify areas requiring improvement.
- Maintain long-term direction.

Without clear objectives, businesses may struggle to coordinate activities and evaluate success.

## 3. Types of Business Objectives

### A. Profit Maximisation

Profit maximisation involves choosing business decisions that aim to achieve the highest possible profit under given conditions.

Profit = Total Revenue - Total Cost

A business may seek to maximise profit by:

- Increasing revenue.
- Reducing unnecessary costs.
- Improving productivity.
- Selecting suitable products.
- Using resources efficiently.

However, maximising short-term profit does not always guarantee long-term business success.

### B. Sales Maximisation

Sales maximisation focuses on increasing the volume or value of sales.

Businesses may pursue this objective to:

- Increase market presence.
- Attract more customers.
- Expand distribution.
- Improve brand recognition.

Higher sales do not necessarily mean higher profit because selling expenses and production costs may also increase.

### C. Growth Maximisation

Growth maximisation involves expanding the size or reach of a business.

Examples include:

- Opening new branches.
- Entering international markets.
- Increasing production capacity.
- Introducing new product lines.
- Acquiring other businesses.

### D. Market Share

Market share represents the proportion of total market sales captured by a particular business.

Market Share = (Business Sales / Total Market Sales) x 100

Example:

A company sells products worth Rs. 20 million in a market with total sales of Rs. 100 million.

Market Share = (20 / 100) x 100 = 20%

### E. Customer Satisfaction

Customer satisfaction refers to the extent to which a product or service meets or exceeds customer expectations.

Businesses improve customer satisfaction through:

- Product quality.
- Fair pricing.
- Reliable delivery.
- Customer support.
- Product safety.
- Effective complaint handling.

### F. Survival

Survival is an important objective, particularly for new businesses and organisations facing financial difficulties.

It involves maintaining sufficient cash flow, controlling costs, and meeting financial obligations.

### G. Social Responsibility

Social responsibility involves considering the effects of business activities on employees, customers, communities, and the environment.

Examples include:

- Fair employment practices.
- Ethical sourcing.
- Environmental protection.
- Community development.
- Product safety.

## 4. SMART Business Objectives

The SMART framework helps businesses create clear and measurable objectives.

**Specific**

The objective should clearly state what needs to be achieved.

**Measurable**

Progress should be capable of being evaluated using indicators.

**Achievable**

The objective should be realistic given available resources.

**Relevant**

The objective should support the organisation's broader strategy.

**Time-Bound**

The objective should include a defined time period.

Example:

Increase monthly online sales by 15% within six months.

This objective identifies the target, measurement, and deadline.

## 5. Meaning of Stakeholders

Stakeholders are individuals, groups, or organisations that affect or are affected by the activities and decisions of a business.

They may have different interests and expectations.

Stakeholders are commonly classified into internal and external groups.

## 6. Internal Stakeholders

Internal stakeholders are individuals or groups directly involved in the organisation.

### A. Owners and Shareholders

Owners and shareholders provide capital and have an ownership interest in the business.

Their interests may include:

- Profitability.
- Business growth.
- Dividends.
- Increase in business value.
- Financial stability.

### B. Employees

Employees contribute labour, skills, and knowledge.

Their interests may include:

- Fair wages.
- Job security.
- Safe working conditions.
- Career development.
- Respectful treatment.
- Work-life balance.

### C. Managers

Managers coordinate resources and organisational activities.

Their interests may include:

- Organisational performance.
- Effective leadership.
- Career development.
- Operational efficiency.
- Achievement of business objectives.

## 7. External Stakeholders

External stakeholders operate outside the organisation but influence or are affected by its activities.

### A. Customers

Customers purchase goods and services.

They expect:

- Quality products.
- Fair prices.
- Reliable service.
- Product safety.
- Accurate information.

### B. Suppliers

Suppliers provide raw materials, components, and services.

Their interests include:

- Timely payments.
- Stable business relationships.
- Reliable orders.
- Fair contractual terms.

### C. Government

Government establishes laws, regulations, and policies affecting business activities.

Its interests include:

- Tax revenue.
- Employment generation.
- Legal compliance.
- Consumer protection.
- Economic development.

### D. Creditors and Banks

Financial institutions and creditors provide loans or credit facilities.

They expect:

- Timely repayment.
- Interest payments.
- Financial stability.
- Reliable financial information.

### E. Local Communities

Communities may be affected by business operations through employment, pollution, traffic, infrastructure, and social investment.

### F. Competitors

Competitors are other businesses operating in the same or related markets.

They influence pricing, innovation, product quality, and market strategy.

## 8. Stakeholder Conflicts

Stakeholder conflicts occur when different groups have competing interests.

Examples include:

| Stakeholders | Possible Conflict |
| --- | --- |
| Owners and employees | Higher dividends versus higher wages |
| Customers and business | Lower prices versus higher profit margins |
| Suppliers and business | Higher supplier prices versus lower purchasing costs |
| Government and business | Environmental compliance costs versus lower operating costs |
| Managers and shareholders | Long-term investment versus short-term returns |

Businesses must balance these interests while maintaining legal and ethical standards.

## 9. Stakeholder Mapping

Stakeholder mapping is a method used to identify stakeholders and understand their influence and interest.

A common method uses a power-interest matrix.

### High Power, High Interest

These stakeholders require close management and regular engagement.

### High Power, Low Interest

These stakeholders should be kept satisfied.

### Low Power, High Interest

These stakeholders should be kept informed.

### Low Power, Low Interest

These stakeholders generally require monitoring.

This framework helps businesses decide how to communicate with different groups.

## 10. Corporate Social Responsibility

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) refers to the responsibility of businesses to consider their social and environmental impact.

CSR activities may include:

- Supporting education.
- Improving healthcare access.
- Protecting the environment.
- Supporting local communities.
- Promoting employee welfare.
- Encouraging responsible business practices.

In India, Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013 establishes CSR-related requirements for companies that meet specified financial thresholds.

CSR should not be treated merely as advertising. Effective CSR involves genuine responsibility and measurable outcomes.

## 11. Business Ethics

Business ethics refers to the moral principles that guide business behaviour and decision-making.

Important ethical principles include:

- Honesty.
- Transparency.
- Fairness.
- Accountability.
- Respect for employees and customers.
- Responsible use of resources.
- Avoidance of corruption.
- Protection of confidential information.

Ethical behaviour can strengthen trust and support long-term business relationships.

## Common Exam Traps

- Business objectives describe what an organisation wants to achieve.
- Stakeholders are broader than shareholders.
- Shareholders own shares, while stakeholders include all groups affected by the business.
- Sales maximisation does not automatically maximise profit.
- Market share is calculated using business sales divided by total market sales.
- SMART objectives include a time-bound element.
- Stakeholder conflicts arise because different groups have different interests.
- CSR involves social and environmental responsibility, not merely charitable advertising.
- Business ethics concerns the principles guiding organisational behaviour.
