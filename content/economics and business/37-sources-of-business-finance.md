title: Sources of Business Finance
level: intermediate
---

Business finance refers to the money required to establish, operate, maintain, and expand a business.

Organisations obtain finance from different sources depending on their size, objectives, financial position, ownership structure, and funding requirements.

Selecting an appropriate source of finance is important because each source has different costs, risks, repayment obligations, and effects on ownership.

## 1. Meaning of Business Finance

Business finance involves obtaining and managing funds required for business activities.

Finance is needed for:

- Starting a business.
- Purchasing machinery and equipment.
- Buying raw materials.
- Paying salaries and operating expenses.
- Developing new products.
- Expanding into new markets.
- Managing unexpected financial difficulties.

The amount and type of finance required depend on the nature and stage of the business.

## 2. Classification of Sources of Finance

Sources of finance can be classified according to several dimensions.

### Internal and External Finance

Internal finance is generated from within the business.

Examples include:

- Retained earnings.
- Sale of surplus assets.
- Reduction of working capital tied up in operations.

External finance is obtained from outside the organisation.

Examples include:

- Bank loans.
- Share capital.
- Bonds.
- Trade credit.
- Venture capital.

### Short-Term, Medium-Term, and Long-Term Finance

| Type | General Duration | Common Uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short-term | Usually up to one year | Inventory and daily expenses |
| Medium-term | Commonly one to five years | Equipment and business improvements |
| Long-term | Usually more than five years | Buildings, expansion, and major investments |

These time categories are general classifications. Actual financing periods depend on the agreement and financial instrument.

## 3. Retained Earnings

Retained earnings are profits kept within a business instead of being distributed to owners or shareholders.

They can be used to finance:

- Expansion.
- Equipment purchases.
- Research and development.
- Debt reduction.
- Additional working capital.

### Advantages

- No mandatory interest payments.
- No need to issue additional ownership shares.
- Can strengthen financial independence.
- May be readily available.

### Limitations

- Limited by the profits generated.
- Retaining too much profit may disappoint shareholders.
- The funds have an opportunity cost because they could have been distributed or invested elsewhere.

## 4. Equity Finance

Equity finance involves raising capital by selling ownership interests in a business.

In a company, equity investors become shareholders.

Common forms include ordinary shares and preference shares.

### Ordinary Shares

Ordinary shareholders generally have ownership rights and may receive dividends when declared.

Their rights commonly include voting on certain company matters.

### Preference Shares

Preference shares generally provide specified dividend or capital repayment preferences over ordinary shares, subject to their terms and applicable law.

Their voting rights may be limited or different from those of ordinary shares.

### Advantages of Equity Finance

- No compulsory repayment of share capital like a conventional loan.
- No fixed interest payment on ordinary shares.
- Can strengthen the capital base.
- May improve borrowing capacity.

### Limitations of Equity Finance

- Existing owners may experience dilution of ownership.
- New shareholders may receive voting rights.
- Issuing shares can involve legal and administrative costs.
- Shareholders expect returns on their investment.

## 5. Debt Finance

Debt finance involves borrowing money that must generally be repaid according to agreed terms.

Common sources include:

- Bank loans.
- Bonds.
- Debentures.
- Credit facilities.
- Other borrowing arrangements.

### Advantages

- Ownership is not normally diluted.
- Interest costs may be tax-deductible under applicable tax rules.
- Borrowing terms can be planned in advance.
- It can help finance expansion without issuing shares.

### Limitations

- Interest and repayment obligations exist.
- Excessive debt increases financial risk.
- Security or collateral may be required.
- Failure to meet repayment terms can create serious financial consequences.

## 6. Bank Loans

A bank loan is an amount borrowed from a financial institution under agreed conditions.

Loan agreements generally specify:

- Principal amount.
- Interest rate.
- Repayment period.
- Repayment schedule.
- Security requirements.
- Other contractual conditions.

Bank loans may be suitable for purchasing machinery, constructing facilities, or financing expansion.

Businesses should evaluate whether expected cash flows can support the repayment obligations.

## 7. Overdraft Facilities

A bank overdraft allows a business to withdraw more money than the available balance in its current account, up to an approved limit.

It is generally used for short-term cash flow requirements.

### Advantages

- Provides flexible access to short-term finance.
- Can help manage temporary cash shortages.
- Interest is generally charged on the amount used, according to the agreement.

### Limitations

- Interest rates and charges may be relatively high.
- The facility may be reviewed or withdrawn by the bank.
- It is not usually appropriate for financing long-term assets.

## 8. Bonds and Debentures

A bond is a debt instrument through which an organisation raises money from investors.

The issuer generally agrees to pay interest and repay the principal according to the bond's terms.

Debenture is a term used for certain forms of corporate debt, with its exact legal meaning depending on the jurisdiction.

Important features include:

- Face value.
- Coupon rate.
- Maturity date.
- Market price.
- Redemption terms.
- Security or ranking.

Bondholders are creditors rather than owners of the issuing company.

## 9. Trade Credit

Trade credit occurs when a supplier allows a business to purchase goods or services and pay at a later date.

For example, a supplier may offer payment terms of 30 days.

### Advantages

- Helps finance inventory and daily operations.
- May reduce immediate cash requirements.
- Can be convenient for established business relationships.

### Limitations

- Late payments may attract penalties.
- Excessive use can damage supplier relationships.
- Early-payment discounts may be lost.
- Suppliers may reduce or withdraw credit facilities.

## 10. Leasing

Leasing is an arrangement in which one party obtains the right to use an asset owned by another party in exchange for agreed payments.

Common leased assets include:

- Vehicles.
- Machinery.
- Office equipment.
- Commercial property.

### Advantages

- Reduces the need for a large initial purchase payment.
- May preserve cash for other activities.
- Can provide access to modern equipment.
- Payment arrangements may be predictable.

### Limitations

- Total payments may exceed the cost of purchasing the asset.
- Ownership may remain with the lessor.
- Contract restrictions may apply.
- Early termination may involve additional costs.

Accounting treatment depends on the applicable accounting standards and lease terms.

## 11. Venture Capital

Venture capital is equity investment provided to businesses with high growth potential, often at an early or developing stage.

Venture capital investors may provide:

- Financial resources.
- Strategic guidance.
- Industry contacts.
- Management support.
- Access to additional investors.

### Advantages

- Supports businesses that may struggle to obtain conventional loans.
- Can provide expertise and business networks.
- Does not generally require fixed interest repayments.

### Limitations

- Founders may give up part of their ownership.
- Investors may seek influence over business decisions.
- Investment agreements can involve significant legal and financial conditions.
- Investors generally expect substantial growth and returns.

## 12. Angel Investors

Angel investors are individuals who invest their own money in early-stage businesses.

They may also provide mentoring and business connections.

Angel investment is often smaller than institutional venture capital, although the actual amounts vary widely.

## 13. Crowdfunding

Crowdfunding involves raising relatively small contributions from a large number of people, commonly through online platforms.

Major types include:

- Donation-based crowdfunding.
- Reward-based crowdfunding.
- Equity crowdfunding.
- Debt-based crowdfunding.

### Advantages

- Can provide access to a large audience.
- Helps test customer interest.
- Can support product launches.
- May build an early customer community.

### Limitations

- Campaigns may fail to reach their targets.
- Platform fees may apply.
- Public campaigns expose business ideas to competitors.
- Equity or debt crowdfunding may involve regulatory obligations.

## 14. Matching Finance with Business Needs

Different financing sources are suitable for different requirements.

| Business Requirement | Possible Sources |
| --- | --- |
| Daily operating expenses | Cash reserves, overdraft, trade credit |
| Inventory purchases | Retained earnings, trade credit, short-term loan |
| Machinery purchase | Term loan, leasing, retained earnings |
| Major expansion | Equity finance, long-term borrowing |
| Early-stage innovation | Angel investment, venture capital |
| Temporary cash shortage | Overdraft or short-term credit |

The appropriate source depends on the business's cash flow, risk tolerance, ownership preferences, and repayment capacity.

## 15. Factors Affecting Financing Decisions

Businesses should consider:

- Cost of finance.
- Repayment obligations.
- Interest rates.
- Ownership dilution.
- Availability of collateral.
- Business creditworthiness.
- Financial risk.
- Duration of funding.
- Flexibility of repayment.
- Legal and regulatory requirements.

A business should match the duration of its financing with the nature of the asset or activity being financed whenever practical.

For example, financing a long-term factory project entirely through very short-term borrowing can create refinancing risk.

## Common Exam Traps

- Retained earnings are an internal source of finance.
- Equity finance provides ownership capital, whereas debt finance creates borrowing obligations.
- Ordinary shareholders are owners; bondholders are creditors.
- An overdraft is generally used for short-term financing needs.
- Trade credit is provided by suppliers, not necessarily by banks.
- Leasing provides the right to use an asset but does not automatically transfer ownership.
- Venture capital generally involves equity investment in businesses with high growth potential.
- Crowdfunding includes donation, reward, equity, and debt models.
- Debt does not normally dilute ownership, but it creates repayment obligations.
