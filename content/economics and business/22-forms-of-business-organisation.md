A business organisation is a structured arrangement through which individuals or groups conduct economic activities. The form of organisation determines ownership, management, liability, capital formation, and the distribution of profits.

Choosing an appropriate business structure is an important decision because it influences the legal responsibilities and future growth of an enterprise.

## 1. Meaning of Business Organisation

A business organisation is an arrangement of people, capital, and resources established to conduct business activities.

The structure determines:

- Who owns the business.
- Who manages its operations.
- How profits are distributed.
- Who is responsible for business debts.
- How capital is raised.
- How the organisation continues or ends.

The choice of structure depends on business size, capital requirements, risk, control, and legal considerations.

## 2. Sole Proprietorship

A sole proprietorship is a business owned and controlled by one individual.

The owner makes business decisions and receives the profits.

### Characteristics

- Single ownership.
- Direct control by the proprietor.
- Generally simple formation.
- Owner receives business profits.
- Usually limited access to large amounts of capital.
- The proprietor generally has unlimited personal liability.

### Advantages

- Easy to establish.
- Quick decision-making.
- Direct supervision.
- Flexible operations.
- Business information can remain relatively private.
- The owner receives the profits.

### Disadvantages

- Unlimited personal liability.
- Limited financial resources.
- Heavy dependence on one individual.
- Limited management expertise.
- Business continuity may be affected by the owner's death or incapacity.

### Suitable Businesses

Examples include:

- Small retail shops.
- Local repair services.
- Independent consultants.
- Small food businesses.

## 3. Partnership

A partnership is a form of business organisation in which two or more individuals agree to carry on a business and share its profits according to their agreement.

In India, traditional partnership firms are governed by the Indian Partnership Act, 1932.

### Characteristics

- Two or more partners.
- Agreement between partners.
- Shared ownership and responsibilities.
- Profit and loss sharing.
- Combined skills and resources.
- Mutual agency in a traditional partnership.

Mutual agency means that each partner may act as an agent of the firm and bind the firm through authorised business activities.

### Partnership Deed

A partnership deed is a written agreement describing the terms and conditions of the partnership.

It may contain:

- Names and addresses of partners.
- Nature of the business.
- Capital contributions.
- Profit-sharing ratio.
- Duties and responsibilities.
- Interest on capital or drawings.
- Admission and retirement conditions.
- Dispute resolution procedures.

### Advantages

- More capital than a typical sole proprietorship.
- Combined knowledge and skills.
- Shared responsibilities.
- Greater flexibility than many corporate structures.
- Easier formation than a company in many cases.

### Disadvantages

- Possibility of disagreements.
- Shared decision-making.
- Traditional partners generally face unlimited liability.
- A partner's actions may create obligations for the firm.
- Continuity can be affected by changes in partnership membership.

## 4. Joint Hindu Family Business

A Joint Hindu Family Business is a traditional form of business organisation found under Hindu personal law.

It is managed by the head of the family, known as the Karta.

### Characteristics

- Membership arises through the relevant family relationship under Hindu law.
- The Karta manages the business.
- Other eligible family members participate as coparceners.
- The Karta generally has broader management authority.
- Liability differs between the Karta and other coparceners.

The precise rights and liabilities depend on applicable personal law.

### Advantages

- Continuity through generations.
- Centralised management.
- Family-based cooperation.
- Traditional business knowledge can be preserved.

### Disadvantages

- Concentration of management authority.
- Potential family disputes.
- Limited managerial expertise.
- Capital may be restricted.
- Business decisions can become difficult when family interests conflict.

## 5. Cooperative Society

A cooperative society is a voluntary association formed by individuals who come together to satisfy common economic, social, or service-related needs.

In India, cooperative societies may be governed by central or state cooperative laws, depending on their area and nature of operation.

### Characteristics

- Voluntary membership.
- Democratic member participation.
- Service-oriented objectives.
- Shared economic interests.
- Member participation in governance.

The principle of democratic control is commonly expressed as one member, one vote, subject to the applicable cooperative law.

### Types of Cooperative Societies

**Consumer Cooperative**

Provides goods to members at reasonable prices.

**Producer Cooperative**

Helps producers obtain resources, process products, or market their output.

**Credit Cooperative**

Provides financial services and credit facilities to members.

**Housing Cooperative**

Helps members obtain or manage residential property.

**Marketing Cooperative**

Supports members in selling their products collectively.

### Advantages

- Encourages cooperation.
- Supports members with common interests.
- Democratic participation.
- Can improve bargaining power.
- Promotes mutual assistance.

### Disadvantages

- Limited access to large amounts of capital.
- Possible management inefficiency.
- Dependence on member participation.
- Decision-making may be slow.
- External competition can create pressure.

## 6. Joint Stock Company

A joint stock company is an incorporated business organisation with a separate legal identity from its shareholders.

Its capital is divided into shares, and ownership is represented by shareholding.

In India, companies are primarily governed by the Companies Act, 2013.

### Characteristics

- Separate legal entity.
- Ownership divided into shares.
- Limited liability for shareholders, subject to applicable law.
- Perpetual succession.
- Ownership and management may be separated.
- Greater ability to raise capital than many small business structures.

### Separate Legal Entity

A company has a legal identity distinct from its shareholders.

It can own property, enter contracts, borrow money, and sue or be sued in its own name.

### Perpetual Succession

The company continues to exist despite changes in its membership, subject to legal requirements and formal dissolution.

### Advantages

- Access to larger sources of capital.
- Limited liability for shareholders.
- Continuity of existence.
- Separate legal identity.
- Possibility of professional management.

### Disadvantages

- More complex formation.
- Regulatory and reporting requirements.
- Higher administrative costs.
- Separation of ownership and control may create conflicts.
- Decision-making can involve several levels of authority.

## 7. Private Company and Public Company

Companies may be classified according to their legal structure and ownership arrangements.

### Private Company

A private company generally restricts the transfer of its shares, limits the number of members as specified by law, and does not invite the public to subscribe to its securities.

Under the Companies Act, 2013, a private company generally requires at least two members and two directors, subject to applicable provisions.

### Public Company

A public company is not classified as a private company under company law.

It may raise capital from the public subject to legal and regulatory requirements.

Under the Companies Act, 2013, a public company generally requires at least seven members and three directors, subject to applicable provisions.

A public company is not automatically listed on a stock exchange. Listing involves additional requirements.

## 8. Limited Liability Partnership

A Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) combines certain features of a partnership and a corporate organisation.

In India, LLPs are governed by the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008.

### Characteristics

- Separate legal entity.
- Perpetual succession.
- Limited liability of partners, subject to applicable law.
- Flexible internal management.
- Partners generally act as agents of the LLP, not of other partners.

### Advantages

- Limited liability.
- Flexible management arrangements.
- Separate legal identity.
- Continuity of existence.
- Often less complex than a company in certain administrative respects.

### Disadvantages

- Compliance requirements.
- Difficulty in raising certain forms of investment capital compared with companies.
- Potential disagreements among partners.
- Regulatory obligations remain applicable.

## 9. Comparison of Business Organisations

| Basis | Sole Proprietorship | Partnership | Cooperative | Company |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership | One person | Two or more partners | Members | Shareholders |
| Legal identity | Generally not separate | Generally not separate | Separate under applicable law | Separate |
| Liability | Generally unlimited | Generally unlimited in traditional firms | Depends on applicable law and structure | Generally limited for shareholders |
| Management | Proprietor | Partners | Elected or appointed representatives | Board and management |
| Capital | Owner's resources | Partners' contributions | Member contributions and other permitted sources | Shares and other permitted sources |
| Continuity | Depends heavily on owner | Depends on agreement and law | Generally continuing | Perpetual succession |

## 10. Choosing a Suitable Business Structure

A business owner should consider the following factors:

- **Capital requirement:** How much funding is needed?
- **Liability:** How much personal financial risk can owners accept?
- **Control:** How much decision-making authority should remain with the owners?
- **Continuity:** Should the organisation continue independently of changes in ownership?
- **Compliance:** What legal and reporting obligations apply?
- **Growth:** Does the business plan to expand or attract investors?
- **Nature of operations:** Is the business small, professional, cooperative, or large-scale?

## Common Exam Traps

- A sole proprietorship is owned by one individual.
- Traditional partnership firms generally involve mutual agency and unlimited liability.
- A partnership deed records the terms of a partnership.
- A cooperative society is generally organised around common member interests.
- A company has a separate legal identity.
- Shareholders of a company generally enjoy limited liability.
- A private company and a public company have different legal requirements.
- A public company is not necessarily listed on a stock exchange.
- An LLP is a separate legal entity, unlike a traditional partnership firm.
- A company's perpetual succession allows it to continue despite changes in membership.
