Pricing is one of the most important decisions in business. It determines how much customers must pay to obtain a product or service and directly influences revenue, profitability, demand, and market position.

A suitable pricing decision must consider production costs, customer expectations, competitor prices, market conditions, and business objectives.

## 1. Meaning of Price and Pricing

**Price** is the amount of money a customer pays to purchase a product or service.

**Pricing** is the process of determining the amount a business should charge for its products or services.

Price is an important element of the marketing mix, commonly represented by the four Ps:

1. Product
2. Price
3. Place
4. Promotion

Unlike many other marketing decisions, price directly generates revenue. Product, promotion, and distribution generally involve expenditure.

## 2. Importance of Pricing

Pricing influences several aspects of business performance.

**Revenue generation:** The selling price determines how much revenue is earned from each unit sold.

**Profitability:** Price affects the difference between revenue and total costs.

**Market positioning:** A high price may communicate premium quality, while a lower price may attract price-sensitive customers.

**Customer perception:** Customers may use price as an indicator of quality, particularly when they have limited information.

**Competitive advantage:** Appropriate pricing can help a business attract customers and compete effectively.

**Demand:** Changes in price can influence the quantity customers are willing to purchase.

## 3. Objectives of Pricing

Businesses select pricing objectives according to their overall strategies and market conditions.

### Profit Maximisation

The business attempts to achieve the highest possible profit.

Profit is calculated as:

Profit = Total Revenue - Total Cost

A business may increase or decrease its price depending on how the change affects sales volume, revenue, and costs.

### Sales Maximisation

The business aims to increase the quantity of products sold.

It may use relatively low prices, discounts, or promotional offers to encourage purchases.

### Market Share Growth

The business attempts to capture a larger proportion of total industry sales.

Competitive pricing may help attract customers from established competitors.

### Survival

Businesses facing intense competition, declining demand, or financial difficulties may prioritise survival.

They may temporarily reduce prices to maintain sales and generate sufficient cash flow.

### Price Stability

Some businesses aim to maintain relatively stable prices over time.

This can help establish customer trust and reduce uncertainty for distributors and buyers.

### Premium Positioning

A business may charge a high price to communicate exclusivity, superior quality, advanced technology, or prestige.

This approach is common in luxury goods and specialised products.

### Social and Ethical Objectives

Some businesses consider affordability, accessibility, and social responsibility when setting prices.

Examples include affordable essential goods, educational services, and socially responsible pricing programmes.

## 4. Factors Affecting Pricing Decisions

Pricing decisions are influenced by internal and external factors.

### Internal Factors

**Business objectives:** Pricing must support the organisation's financial and strategic goals.

**Production costs:** Materials, labour, manufacturing, and operating expenses influence the minimum sustainable price.

**Marketing strategy:** Product quality, brand positioning, promotion, and distribution affect pricing decisions.

**Product characteristics:** Unique, innovative, or specialised products may support higher prices.

**Organisational structure:** In some businesses, senior management determines prices, while others delegate pricing authority to departmental managers.

### External Factors

**Customer demand:** The willingness and ability of customers to purchase a product influence acceptable prices.

**Competition:** Competitor prices and available alternatives affect pricing decisions.

**Economic conditions:** Inflation, interest rates, unemployment, and income levels influence purchasing power.

**Government regulations:** Taxes, price controls, consumer protection laws, and competition regulations may affect prices.

**Market conditions:** Changes in supply, demand, technology, and industry trends influence pricing opportunities.

## 5. Cost Concepts in Pricing

Businesses must understand different types of costs before establishing prices.

### Fixed Costs

Fixed costs remain constant within a relevant range of production, regardless of the number of units produced.

Examples:

- Factory rent.
- Insurance.
- Salaries of permanent administrative employees.
- Certain equipment expenses.

### Variable Costs

Variable costs change with the quantity produced or sold.

Examples:

- Raw materials.
- Direct production labour.
- Packaging materials.
- Production-related electricity consumption.

### Total Cost

Total cost is the sum of fixed and variable costs.

Total Cost = Fixed Cost + Variable Cost

### Average Cost

Average cost represents the cost of producing one unit.

Average Cost = Total Cost / Number of Units Produced

### Marginal Cost

Marginal cost is the additional cost of producing one more unit.

Marginal Cost = Change in Total Cost / Change in Quantity Produced

Understanding these costs helps businesses determine whether a proposed selling price is financially sustainable.

## 6. Revenue Concepts in Pricing

Revenue is the income generated from selling products or services.

### Total Revenue

Total revenue is calculated by multiplying the selling price by the quantity sold.

Total Revenue = Price x Quantity Sold

### Average Revenue

Average revenue represents revenue earned per unit sold.

Average Revenue = Total Revenue / Quantity Sold

When every unit is sold at the same price, average revenue equals the selling price.

### Marginal Revenue

Marginal revenue is the additional revenue generated by selling one more unit.

Marginal Revenue = Change in Total Revenue / Change in Quantity Sold

These concepts help businesses understand how changes in price and sales volume affect financial performance.

## 7. Demand and Price

Demand refers to the quantity of a product customers are willing and able to purchase at different prices during a particular period.

According to the law of demand, other factors remaining constant, an increase in price generally leads to a decrease in quantity demanded, while a decrease in price generally leads to an increase in quantity demanded.

However, the relationship may differ for certain goods and under unusual market conditions.

### Demand Schedule

A demand schedule shows the relationship between price and quantity demanded.

| Price per Unit | Quantity Demanded |
| --- | --- |
| 100 | 20 |
| 80 | 30 |
| 60 | 45 |
| 40 | 65 |
| 20 | 90 |

The table illustrates an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

## 8. Price Elasticity of Demand

Price Elasticity of Demand measures how strongly quantity demanded responds to a change in price.

It is calculated as:

Price Elasticity of Demand = Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded / Percentage Change in Price

The absolute value is commonly used when classifying demand elasticity.

### Elastic Demand

Demand is elastic when the absolute value of price elasticity is greater than 1.

A percentage change in price produces a proportionally larger percentage change in quantity demanded.

Customers are highly responsive to price changes.

### Inelastic Demand

Demand is inelastic when the absolute value of price elasticity is less than 1.

A percentage change in price produces a proportionally smaller percentage change in quantity demanded.

Customers are relatively less responsive to price changes.

### Unitary Elastic Demand

Demand is unitary elastic when the absolute value of price elasticity equals 1.

The percentage change in quantity demanded is equal to the percentage change in price.

### Perfectly Elastic Demand

Demand is perfectly elastic when customers are willing to purchase at one particular price but demand falls to zero if the price increases above it.

### Perfectly Inelastic Demand

Demand is perfectly inelastic when quantity demanded remains unchanged regardless of price changes.

## 9. Factors Affecting Price Elasticity

Several factors determine how sensitive customers are to price changes.

**Availability of substitutes:** Products with many close substitutes generally have more elastic demand.

**Necessity:** Essential products often have relatively inelastic demand.

**Proportion of income:** Expensive products that consume a large proportion of income may have more elastic demand.

**Time period:** Customers may become more responsive to price changes over time as they find alternatives.

**Brand loyalty:** Strong brand loyalty can reduce customer sensitivity to price changes.

**Nature of the product:** Luxury products often have more elastic demand than essential goods.

## 10. Relationship Between Price, Demand, and Revenue

The relationship between price elasticity and total revenue is important for pricing decisions.

| Demand Type | Effect of Price Increase | Effect of Price Decrease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elastic | Total revenue generally decreases | Total revenue generally increases |
| Inelastic | Total revenue generally increases | Total revenue generally decreases |
| Unitary elastic | Total revenue remains approximately unchanged | Total revenue remains approximately unchanged |

This relationship assumes other relevant factors remain constant.

Businesses can use elasticity estimates to evaluate whether changing prices is likely to increase or decrease total revenue.

## 11. Break-Even Pricing Considerations

A business must consider the quantity it needs to sell to cover its total costs.

The break-even point is the level of sales at which total revenue equals total cost.

Break-Even Quantity = Fixed Costs / (Selling Price per Unit - Variable Cost per Unit)

The difference between selling price and variable cost per unit is called the contribution per unit.

Example:

A business has fixed costs of 50,000, a selling price of 150 per unit, and a variable cost of 100 per unit.

Contribution per Unit = 150 - 100 = 50

Break-Even Quantity = 50,000 / 50 = 1,000 units

The business must sell 1,000 units to cover its total costs.

## 12. Ethical and Legal Considerations in Pricing

Businesses must ensure that pricing decisions comply with applicable laws and ethical standards.

Important considerations include:

- Avoiding misleading price advertisements.
- Clearly communicating additional charges.
- Following applicable consumer protection requirements.
- Avoiding unlawful collusion with competitors.
- Respecting relevant price-control regulations.
- Avoiding deceptive discount claims.
- Considering fairness when pricing essential goods and services.

Pricing decisions should balance commercial objectives with legal responsibilities and customer interests.

## Common Exam Traps

- Price directly generates revenue, whereas other marketing mix elements generally involve expenditure.
- Fixed costs do not change with production volume within the relevant range.
- Variable costs change with production volume.
- Marginal cost refers to the additional cost of producing another unit.
- Marginal revenue refers to the additional revenue from selling another unit.
- Elastic demand has an absolute price elasticity greater than 1.
- Inelastic demand has an absolute price elasticity below 1.
- A price increase generally reduces total revenue when demand is elastic.
- Break-even occurs when total revenue equals total cost.
- Pricing decisions must consider both internal and external factors.

- [ ] 500 units

> Explanation: Contribution per unit is 100 - 60 = 40. Break-even quantity is 40,000 / 40 = 1,000 units.
