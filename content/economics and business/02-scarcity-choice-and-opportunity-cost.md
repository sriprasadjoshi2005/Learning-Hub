Scarcity is the fundamental economic problem that arises because human wants are unlimited while the resources available to satisfy them are limited.

Individuals, businesses and governments must make choices about how to use their available resources. Every choice involves selecting one alternative while giving up another.

Understanding scarcity, choice and opportunity cost is essential for analysing economic decisions.

## 1. The Concept of Scarcity

**Scarcity** refers to the condition in which available resources are insufficient to satisfy all human wants simultaneously.

Human wants can continuously expand, but resources such as land, labour, capital, time and raw materials are limited.

Examples:

- A household has a limited monthly income and must prioritise its expenses.
- A company has limited funds and must choose between purchasing machinery and expanding its workforce.
- A government has a limited budget and must allocate funds among healthcare, education, infrastructure and defence.

Scarcity does not necessarily mean that a resource is extremely rare. It means that the resource is limited relative to demand.

## 2. Human Wants and Resources

Human wants are desires for goods and services that provide satisfaction.

### Characteristics of Human Wants

- Human wants are numerous.
- They differ between individuals.
- They change over time.
- Some wants are recurring.
- The intensity of wants varies.
- Satisfying one want may create additional wants.

### Economic Resources

The major economic resources include:

| Resource | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Land | Natural resources used in production | Soil, minerals and forests |
| Labour | Human physical and mental effort | Engineers and workers |
| Capital | Produced resources used for further production | Machinery and equipment |
| Entrepreneurship | Ability to organise resources and undertake business risks | Starting a manufacturing company |

These resources are also known as factors of production.

## 3. The Necessity of Choice

Because resources are scarce, economic agents must choose between competing alternatives.

For example, a business with a budget of Rs. 10 lakh might consider:

- Purchasing new machinery.
- Hiring additional employees.
- Increasing advertising expenditure.
- Developing a new product.

If the company cannot afford all four alternatives, it must select the combination that best serves its objectives.

Economic choice involves comparing expected benefits and costs.

## 4. Opportunity Cost

**Opportunity cost** is the value of the next best alternative that is sacrificed when a particular choice is made.

It represents the benefit that could have been obtained from the best alternative not selected.

### Example 1: Personal Decision

Suppose a person has Rs. 1,000 and can choose between:

- Buying a textbook worth Rs. 1,000.
- Purchasing a pair of headphones worth Rs. 1,000.

If the person purchases the textbook, the opportunity cost is the benefit that could have been obtained from purchasing the headphones.

### Example 2: Business Decision

A company has Rs. 50 lakh available for investment.

It can invest in:

- Project A, expected return of Rs. 8 lakh.
- Project B, expected return of Rs. 6 lakh.
- Project C, expected return of Rs. 5 lakh.

If the company chooses Project A, its opportunity cost is the expected return of Project B, assuming Project B is the next best alternative.

Opportunity cost is not necessarily the total value of all rejected alternatives. It is the value of the next best alternative.

## 5. Explicit and Implicit Costs

Economic costs include both direct monetary expenses and costs associated with resources owned by the decision-maker.

### Explicit Costs

Explicit costs are direct monetary payments made by a business.

Examples:
- Employee salaries.
- Rent.
- Electricity bills.
- Raw materials.
- Transportation expenses.

### Implicit Costs

Implicit costs represent the value of resources owned by a business or individual that are used without direct monetary payment.

Examples:
- An entrepreneur using their own building instead of renting it out.
- An owner working in their business without receiving a formal salary.
- Personal savings invested in a business instead of earning interest elsewhere.

## 6. Economic Cost and Accounting Cost

Accounting cost generally records explicit monetary expenses.

Economic cost includes both explicit and implicit costs.

Economic Cost = Explicit Cost + Implicit Cost

### Example

A business reports the following annual figures:

- Revenue = Rs. 20 lakh
- Explicit costs = Rs. 12 lakh
- Implicit costs = Rs. 3 lakh

Accounting Profit = Revenue - Explicit Costs

Accounting Profit = 20 - 12 = Rs. 8 lakh

Economic Profit = Revenue - Economic Costs

Economic Profit = 20 - 12 - 3 = Rs. 5 lakh

Economic profit provides a broader measure of profitability because it considers opportunity costs.

## 7. Marginal Decision-Making

Marginal analysis involves comparing the additional benefit and additional cost of an activity.

**Marginal Benefit (MB)** is the additional benefit obtained from consuming or producing one more unit.

**Marginal Cost (MC)** is the additional cost incurred from consuming or producing one more unit.

A rational decision-maker generally continues an activity when:

Marginal Benefit > Marginal Cost

The activity reaches its optimal level when:

Marginal Benefit = Marginal Cost

### Example

A company considers producing one additional unit of a product.

- Additional revenue = Rs. 500
- Additional production cost = Rs. 350

Marginal Benefit = Rs. 500

Marginal Cost = Rs. 350

Since the additional benefit exceeds the additional cost, producing the additional unit increases profit by Rs. 150.

## 8. Rational Economic Decision-Making

Rational economic decision-making involves selecting alternatives that best satisfy an individual's or organisation's objectives, subject to available resources and information.

A rational decision-maker generally:

1. Identifies the problem.
2. Determines available alternatives.
3. Evaluates costs and benefits.
4. Considers opportunity costs.
5. Selects the most suitable alternative.
6. Evaluates the outcome.

In practice, decisions may be influenced by uncertainty, incomplete information, habits and psychological factors.

## Common Exam Traps

- Scarcity exists because resources are limited relative to wants.
- Scarcity does not mean that every resource is absolutely rare.
- Opportunity cost is the next best alternative sacrificed, not every rejected alternative combined.
- Explicit costs involve direct monetary payments.
- Implicit costs may exist even when no money is paid.
- Economic profit considers implicit costs, whereas accounting profit generally does not.
- Marginal benefit and marginal cost refer to additional units, not total benefits and costs.
- A rational decision is not necessarily the cheapest decision; it is the decision that best satisfies the objective.
