Economics and business are closely connected fields that explain how individuals, organisations, governments and societies make decisions about resources, production, consumption and wealth creation.

Economics studies how scarce resources are allocated to satisfy human wants, while business studies how organisations use resources to produce goods and services, serve customers and generate value.

Understanding both subjects provides a foundation for analysing markets, financial decisions, entrepreneurship and economic development.

## 1. What Is Economics?

**Economics** is a social science that studies how individuals and societies allocate limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants.

Human wants include necessities such as food, clothing and shelter, as well as additional desires such as entertainment, travel and technology.

Since resources are limited, people must make choices about what to produce, purchase, save or consume.

### Main Branches of Economics

| Branch | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microeconomics | Studies individual consumers, firms and markets | Pricing of mobile phones |
| Macroeconomics | Studies the economy as a whole | Inflation and unemployment |
| International Economics | Studies economic relationships between countries | International trade |
| Development Economics | Studies economic growth and development | Poverty reduction |
| Public Economics | Studies government taxation and expenditure | Government budgets |

## 2. What Is Business?

**Business** refers to an organised economic activity involving the production, purchase or sale of goods and services to satisfy customer needs and create value.

Businesses operate in different industries, including manufacturing, agriculture, technology, transportation, healthcare and financial services.

### Main Characteristics of Business

- Economic activity: Business involves producing or exchanging goods and services.
- Customer satisfaction: Businesses attempt to satisfy customer needs and wants.
- Regular operations: Business activities are generally conducted continuously.
- Risk and uncertainty: Business outcomes are not always predictable.
- Profit or value creation: Businesses generally seek financial returns or sustainable value.
- Resource utilisation: Businesses combine labour, capital, materials and technology.

## 3. Goods and Services

Economic output is commonly divided into goods and services.

**Goods** are tangible products that can generally be seen and touched.

Examples:
- Food products
- Clothing
- Furniture
- Computers
- Vehicles

**Services** are intangible activities performed to satisfy customer needs.

Examples:
- Banking
- Education
- Transportation
- Healthcare
- Consulting

### Classification of Goods

| Type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consumer goods | Purchased for final consumption | Food |
| Capital goods | Used to produce other goods and services | Industrial machinery |
| Durable goods | Last for an extended period | Refrigerators |
| Non-durable goods | Consumed relatively quickly | Milk |
| Intermediate goods | Used as inputs in production | Steel used in automobiles |

## 4. Relationship Between Economics and Business

Economics provides the theoretical foundation for understanding markets, prices, demand, supply and resource allocation.

Business applies these economic principles when making organisational decisions.

For example, a company producing smartphones must consider:

- Consumer demand for smartphones.
- Availability and cost of components.
- Labour and manufacturing expenses.
- Competition from other manufacturers.
- Government taxation and regulations.
- Expected revenue and profitability.

Economic conditions influence business performance, while business activities contribute to employment, production, investment and economic growth.

## 5. Economic Agents

Economic agents are individuals or organisations that participate in economic activities.

### Households

Households consume goods and services, supply labour and receive income.

### Firms

Firms organise production, employ workers, invest capital and sell goods and services.

### Government

Government collects taxes, provides public services, regulates economic activities and influences economic stability.

### Financial Institutions

Banks and other financial institutions connect savers and borrowers and facilitate investment.

### Foreign Sector

The foreign sector represents economic interactions with other countries through imports, exports, investment and financial transactions.

## 6. Basic Economic Activities

Economic activity can be divided into four major stages.

1. Production: Creating goods and services.
2. Distribution: Making products and income available to different participants.
3. Exchange: Buying and selling goods and services.
4. Consumption: Using goods and services to satisfy wants.

These activities are interconnected and form the foundation of economic systems.

## 7. Importance of Studying Economics and Business

Studying these subjects helps individuals:

- Understand how markets operate.
- Make informed financial decisions.
- Analyse business opportunities.
- Understand inflation and unemployment.
- Evaluate government economic policies.
- Develop entrepreneurial skills.
- Understand international trade.
- Interpret financial and economic information.

## Common Exam Traps

- Economics is broader than the study of money; it examines resource allocation and decision-making.
- Business does not necessarily involve manufacturing. Service providers also conduct business activities.
- Microeconomics studies individual economic units, whereas macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole.
- Goods are generally tangible, while services are generally intangible.
- Production and consumption are different stages of economic activity.
- Capital goods are used in production, whereas consumer goods are intended for final consumption.
