Consumer behaviour studies how individuals make decisions about purchasing and consuming goods and services.

Consumers have limited income but numerous wants. They must therefore allocate their available resources among different products to obtain the greatest possible satisfaction.

Utility theory provides an economic framework for understanding consumer preferences, satisfaction and purchasing decisions.

## 1. Meaning of Consumer Behaviour

**Consumer behaviour** refers to the decisions and actions individuals undertake when selecting, purchasing, using and evaluating goods and services.

Consumer decisions are influenced by:

- Income.
- Prices.
- Preferences.
- Advertising.
- Social influences.
- Expectations.
- Product quality.
- Availability of substitutes.

Understanding consumer behaviour helps businesses develop products, design marketing strategies and predict demand.

## 2. Meaning of Utility

**Utility** refers to the satisfaction or benefit a consumer obtains from consuming a good or service.

Utility is a subjective concept because different individuals may obtain different levels of satisfaction from the same product.

For example:

- A person who enjoys music may obtain high utility from purchasing headphones.
- A person who prefers reading may obtain greater utility from purchasing books.

Utility is not necessarily the same as usefulness in an ordinary sense.

A product can provide economic utility even if its practical importance differs between consumers.

## 3. Types of Utility

### Form Utility

Form utility is created by transforming raw materials into finished products.

Example: Converting cotton into clothing.

### Place Utility

Place utility is created by making products available where consumers need them.

Example: Transporting agricultural products from farms to urban markets.

### Time Utility

Time utility is created by making products available when consumers need them.

Example: Storing agricultural products for sale during periods of higher demand.

### Possession Utility

Possession utility is created by transferring ownership or control of a product to a consumer.

Example: Purchasing a laptop through a retail store.

### Service Utility

Service utility arises from activities that directly satisfy consumer needs.

Example: Healthcare, transportation and education.

## 4. Total Utility and Marginal Utility

### Total Utility

Total Utility (TU) refers to the total satisfaction obtained from consuming a given quantity of a product.

### Marginal Utility

Marginal Utility (MU) refers to the additional satisfaction obtained from consuming one additional unit of a product.

Formula:

MU = Change in Total Utility / Change in Quantity Consumed

When consumption increases by one unit:

MU = TU of Current Unit - TU of Previous Unit

### Example

Consider the following utility schedule for a consumer eating slices of pizza.

| Slices Consumed | Total Utility | Marginal Utility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | - |
| 1 | 20 | 20 |
| 2 | 35 | 15 |
| 3 | 45 | 10 |
| 4 | 50 | 5 |
| 5 | 50 | 0 |
| 6 | 45 | -5 |

The table shows that total utility initially increases, but marginal utility decreases as additional slices are consumed.

## 5. Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility

The **Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility** states that, other things remaining constant, the marginal utility obtained from consuming successive units of a good generally decreases.

### Example

A person drinking glasses of water when thirsty may experience:

- High satisfaction from the first glass.
- Lower additional satisfaction from the second glass.
- Even lower additional satisfaction from the third glass.

Eventually, additional consumption may provide no extra satisfaction or may even cause discomfort.

### Assumptions

The traditional law assumes:

- Units consumed are reasonably similar.
- Consumption occurs within a relevant period.
- Consumer preferences remain unchanged.
- Consumption is continuous or sufficiently close.
- The consumer behaves consistently.

## 6. Relationship Between Total Utility and Marginal Utility

The relationship between total utility and marginal utility can be summarised as follows.

| Marginal Utility | Total Utility |
| --- | --- |
| Positive | Total utility increases |
| Zero | Total utility reaches its maximum under the standard model |
| Negative | Total utility decreases |

Marginal utility is the additional satisfaction from consumption, whereas total utility is the accumulated satisfaction.

## 7. Law of Equi-Marginal Utility

The **Law of Equi-Marginal Utility** explains how a consumer allocates limited income among different goods to maximise total satisfaction.

A consumer reaches equilibrium when the marginal utility obtained per unit of expenditure is equal across goods, subject to the consumer's budget constraint and other assumptions.

For two goods:

MUx / Px = MUy / Py

Where:

- MUx = Marginal utility of Good X.
- MUy = Marginal utility of Good Y.
- Px = Price of Good X.
- Py = Price of Good Y.

### Example

Suppose a consumer purchases two products.

| Product | Marginal Utility | Price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X | 40 | Rs. 10 |
| Y | 30 | Rs. 5 |

For Product X:

MUx / Px = 40 / 10 = 4

For Product Y:

MUy / Py = 30 / 5 = 6

The consumer obtains greater marginal utility per rupee from Product Y.

Under the traditional model, the consumer can improve total satisfaction by reallocating some expenditure towards Product Y, subject to the budget constraint.

Consumer equilibrium is reached when the marginal utility per rupee is equal across goods.

## 8. Consumer Budget Constraint

A consumer budget constraint represents the combinations of goods that a consumer can afford with a given income and market prices.

For two goods:

PxQx + PyQy = M

Where:

- Px = Price of Good X.
- Qx = Quantity of Good X.
- Py = Price of Good Y.
- Qy = Quantity of Good Y.
- M = Consumer income or available expenditure.

### Example

A consumer has Rs. 100 and purchases two goods.

- Price of Good X = Rs. 10.
- Price of Good Y = Rs. 20.

Budget equation:

10Qx + 20Qy = 100

Possible combinations include:

- 10 units of X and 0 units of Y.
- 6 units of X and 2 units of Y.
- 2 units of X and 4 units of Y.
- 0 units of X and 5 units of Y.

All these combinations use the entire budget.

## 9. Indifference Curve Analysis

Indifference curve analysis explains consumer preferences using combinations of two goods that provide equal satisfaction.

An **indifference curve** represents combinations of two goods between which a consumer is indifferent because each combination provides the same level of satisfaction.

### Characteristics of Indifference Curves

- They generally slope downward from left to right.
- Higher indifference curves represent higher satisfaction when preferences are well-behaved.
- They generally do not intersect.
- They are generally convex to the origin under the assumption of diminishing marginal rate of substitution.

### Marginal Rate of Substitution

The Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS) measures the quantity of one good a consumer is willing to sacrifice to obtain an additional unit of another good while maintaining the same satisfaction level.

For goods X and Y:

MRSxy = Amount of Y Sacrificed / Additional Unit of X

The marginal rate of substitution generally diminishes as a consumer obtains more of Good X and less of Good Y.

## 10. Consumer Equilibrium Using Indifference Curves

Consumer equilibrium occurs at the highest attainable indifference curve given the consumer's budget constraint.

For a standard interior equilibrium:

MRSxy = Px / Py

Where:

- MRSxy = Marginal rate of substitution of X for Y.
- Px = Price of Good X.
- Py = Price of Good Y.

This condition indicates that the consumer's willingness to exchange the two goods matches their market price ratio.

The equilibrium must also satisfy the budget constraint.

## 11. Factors Influencing Consumer Decisions

### Income

Consumers generally purchase more normal goods when their income increases.

### Price

Changes in product prices influence affordability and purchasing decisions.

### Preferences

Personal tastes, lifestyle and habits influence product selection.

### Advertising and Branding

Marketing can influence awareness, perceptions and preferences.

### Social and Cultural Factors

Family, peers, culture and social trends can affect consumption.

### Expectations

Expectations about future prices, income and availability may influence present purchases.

### Product Quality

Consumers may consider durability, reliability, performance and after-sales service.

## 12. Importance of Consumer Behaviour in Business

Businesses study consumer behaviour to:

- Identify target customers.
- Develop suitable products.
- Determine pricing strategies.
- Improve product quality.
- Design advertising campaigns.
- Understand brand preferences.
- Forecast market demand.
- Improve customer satisfaction and retention.

Understanding consumer preferences allows businesses to make more effective marketing and production decisions.

## Common Exam Traps

- Utility means satisfaction obtained from consumption, not necessarily practical usefulness.
- Total utility measures overall satisfaction, whereas marginal utility measures additional satisfaction.
- Positive marginal utility increases total utility.
- Zero marginal utility corresponds to maximum total utility under the standard diminishing marginal utility model.
- Negative marginal utility reduces total utility.
- Diminishing marginal utility does not necessarily mean that total utility decreases.
- The law of equi-marginal utility concerns equal marginal utility per unit of expenditure.
- An indifference curve represents combinations providing equal satisfaction.
- Higher indifference curves generally represent higher satisfaction.
- Consumer equilibrium using indifference curves occurs where the budget line is tangent to the highest attainable indifference curve.
- MRS is based on the consumer's willingness to exchange goods while maintaining satisfaction.
- Consumer behaviour is influenced by economic, psychological and social factors.
