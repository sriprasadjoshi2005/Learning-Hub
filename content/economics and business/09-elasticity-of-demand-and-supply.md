Elasticity measures how strongly one economic variable responds to a change in another variable.

In economics, elasticity is particularly useful for understanding how consumers and producers respond to changes in prices, income and related market conditions.

Businesses use elasticity to make pricing decisions, forecast revenue and understand customer behaviour.

## 1. Meaning of Elasticity

**Elasticity** measures the responsiveness of one variable to a percentage change in another variable.

For example, price elasticity of demand measures how much quantity demanded changes in response to a percentage change in price.

Elasticity is generally expressed as a ratio of percentage changes.

## 2. Price Elasticity of Demand

Price Elasticity of Demand (PED) measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the price of a product.

Formula:

PED = Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded / Percentage Change in Price

Because price and quantity demanded generally move in opposite directions, PED usually has a negative value.

In many introductory economics applications, the absolute value of PED is used for classification.

### Example

Suppose the price of a product increases by 10%, and quantity demanded decreases by 20%.

PED = -20% / 10%

PED = -2

Absolute PED = 2

This indicates elastic demand.

## 3. Degrees of Price Elasticity of Demand

### Perfectly Inelastic Demand

PED = 0

Quantity demanded does not change when price changes.

The demand curve is vertical.

### Relatively Inelastic Demand

0 < Absolute PED < 1

Quantity demanded changes by a smaller percentage than price.

### Unitary Elastic Demand

Absolute PED = 1

Quantity demanded changes by the same percentage as price.

### Relatively Elastic Demand

Absolute PED > 1

Quantity demanded changes by a greater percentage than price.

### Perfectly Elastic Demand

Absolute PED = Infinity

Consumers are willing to purchase at one particular price, but even a very small price increase may reduce quantity demanded to zero in the idealised model.

The demand curve is horizontal.

## 4. Factors Affecting Price Elasticity of Demand

### Availability of Substitutes

Products with many close substitutes generally have more elastic demand.

Example: Different brands of packaged snacks.

### Nature of the Product

Necessities often have relatively inelastic demand, while luxury goods may have more elastic demand.

### Proportion of Income Spent

Products that consume a large proportion of a consumer's income may have more elastic demand.

### Time Period

Demand often becomes more elastic over longer periods because consumers have more time to adjust their behaviour.

### Habit and Addiction

Products associated with strong habits or dependence may have relatively inelastic demand.

### Definition of the Market

Demand for a narrowly defined product is often more elastic than demand for a broad category.

For example, demand for one particular smartphone brand may be more elastic than demand for smartphones as a whole.

## 5. Price Elasticity and Total Revenue

Total Revenue (TR) is the total amount received by a business from selling its products.

TR = Price x Quantity Sold

The relationship between price elasticity of demand and total revenue is important for business pricing decisions.

| Demand Type | Price Increase | Price Decrease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elastic | Total revenue generally decreases | Total revenue generally increases |
| Inelastic | Total revenue generally increases | Total revenue generally decreases |
| Unitary elastic | Total revenue remains unchanged for small changes | Total revenue remains unchanged for small changes |

### Example: Elastic Demand

Initial situation:

- Price = Rs. 100
- Quantity = 100 units
- Revenue = Rs. 10,000

New situation:

- Price = Rs. 90
- Quantity = 130 units
- Revenue = Rs. 11,700

Although price decreases, total revenue increases because quantity demanded rises sufficiently.

## 6. Income Elasticity of Demand

Income Elasticity of Demand (YED) measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in consumer income.

Formula:

YED = Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded / Percentage Change in Income

### Types of Income Elasticity

**Positive YED:** Demand increases when income increases. This generally indicates a normal good.

**Negative YED:** Demand decreases when income increases. This indicates an inferior good.

**YED Greater Than 1:** Demand increases proportionately more than income. This is often associated with income-elastic or luxury goods.

**YED Between 0 and 1:** Demand increases proportionately less than income. This is often associated with necessities.

**YED Equal to 0:** Demand does not respond to income changes in the measured range.

## 7. Cross Elasticity of Demand

Cross Elasticity of Demand (XED) measures how the quantity demanded of one product responds to a percentage change in the price of another product.

Formula:

XED = Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded of Good A / Percentage Change in Price of Good B

### Substitute Goods

Substitute goods generally have positive cross elasticity.

Example: If the price of coffee increases, demand for tea may increase.

### Complementary Goods

Complementary goods generally have negative cross elasticity.

Example: If the price of printers increases, demand for printer ink cartridges may decrease.

### Unrelated Goods

Unrelated goods generally have cross elasticity close to zero.

## 8. Price Elasticity of Supply

Price Elasticity of Supply (PES) measures the responsiveness of quantity supplied to a percentage change in price.

Formula:

PES = Percentage Change in Quantity Supplied / Percentage Change in Price

Supply elasticity generally has a positive value because price and quantity supplied usually move in the same direction.

### Degrees of Price Elasticity of Supply

| Type | Elasticity | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perfectly inelastic | PES = 0 | Quantity supplied does not change |
| Relatively inelastic | 0 < PES < 1 | Quantity changes proportionately less than price |
| Unitary elastic | PES = 1 | Quantity changes proportionately equally to price |
| Relatively elastic | PES > 1 | Quantity changes proportionately more than price |
| Perfectly elastic | PES = Infinity | Supply responds at one particular price in the idealised model |

## 9. Factors Affecting Price Elasticity of Supply

### Time Period

Supply is generally more elastic over longer periods because producers can adjust production capacity.

### Availability of Resources

The availability of raw materials, labour and machinery influences the ability to increase output.

### Spare Production Capacity

Businesses with unused production capacity can generally respond more quickly to price increases.

### Storage Possibilities

Products that can be stored easily may have more flexible market supply.

### Production Complexity

Products requiring long manufacturing processes may have relatively inelastic short-run supply.

## 10. Elasticity and Business Decisions

Businesses use elasticity to:

- Determine suitable pricing strategies.
- Estimate the revenue effects of price changes.
- Identify customer sensitivity.
- Compare competing products.
- Forecast demand.
- Plan production capacity.
- Evaluate promotional offers.

For example, a business selling a product with elastic demand may increase revenue by reducing its price, provided the increase in sales volume is sufficiently large.

## Common Exam Traps

- Price elasticity of demand usually has a negative sign, but classification commonly uses its absolute value.
- Elastic demand has an absolute PED greater than 1.
- Inelastic demand has an absolute PED between 0 and 1.
- Unitary elastic demand has an absolute PED equal to 1.
- Necessities often have relatively inelastic demand.
- Luxury goods may have relatively elastic demand.
- Substitute goods generally have positive cross elasticity.
- Complementary goods generally have negative cross elasticity.
- Normal goods generally have positive income elasticity.
- Inferior goods generally have negative income elasticity.
- Elasticity measures percentage responsiveness, not absolute changes in quantity.
- Total revenue depends on both price and quantity sold.

## Quiz

1. If price increases by 10% and quantity demanded decreases by 30%, what is the absolute price elasticity of demand?

- [ ] 0.3
- [ ] 1
- [x] 3
- [ ] 10

> Absolute PED = 30% / 10% = 3. Since the value is greater than 1, demand is elastic.

2. Which type of goods generally has negative cross elasticity of demand?

- [ ] Substitute goods
- [x] Complementary goods
- [ ] Luxury goods
- [ ] Unrelated goods

> Complementary goods are used together. An increase in the price of one generally reduces demand for the other.

3. A product has an absolute price elasticity of demand of 0.5. How is its demand classified?

- [ ] Perfectly elastic
- [ ] Relatively elastic
- [x] Relatively inelastic
- [ ] Unitary elastic

> Since absolute PED is greater than zero but less than one, demand is relatively inelastic.
