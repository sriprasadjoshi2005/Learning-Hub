Businesses communicate with customers through advertising, public relations, sales promotions, direct marketing, digital platforms, and other promotional activities.

Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC) ensures that these communication methods work together to deliver a consistent brand message.

Advertising is one of the most widely used promotional tools and helps businesses inform, persuade, and remind customers about their products and services.

## 1. Meaning of Marketing Communication

Marketing communication refers to the methods businesses use to inform, persuade, and remind customers about their products, services, brands, and offerings.

Its main purposes include:

- Creating product awareness.
- Communicating product benefits.
- Influencing customer attitudes.
- Encouraging purchases.
- Building brand recognition.
- Maintaining customer relationships.
- Supporting long-term brand loyalty.

Marketing communication connects a business with its target audience.

## 2. Meaning of Integrated Marketing Communication

Integrated Marketing Communication is the strategic coordination of different promotional tools and communication channels to deliver a consistent and unified message.

For example, a company launching a new smartphone may use:

- Television advertisements.
- Social media campaigns.
- Product demonstrations.
- Influencer partnerships.
- Public relations activities.
- Retail promotions.
- Email marketing.

All these activities should communicate a consistent brand identity and product message.

## 3. Importance of Integrated Marketing Communication

### Consistent Brand Message

Customers receive similar information across different communication channels.

### Better Brand Recognition

Repeated and consistent communication helps customers recognise the brand.

### Improved Customer Experience

Customers encounter fewer contradictions between advertisements, websites, sales representatives, and product information.

### Efficient Resource Utilisation

Coordination reduces unnecessary duplication of promotional activities.

### Stronger Customer Relationships

Businesses can maintain communication with customers at different stages of the purchasing process.

### Improved Marketing Effectiveness

Combining communication channels can increase the overall impact of promotional campaigns.

## 4. Elements of the Promotion Mix

The promotion mix consists of different tools used to communicate with target customers.

### Advertising

Paid, non-personal communication through identified sponsors using media such as television, newspapers, websites, and social platforms.

### Sales Promotion

Short-term incentives designed to encourage immediate purchases.

Examples include discounts, coupons, samples, and contests.

### Personal Selling

Direct interaction between sales representatives and potential customers.

### Public Relations

Activities designed to maintain a favourable relationship between an organisation and its stakeholders.

### Direct Marketing

Communication directed towards selected customers through channels such as email, SMS, catalogues, and direct mail.

### Digital Marketing

Marketing activities conducted through digital platforms, including websites, search engines, social media, and mobile applications.

## 5. Meaning of Advertising

Advertising is a paid form of non-personal communication used by an identified sponsor to promote products, services, ideas, or brands.

Advertising allows businesses to communicate with large audiences through various media channels.

Examples include:

- Television commercials.
- Newspaper advertisements.
- Radio advertisements.
- Online video advertisements.
- Social media advertisements.
- Outdoor billboards.
- Search engine advertisements.

## 6. Objectives of Advertising

Advertising can serve several objectives.

### Informative Advertising

Informative advertising provides information about a product, service, or brand.

It is especially useful when introducing new products.

Example:

An advertisement explaining the features and benefits of a newly launched electric vehicle.

### Persuasive Advertising

Persuasive advertising attempts to influence customer preferences and encourage purchases.

It often highlights competitive advantages and product benefits.

### Reminder Advertising

Reminder advertising keeps an established product or brand in customers' minds.

It is commonly used for products with strong market recognition.

### Reinforcement Advertising

Reinforcement advertising reassures existing customers that they made a good purchasing decision.

It may emphasise customer satisfaction, product reliability, and after-sales support.

## 7. Types of Advertising Media

### Print Advertising

Print advertising uses physical publications.

Examples:

- Newspapers.
- Magazines.
- Brochures.
- Flyers.
- Catalogues.

Advantages:

- Can provide detailed information.
- May reach specialised audiences.
- Physical materials can be retained.

Limitations:

- Printing and distribution costs.
- Limited interaction.
- Some printed advertisements have a short useful life.

### Broadcast Advertising

Broadcast advertising uses television and radio.

Advantages:

- Can reach large audiences.
- Audio and visual presentation can attract attention.
- Useful for building brand awareness.

Limitations:

- Production and placement can be expensive.
- Some audiences may ignore advertisements.
- Measuring actual purchasing effects can be difficult.

### Outdoor Advertising

Outdoor advertising communicates messages in public spaces.

Examples:

- Billboards.
- Transit advertisements.
- Posters.
- Digital outdoor displays.

Advantages:

- Repeated exposure.
- Good visibility in busy locations.
- Useful for local awareness.

Limitations:

- Limited space for detailed information.
- Audience attention may be brief.
- Effectiveness depends on location.

### Digital Advertising

Digital advertising uses internet-connected platforms and technologies.

Examples:

- Search advertisements.
- Display advertisements.
- Social media advertisements.
- Online video advertisements.
- Mobile application advertisements.

Advantages:

- Audience targeting.
- Measurable performance.
- Interactive communication.
- Flexible campaign management.

Limitations:

- Advertising fatigue.
- Privacy concerns.
- Ad-blocking software.
- Competition for online attention.

## 8. Advertising Appeals

An advertising appeal is the method used to attract customer attention and influence attitudes.

### Rational Appeal

Rational advertising focuses on practical benefits and logical reasons to purchase.

Examples:

- Lower operating costs.
- Better performance.
- Longer product life.
- Improved efficiency.

### Emotional Appeal

Emotional advertising attempts to connect with feelings such as happiness, trust, nostalgia, belonging, or excitement.

### Fear Appeal

Fear-based advertising highlights potential risks or negative consequences to encourage a particular action.

It may be used in public safety and health-awareness campaigns.

### Humour Appeal

Humorous advertising uses entertainment to attract attention and improve brand recall.

### Social Appeal

Social appeals connect products with social acceptance, status, identity, or belonging.

## 9. The Advertising Communication Process

Advertising communication generally involves the following elements.

**Sender:** The business or organisation communicating the message.

**Encoding:** Converting the intended message into words, images, symbols, or other forms.

**Message:** The actual information being communicated.

**Media:** The channel through which the message is delivered.

**Receiver:** The intended audience.

**Decoding:** The process through which the audience interprets the message.

**Response:** The audience's reaction to the communication.

**Feedback:** Information received by the business about the audience's response.

**Noise:** Any factor that interferes with the intended communication.

Example:

A company creates a video advertisement for a new product. Customers watch the advertisement, interpret its message, and may visit the company's website. Their clicks, comments, and purchases provide feedback.

## 10. Advertising Campaign Planning

An advertising campaign is a coordinated series of advertisements designed to achieve specific marketing objectives.

Important planning stages include:

### Define Advertising Objectives

The business identifies what the campaign should achieve.

Examples include increasing brand awareness, generating website visits, or encouraging product trials.

### Identify the Target Audience

The business determines the customer group it wants to reach.

Segmentation may be based on:

- Age.
- Location.
- Income.
- Interests.
- Lifestyle.
- Purchasing behaviour.

### Develop the Advertising Message

The business decides what information should be communicated and what action customers should take.

### Select Advertising Media

The business selects channels according to audience characteristics, campaign objectives, and budget.

### Establish the Advertising Budget

The organisation determines how much money it can allocate to the campaign.

### Execute the Campaign

Advertisements are produced, scheduled, distributed, and monitored.

### Evaluate Performance

The business measures whether the campaign achieved its objectives.

## 11. Advertising Budgeting Methods

Businesses use different methods to determine advertising expenditure.

### Affordable Method

The business spends what it believes it can afford after considering other expenses.

### Percentage-of-Sales Method

Advertising expenditure is calculated as a percentage of current or expected sales.

### Competitive-Parity Method

The business considers competitors' advertising expenditure when setting its own budget.

### Objective-and-Task Method

The business identifies advertising objectives, determines the activities needed to achieve them, and estimates their costs.

This method directly links the advertising budget to campaign goals.

## 12. Advertising Effectiveness

Advertising effectiveness refers to how successfully an advertisement achieves its intended objectives.

Common measurement indicators include:

**Reach:** The number of different people exposed to an advertisement.

**Impressions:** The total number of times an advertisement is displayed.

**Click-through rate:** The percentage of impressions that result in clicks.

Click-Through Rate = (Number of Clicks / Number of Impressions) x 100

**Conversion rate:** The percentage of relevant visitors or users who complete a desired action.

Conversion Rate = (Number of Conversions / Number of Visitors) x 100

**Return on Advertising Spend:**

ROAS = Advertising-Attributed Revenue / Advertising Cost

**Brand awareness:** The extent to which customers recognise or recall a brand.

Businesses should select metrics that match their campaign objectives.

## 13. Digital Advertising and Targeting

Digital advertising allows businesses to reach selected audiences using online platforms and data.

Common targeting methods include:

**Demographic targeting:** Selecting audiences based on characteristics such as age and income.

**Geographic targeting:** Showing advertisements to people in selected locations.

**Interest-based targeting:** Reaching people according to their expressed interests and activities.

**Behavioural targeting:** Using relevant online behaviour to determine which advertisements may be suitable.

**Contextual targeting:** Displaying advertisements according to the subject of the webpage or content being viewed.

Digital targeting can improve relevance, but businesses must respect privacy requirements and applicable data protection laws.

## 14. Ethical and Legal Issues in Advertising

Advertising must be truthful, responsible, and legally compliant.

Important considerations include:

- Avoiding false or misleading claims.
- Clearly identifying paid promotions.
- Respecting customer privacy.
- Avoiding deceptive product demonstrations.
- Presenting prices and conditions transparently.
- Avoiding harmful stereotypes.
- Following advertising regulations.
- Taking special care with advertisements directed at children.

Ethical advertising helps establish long-term customer trust.

- [ ] To use only one promotional channel
- [x] To coordinate communication channels and deliver a consistent message
- [ ] To replace product development activities

> Explanation: IMC coordinates promotional tools so that customers receive a consistent brand message across different channels.

- [ ] Reminder advertising
- [x] Informative advertising
- [ ] Reinforcement advertising
- [ ] Prestige advertising

> Explanation: Informative advertising communicates product features, benefits, and other essential information to potential customers.

- [ ] 2%
- [ ] 10%
- [x] 5%
- [ ] 20%

> Explanation: Click-through rate = (500 / 10,000) x 100 = 5%.
