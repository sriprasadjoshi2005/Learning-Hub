Market research is the systematic process of collecting, analysing, and interpreting information about customers, competitors, and markets.

Consumer behaviour examines how individuals and groups select, purchase, use, and evaluate products and services.

Together, these subjects help businesses understand demand, identify market opportunities, develop suitable products, and make effective marketing decisions.

## 1. Meaning of Market Research

Market research involves gathering information to reduce uncertainty in marketing decisions.

It helps organisations understand:

- Customer needs.
- Purchasing preferences.
- Market size.
- Competitor activities.
- Product expectations.
- Price sensitivity.
- Brand awareness.
- Customer satisfaction.

**Example:**

A company planning to introduce a new energy-efficient refrigerator may research household preferences, acceptable prices, preferred features, and competing brands.

This information helps the company decide whether the product has a realistic market opportunity.

## 2. Importance of Market Research

Market research supports business decisions by providing relevant evidence.

Its importance includes:

- Identifying customer requirements.
- Estimating market demand.
- Understanding competitors.
- Evaluating new product ideas.
- Selecting target markets.
- Determining suitable prices.
- Reducing business uncertainty.
- Measuring customer satisfaction.
- Identifying emerging trends.

Research does not eliminate uncertainty, but it can improve the quality of decisions.

## 3. Types of Market Research

### Primary Research

Primary research involves collecting original information directly for a particular research objective.

Common methods include:

- Surveys.
- Interviews.
- Focus groups.
- Observation.
- Experiments.
- Product testing.

Advantages:

- Information can be specific to the research problem.
- Data can be collected from a selected target audience.
- Questions can be designed according to business requirements.

Limitations:

- Can be expensive.
- May require considerable time.
- Poor research design can produce biased results.

### Secondary Research

Secondary research uses information that has already been collected for another purpose.

Sources include:

- Government reports.
- Industry publications.
- Academic studies.
- Company records.
- Market research reports.
- Public databases.
- Published statistics.

Advantages:

- Often less expensive.
- Can provide historical information.
- Useful for understanding broad market trends.

Limitations:

- Information may be outdated.
- Data may not match the exact research question.
- Methodology and reliability may vary.

## 4. Qualitative and Quantitative Research

### Qualitative Research

Qualitative research examines opinions, experiences, motivations, and attitudes.

Common methods include interviews, focus groups, and open-ended questions.

It helps explain why customers behave in particular ways.

### Quantitative Research

Quantitative research collects numerical information that can be analysed statistically.

Examples include:

- Purchase frequency.
- Customer ratings.
- Market share.
- Sales volume.
- Survey percentages.

It helps measure patterns and relationships.

| Basis | Qualitative | Quantitative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main focus | Meanings and experiences | Numerical measurement |
| Data | Words and observations | Numbers |
| Methods | Interviews and focus groups | Structured surveys and experiments |
| Analysis | Thematic interpretation | Statistical analysis |
| Typical purpose | Understanding why | Measuring how much or how often |

Both methods can be combined in mixed-method research.

## 5. Market Research Process

A systematic market research process usually involves the following stages:

1. Define the research problem.
2. Establish research objectives.
3. Develop a research plan.
4. Identify required information.
5. Select research methods.
6. Determine the target population and sample.
7. Collect data.
8. Analyse and interpret the data.
9. Present findings.
10. Make decisions based on the findings.

Clearly defining the problem is essential because collecting large amounts of irrelevant information wastes resources.

## 6. Research Methods

### Surveys

Surveys collect information from respondents using structured questions.

They may be conducted online, by telephone, through printed forms, or in person.

Surveys are useful for measuring customer preferences and satisfaction.

### Interviews

Interviews involve direct conversations with respondents.

They may be structured, semi-structured, or unstructured.

Interviews can provide detailed explanations of customer attitudes and experiences.

### Focus Groups

A focus group is a guided discussion involving a small group of selected participants.

It helps researchers explore opinions, perceptions, and reactions to products or ideas.

### Observation

Observation involves recording actual customer behaviour.

For example, a retailer may observe how customers move through a store and interact with product displays.

### Experiments

Experiments examine the effect of changing one or more variables while controlling other relevant conditions.

Example:

A company may test two different website layouts to determine which produces a higher purchase conversion rate.

## 7. Sampling

Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of individuals or units from a larger population for research.

A population is the complete group that the research intends to study.

A sample is the selected portion of that population.

Sampling helps reduce the time and cost required to collect information.

### Probability Sampling

In probability sampling, members of the population have a known chance of selection.

Common methods include:

**Simple Random Sampling**

Every member of the population has an equal chance of selection.

**Systematic Sampling**

Researchers select members at regular intervals from an ordered list after choosing a starting point.

**Stratified Sampling**

The population is divided into relevant groups, and samples are selected from each group.

**Cluster Sampling**

The population is divided into clusters, and selected clusters are studied.

### Non-Probability Sampling

In non-probability sampling, selection does not use random procedures with known selection probabilities.

Common methods include:

**Convenience Sampling**

Participants are selected because they are easily accessible.

**Judgement Sampling**

Researchers select participants based on their expertise or judgement.

**Quota Sampling**

Participants are selected to meet specified group proportions.

**Snowball Sampling**

Existing participants help researchers identify additional participants.

## 8. Sampling Errors and Research Bias

Sampling error occurs because information is collected from a sample rather than the entire population.

Other research problems include:

- Selection bias.
- Non-response bias.
- Poorly worded questions.
- Leading questions.
- Inaccurate responses.
- Researcher bias.
- Inadequate sample size.

Researchers should use suitable sampling methods, clear questions, and appropriate data collection procedures.

## 9. Meaning of Consumer Behaviour

Consumer behaviour examines how people make decisions about purchasing, using, and disposing of products and services.

It considers the psychological, social, cultural, and economic influences on purchasing decisions.

Understanding consumer behaviour helps businesses develop relevant products and effective marketing strategies.

## 10. Factors Influencing Consumer Behaviour

### Cultural Factors

Culture influences values, beliefs, preferences, and consumption patterns.

Subcultures and social traditions can also influence purchasing decisions.

### Social Factors

Social influences include:

- Family.
- Friends.
- Reference groups.
- Social roles.
- Social status.
- Online communities.

For example, recommendations from friends may influence the choice of a smartphone brand.

### Personal Factors

Personal factors include:

- Age.
- Occupation.
- Income.
- Lifestyle.
- Personality.
- Life stage.

Different consumers may prefer different products because their circumstances and priorities vary.

### Psychological Factors

Psychological factors include:

- Motivation.
- Perception.
- Learning.
- Beliefs.
- Attitudes.

These factors influence how customers interpret marketing messages and evaluate products.

## 11. Consumer Buying Decision Process

A common model identifies five stages in the consumer buying process.

### Need Recognition

The consumer recognises a need or problem.

Example: A person realises that their existing laptop is too slow for their work.

### Information Search

The consumer gathers information from personal, commercial, public, and experiential sources.

### Evaluation of Alternatives

The consumer compares available products using factors such as price, quality, features, and reliability.

### Purchase Decision

The consumer selects and purchases a product.

The final purchase may still be affected by availability, payment conditions, or opinions of others.

### Post-Purchase Behaviour

After purchasing, the consumer evaluates whether the product meets expectations.

Positive experiences may encourage repeat purchases, while dissatisfaction may result in complaints or brand switching.

## 12. Types of Consumer Buying Behaviour

### Complex Buying Behaviour

Occurs when purchasing decisions involve significant differences between brands and high consumer involvement.

Example: Purchasing an expensive vehicle.

### Dissonance-Reducing Buying Behaviour

Occurs when involvement is high but perceived differences between brands are relatively small.

Consumers may experience uncertainty after purchase.

### Habitual Buying Behaviour

Occurs when involvement is low and perceived differences between brands are limited.

Example: Purchasing familiar everyday household products.

### Variety-Seeking Buying Behaviour

Occurs when involvement is relatively low but consumers perceive meaningful differences between brands.

Example: Trying different snack brands.

## 13. Customer Satisfaction

Customer satisfaction reflects the extent to which a product or service meets or exceeds customer expectations.

A simplified relationship is:

- Performance below expectations: Dissatisfaction.
- Performance equal to expectations: Satisfaction.
- Performance above expectations: Potential delight.

Customer satisfaction can be measured through surveys, reviews, complaints, repeat purchases, and other indicators.

## 14. Customer Loyalty

Customer loyalty refers to a customer's continuing preference for and relationship with a business or brand.

Loyal customers may:

- Purchase repeatedly.
- Recommend products to others.
- Resist switching to competitors.
- Provide useful feedback.
- Participate in loyalty programmes.

However, repeat purchasing does not always indicate strong emotional loyalty. Customers may also continue purchasing because of convenience, habit, or limited alternatives.

## 15. Digital Consumer Behaviour

Digital platforms have changed how consumers discover, evaluate, purchase, and review products.

Important influences include:

- Search engines.
- Online reviews.
- Social media.
- Influencer recommendations.
- Product comparison websites.
- Personalised advertising.
- E-commerce platforms.
- Customer ratings.

Businesses should use customer data responsibly and respect privacy and consent requirements.

## 16. Research Ethics

Market research should protect participants and maintain the integrity of collected information.

Important principles include:

- Informed consent.
- Privacy protection.
- Confidentiality.
- Accurate reporting.
- Avoidance of deception.
- Responsible handling of personal information.
- Voluntary participation.

Ethical research improves public trust and the reliability of business decisions.

## Common Exam Traps

- Primary research collects original data; secondary research uses existing information.
- Qualitative research explores opinions and motivations; quantitative research measures numerical information.
- A population is the complete group under study, while a sample is a selected portion.
- Stratified sampling selects participants from different population groups.
- Convenience sampling is a non-probability method.
- Observation studies actual behaviour, while interviews collect reported experiences and opinions.
- Consumer behaviour is influenced by cultural, social, personal, and psychological factors.
- The buying decision process includes need recognition, information search, evaluation, purchase, and post-purchase behaviour.
- Customer satisfaction and customer loyalty are related but not identical.
