Businesses use several promotional techniques to attract customers, increase sales, develop relationships, and establish a positive public image.

Sales promotion encourages immediate purchasing through incentives. Personal selling involves direct interaction between sales representatives and customers. Public relations focuses on maintaining favourable relationships between an organisation and its stakeholders.

## 1. Meaning of Sales Promotion

Sales promotion refers to short-term incentives designed to encourage customers or distributors to purchase products or services.

It is commonly used alongside advertising and other promotional activities.

Examples include:

- Discounts.
- Coupons.
- Free samples.
- Cashback offers.
- Contests.
- Loyalty rewards.
- Buy-one-get-one offers.
- Limited-period promotions.

## 2. Objectives of Sales Promotion

Sales promotion serves several business objectives.

**Increase immediate sales:** Temporary incentives encourage customers to purchase sooner.

**Attract new customers:** Offers can encourage people to try unfamiliar products.

**Clear inventory:** Discounts help businesses sell excess or seasonal stock.

**Encourage repeat purchases:** Loyalty rewards motivate customers to continue buying.

**Support new product launches:** Samples and introductory offers reduce the perceived risk of trying new products.

**Encourage larger purchases:** Quantity discounts and bundled offers may increase average transaction value.

**Support distributors:** Trade promotions encourage retailers and wholesalers to stock and promote products.

## 3. Consumer Sales Promotion Techniques

Consumer sales promotions are directed towards final customers.

### Coupons

Coupons provide customers with a specified discount when purchasing a product.

They may be distributed through newspapers, websites, mobile applications, or promotional messages.

### Discounts

Discounts reduce the normal selling price for a limited period or under specified conditions.

### Free Samples

Free samples allow customers to experience a product before purchasing it.

They are particularly useful for introducing new food, cosmetic, and personal-care products.

### Cashback Offers

Customers receive part of the purchase amount back after completing a qualifying transaction.

### Buy-One-Get-One Offers

Customers receive an additional product when purchasing a qualifying item.

### Contests and Competitions

Customers participate in a promotional activity for an opportunity to receive prizes.

### Loyalty Programmes

Customers receive rewards based on repeat purchases or continued engagement.

### Product Demonstrations

Businesses demonstrate product features and performance to encourage customers to make purchasing decisions.

## 4. Trade Sales Promotion

Trade promotions are directed towards intermediaries such as wholesalers, distributors, and retailers.

Common techniques include:

**Trade discounts:** Reduced prices offered to channel members.

**Display allowances:** Financial support provided to retailers for displaying products.

**Dealer incentives:** Rewards for achieving specified sales targets.

**Free goods:** Additional products supplied when a distributor purchases a qualifying quantity.

**Cooperative advertising:** Manufacturers and retailers share promotional expenditure.

Trade promotions help businesses encourage intermediaries to stock, display, and recommend their products.

## 5. Sales Force Promotion

Sales force promotion consists of incentives provided to an organisation's sales employees.

Examples include:

- Sales commissions.
- Performance bonuses.
- Recognition awards.
- Sales competitions.
- Target-based rewards.
- Training incentives.

These methods aim to improve employee motivation and sales performance.

## 6. Advantages and Limitations of Sales Promotion

### Advantages

- Can generate immediate sales.
- Encourages product trials.
- Helps clear excess inventory.
- Attracts price-sensitive customers.
- Supports new product launches.
- Can improve retailer participation.

### Limitations

- Reduces profit margins.
- May create dependence on discounts.
- Can weaken premium brand perception.
- May attract customers who do not become regular buyers.
- Competitors can quickly introduce similar offers.

Sales promotion should support long-term marketing objectives rather than become the only reason customers purchase a product.

## 7. Meaning of Personal Selling

Personal selling is a promotional method in which a salesperson communicates directly with potential or existing customers to explain products, understand requirements, answer questions, and encourage purchases.

It may take place face-to-face, over telephone calls, or through online meetings.

Personal selling is particularly useful for expensive, technical, customised, or complex products.

Examples include:

- Selling industrial machinery.
- Explaining financial services.
- Demonstrating enterprise software.
- Selling automobiles.
- Providing customised business solutions.

## 8. Characteristics of Personal Selling

### Direct Interaction

The salesperson communicates directly with the customer.

### Two-Way Communication

Customers can ask questions and provide immediate feedback.

### Personalised Communication

The salesperson can adjust the explanation according to individual customer requirements.

### Relationship Building

Personal selling can establish long-term relationships and customer trust.

### Immediate Feedback

Sales representatives can understand customer objections and respond during the interaction.

## 9. Personal Selling Process

Personal selling generally follows a sequence of activities.

### Prospecting

Identifying potential customers who may have a need for the product or service.

### Pre-Approach

Collecting information about the potential customer before making contact.

### Approach

Making the initial contact and establishing communication.

### Presentation and Demonstration

Explaining the product's features, advantages, and benefits.

### Handling Objections

Responding to customer concerns regarding price, quality, suitability, reliability, or other issues.

### Closing the Sale

Encouraging the customer to complete the purchase when appropriate.

### Follow-Up

Maintaining contact after the purchase to check satisfaction, provide assistance, and develop future business opportunities.

## 10. Skills Required for Effective Personal Selling

An effective salesperson should possess:

- Communication skills.
- Product knowledge.
- Listening skills.
- Persuasion skills.
- Problem-solving ability.
- Negotiation skills.
- Emotional awareness.
- Confidence.
- Honesty and professionalism.

A successful salesperson should understand customer needs rather than focus only on completing transactions.

## 11. Advantages and Limitations of Personal Selling

### Advantages

- Provides personalised communication.
- Allows immediate clarification of doubts.
- Supports complex product demonstrations.
- Helps build customer relationships.
- Allows flexible negotiation.
- Provides direct customer feedback.

### Limitations

- Can be expensive per customer reached.
- Requires trained sales personnel.
- Reaching large audiences is difficult.
- Sales performance may vary between employees.
- The process can require significant time.

## 12. Meaning of Public Relations

Public Relations (PR) refers to the planned management of communication and relationships between an organisation and its stakeholders.

Its purpose is to develop understanding, credibility, goodwill, and a favourable organisational image.

Stakeholders may include:

- Customers.
- Employees.
- Investors.
- Suppliers.
- Government authorities.
- Local communities.
- Media organisations.

Public relations is broader than product promotion because it also concerns the organisation's overall reputation and relationships.

## 13. Objectives of Public Relations

**Build organisational reputation:** Develop a favourable public image.

**Establish credibility:** Communicate trustworthy information about the organisation.

**Maintain stakeholder relationships:** Improve communication with customers, employees, investors, and communities.

**Manage public opinion:** Respond to concerns and communicate organisational activities.

**Support product launches:** Generate public interest through media coverage and events.

**Manage crises:** Provide accurate information during unexpected events.

**Build community goodwill:** Support social initiatives and responsible business activities.

## 14. Public Relations Tools

### Press Releases

Official statements distributed to media organisations to communicate important announcements.

### Press Conferences

Events where organisational representatives communicate with journalists and answer questions.

### Media Relations

Building professional relationships with journalists, editors, and media organisations.

### Public Events

Organising exhibitions, product launches, seminars, and community activities.

### Sponsorships

Supporting events, organisations, or activities to develop visibility and goodwill.

### Community Relations

Developing positive relationships with local communities through engagement and support.

### Corporate Social Responsibility

Communicating and carrying out responsible initiatives related to society and the environment.

### Corporate Publications

Producing newsletters, reports, magazines, and other materials to communicate organisational information.

## 15. Crisis Public Relations

Crisis public relations involves managing communication during events that may damage an organisation's reputation or operations.

Examples include:

- Product safety problems.
- Data breaches.
- Environmental incidents.
- Workplace accidents.
- Service failures.
- Public controversies.

Effective crisis communication should include:

1. Responding quickly with verified information.
2. Acknowledging legitimate concerns.
3. Communicating clearly and honestly.
4. Explaining actions being taken.
5. Providing regular updates.
6. Correcting misinformation.
7. Evaluating the response after the crisis.

Organisations should avoid hiding important information or making unsupported claims.

## 16. Difference Between Advertising, Sales Promotion, Personal Selling, and Public Relations

| Basis | Advertising | Sales Promotion | Personal Selling | Public Relations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main purpose | Build awareness and influence attitudes | Encourage immediate purchases | Develop customer relationships and generate sales | Build reputation and goodwill |
| Communication | Mostly non-personal | Uses various promotional incentives | Direct personal interaction | Uses various communication channels |
| Time orientation | Often long-term | Usually short-term | Can be short-term or long-term | Generally long-term |
| Audience | Broad or targeted audience | Consumers and trade members | Individual or organisational customers | Multiple stakeholder groups |
| Example | Television advertisement | Limited-period discount | Product demonstration | Press conference |

## 17. Measuring Promotional Effectiveness

Businesses should evaluate promotional activities using measurable indicators.

### Sales Promotion Metrics

- Number of coupons redeemed.
- Number of promotional purchases.
- Increase in sales volume.
- Average transaction value.
- Number of new customers acquired.

### Personal Selling Metrics

- Number of sales calls.
- Conversion rate.
- Sales revenue per salesperson.
- Average deal value.
- Customer retention.
- Follow-up success.

### Public Relations Metrics

- Media coverage.
- Public sentiment.
- Event participation.
- Website referral traffic.
- Brand awareness.
- Stakeholder feedback.

No single measurement provides a complete picture of promotional effectiveness. Businesses should use several relevant indicators.

## 18. Ethical Considerations in Promotion

Promotional activities must be honest and responsible.

Businesses should:

- Avoid false claims.
- Clearly communicate promotional conditions.
- Avoid misleading discounts.
- Respect customer privacy.
- Avoid high-pressure selling.
- Disclose sponsored relationships.
- Provide accurate product information.
- Honour valid promotional commitments.

Ethical promotion supports customer confidence and long-term business reputation.

## Common Exam Traps

- Sales promotion uses short-term incentives to encourage purchases.
- Consumer promotions are directed towards final customers.
- Trade promotions are directed towards intermediaries.
- Personal selling involves direct interaction between salespeople and customers.
- The personal selling process includes prospecting, approach, presentation, handling objections, closing, and follow-up.
- Public relations focuses on organisational reputation and stakeholder relationships.
- Public relations is broader than product advertising.
- Press releases communicate official information to media organisations.
- Crisis PR requires accurate and timely communication.
- Excessive discounting can reduce profitability and damage brand perception.
