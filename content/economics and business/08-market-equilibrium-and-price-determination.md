title: Market Equilibrium and Price Determination
level: foundation

Market equilibrium occurs when the quantity demanded by consumers equals the quantity supplied by producers at a particular price.

At this point, the market has no inherent tendency to change its price because planned purchases and planned sales are equal.

Understanding market equilibrium explains how prices are determined, how shortages and surpluses arise and how changes in demand and supply influence markets.

## 1. Meaning of Market Equilibrium

**Market equilibrium** is a situation in which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied at a particular market price.

At equilibrium:

Quantity Demanded = Quantity Supplied

The corresponding price is called the **equilibrium price**, and the corresponding quantity is called the **equilibrium quantity**.

Market equilibrium is established through the interaction of demand and supply.

## 2. Demand and Supply Schedule

Consider the following market for a product.

| Price (Rs.) | Quantity Demanded | Quantity Supplied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 100 | 20 |
| 20 | 80 | 40 |
| 30 | 60 | 60 |
| 40 | 40 | 80 |
| 50 | 20 | 100 |

At a price of Rs. 30:

- Quantity demanded = 60 units.
- Quantity supplied = 60 units.

Therefore:

Equilibrium Price = Rs. 30

Equilibrium Quantity = 60 units

## 3. Graphical Determination of Equilibrium

Market equilibrium can be identified using the intersection of demand and supply curves.

- The demand curve generally slopes downward.
- The supply curve generally slopes upward.
- Their intersection represents market equilibrium.

The intersection determines the equilibrium price and quantity.

## 4. Excess Demand and Shortage

A shortage occurs when quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the prevailing market price.

Shortage = Quantity Demanded - Quantity Supplied

### Example

At a price of Rs. 20:

- Quantity demanded = 80 units.
- Quantity supplied = 40 units.

Shortage = 80 - 40 = 40 units.

### Market Adjustment

A shortage creates upward pressure on prices.

As prices rise:

- Quantity demanded generally decreases.
- Quantity supplied generally increases.

The market moves towards equilibrium.

## 5. Excess Supply and Surplus

A surplus occurs when quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded at the prevailing market price.

Surplus = Quantity Supplied - Quantity Demanded

### Example

At a price of Rs. 40:

- Quantity demanded = 40 units.
- Quantity supplied = 80 units.

Surplus = 80 - 40 = 40 units.

### Market Adjustment

A surplus creates downward pressure on prices.

As prices fall:

- Quantity demanded generally increases.
- Quantity supplied generally decreases.

The market moves towards equilibrium.

## 6. Changes in Market Equilibrium

Equilibrium changes when demand, supply or both change.

### Increase in Demand

If supply remains unchanged, an increase in demand generally causes:

- Equilibrium price to increase.
- Equilibrium quantity to increase.

### Decrease in Demand

If supply remains unchanged, a decrease in demand generally causes:

- Equilibrium price to decrease.
- Equilibrium quantity to decrease.

### Increase in Supply

If demand remains unchanged, an increase in supply generally causes:

- Equilibrium price to decrease.
- Equilibrium quantity to increase.

### Decrease in Supply

If demand remains unchanged, a decrease in supply generally causes:

- Equilibrium price to increase.
- Equilibrium quantity to decrease.

## 7. Combined Changes in Demand and Supply

When demand and supply change simultaneously, the final equilibrium depends on the relative magnitude of the changes.

| Change | Equilibrium Price | Equilibrium Quantity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand increases, supply unchanged | Increases | Increases |
| Demand decreases, supply unchanged | Decreases | Decreases |
| Supply increases, demand unchanged | Decreases | Increases |
| Supply decreases, demand unchanged | Increases | Decreases |
| Demand and supply both increase | Uncertain | Increases |
| Demand and supply both decrease | Uncertain | Decreases |
| Demand increases and supply decreases | Increases | Uncertain |
| Demand decreases and supply increases | Decreases | Uncertain |

Uncertain means that the result depends on the relative magnitude of the changes.

## 8. Algebraic Determination of Equilibrium

Market equilibrium can also be calculated using demand and supply equations.

Consider:

Demand Function:

Qd = 100 - 2P

Supply Function:

Qs = 20 + 2P

At equilibrium:

Qd = Qs

100 - 2P = 20 + 2P

100 - 20 = 2P + 2P

80 = 4P

P = 20

Substituting the equilibrium price into the demand equation:

Qd = 100 - 2(20)

Qd = 60

Therefore:

Equilibrium Price = Rs. 20

Equilibrium Quantity = 60 units

## 9. Government Intervention in Market Prices

Governments sometimes intervene in markets to influence prices and protect consumers or producers.

### Price Ceiling

A price ceiling is a legally established maximum price that sellers are permitted to charge.

If it is set below the equilibrium price in a competitive market, it can create a shortage.

Examples may include rent controls and certain regulated essential-goods prices.

### Price Floor

A price floor is a legally established minimum price below which a product cannot legally be sold.

If it is set above the equilibrium price in a competitive market, it can create a surplus.

Examples include certain agricultural minimum support arrangements and minimum wage regulations.

A price ceiling above equilibrium or a price floor below equilibrium is generally non-binding.

## 10. Market Equilibrium and Business Decisions

Businesses use market equilibrium analysis to understand:

- Appropriate pricing strategies.
- Expected sales volumes.
- Production requirements.
- Market shortages.
- Excess inventory.
- Consumer purchasing behaviour.
- The effects of competitors and market changes.

Changes in demand and supply can significantly influence business revenue and profitability.

## Common Exam Traps

- Market equilibrium occurs when quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
- Equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity are determined together.
- A shortage occurs when quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied.
- A surplus occurs when quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.
- Shortages create upward pressure on prices under ordinary competitive market conditions.
- Surpluses create downward pressure on prices under ordinary competitive market conditions.
- An increase in demand generally raises both equilibrium price and quantity when supply is unchanged.
- An increase in supply generally lowers equilibrium price and increases equilibrium quantity when demand is unchanged.
- A binding price ceiling below equilibrium can create a shortage.
- A binding price floor above equilibrium can create a surplus.
