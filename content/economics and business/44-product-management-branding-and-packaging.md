Product management involves planning, developing, improving, and managing products throughout their market life.

A product is anything offered to customers to satisfy a need or want. It may be a physical good, a service, an experience, or a combination of these.

Branding and packaging help differentiate products, communicate value, protect goods, and influence customer perceptions.

## 1. Meaning of a Product

A product is an offering designed to satisfy customer needs or wants.

Products may include:

- Physical goods.
- Services.
- Digital products.
- Experiences.
- Ideas.
- Combinations of goods and services.

Examples include smartphones, banking services, online courses, software subscriptions, and travel experiences.

## 2. Levels of a Product

The product concept can be understood through three major levels.

### Core Benefit

The core benefit is the fundamental need or problem that the customer wants to satisfy.

Example: A customer purchasing a laptop wants computing capability and access to digital activities.

### Actual Product

The actual product is the tangible or identifiable offering delivered to the customer.

It includes:

- Design.
- Features.
- Quality.
- Brand.
- Packaging.

Example: A laptop with a particular processor, screen, keyboard, and storage capacity.

### Augmented Product

The augmented product includes additional services and benefits associated with the main offering.

Examples include:

- Warranty.
- Technical support.
- Installation.
- Delivery.
- Training.
- After-sales service.

These additional benefits can differentiate products in competitive markets.

## 3. Product Classification

Products can be classified according to their intended use and purchasing characteristics.

### Consumer Products

Consumer products are purchased by individuals or households for personal use.

They are commonly divided into four categories.

**Convenience Products**

Products purchased frequently with relatively little effort.

Examples:
- Bread.
- Soap.
- Bottled water.
- Everyday household items.

**Shopping Products**

Products that consumers compare before purchasing.

Examples:
- Furniture.
- Clothing.
- Home appliances.

**Specialty Products**

Products with distinctive characteristics or brand identity for which customers are willing to make a special purchasing effort.

Examples:
- Luxury watches.
- Specialist musical instruments.
- Certain high-performance vehicles.

**Unsought Products**

Products that customers may not normally think about purchasing or actively seek.

Examples may include certain insurance products and specialised emergency services.

### Industrial Products

Industrial products are purchased for use in production or business operations.

They include:

- Raw materials.
- Components.
- Capital equipment.
- Operating supplies.
- Business services.

## 4. Product Attributes

Product attributes are characteristics that influence customer evaluation and product performance.

Important attributes include:

### Quality

Quality concerns how consistently a product meets requirements and customer expectations.

### Features

Features are specific characteristics that provide functional or additional benefits.

### Design

Design concerns the appearance, usability, and functional arrangement of a product.

### Style

Style refers primarily to the visual or aesthetic character of a product.

### Durability

Durability refers to the expected useful life of a product.

### Reliability

Reliability concerns the ability of a product to perform consistently without failure.

### Functionality

Functionality refers to the tasks and purposes a product can perform.

## 5. Product Line and Product Mix

### Product Line

A product line is a group of related products offered by a business.

Products within a line may share similar uses, customer groups, distribution channels, or price ranges.

Example:

A company may offer several models of smartphones.

### Product Mix

A product mix is the complete collection of product lines and individual products offered by an organisation.

Four important dimensions are commonly used.

**Width**

The number of different product lines offered.

**Length**

The total number of individual products across all product lines.

**Depth**

The number of variations available within a particular product line or product.

**Consistency**

The degree to which different product lines are related in terms of use, production, distribution, or other characteristics.

## 6. Product Mix Decisions

Businesses make product mix decisions to improve market coverage and profitability.

Common decisions include:

- Adding a new product line.
- Expanding an existing product line.
- Reducing unprofitable products.
- Introducing new variations.
- Improving product quality.
- Discontinuing outdated products.

These decisions should consider customer demand, production capability, competition, and financial performance.

## 7. Branding

A brand is a name, term, symbol, design, or combination of elements that identifies and differentiates an offering.

Branding is the process of creating and managing a distinctive identity for a product or organisation.

### Importance of Branding

Branding helps:

- Identify products.
- Differentiate offerings.
- Build customer recognition.
- Communicate quality and value.
- Develop customer loyalty.
- Support premium pricing when justified.
- Create emotional associations.
- Reduce customer uncertainty.

## 8. Brand Elements

Brand elements are the components used to identify and communicate a brand.

They include:

### Brand Name

The word or combination of words used to identify the brand.

### Logo

A visual symbol or design associated with the brand.

### Colour Scheme

A consistent selection of colours used across brand communication.

### Slogan

A short phrase communicating a brand message or promise.

### Typography

The style and arrangement of written characters used in brand communication.

### Brand Character

A personality or set of human-like characteristics associated with the brand.

## 9. Brand Identity and Brand Image

Brand identity refers to how an organisation intends its brand to be recognised and understood.

Brand image refers to how customers actually perceive the brand.

For example, a company may intend to be perceived as innovative and environmentally responsible, but customers may form a different impression based on their experiences.

Successful branding requires consistency between intended identity and customer experience.

## 10. Brand Equity

Brand equity refers to the additional value associated with a product because of its brand name and customer perceptions.

Important dimensions include:

- Brand awareness.
- Brand associations.
- Perceived quality.
- Brand loyalty.
- Other distinctive brand assets.

Strong brand equity may support customer preference, repeat purchasing, and greater pricing power.

## 11. Branding Strategies

### Individual Branding

Different products are marketed under separate brand names.

### Family Branding

Several related products use a common brand name.

### Corporate Branding

The organisation's name and identity are used to represent its overall products and services.

### Co-Branding

Two or more brands collaborate in a product or marketing activity.

### Brand Extension

An existing brand name is used to introduce a product in a new category.

Brand extensions can benefit from existing recognition but may also create confusion if the new offering does not fit the brand.

### Line Extension

A business introduces additional variations within an existing product category.

Examples include new flavours, sizes, colours, or formulations.

## 12. Packaging

Packaging refers to the design and production of the container or wrapper used to protect and present a product.

It performs functional and marketing roles.

### Functions of Packaging

**Protection**

Protects products from damage, contamination, and environmental conditions.

**Convenience**

Makes products easier to store, transport, open, and use.

**Information**

Communicates product details, instructions, ingredients, warnings, and other required information.

**Promotion**

Attracts attention and communicates brand identity.

**Differentiation**

Helps products stand out from competing alternatives.

## 13. Levels of Packaging

### Primary Packaging

The packaging directly containing the product.

Example: A bottle containing a beverage.

### Secondary Packaging

Packaging that groups or protects primary packages.

Example: A cardboard box containing several beverage bottles.

### Tertiary Packaging

Packaging used for bulk storage, handling, and transportation.

Example: A pallet containing multiple boxes.

## 14. Labelling

A label is information attached to or printed on product packaging.

It may include:

- Product name.
- Brand name.
- Ingredients.
- Manufacturing information.
- Expiry or best-before information where applicable.
- Instructions for use.
- Safety warnings.
- Net quantity.
- Price information.
- Manufacturer details.

Labelling requirements depend on the product category and applicable regulations.

Accurate labelling supports customer information, safety, and regulatory compliance.

## 15. Sustainable Packaging

Sustainable packaging aims to reduce environmental impacts across packaging production, use, and disposal.

Common approaches include:

- Using recyclable materials.
- Reducing unnecessary packaging.
- Using reusable containers.
- Increasing recycled content.
- Designing packaging for efficient transportation.
- Reducing difficult-to-separate material combinations.

Businesses should consider the full life cycle of packaging rather than assuming that a material is environmentally preferable simply because it is labelled recyclable or biodegradable.

## 16. Product Differentiation

Product differentiation involves developing meaningful differences between an offering and competing products.

Differentiation may be based on:

- Features.
- Quality.
- Design.
- Technology.
- Durability.
- Customer service.
- Convenience.
- Brand identity.
- Environmental characteristics.

Effective differentiation should provide benefits that target customers value.

## 17. Product Management Challenges

Product managers may face several challenges:

- Changing customer preferences.
- Strong competition.
- Increasing production costs.
- Product quality problems.
- Technological changes.
- Brand reputation risks.
- Shorter product life cycles.
- Product development uncertainty.

Continuous customer feedback and performance evaluation help businesses respond to these challenges.

## Common Exam Traps

- The core product represents the fundamental benefit customers seek.
- The actual product includes identifiable features, design, quality, and brand.
- The augmented product includes additional services and benefits.
- Convenience products are generally purchased frequently with little effort.
- Shopping products involve comparison before purchase.
- A product line is a group of related products, while a product mix includes all product lines.
- Product mix width refers to the number of product lines.
- Brand identity is what the organisation intends to communicate; brand image is how customers perceive it.
- Primary packaging directly contains the product.
- Tertiary packaging supports bulk handling and transportation.
- Brand extension enters a new product category, whereas line extension adds variations within an existing category.
