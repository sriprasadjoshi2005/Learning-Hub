## Learning objective

Connect derivative and integral ideas through the fundamental theorem and practise selecting the right calculus method.

## Core concepts

- Differentiation measures local rate of change; integration accumulates quantities. Under suitable continuity conditions, they are inverse processes.
- If F'=f, then d/dx[∫_a^x f(t)dt]=f(x). The variable of integration t is a dummy variable.
- For a variable upper and lower limit, d/dx[∫_{g(x)}^{h(x)} f(t)dt]=f(h(x))h'(x)−f(g(x))g'(x).
- Choose differentiation for tangent slopes, monotonicity and rates; choose integration for accumulated change, area and antiderivatives.
- A good solution identifies the requested quantity first, then selects a definition, theorem or standard rule.

## Essential formulae and results

d/dx[∫_{g(x)}^{h(x)}f(t)dt]=f(h(x))h'(x)−f(g(x))g'(x).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** d/dx[∫_0^{x²} cos t dt]=cos(x²)×2x.
**Example 2.** d/dx[∫_x^{2x} t²dt]=(2x)²×2−x²×1=8x²−x²=7x².

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Do not differentiate the integrand with respect to x when the upper limit is x; apply the fundamental theorem.
- For a variable lower limit, subtract its contribution.
- Keep the dummy integration variable distinct from the outside variable.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quiz

1. d/dx[∫_0^x t³dt] equals:
- [x] x³
- [ ] 3x²
- [ ] x⁴/4
- [ ] 0
> By the fundamental theorem, the derivative is the integrand evaluated at x.

2. d/dx[∫_0^{3x} f(t)dt] is:
- [ ] f(3x)
- [x] 3f(3x)
- [ ] f'(3x)
- [ ] 3f'(3x)
> Apply the chain rule to the upper limit.

3. For a variable lower limit x, differentiation contributes:
- [ ] +f(x)
- [x] −f(x)
- [ ] f'(x)
- [ ] 0
> The lower-limit term is subtracted.
