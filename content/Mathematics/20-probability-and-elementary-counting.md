## Learning objective

Model uncertainty using sample spaces, events, complements and basic probability rules.

## Core concepts

- For a finite sample space with equally likely outcomes, P(A)=n(A)/n(S). Probability always lies between 0 and 1.
- The complement rule is P(A')=1−P(A). The addition rule is P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B).
- If A and B are mutually exclusive, P(A∩B)=0. If independent, P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B). These terms are not interchangeable.
- Conditional probability is P(A|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B), provided P(B)>0.
- For equally likely outcomes, count carefully; use permutations or combinations according to whether order matters.

## Essential formulae and results

P(A')=1−P(A); P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** A fair die is rolled. P(even)=3/6=1/2.
**Example 2.** Two fair coins are tossed. The sample space is {HH, HT, TH, TT}; the probability of exactly one head is 2/4=1/2.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Mutually exclusive events with positive probabilities cannot be independent.
- The denominator must describe the correct sample space after any condition is imposed.
- For a complement, subtract from 1.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.
