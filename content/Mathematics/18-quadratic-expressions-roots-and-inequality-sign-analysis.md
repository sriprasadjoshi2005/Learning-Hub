## Learning objective

Use roots and sign charts to solve quadratic inequalities and understand how polynomial graphs determine solution intervals.

## Core concepts

- For a quadratic with distinct real roots α<β and positive leading coefficient, the expression is positive outside the roots and negative between them.
- If the leading coefficient is negative, reverse the signs: the expression is negative outside and positive between.
- For a product of factors, mark all real zeros on a number line and test one point in each interval, or use sign alternation for simple roots.
- A repeated real root does not cause a sign change because the factor has even multiplicity.
- Include endpoints for ≤ or ≥; exclude them for strict inequalities.

## Essential formulae and results

For a quadratic with positive leading coefficient and roots α<β, it is negative between roots and positive outside.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Solve (x−1)(x−4)≤0. The upward-opening quadratic is non-positive between its roots, so x∈[1,4].
**Example 2.** Solve −(x+2)(x−3)>0. The product must be negative, so (x+2)(x−3)<0; hence −2<x<3.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Do not include roots in strict inequalities.
- A repeated root may touch the axis without crossing it.
- For rational inequalities, also exclude points where the denominator is zero.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quiz

1. Solve (x−2)(x−5)>0.
- [ ] 2<x<5
- [x] x<2 or x>5
- [ ] x≤2 or x≥5
- [ ] All real x
> An upward quadratic is positive outside its distinct roots.

2. Solve (x−1)²≥0.
- [ ] x>1
- [ ] x<1
- [x] All real x
- [ ] x=1 only
> **Explanation:** A real square is always non-negative.

3. For x²−9<0, the solution is:
- [ ] x<−3 or x>3
- [x] −3<x<3
- [ ] −3≤x≤3
- [ ] No real values
> The upward parabola is below zero between −3 and 3.
