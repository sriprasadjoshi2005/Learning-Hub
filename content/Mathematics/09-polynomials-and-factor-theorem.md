## Learning objective

Study polynomial structure, degree, roots and factorisation methods used in algebra and calculus.

## Core concepts

- A polynomial in x has the form a_nx^n+...+a_1x+a_0, where exponents are non-negative integers and coefficients are constants.
- The degree is the highest exponent with a non-zero coefficient. A non-zero constant has degree zero.
- The remainder theorem states that the remainder on division by (x−a) is P(a). The factor theorem says (x−a) is a factor exactly when P(a)=0.
- For a quadratic ax²+bx+c, the sum of roots is −b/a and product is c/a, when a≠0.
- Polynomial division, grouping and identities help factorise expressions. A polynomial of degree n has at most n distinct roots.

## Essential formulae and results

For ax²+bx+c, root sum=−b/a and product=c/a.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** For P(x)=x²−5x+6, P(2)=4−10+6=0, so (x−2) is a factor. The full factorisation is (x−2)(x−3).
**Example 2.** For 2x²−7x+3, the roots are 3 and 1/2; their sum is 7/2 and product is 3/2, matching −b/a and c/a.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- A root r corresponds to factor (x−r), not (x+r).
- The degree can drop if the leading coefficient is zero; use the actual highest non-zero power.
- Repeated roots count with multiplicity.

## Quiz

1. If P(3)=0, which is a factor?
- [ ] x+3
- [x] x−3
- [ ] x²−3
- [ ] 3x−1
> By the factor theorem, P(3)=0 implies x−3 is a factor.

2. The sum of roots of 2x²−6x+1=0 is:
- [ ] −3
- [x] 3
- [ ] 1/2
- [ ] −1/2
> The sum is −b/a = 6/2 = 3.

3. A non-zero polynomial of degree 4 has at most how many distinct real roots?
- [ ] 2
- [ ] 3

> A degree-n polynomial has at most n distinct roots.
