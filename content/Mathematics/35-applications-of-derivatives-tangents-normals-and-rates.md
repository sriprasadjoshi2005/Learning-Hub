# Lesson 35: Applications of Derivatives: Tangents, Normals and Rates

## Learning objective

Apply derivatives to tangent and normal equations, related rates and local linear approximation.

## Core concepts

- At a point x=a on y=f(x), tangent slope is f'(a). The tangent equation is y−f(a)=f'(a)(x−a).
- The normal is perpendicular to the tangent, so its slope is −1/f'(a) when f'(a)≠0. Handle horizontal tangents separately.
- Related-rate problems connect quantities that vary with time. Differentiate the relationship with respect to time, then substitute the instant's values.
- The differential dy≈f'(x)dx gives a local linear approximation for small changes.
- Units matter: if y is metres and t is seconds, dy/dt is metres per second.

## Essential formulae and results

Tangent at x=a: y−f(a)=f'(a)(x−a).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** For y=x² at x=2, point=(2,4), slope=4; tangent is y−4=4(x−2), or y=4x−4.
**Example 2.** If A=πr², differentiating with respect to time gives dA/dt=2πr dr/dt.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Substitute the point only after differentiating the function.
- The normal slope is not simply the negative of the tangent slope; it is the negative reciprocal.
- Keep rates and units consistent.

## Quick quiz

**1. The tangent slope to y=x³ at x=2 is:**
- [ ] 6
- [ ] 8
- [x] 12
- [ ] 3
> **Explanation:** dy/dx=3x², so at 2 the slope is 12.

**2. The tangent to y=x² at (1,1) is:**
- [ ] y=x+1
- [x] y=2x−1
- [ ] y=2x+1
- [ ] y=x−1
> **Explanation:** Slope is 2; y−1=2(x−1), so y=2x−1.

**3. If A=πr², dA/dt equals:**
- [ ] π dr/dt
- [x] 2πr dr/dt
- [ ] 2πr
- [ ] πr²
> **Explanation:** Differentiate with respect to time using the chain rule.
