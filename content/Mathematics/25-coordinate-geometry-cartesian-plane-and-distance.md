## Learning objective

Represent points algebraically and use distance, section and midpoint formulae to solve geometric problems.

## Core concepts

- A point is represented by ordered coordinates (x,y). The x-axis has y=0 and the y-axis has x=0.
- Distance between (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂) is √[(x₂−x₁)²+(y₂−y₁)²].
- The midpoint is ((x₁+x₂)/2,(y₁+y₂)/2).
- A point dividing A(x₁,y₁) and B(x₂,y₂) internally in ratio m:n has coordinates ((mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n),(my₂+ny₁)/(m+n)).
- The area of a triangle with vertices (x₁,y₁),(x₂,y₂),(x₃,y₃) is half the absolute value of the corresponding 3×3 determinant.

## Essential formulae and results

Distance=√[(x₂−x₁)²+(y₂−y₁)²]; midpoint=((x₁+x₂)/2,(y₁+y₂)/2).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Distance between (1,2) and (4,6) is √(3²+4²)=5.
**Example 2.** The midpoint of (−2,4) and (6,0) is (2,2).

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Keep the order of coordinates consistent: x first, y second.
- Use absolute value for geometric area.
- The section formula depends on which endpoint is multiplied by which ratio; test a simple ratio if unsure.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.
