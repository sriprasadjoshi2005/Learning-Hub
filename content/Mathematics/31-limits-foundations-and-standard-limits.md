# Lesson 31: Limits: Foundations and Standard Limits

## Learning objective

Introduce limits as the value a function approaches, and learn algebraic methods and standard trigonometric limits.

## Core concepts

- lim(x→a)f(x)=L means f(x) can be made arbitrarily close to L by taking x sufficiently close to a, without requiring x=a.
- For polynomials and rational functions with non-zero denominator at a, direct substitution usually works.
- For indeterminate 0/0 forms, factorise, cancel a common factor valid for nearby x, rationalise or use standard limits.
- In radians, lim(x→0) sin x/x=1 and lim(x→0)(1−cos x)/x²=1/2. Also lim(x→0) tan x/x=1.
- One-sided limits must agree for a two-sided limit to exist. Infinite limits describe unbounded behaviour, not a finite limit.

## Essential formulae and results

lim(x→0)sinx/x=1 and lim(x→0)(1−cosx)/x²=1/2 (radians).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** lim(x→2)(x²+3x)=4+6=10 by substitution.
**Example 2.** lim(x→3)(x²−9)/(x−3): factorise (x−3)(x+3), cancel for x≠3, then the limit is 6.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Do not substitute into 0/0 and conclude the limit is zero or undefined.
- The sine standard limit requires radians.
- A function's value at the point can differ from its limit.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quick quiz

**1. lim(x→2)(3x+1) equals:**
- [ ] 5
- [ ] 6
- [x] 7
- [ ] 8
> **Explanation:** Direct substitution gives 3×2+1=7.

**2. lim(x→0) sin x/x equals (radians):**
- [ ] 0
- [x] 1
- [ ] ∞
- [ ] Does not exist
> **Explanation:** This is a fundamental standard limit.

**3. lim(x→1)(x²−1)/(x−1) equals:**
- [ ] 0
- [ ] 1
- [x] 2
- [ ] Does not exist
> **Explanation:** Factorise to x+1 and evaluate at 1.
