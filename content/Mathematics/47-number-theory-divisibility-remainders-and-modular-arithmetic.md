# Lesson 47: Number Theory: Divisibility, Remainders and Modular Arithmetic

## Learning objective

Use divisibility rules, prime factorisation, greatest common divisors and modular arithmetic to solve integer problems.

## Core concepts

- An integer a divides b if b=ak for some integer k. A prime is an integer greater than 1 with exactly two positive divisors.
- The fundamental theorem of arithmetic gives a unique prime factorisation for every integer greater than 1, apart from order.
- The gcd is the greatest common divisor; the lcm is the least common multiple. For positive integers a,b, gcd(a,b)×lcm(a,b)=ab.
- Congruence a≡b (mod m) means m divides a−b. Addition and multiplication preserve congruences.
- For powers modulo m, look for repeating remainder cycles. Reduce the exponent using the cycle length only when the relevant conditions are satisfied.

## Essential formulae and results

For positive integers a,b: gcd(a,b)×lcm(a,b)=ab.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** gcd(18,24)=6 and lcm(18,24)=72; their product 432 equals 18×24.
**Example 2.** Find 2^10 mod 7: powers cycle 2,4,1; since 10 mod 3=1, the remainder is 2.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Do not confuse gcd and lcm.
- Congruence is about equal remainders, not ordinary equality.
- Check zero and negative integers when applying divisibility statements.

## Quick quiz

**1. gcd(12,18) is:**
- [ ] 2
- [ ] 3
- [x] 6
- [ ] 36
> **Explanation:** The greatest common divisor is 6.

**2. 17 mod 5 equals:**
- [ ] 1
- [x] 2
- [ ] 3
- [ ] 5
> **Explanation:** 17=3×5+2.

**3. Which is prime?**
- [ ] 21
- [ ] 27
- [x] 29
- [ ] 33
> **Explanation:** 29 has no positive divisors other than 1 and 29.
