title: Continuity and Differentiability
level: intermediate

## Learning objective

Connect limits to continuity and derivatives, and learn the conditions that guarantee smooth local behaviour.

## Core concepts

- f is continuous at x=a if f(a) is defined, the two-sided limit exists and lim(x→a)f(x)=f(a).
- A removable discontinuity can often be fixed by defining the missing point value. Jump discontinuities have unequal one-sided limits.
- Differentiability at a means the finite derivative limit f'(a)=lim(h→0)[f(a+h)−f(a)]/h exists.
- Differentiability implies continuity, but continuity does not imply differentiability; y=|x| is continuous at zero but has a sharp corner there.
- For piecewise functions, check continuity first and then equate left and right derivatives when testing differentiability at the joining point.

## Essential formulae and results

Continuity at a: lim(x→a)f(x)=f(a); differentiability implies continuity.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** For f(x)=x², f'(x)=2x; at x=3, slope=6.
**Example 2.** For f(x)=|x|, left derivative at zero is −1 and right derivative is 1, so it is not differentiable at zero.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Checking only f(a) is not enough for continuity.
- A graph can be continuous but have a corner or vertical tangent.
- At a piecewise boundary, use one-sided limits and one-sided derivatives.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

- [ ] Discontinuous
- [ ] Differentiable
