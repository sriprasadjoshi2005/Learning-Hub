## Learning objective

Understand signed numbers and rational numbers, then learn reliable methods for fraction and decimal calculations.

## Core concepts

- Integers include negative whole numbers, zero and positive whole numbers. On a number line, numbers farther right are greater.
- For addition of unlike signs, subtract magnitudes and keep the sign of the larger magnitude. For multiplication or division, equal signs give a positive result and unlike signs give a negative result.
- A fraction a/b represents a ÷ b, with b ≠ 0. Equivalent fractions are formed by multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.
- To add fractions, use a common denominator. To multiply, multiply numerators and denominators. To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.
- A terminating decimal has finitely many digits. A recurring decimal repeats a block of digits. Every rational number has a terminating or recurring decimal expansion.

## Essential formulae and results

a/b = a ÷ b (b ≠ 0); to divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Compute 3/4 + 2/3. The common denominator is 12, so 9/12 + 8/12 = 17/12 = 1 5/12.
**Example 2.** Compute (0.6 × 0.25). Since 0.6 = 6/10 and 0.25 = 1/4, the product is 6/40 = 0.15.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Adding denominators directly: 1/2 + 1/3 is 5/6, not 2/5.
- For negative numbers, remember that −8 is less than −3.
- When dividing fractions, invert only the divisor.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.
