# Lesson 16: Permutations and Combinations

## Learning objective

Distinguish arrangements from selections and apply counting principles to examination problems.

## Core concepts

- The multiplication principle: if a task has successive independent stages with m and n choices, there are mn combined outcomes.
- A permutation is an arrangement where order matters: nP_r=n!/(n−r)!.
- A combination is a selection where order does not matter: nC_r=n!/[r!(n−r)!].
- For n distinct objects arranged in a circle, rotations are treated as equivalent, giving (n−1)! circular arrangements.
- When objects repeat, divide n! by the factorials of the repetition counts. For example, arrangements of MISSISSIPPI are 11!/(4!4!2!).

## Essential formulae and results

nP_r=n!/(n−r)!; nC_r=n!/[r!(n−r)!].

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Choose 3 people from 8: 8C3=8×7×6/(3×2×1)=56.
**Example 2.** Award gold, silver and bronze among 8 people: 8P3=8×7×6=336 because positions matter.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Ask whether changing order creates a new outcome before choosing P or C.
- Check whether repetition is allowed; the standard nP_r and nC_r formulas assume selection without replacement.
- For circular arrangements, fix one object to remove rotational duplicates.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quick quiz

**1. How many ways can 2 people be selected from 5?**
- [x] 10
- [ ] 20
- [ ] 25
- [ ] 5
> **Explanation:** Selection ignores order: 5C2=10.

**2. How many 3-letter arrangements can be made from 4 distinct letters without repetition?**
- [ ] 4
- [ ] 12
- [x] 24
- [ ] 64
> **Explanation:** 4P3=4×3×2=24.

**3. The number of arrangements of 4 distinct objects in a circle is:**
- [ ] 24
- [ ] 12
- [x] 6
- [ ] 4
> **Explanation:** (4−1)!=6.
