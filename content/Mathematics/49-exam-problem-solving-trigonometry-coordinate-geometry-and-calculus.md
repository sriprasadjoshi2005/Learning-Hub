## Learning objective

Practise choosing efficient methods across major JEE Main and state-entrance topics while keeping algebraic conditions explicit.

## Core concepts

- Trigonometry: convert expressions to a common angle or use identities before expanding. Check periodicity and principal ranges.
- Coordinate geometry: identify the conic's standard form, read its parameters, then use focus, directrix, tangent or asymptote formulae.
- Calculus: determine domain and differentiability before applying derivative rules; for optimisation, check critical points and endpoints as required.
- For definite integrals, inspect symmetry and substitutions before lengthy algebra. For area, identify upper and lower curves and intersection limits.
- When an MCQ has options, use dimensional checks, sign checks or substitution to eliminate impossible answers, but verify the final choice.

## Essential formulae and results

Use domain, sign, interval and endpoint checks before accepting a result.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** For x²+y²=25, the tangent at (3,4) is 3x+4y=25; verify the point lies on the circle first.
**Example 2.** For f(x)=x²−4x on [0,3], f'=2x−4 gives critical point x=2. Compare f(0)=0, f(2)=−4 and f(3)=−3; the absolute minimum is −4 and maximum is 0.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- A memorised formula is useful only when its conditions are met.
- Check endpoint inclusion in optimisation and trigonometric intervals.
- Keep exact values until the final step unless approximation is requested.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quiz

1. For f(x)=x² on [−1,2], the absolute maximum is:
- [ ] 0
- [ ] 1
- [ ] 2
- [x] 4
> The endpoint values are 1 and 4; the minimum at 0 is 0.

2. The tangent to x²+y²=9 at (0,3) is:
- [ ] x+y=3
- [x] y=3
- [ ] x=3
- [ ] y=0
> Use xx₁+yy₁=9: 3y=9.

3. If an integral's integrand is odd and limits are −a to a, the value is:
- [ ] 1
- [ ] 2
- [x] 0
- [ ] Depends on a
> Odd symmetry makes the two halves cancel.
