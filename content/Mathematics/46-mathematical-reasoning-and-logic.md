# Lesson 46: Mathematical Reasoning and Logic

## Learning objective

Read mathematical statements precisely and use implication, converse, quantifiers and truth tables.

## Core concepts

- A proposition is a declarative statement that is either true or false. Questions and commands are not propositions.
- Negation ¬p reverses truth. Conjunction p∧q is true only when both are true; disjunction p∨q is true when at least one is true.
- Implication p⇒q is false only when p is true and q is false. Its contrapositive ¬q⇒¬p is logically equivalent to it; the converse q⇒p need not be.
- A biconditional p⇔q means both implications hold. 'If and only if' expresses necessary and sufficient conditions.
- The negation of 'for all x, P(x)' is 'there exists x such that not P(x)'; the negation of 'there exists x, P(x)' is 'for all x, not P(x)'.

## Essential formulae and results

Contrapositive of p⇒q is ¬q⇒¬p; negating ∀ gives ∃¬.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** 'If n is divisible by 4, then n is even' is true. Its converse is also true for integers? No: n=6 is even but not divisible by 4.
**Example 2.** The negation of 'all real x satisfy x²≥0' is 'there exists a real x such that x²<0'.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- The converse is not automatically equivalent to the original implication.
- In inclusive OR, both statements may be true.
- Negate quantifiers as well as the predicate.

## Exam approach

1. Identify the topic and write down the relevant definition or formula.
2. Check the conditions: domain, non-zero denominators, interval, sign and units where applicable.
3. Show the essential algebraic steps instead of jumping to an answer.
4. Substitute the result into the original condition whenever extraneous solutions are possible.
5. Keep exact values (fractions, radicals and π) until a decimal approximation is requested.

## Quick quiz

**1. p∧q is true when:**
- [ ] At least one is true
- [x] Both are true
- [ ] Both are false
- [ ] p is false
> **Explanation:** Conjunction requires both propositions to be true.

**2. The contrapositive of p⇒q is:**
- [ ] q⇒p
- [ ] ¬p⇒¬q
- [x] ¬q⇒¬p
- [ ] p∧q
> **Explanation:** Reverse the order and negate both statements.

**3. The negation of 'all A are B' is:**
- [ ] No A are B
- [x] Some A are not B
- [ ] All B are A
- [ ] Some B are A
> **Explanation:** A counterexample is an A that is not B.
