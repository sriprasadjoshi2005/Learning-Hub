---
title: Multi-Step Organic Problems
level: exam
---

Most hard organic questions ask you to convert one compound into another in several steps, or to deduce an unknown from its reactions. Two habits solve most of them: **track the carbon count** and **work backwards**.

## Changing the carbon chain

| To... | Use |
|---|---|
| add one carbon | $\mathrm{KCN}$ then hydrolysis (not for aryl halides); Grignard + $\mathrm{CO_2}$ then $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$ |
| add a longer chain | Grignard + aldehyde or ketone; aldol; Wurtz |
| remove one carbon | Hofmann bromamide; decarboxylation (soda lime); haloform reaction |
| keep the same carbon count | $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$, hydrolysis, oxidation, esterification |

Aryl halides are very unreactive toward nucleophilic substitution, so $\mathrm{C_6H_5Br} + \mathrm{KCN}$ does **not** work. Make the Grignard reagent instead.

## Useful routes

- Aryl halide $\rightarrow$ aryl carboxylic acid: $\mathrm{Mg}$/dry ether, then $\mathrm{CO_2}$, then $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$.
- Carboxylic acid $\rightarrow$ amine with one carbon fewer: $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$, then $\mathrm{NH_3}$, then $\mathrm{Br_2/NaOH}$.
- Alkene $\rightarrow$ alcohol: Markovnikov (acid-catalysed hydration or oxymercuration) or anti-Markovnikov (hydroboration-oxidation).
- Aniline $\rightarrow$ many aromatics through the diazonium salt.

## Reasoning with tests

| Test | Positive for |
|---|---|
| Tollens', Fehling's | aldehydes (Fehling's: aliphatic only) |
| Iodoform | $\mathrm{CH_3CO-}$ and $\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)-}$ |
| $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$ | carboxylic acids |
| Neutral $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$ | phenols |
| Carbylamine | primary amines |
| Lucas | distinguishes $1^\circ$, $2^\circ$ and $3^\circ$ alcohols |

A pair can be distinguished only if exactly one gives a positive result. Always check both compounds.

## Quiz

1. Which sequence converts bromobenzene into benzoic acid?
   - [x] $\mathrm{Mg}$ in dry ether, then $\mathrm{CO_2}$, then $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{NaCN}$, then $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{KMnO_4/H^+}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{NaOH}$, then $\mathrm{CO_2}$
   > An aryl halide does not undergo $S_N2$ with cyanide, but it forms a Grignard reagent, which adds to $\mathrm{CO_2}$ to give the carboxylate. $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ would have no side chain to oxidise.

2. Which sequence converts propanoic acid into ethanamine?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$, then $\mathrm{NH_3}$, then $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{NH_3}$ (heat), then $\mathrm{H_2/Ni}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$, then $\mathrm{NH_3}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$, then $\mathrm{NH_3}$, then $\mathrm{Br_2/KOH}$
   > The amine has one carbon fewer than the acid, which signals the Hofmann bromamide reaction. Reducing the amide with $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ would give propan-1-amine, which keeps all three carbons.

3. Which pair of compounds **cannot** be distinguished by the iodoform test?
   - [ ] Pentan-2-one and pentan-3-one
   - [ ] Ethanol and methanol
   - [x] Ethanol and propan-2-ol
   - [ ] Propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol
   > Both ethanol ($\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}$) and propan-2-ol contain the $\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)-}$ unit, so both give a positive test. In each of the other pairs, exactly one member is positive.
