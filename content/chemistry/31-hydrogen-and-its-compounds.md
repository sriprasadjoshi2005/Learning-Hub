# Lesson 31: Hydrogen and Its Compounds

Hydrogen is the lightest and most abundant element in the universe. Although it is placed in Group 1 because it has one valence electron, its properties differ significantly from those of alkali metals. Hydrogen forms covalent compounds with many non-metals, ionic hydrides with highly electropositive metals, and participates in important oxidation-reduction reactions.

This lesson covers the position of hydrogen in the periodic table, its isotopes, preparation and properties, hydrides, water, and hydrogen peroxide.

## 1. Position of Hydrogen in the Periodic Table

Hydrogen has atomic number 1 and electronic configuration:

\[
1s^1
\]

It has one electron in its outermost shell.

Hydrogen resembles Group 1 elements because it can lose one electron to form \(H^+\). However, it also resembles Group 17 elements because it requires one additional electron to achieve a stable electronic configuration.

### 1.1 Similarities with Alkali Metals

- Hydrogen has one valence electron.
- It can show an oxidation state of \(+1\).
- It can form compounds with electronegative elements.

### 1.2 Similarities with Halogens

- Hydrogen requires one electron to complete its first shell.
- It can gain one electron to form the hydride ion, \(H^-\).
- It exists as a diatomic molecule, \(H_2\), like several halogens.

Because hydrogen has similarities with both groups but is identical to neither, it is often given a unique position in the periodic table.

## 2. Isotopes of Hydrogen

Isotopes are atoms of the same element having the same atomic number but different mass numbers.

Hydrogen has three important isotopes.

| Isotope | Symbol | Protons | Neutrons |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Protium | \(^1_1H\) | 1 | 0 |
| Deuterium | \(^2_1H\) or D | 1 | 1 |
| Tritium | \(^3_1H\) or T | 1 | 2 |

### 2.1 Protium

- Most abundant isotope of hydrogen.
- Contains one proton and no neutron.
- Stable and non-radioactive.

### 2.2 Deuterium

- Contains one proton and one neutron.
- Stable.
- Its oxide, \(D_2O\), is called heavy water.
- Used as a neutron moderator in certain nuclear reactors.

### 2.3 Tritium

- Contains one proton and two neutrons.
- Radioactive.
- Undergoes beta decay.
- Used in scientific research and certain nuclear applications.

The chemical properties of isotopes are generally similar because they have the same electronic configuration. Their physical properties can differ because their masses are different.

## 3. Preparation of Hydrogen

Hydrogen can be prepared through laboratory reactions and industrial processes.

### 3.1 Reaction of Metals with Dilute Acids

Reactive metals displace hydrogen from dilute acids.

\[
Zn+2HCl\rightarrow ZnCl_2+H_2
\]

Zinc reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to produce hydrogen gas.

### 3.2 Reaction of Metals with Water

Highly reactive metals react with water to produce hydrogen and metal hydroxides.

\[
2Na+2H_2O\rightarrow 2NaOH+H_2
\]

Some metals react with steam rather than cold water.

\[
3Fe+4H_2O(g)\rightarrow Fe_3O_4+4H_2
\]

### 3.3 Electrolysis of Water

Water decomposes into hydrogen and oxygen when an electric current is passed through it.

\[
2H_2O(l)\rightarrow 2H_2(g)+O_2(g)
\]

The volume of hydrogen produced is twice the volume of oxygen under the same temperature and pressure.

### 3.4 Industrial Production

Hydrogen can be produced through steam reforming of natural gas.

\[
CH_4+H_2O(g)\rightarrow CO+3H_2
\]

The carbon monoxide can subsequently react with steam in the water-gas shift reaction.

\[
CO+H_2O(g)\rightarrow CO_2+H_2
\]

## 4. Properties and Uses of Hydrogen

Hydrogen is:

- Colourless, odourless and tasteless.
- Highly combustible.
- Very light compared with most gases.
- Only sparingly soluble in water.
- A strong reducing agent at elevated temperatures.

Combustion of hydrogen produces water.

\[
2H_2+O_2\rightarrow 2H_2O
\]

Hydrogen reacts with chlorine in the presence of light.

\[
H_2+Cl_2\rightarrow 2HCl
\]

### 4.1 Uses

- Manufacture of ammonia.
- Hydrogenation of vegetable oils.
- Production of methanol and other chemicals.
- Fuel cells.
- Reducing metal oxides.
- Rocket propulsion.

## 5. Hydrides

Hydrides are binary compounds of hydrogen with other elements.

They are broadly classified into three categories.

### 5.1 Ionic or Saline Hydrides

These are formed by highly electropositive metals, particularly alkali metals and some alkaline earth metals.

Examples:

\[
NaH,\quad KH,\quad CaH_2
\]

They contain the hydride ion, \(H^-\).

Ionic hydrides react with water to release hydrogen.

\[
NaH+H_2O\rightarrow NaOH+H_2
\]

### 5.2 Covalent or Molecular Hydrides

These are formed mainly by non-metals.

Examples:

\[
CH_4,\quad NH_3,\quad H_2O,\quad HCl
\]

They contain covalent bonds.

Covalent hydrides can be classified according to their electron count.

- Electron-deficient: \(B_2H_6\)
- Electron-precise: \(CH_4\)
- Electron-rich: \(NH_3\), \(H_2O\)

### 5.3 Metallic or Interstitial Hydrides

Many transition metals absorb hydrogen into spaces within their metal lattices.

Examples include hydrides of palladium and titanium.

Some metallic hydrides have variable compositions and can be useful for hydrogen storage.

## 6. Water

Water is an essential compound with the molecular formula \(H_2O\).

Its molecular geometry is bent because oxygen has two bonding pairs and two lone pairs.

The H–O–H bond angle is approximately:

\[
104.5^\circ
\]

Oxygen is more electronegative than hydrogen, making water a polar molecule.

### 6.1 Hydrogen Bonding in Water

Water molecules form intermolecular hydrogen bonds.

These interactions explain several unusual properties:

- Relatively high boiling point.
- High specific heat capacity.
- High surface tension.
- High enthalpy of vaporisation.
- Ice being less dense than liquid water.

### 6.2 Hardness of Water

Hard water contains dissolved calcium and magnesium ions.

Temporary hardness is mainly caused by bicarbonates.

\[
Ca(HCO_3)_2
\]

It can be removed by boiling.

\[
Ca(HCO_3)_2\rightarrow CaCO_3+CO_2+H_2O
\]

Permanent hardness is commonly caused by calcium and magnesium chlorides or sulfates.

It can be removed using methods such as ion exchange.

### 6.3 Hydrogen Bonding and Ice

In ice, hydrogen bonding creates an open structure. This makes ice less dense than liquid water, allowing it to float.

## 7. Hydrogen Peroxide

Hydrogen peroxide has the molecular formula:

\[
H_2O_2
\]

Its structure is:

\[
H-O-O-H
\]

It contains an oxygen-oxygen single bond.

### 7.1 Preparation

A common laboratory preparation involves the reaction of a suitable peroxide with dilute acid.

\[
BaO_2+H_2SO_4\rightarrow BaSO_4+H_2O_2
\]

### 7.2 Properties

Hydrogen peroxide is a pale blue liquid in its pure form. It acts as both an oxidising agent and a reducing agent, depending on the reaction.

It decomposes into water and oxygen.

\[
2H_2O_2\rightarrow 2H_2O+O_2
\]

Manganese dioxide can catalyse this decomposition.

### 7.3 Oxidising Action

Hydrogen peroxide oxidises iodide ions to iodine in acidic solution.

\[
H_2O_2+2I^-+2H^+\rightarrow I_2+2H_2O
\]

### 7.4 Reducing Action

Against a stronger oxidising agent, hydrogen peroxide can act as a reducing agent.

For example, it reduces acidified permanganate ions while itself being oxidised to oxygen.

### 7.5 Uses

- Disinfection and antiseptic formulations.
- Bleaching of paper and textiles.
- Wastewater treatment.
- Chemical synthesis.
- Oxidation reactions.

## 9. Common Mistakes and Exam Points

- Hydrogen is not an ordinary alkali metal despite having one valence electron.
- Isotopes differ in neutron number, not proton number.
- Deuterium is stable, whereas tritium is radioactive.
- Ionic hydrides contain \(H^-\), not \(H^+\).
- Water has a bent geometry, not a linear geometry.
- Hydrogen bonding contributes to the unusual physical properties of water.
- Hydrogen peroxide can act as both an oxidising and reducing agent.

## 10. Lesson Recap

- Hydrogen has a unique position in the periodic table.
- Its three major isotopes are protium, deuterium and tritium.
- Hydrides are classified as ionic, covalent and metallic.
- Water is polar and exhibits extensive hydrogen bonding.
- Hydrogen peroxide decomposes into water and oxygen and can act as either an oxidising or reducing agent.

## Quiz

1. Which isotope of hydrogen is radioactive?
   - [ ] Protium
   - [ ] Deuterium
   - [x] Tritium
   - [ ] All three
   > Tritium contains one proton and two neutrons and is radioactive. Protium and deuterium are stable isotopes.

2. Which ion is present in ionic hydrides?
   - [ ] \(H^+\)
   - [x] \(H^-\)
   - [ ] \(H_3O^+\)
   - [ ] \(OH^-\)
   > Ionic hydrides contain hydrogen in the -1 oxidation state as the hydride ion, \(H^-\).

3. Why is ice less dense than liquid water?
   - [ ] Ice contains fewer oxygen atoms.
   - [ ] Ice has stronger covalent bonds.
   - [x] Hydrogen bonding creates an open crystal structure.
   - [ ] Ice has a lower molecular mass.
   > Hydrogen bonds hold water molecules in an open arrangement in ice, increasing its volume and decreasing its density.
