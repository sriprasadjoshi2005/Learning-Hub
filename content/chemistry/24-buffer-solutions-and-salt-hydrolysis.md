# Lesson 24: Buffer Solutions and Salt Hydrolysis

Buffer solutions resist significant changes in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added. They are essential in biological systems, chemical analysis, pharmaceutical preparation, and industrial processes. Salt hydrolysis explains how salts react with water to produce acidic, basic, or neutral solutions.

## 1. Introduction to Buffer Solutions

A buffer solution is a solution that resists changes in pH when small quantities of acid or base are added.

Buffers generally contain:
- A weak acid and its conjugate base.
- A weak base and its conjugate acid.

### 1.1 Acidic Buffer

An acidic buffer contains a weak acid and its salt with a strong base.

Example:

\[
CH_3COOH+CH_3COONa
\]

The weak acid supplies CH₃COOH, while the salt supplies CH₃COO⁻ ions.

### 1.2 Basic Buffer

A basic buffer contains a weak base and its salt with a strong acid.

Example:

\[
NH_4OH+NH_4Cl
\]

The weak base and its conjugate acid help resist pH changes.

## 2. Buffer Action

### 2.1 Action of an Acidic Buffer

Consider an acetic acid-acetate buffer.

\[
CH_3COOH\rightleftharpoons H^++CH_3COO^-
\]

When a small amount of acid is added, acetate ions consume much of the added hydrogen ions:

\[
CH_3COO^-+H^+\rightarrow CH_3COOH
\]

When a small amount of base is added, acetic acid neutralises hydroxide ions:

\[
CH_3COOH+OH^-\rightarrow CH_3COO^-+H_2O
\]

The pH changes only slightly because the added acid or base is consumed by a buffer component.

### 2.2 Action of a Basic Buffer

Consider an ammonia-ammonium buffer.

\[
NH_3+H_2O\rightleftharpoons NH_4^++OH^-
\]

When acid is added:

\[
NH_3+H^+\rightarrow NH_4^+
\]

When base is added:

\[
NH_4^++OH^-\rightarrow NH_3+H_2O
\]

## 3. Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation

For a weak acid buffer:

\[
HA\rightleftharpoons H^++A^-
\]

The acid dissociation constant is:

\[
K_a=\frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}
\]

Rearranging and taking negative logarithms:

\[
\boxed{pH=pK_a+\log\frac{[A^-]}{[HA]}}
\]

For a basic buffer:

\[
\boxed{pOH=pK_b+\log\frac{[BH^+]}{[B]}}
\]

At 25°C:

\[
pH=14-pOH
\]

These equations are commonly applied using the ratio of conjugate-base to acid concentrations or, when the same solution volume applies, their corresponding mole ratio.

## 4. Buffer Capacity

Buffer capacity measures the ability of a buffer to resist pH changes.

It depends on:
- Total concentration of buffer components.
- Ratio of conjugate base to weak acid.
- Amount of strong acid or base added.

A buffer generally has its greatest effectiveness when:

\[
[A^-]\approx[HA]
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{pH\approx pK_a}
\]

A buffer is commonly most effective within approximately one pH unit above or below its \(pK_a\).

## 5. Salt Hydrolysis

Salt hydrolysis is the reaction of salt ions with water to produce an acidic or basic solution.

The pH of a salt solution depends on the strengths of the acid and base from which the salt is formed.

## 6. Types of Salts and Their Solutions

### 6.1 Strong Acid and Strong Base

Example:

\[
NaCl
\]

NaCl is formed from HCl and NaOH.

Its ions do not appreciably hydrolyse in water.

The solution is approximately neutral at 25°C.

### 6.2 Strong Acid and Weak Base

Example:

\[
NH_4Cl
\]

The ammonium ion reacts with water:

\[
NH_4^++H_2O\rightleftharpoons NH_3+H_3O^+
\]

The solution is acidic.

### 6.3 Weak Acid and Strong Base

Example:

\[
CH_3COONa
\]

The acetate ion reacts with water:

\[
CH_3COO^-+H_2O\rightleftharpoons CH_3COOH+OH^-
\]

The solution is basic.

### 6.4 Weak Acid and Weak Base

Example:

\[
NH_4CH_3COO
\]

Both ions may undergo hydrolysis.

The approximate pH depends on the relative strengths of the acid and base:

\[
\boxed{pH\approx7+\frac{1}{2}(pK_a-pK_b)}
\]

This expression applies at 25°C under the usual ideal dilute-solution approximation.

## 7. Hydrolysis Constants

For the salt of a weak acid and strong base:

\[
A^-+H_2O\rightleftharpoons HA+OH^-
\]

The hydrolysis constant is:

\[
\boxed{K_h=\frac{K_w}{K_a}}
\]

For the salt of a strong acid and weak base:

\[
BH^++H_2O\rightleftharpoons B+H_3O^+
\]

\[
\boxed{K_h=\frac{K_w}{K_b}}
\]

For a salt formed from a weak acid and a weak base:

\[
\boxed{K_h=\frac{K_w}{K_aK_b}}
\]

## 8. Degree of Hydrolysis

The degree of hydrolysis is the fraction of the total salt concentration that undergoes hydrolysis.

It is represented by h.

For a salt of a weak acid and strong base, under the usual approximation:

\[
\boxed{h\approx\sqrt{\frac{K_h}{C}}}
\]

Where C is the salt concentration.

The approximation assumes that hydrolysis is small and the solution behaves ideally.

## 9. Common Buffer Applications

### 9.1 Biological Systems

Blood contains buffer systems that help maintain its pH within a narrow range.

The carbonic acid-bicarbonate system is particularly important:

\[
CO_2+H_2O\rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3
\]

\[
H_2CO_3\rightleftharpoons H^++HCO_3^-
\]

### 9.2 Laboratory Analysis

Buffers are used to:
- Maintain the pH of reaction mixtures.
- Calibrate pH meters.
- Control conditions during titrations.
- Improve reproducibility in experiments.

### 9.3 Industrial Applications

Buffers are used in:
- Pharmaceutical production.
- Food processing.
- Fermentation.
- Water treatment.
- Biochemical manufacturing.

## 10. Common Exam Traps

- A buffer does not prevent every pH change; it only resists changes within its capacity.
- A weak acid alone is not normally an effective buffer.
- An acidic buffer contains a weak acid and its conjugate base.
- A basic buffer contains a weak base and its conjugate acid.
- Salts formed from strong acids and strong bases generally produce neutral solutions.
- Salts containing conjugate ions of weak acids or weak bases may undergo hydrolysis.
- Buffer pH depends on the ratio of components, while buffer capacity also depends strongly on their total concentration.
