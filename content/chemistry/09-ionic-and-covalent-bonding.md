Atoms bond to reach a lower energy, usually by completing an outer shell.

## Ionic bonding

An ionic bond forms by electron transfer between a metal and a non-metal, giving ions held together by electrostatic attraction. The stability of the solid is measured by **lattice enthalpy**, which grows with ionic charge and falls as ions get larger:

$$U \propto \frac{q_+ \, q_-}{r_+ + r_-}$$

That is why $\mathrm{MgO}$ (charges $2+$ and $2-$) melts at a far higher temperature than $\mathrm{NaCl}$.

## Covalent bonding and Lewis structures

A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons. To draw a Lewis structure, count total valence electrons, connect the atoms with single bonds, complete octets, and use multiple bonds if needed. Compare candidate structures using **formal charge**:

$$\text{formal charge} = (\text{valence electrons}) - (\text{non-bonding electrons}) - \tfrac12(\text{bonding electrons})$$

The best structure has formal charges as close to zero as possible, with any negative charge on the more electronegative atom. When more than one valid structure exists, the real molecule is a **resonance hybrid**, and the actual bond lengths are equal and intermediate.

## Fajans' rules: covalent character in ionic bonds

No bond is purely ionic. A small, highly charged cation **polarises** the electron cloud of a large anion, giving the bond covalent character. Covalent character increases with a higher cation charge, a smaller cation, and a larger anion. Hence $\mathrm{NaCl} < \mathrm{MgCl_2} < \mathrm{AlCl_3}$ in covalent character, and $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$ really exists as a covalent molecule ($\mathrm{Al_2Cl_6}$ dimer) in the vapour.

## Polarity and dipole moment

A bond between atoms of different electronegativity is polar. The dipole moment of a molecule is the **vector sum** of its bond dipoles, so a molecule with polar bonds can still be non-polar if they cancel by symmetry ($\mathrm{CO_2}$, $\mathrm{BF_3}$, $\mathrm{CCl_4}$). In $\mathrm{NF_3}$ the lone pair's dipole opposes the N-F dipoles, so the net value is small but not zero.

## Quiz

1. What is the formal charge on the central oxygen atom in the best Lewis structure of ozone, $\mathrm{O_3}$?
   - [ ] 0
   - [ ] -1
   - [x] +1
   - [ ] +2
   > The central O has one lone pair (2 non-bonding electrons) and three bonds' worth of shared electrons (6): $6-2-3=+1$. One terminal oxygen carries $-1$ and the other $0$, so the charges sum to zero.

2. Which of the following lists the chlorides in increasing order of covalent character?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{AlCl_3} < \mathrm{MgCl_2} < \mathrm{NaCl}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{NaCl} < \mathrm{MgCl_2} < \mathrm{AlCl_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{MgCl_2} < \mathrm{NaCl} < \mathrm{AlCl_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{NaCl} < \mathrm{AlCl_3} < \mathrm{MgCl_2}$
   > By Fajans' rules, a higher cation charge and smaller size raise polarising power: $\mathrm{Na^+} < \mathrm{Mg^{2+}} < \mathrm{Al^{3+}}$, so covalent character rises in the same order.

3. Which of the following molecules has a non-zero dipole moment?
   - [x] $\mathrm{NF_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CCl_4}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{BF_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{XeF_4}$
   > $\mathrm{CCl_4}$ (tetrahedral), $\mathrm{BF_3}$ (trigonal planar) and $\mathrm{XeF_4}$ (square planar) are all symmetric, so their bond dipoles cancel. $\mathrm{NF_3}$ is pyramidal with a lone pair, so the dipoles do not cancel.
