---
title: Coordination Compounds: Nomenclature and Isomerism
level: advanced
---

A **coordination compound** has a central metal atom or ion bonded to a set of **ligands** (ions or molecules that donate electron pairs). In $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3}$ the square brackets enclose the **coordination sphere**; the $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ outside are **counter ions** and ionise freely in water.

- **Coordination number:** the number of ligand donor atoms bonded to the metal (6 for octahedral, 4 for tetrahedral or square planar).
- **Denticity:** the number of donor atoms a ligand uses. $\mathrm{NH_3}$ is monodentate, ethane-1,2-diamine (en) is bidentate, and EDTA is hexadentate. Polydentate ligands form rings called **chelates**, which are especially stable.
- **Oxidation state** of the metal comes from the overall charge: in $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2]^+}$, $x + 0 + 2(-1) = +1$, so $x = +3$.

## IUPAC naming

1. Name the cation first, then the anion.
2. Inside the complex, list ligands in **alphabetical order** (ignoring prefixes such as di-, tri-), then the metal.
3. Anionic ligands end in "-o" (chlorido, cyanido, hydroxido); neutral ligands keep their names, except aqua ($\mathrm{H_2O}$) and ammine ($\mathrm{NH_3}$).
4. Use di-, tri-, tetra- for simple ligands and bis-, tris- for complex ones.
5. Give the metal's oxidation state in Roman numerals in brackets. If the complex is an **anion**, the metal name ends in "-ate" (cobaltate, ferrate, cuprate).

## Isomerism

**Structural isomers** differ in connectivity:

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Ionisation | $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4}$ and $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5SO_4]Br}$ |
| Hydrate | $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_6]Cl_3}$ and $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_5Cl]Cl_2\cdot H_2O}$ |
| Linkage | $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5(NO_2)]^{2+}}$ and $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5(ONO)]^{2+}}$ |
| Coordination | $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6][Cr(CN)_6]}$ and $\mathrm{[Cr(NH_3)_6][Co(CN)_6]}$ |

**Stereoisomers** have the same connectivity but a different arrangement in space. **Geometrical** isomers (cis/trans, or fac/mer) occur in square planar $\mathrm{MA_2B_2}$ and octahedral $\mathrm{MA_4B_2}$ and $\mathrm{MA_3B_3}$. **Optical** isomers are non-superimposable mirror images, as in the cis form of $\mathrm{[Co(en)_2Cl_2]^+}$. Tetrahedral complexes show no geometrical isomerism.

## Quiz

1. What is the IUPAC name of $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2]Cl}$?
   - [x] Tetraamminedichloridocobalt(III) chloride
   - [ ] Dichloridotetraamminecobalt(III) chloride
   - [ ] Tetraamminedichlorocobalt(II) chloride
   - [ ] Tetraamminedichloridocobaltate(III) chloride
   > Ligands are listed alphabetically: ammine comes before chlorido. The charge on the complex ion is $+1$ (balanced by one $\mathrm{Cl^-}$), so Co is $+3$. A cationic complex does not take "-ate".

2. How many stereoisomers (geometrical and optical together) does $\mathrm{[Co(en)_2Cl_2]^+}$ have?
   - [ ] 2
   - [ ] 4
   - [ ] 5
   - [x] 3
   > The trans form has a mirror plane, so it is a single optically inactive isomer. The cis form has no mirror plane, so it exists as a pair of enantiomers. Total: $1 + 2 = 3$.

3. Which pair of compounds are linkage isomers?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4}$ and $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5SO_4]Br}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_6]Cl_3}$ and $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_5Cl]Cl_2\cdot H_2O}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5(NO_2)]Cl_2}$ and $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5(ONO)]Cl_2}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6][Cr(CN)_6]}$ and $\mathrm{[Cr(NH_3)_6][Co(CN)_6]}$
   > Linkage isomerism needs an ambidentate ligand that can attach through different atoms. $\mathrm{NO_2^-}$ binds through N (nitrito-N) or through O (nitrito-O). The other pairs are ionisation, hydrate and coordination isomers, respectively.
