---
title: Carboxylic Acids and Their Derivatives
level: advanced
---

Carboxylic acids contain the $-\mathrm{COOH}$ group. They are much more acidic than alcohols or phenols, because the carboxylate ion has **two equivalent resonance structures** that share the negative charge between both oxygens. They dissolve in aqueous $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$ with effervescence of $\mathrm{CO_2}$, which phenols do not, and this is the usual test.

## Acidity trends

Electron-withdrawing groups strengthen the acid and electron-donating groups weaken it. Approximate $\mathrm{p}K_a$ values: $\mathrm{HCOOH}$ 3.75, $\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH}$ 4.20, $\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$ 4.76, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COOH}$ 4.87. Formic acid is the strongest because it has no donating alkyl group.

## Key reactions

- **Esterification (Fischer):** $\mathrm{RCOOH + R'OH \rightleftharpoons RCOOR' + H_2O}$ (acid catalyst). It is reversible, so the yield improves with excess alcohol or by removing water.
- **Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky:** $\mathrm{RCH_2COOH}$ with $\mathrm{Br_2/red\ P}$ gives the $\alpha$-bromo acid.
- **Reduction:** $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ gives the primary alcohol; $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ does not reduce acids.
- **Decarboxylation:** heating the sodium salt with soda lime gives an alkane with one carbon fewer.

## Derivatives and their reactivity

Acid derivatives react by **nucleophilic acyl substitution**: the nucleophile adds to the carbonyl carbon, and then the leaving group departs. Reactivity depends on how good the leaving group is, which is related to how weak a base it is:

$$\text{acid chloride} > \text{acid anhydride} > \text{ester} > \text{amide}$$

- Acid chlorides ($\mathrm{RCOCl}$) are made with $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$ and react with water, alcohols and ammonia readily.
- **Amides** are the least reactive, because the nitrogen lone pair delocalises into C=O. The **Hofmann bromamide** reaction ($\mathrm{Br_2/NaOH}$) converts $\mathrm{RCONH_2}$ into $\mathrm{RNH_2}$, **one carbon shorter**.
- Nitriles are hydrolysed to acids, and reduced to primary amines.

## Quiz

1. For $\mathrm{CH_3COOH + C_2H_5OH \rightleftharpoons CH_3COOC_2H_5 + H_2O}$, $K_c = 4$. If $1$ mol of acid is mixed with $3$ mol of ethanol, how many moles of ester form at equilibrium?
   - [ ] 0.67
   - [ ] 0.75
   - [x] 0.90
   - [ ] 1.00
   > With $x$ mol of ester, $K_c = x^2/[(1-x)(3-x)] = 4$ gives $3x^2 - 16x + 12 = 0$, so $x = 0.90$. With a 1:1 mixture only $0.67$ mol would form, so excess alcohol pushes the equilibrium toward the ester. It can never reach 1.00 exactly.

2. What is the product of the Hofmann bromamide reaction of propanamide, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CONH_2}$?
   - [ ] Propan-1-amine
   - [x] Ethanamine
   - [ ] Propanoic acid
   - [ ] Ethanol
   > The $\mathrm{C{=}O}$ carbon is lost (as carbonate) and the amine nitrogen attaches to the ethyl group. The product has one carbon fewer than the amide.

3. Which of the following arranges the derivatives in decreasing reactivity toward nucleophilic acyl substitution?
   - [x] Acid chloride > anhydride > ester > amide
   - [ ] Amide > ester > anhydride > acid chloride
   - [ ] Ester > acid chloride > amide > anhydride
   - [ ] Anhydride > acid chloride > amide > ester
   > $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ is the best leaving group (weakest base) and $\mathrm{NH_2^-}$ the worst, with the amide's resonance further reducing reactivity.
