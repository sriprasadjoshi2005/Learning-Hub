# Chemistry syllabus plan (50 lessons, foundation to university entrance-exam level)

**Phase 1: Fundamental concepts and atomic structure (Lessons 1–10):** Introduction to chemistry and its applications, matter and its classification, physical and chemical properties and changes, measurement and SI units in chemistry, laws of chemical combination, atomic and molecular masses, mole concept and Avogadro's constant, stoichiometry and chemical calculations, atomic structure and subatomic particles, and quantum numbers and electronic configuration.

**Phase 2: Chemical bonding, states of matter and physical chemistry (Lessons 11–20):** Periodic table and periodic properties, chemical bonding and Lewis structures, ionic and covalent bonding, VSEPR theory and molecular geometry, hybridisation and molecular orbital theory, intermolecular forces and hydrogen bonding, states of matter and gas laws, kinetic theory of gases and real gases, liquids and solids, and solutions and their concentration terms.

**Phase 3: Thermodynamics, equilibrium and electrochemistry (Lessons 21–30):** Chemical thermodynamics and thermochemistry, enthalpy and Hess's law, entropy and Gibbs free energy, chemical equilibrium and equilibrium constants, Le Chatelier's principle, ionic equilibrium and acid-base theories, pH, buffer solutions and solubility equilibria, redox reactions and oxidation numbers, electrochemical cells and electrode potentials, and electrolysis, conductance and chemical kinetics.

**Phase 4: Inorganic and organic chemistry (Lessons 31–40):** Hydrogen and its compounds, s-block elements and their compounds, p-block elements and their compounds, d-block and f-block elements, coordination compounds and their nomenclature, introduction to organic chemistry and IUPAC nomenclature, structural isomerism and stereoisomerism, fundamental organic reaction mechanisms and electronic effects, hydrocarbons including alkanes, alkenes, alkynes and aromatic compounds, and aromatic chemistry and important organic reactions.

**Phase 5: Advanced organic, analytical and applied chemistry (Lessons 41–50):** Haloalkanes and haloarenes, alcohols, phenols and ethers, aldehydes and ketones, carboxylic acids and their derivatives, amines and diazonium salts, polymers, biomolecules and chemistry in everyday life, food chemistry, environmental chemistry and green chemistry, industrial chemistry and metallurgy, qualitative and quantitative analytical chemistry, and spectroscopy, integrated numerical problems and comprehensive examination practice.

The sequence progresses from fundamental chemical concepts and atomic structure to bonding, physical chemistry, inorganic chemistry, organic chemistry, analytical techniques and industrial applications. It incorporates conceptual explanations, mathematical derivations, balanced chemical equations, reaction mechanisms, molecular structures, numerical problems, experimental methods, laboratory procedures and real-world applications.

The final lessons consolidate important areas that cannot be explored individually within the 50-lesson structure, including advanced polymer chemistry, biochemical processes, medicinal chemistry, food additives and preservation, water treatment, atmospheric chemistry, pollution control, sustainable chemical processes, industrial manufacturing, extraction and refining of metals, qualitative ion analysis, volumetric and gravimetric analysis, chromatography, UV-Visible spectroscopy, infrared spectroscopy, nuclear magnetic resonance, mass spectrometry and integrated chemical problem-solving. This ensures that the curriculum provides a broad introduction to both foundational and advanced chemistry without exceeding the 50-lesson limit.

The examination-oriented content is designed to develop the mathematical, analytical and conceptual foundations needed for university-level chemistry and related entrance examinations. It also supports preparation for chemistry-related topics in examinations such as JEE Main, JEE Advanced and CUET, where applicable. Check the latest official syllabus for the examination being attempted; this is a broad chemistry learning pathway, not an official examination syllabus.
