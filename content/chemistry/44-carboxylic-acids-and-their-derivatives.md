# Lesson 44: Carboxylic Acids and Their Derivatives

Carboxylic acids contain the carboxyl group, which combines a carbonyl group and a hydroxyl group. Their derivatives are formed when the hydroxyl portion is replaced by another group. These compounds are important in esterification, hydrolysis, acyl substitution, and biological chemistry.

## 1. Structure and Classification

The carboxyl group is:

\[
-COOH
\]

General formula:

\[
RCOOH
\]

Examples:
- HCOOH: methanoic acid
- CH₃COOH: ethanoic acid
- CH₃CH₂COOH: propanoic acid
- C₆H₅COOH: benzoic acid

Carboxylic acids may be saturated, unsaturated, aromatic, monocarboxylic, or polycarboxylic.

## 2. Nomenclature

The longest chain containing the carboxyl carbon is selected.

The suffix is `-oic acid`.

Examples:
- HCOOH: methanoic acid
- CH₃COOH: ethanoic acid
- CH₃CH₂COOH: propanoic acid
- HOOC–COOH: ethanedioic acid

The carboxyl carbon is assigned position 1.

## 3. Physical Properties

Carboxylic acids form strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds and may exist as dimers in the liquid or vapour phase under suitable conditions.

Consequences:
- Relatively high boiling points.
- Lower volatility than comparable aldehydes and ketones.
- Smaller members are highly soluble in water.
- Water solubility generally decreases as the hydrocarbon chain grows.

## 4. Acidity of Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids are weak acids that partially ionise in water.

\[
RCOOH\rightleftharpoons RCOO^-+H^+
\]

The carboxylate ion is resonance-stabilised because the negative charge is delocalised over two oxygen atoms.

This stabilisation explains why carboxylic acids are more acidic than ordinary alcohols.

### 4.1 Effect of Substituents

Electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity by stabilising the carboxylate ion.

Example:

\[
ClCH_2COOH>CH_3COOH
\]

Electron-donating groups generally decrease acidity.

The effect becomes weaker as the substituent moves farther from the carboxyl group.

## 5. Preparation of Carboxylic Acids

### 5.1 Oxidation of Primary Alcohols and Aldehydes

\[
RCH_2OH+2[O]\rightarrow RCOOH+H_2O
\]

\[
RCHO+[O]\rightarrow RCOOH
\]

### 5.2 Hydrolysis of Nitriles

Nitriles can be hydrolysed under acidic or basic conditions to form carboxylic acids or carboxylate salts.

\[
RCN+2H_2O+H^+\rightarrow RCOOH+NH_4^+
\]

### 5.3 Carboxylation of Grignard Reagents

Grignard reagents react with carbon dioxide followed by acidic work-up.

\[
RMgX+CO_2\rightarrow RCOOMgX
\]

\[
RCOOMgX+H^+\rightarrow RCOOH
\]

This reaction increases the carbon chain by one carbon atom.

## 6. Chemical Reactions of Carboxylic Acids

### 6.1 Reaction with Metals

\[
2RCOOH+2Na\rightarrow2RCOONa+H_2
\]

### 6.2 Reaction with Bases

\[
RCOOH+NaOH\rightarrow RCOONa+H_2O
\]

### 6.3 Reaction with Carbonates

\[
2RCOOH+Na_2CO_3
\rightarrow2RCOONa+H_2O+CO_2
\]

The release of carbon dioxide produces effervescence.

### 6.4 Esterification

Carboxylic acids react with alcohols in the presence of an acid catalyst to form esters.

\[
RCOOH+R'OH
\rightleftharpoons RCOOR'+H_2O
\]

Example:

\[
CH_3COOH+C_2H_5OH
\rightleftharpoons CH_3COOC_2H_5+H_2O
\]

The reaction is reversible. Removing water or using an excess of one reactant can favour ester formation.

### 6.5 Reduction

Strong reducing agents such as LiAlH₄ can reduce carboxylic acids to primary alcohols.

\[
RCOOH+4[H]\rightarrow RCH_2OH+H_2O
\]

### 6.6 Decarboxylation

Carboxylate salts may lose carbon dioxide under suitable conditions.

Sodium salts of carboxylic acids react with soda lime to produce alkanes containing one fewer carbon atom.

\[
RCOONa+NaOH
\xrightarrow{CaO,\,heat}
RH+Na_2CO_3
\]

Example:

\[
CH_3COONa+NaOH
\xrightarrow{CaO,\,heat}
CH_4+Na_2CO_3
\]

## 7. Important Derivatives of Carboxylic Acids

The main derivatives include:
- Acid chlorides: RCOCl
- Acid anhydrides: (RCO)₂O
- Esters: RCOOR′
- Amides: RCONH₂

They undergo nucleophilic acyl substitution.

The nucleophile attacks the carbonyl carbon, and a leaving group is eventually displaced.

## 8. Acid Chlorides

Acid chlorides are highly reactive carboxylic acid derivatives.

### 8.1 Hydrolysis

\[
RCOCl+H_2O\rightarrow RCOOH+HCl
\]

### 8.2 Reaction with Alcohols

\[
RCOCl+R'OH\rightarrow RCOOR'+HCl
\]

### 8.3 Reaction with Ammonia

\[
RCOCl+2NH_3\rightarrow RCONH_2+NH_4Cl
\]

## 9. Esters

Esters often have characteristic fruity odours and are used in flavourings, fragrances, solvents, and polymer manufacture.

### 9.1 Acidic Hydrolysis

\[
RCOOR'+H_2O
\rightleftharpoons RCOOH+R'OH
\]

### 9.2 Alkaline Hydrolysis

Alkaline hydrolysis produces a carboxylate salt and an alcohol.

\[
RCOOR'+NaOH\rightarrow RCOONa+R'OH
\]

This process is called saponification when fats or oils are converted into soap.

## 10. Amides

Amides contain the group:

\[
-CONH_2
\]

They can be prepared by reacting acid derivatives with ammonia or suitable amines.

Amides undergo hydrolysis under acidic or basic conditions.

Acidic hydrolysis produces a carboxylic acid and an ammonium species. Basic hydrolysis produces a carboxylate salt and ammonia or an amine.

## 11. Relative Reactivity of Acid Derivatives

A common general order of reactivity towards nucleophilic acyl substitution is:

\[
\text{Acid chloride}>\text{Anhydride}>\text{Ester}\approx\text{Carboxylic acid}>\text{Amide}
\]

The exact behaviour depends on reaction conditions and the nucleophile.

A major reason for the low reactivity of amides is resonance donation from nitrogen into the carbonyl group, which reduces the electrophilic character of the carbonyl carbon.

## 12. Common Exam Traps

- Carboxylate ions are resonance-stabilised.
- Electron-withdrawing groups generally increase carboxylic acid strength.
- Esterification is reversible.
- Saponification is alkaline ester hydrolysis.
- Soda-lime decarboxylation reduces the carbon count by one.
- Grignard carboxylation increases the carbon count by one.
- Acid chlorides are more reactive than esters and amides.
- Amides are relatively unreactive because of resonance stabilisation.

## 13. Summary

- Carboxylic acids contain the –COOH group.
- They are more acidic than ordinary alcohols because their conjugate bases are resonance-stabilised.
- They undergo esterification, reduction, and decarboxylation.
- Acid chlorides, anhydrides, esters, and amides are important derivatives.
- Nucleophilic acyl substitution is a major reaction pathway of acid derivatives.
