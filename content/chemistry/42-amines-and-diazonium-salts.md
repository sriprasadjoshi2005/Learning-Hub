title: Amines and Diazonium Salts
level: advanced
---

Amines are derivatives of ammonia, classified as primary, secondary or tertiary by the number of carbon groups on nitrogen. The nitrogen lone pair makes them **basic and nucleophilic**.

## Basicity

In the gas phase, basicity follows alkyl substitution ($3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{NH_3}$) because alkyl groups donate electrons. In **water**, solvation (hydrogen bonding with the ammonium ion) and steric effects change the order. For methyl-substituted amines:

$$\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH} > \mathrm{CH_3NH_2} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_3N} > \mathrm{NH_3}$$

Aromatic amines such as aniline are much weaker bases than aliphatic ones, because the lone pair is delocalised into the ring. An electron-withdrawing group ($-\mathrm{NO_2}$) lowers basicity further.

## Preparation and tests

- **Reduction** of nitro compounds, nitriles or amides.
- **Gabriel phthalimide synthesis** gives **pure primary aliphatic** amines only (it fails for aryl halides).
- **Hofmann bromamide** degradation gives a primary amine with one carbon fewer.
- **Carbylamine test:** only **primary** amines give a foul-smelling isocyanide with $\mathrm{CHCl_3/KOH}$.
- **Hinsberg's test** (benzenesulfonyl chloride): a primary amine gives a sulfonamide that **dissolves** in alkali; a secondary amine gives one that is **insoluble**; a tertiary amine does not react.

## Diazonium salts

Primary aromatic amines react with $\mathrm{NaNO_2/HCl}$ at $273$-$278$ K to give **diazonium salts**, $\mathrm{ArN_2^+Cl^-}$. They are unstable above $5\,^\circ$C, but aromatic diazonium ions are stabilised by resonance, whereas aliphatic ones decompose at once. They are valuable because $\mathrm{N_2}$ is an excellent leaving group:

| Reagent | Product | Name |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathrm{CuCl}$, $\mathrm{CuBr}$, $\mathrm{CuCN}$ | $\mathrm{ArCl}$, $\mathrm{ArBr}$, $\mathrm{ArCN}$ | Sandmeyer |
| $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$ | $\mathrm{ArH}$ | reduction |
| $\mathrm{H_2O}$, warm | $\mathrm{ArOH}$ | hydrolysis |
| $\mathrm{HBF_4}$, then heat | $\mathrm{ArF}$ | Balz-Schiemann |
| phenol or aniline | $\mathrm{Ar{-}N{=}N{-}Ar'OH}$ | coupling (azo dye) |

Coupling occurs at the **para** position of the activated ring (alkaline for phenols, mildly acidic for amines).

## Quiz

1. Which sequence shows decreasing basic strength in aqueous solution for the methylamines?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3N} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH} > \mathrm{CH_3NH_2} > \mathrm{NH_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CH_3NH_2} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_3N} > \mathrm{NH_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_3N} > \mathrm{CH_3NH_2} > \mathrm{NH_3}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH} > \mathrm{CH_3NH_2} > \mathrm{(CH_3)_3N} > \mathrm{NH_3}$
   > The secondary amine wins: it has two donating groups and still has an N-H to stabilise the ion through hydrogen bonding with water. The tertiary amine loses most of that solvation, so it ranks below the primary amine in water.

2. Which sequence converts aniline into chlorobenzene?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{Cl_2/FeCl_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{NaNO_2/HCl}$ at $273$ K, then $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{NaNO_2/HCl}$ at $273$ K, then $\mathrm{CuCl}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{HNO_3/H_2SO_4}$, then $\mathrm{HCl}$
   > Diazotisation gives $\mathrm{C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-}$, and the Sandmeyer reaction with $\mathrm{CuCl}$ replaces $\mathrm{N_2}$ with Cl. Treating it with $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$ would give benzene instead. Direct chlorination of aniline gives ortho/para-substituted products and not chlorobenzene.

3. A compound reacts with benzenesulfonyl chloride to give a product that is insoluble in aqueous NaOH. The compound is most likely:
   - [ ] a primary amine
   - [x] a secondary amine
   - [ ] a tertiary amine
   - [ ] ammonia
   > A secondary amine gives an N,N-disubstituted sulfonamide with no acidic N-H, so it does not dissolve in alkali. A primary amine's product has an acidic N-H and dissolves, and a tertiary amine gives no product at all.
