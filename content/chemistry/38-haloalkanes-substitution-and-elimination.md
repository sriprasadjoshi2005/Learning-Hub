---
title: Haloalkanes: Substitution and Elimination
level: advanced
---

In a haloalkane the C-X carbon is electrophilic, so nucleophiles can replace the halogen (**substitution**), and bases can remove a $\beta$-hydrogen (**elimination**). Four mechanisms are compared below.

## $S_N2$: one step, bimolecular

The nucleophile attacks from the **back side** as the leaving group departs, giving **inversion of configuration**. The rate is $k[\mathrm{RX}][\mathrm{Nu}]$ (second order). It is fastest for **methyl and primary** halides, and slowed by steric hindrance, so $\mathrm{CH_3X} > 1^\circ > 2^\circ \gg 3^\circ$. It is favoured by strong nucleophiles and polar **aprotic** solvents (DMSO, acetone).

## $S_N1$: two steps, unimolecular

The C-X bond breaks slowly to give a carbocation, which is then attacked by the nucleophile. The rate is $k[\mathrm{RX}]$ and independent of the nucleophile. It is fastest for **tertiary** (and benzylic or allylic) halides, in polar **protic** solvents, and gives **racemisation** (the nucleophile attacks the planar cation from either face), often with rearrangement.

## Elimination

- **E2:** one step, rate $k[\mathrm{RX}][\mathrm{base}]$, with the H and X anti-periplanar. Favoured by strong bases, high temperature, and alcoholic KOH.
- **E1:** competes with $S_N1$ through the same carbocation.
- **Saytzeff's rule:** the more substituted alkene is the major product. A bulky base (e.g. $t$-BuOK) gives the less substituted (Hofmann) alkene instead.

Substitution versus elimination: aqueous KOH favours substitution; **alcoholic** KOH favours elimination.

## Leaving groups and nucleophiles

Leaving ability follows $\mathrm{I^-} > \mathrm{Br^-} > \mathrm{Cl^-} \gg \mathrm{F^-}$ (weaker bases leave more easily, and the C-I bond is weakest). Nucleophilicity increases with negative charge and, in protic solvents, with size ($\mathrm{I^-} > \mathrm{Br^-} > \mathrm{Cl^-}$).

## Quiz

1. For an $S_N2$ reaction, by what factor does the rate change if the concentrations of both the alkyl halide and the nucleophile are doubled?
   - [ ] 1 (no change)
   - [ ] 2
   - [ ] 8
   - [x] 4
   > Rate $= k[\mathrm{RX}][\mathrm{Nu}]$ is first order in each reactant, so doubling both gives $2\times2 = 4$. For an $S_N1$ reaction, the same change would only double the rate.

2. What is the product of the $S_N2$ reaction of $(R)$-2-bromobutane with $\mathrm{OH^-}$?
   - [ ] $(R)$-Butan-2-ol
   - [ ] A racemic mixture of butan-2-ol
   - [x] $(S)$-Butan-2-ol
   - [ ] But-2-ene
   > $S_N2$ inverts the configuration. Because OH has the same priority rank as Br (highest) on the stereocentre, the descriptor changes from $R$ to $S$.

3. What is the major product when 2-bromo-2-methylbutane is heated with alcoholic KOH?
   - [ ] 2-Methylbut-1-ene
   - [x] 2-Methylbut-2-ene
   - [ ] 3-Methylbut-1-ene
   - [ ] 2-Methylbutan-2-ol
   > Alcoholic KOH promotes elimination, and Saytzeff's rule favours the more substituted alkene, 2-methylbut-2-ene (trisubstituted) over 2-methylbut-1-ene (disubstituted).
