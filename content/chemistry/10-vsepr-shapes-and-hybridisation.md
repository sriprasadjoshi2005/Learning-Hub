The **VSEPR** model says that electron pairs around a central atom repel each other and arrange themselves as far apart as possible. Count the **electron domains** (bonding pairs plus lone pairs; a double bond counts as one domain). The domain count fixes the electron geometry, and the lone pairs then fix the molecular shape.

| Domains | Electron geometry | Hybridisation | Examples (shape) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Linear | $sp$ | $\mathrm{BeCl_2}$ (linear) |
| 3 | Trigonal planar | $sp^2$ | $\mathrm{BF_3}$ (planar), $\mathrm{SO_2}$ (bent) |
| 4 | Tetrahedral | $sp^3$ | $\mathrm{CH_4}$, $\mathrm{NH_3}$ (pyramidal), $\mathrm{H_2O}$ (bent) |
| 5 | Trigonal bipyramidal | $sp^3d$ | $\mathrm{PCl_5}$, $\mathrm{SF_4}$ (see-saw), $\mathrm{ClF_3}$ (T-shaped), $\mathrm{I_3^-}$ (linear) |
| 6 | Octahedral | $sp^3d^2$ | $\mathrm{SF_6}$, $\mathrm{XeF_4}$ (square planar) |

## Where lone pairs go

In a trigonal bipyramid, lone pairs occupy the **equatorial** positions, where they have fewer close neighbours at $90^\circ$. That is why $\mathrm{SF_4}$ is see-saw, $\mathrm{ClF_3}$ is T-shaped, and $\mathrm{XeF_2}$ and $\mathrm{I_3^-}$ are linear (three equatorial lone pairs). In an octahedron, two lone pairs sit opposite each other, giving square planar $\mathrm{XeF_4}$.

## Bond angles

Lone pairs repel more strongly than bonding pairs, so they compress the angles between bonds: $\mathrm{CH_4}$ ($109.5^\circ$), $\mathrm{NH_3}$ ($107^\circ$), $\mathrm{H_2O}$ ($104.5^\circ$). A more electronegative terminal atom pulls the bonding pairs away from the centre and also reduces the bond angle: $\mathrm{NF_3}$ ($102^\circ$) is smaller than $\mathrm{NH_3}$.

## Hybridisation

Hybridisation is the mixing of atomic orbitals on one atom to form equivalent hybrid orbitals. The number of hybrid orbitals equals the number of domains. In $\mathrm{C_2H_4}$, each carbon has three domains ($sp^2$): three $\sigma$ bonds, with the remaining $p$ orbitals forming the $\pi$ bond. In general a double bond is one $\sigma$ plus one $\pi$, and a triple bond is one $\sigma$ plus two $\pi$.

## Quiz

1. What is the shape of the $\mathrm{ClF_3}$ molecule?
   - [ ] Trigonal planar
   - [ ] Trigonal pyramidal
   - [ ] See-saw
   - [x] T-shaped
   > Chlorine has 7 valence electrons, 3 used in bonds, leaving 2 lone pairs: 5 domains in total, trigonal bipyramidal. Both lone pairs sit in equatorial positions, leaving the three fluorines in a T shape.

2. What are the hybridisation of the central atom and the shape of the $\mathrm{I_3^-}$ ion?
   - [ ] $sp$, linear
   - [ ] $sp^3$, bent
   - [x] $sp^3d$, linear
   - [ ] $sp^3d$, T-shaped
   > The central iodine has 2 bonding pairs and 3 lone pairs, so 5 domains and $sp^3d$ hybridisation. The three lone pairs occupy the equatorial plane, which pushes the two bonded iodines to opposite ends, giving a linear shape.

3. Which of the following arranges the molecules in increasing order of bond angle?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{NH_3} < \mathrm{H_2O} < \mathrm{CH_4} < \mathrm{BF_3}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{H_2O} < \mathrm{NH_3} < \mathrm{CH_4} < \mathrm{BF_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{H_2O} < \mathrm{CH_4} < \mathrm{NH_3} < \mathrm{BF_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{BF_3} < \mathrm{CH_4} < \mathrm{NH_3} < \mathrm{H_2O}$
   > $\mathrm{H_2O}$ has $104.5^\circ$ (two lone pairs), $\mathrm{NH_3}$ $107^\circ$ (one lone pair), $\mathrm{CH_4}$ $109.5^\circ$ (none) and $\mathrm{BF_3}$ $120^\circ$ (trigonal planar). More lone pairs means a smaller bond angle.
