## Group 15 (N, P, As, Sb, Bi)

Nitrogen exists as $\mathrm{N_2}$ with a very strong triple bond, so it is unreactive. Ammonia, $\mathrm{NH_3}$, is made by the Haber process and is a base and a ligand; its high boiling point comes from hydrogen bonding. The **basic strength of the hydrides decreases down the group**:

$$\mathrm{NH_3} > \mathrm{PH_3} > \mathrm{AsH_3} > \mathrm{SbH_3} > \mathrm{BiH_3}$$

because the lone pair sits in an increasingly large, diffuse orbital and the central atom's electron density falls.

Nitric acid is made by the Ostwald process: $\mathrm{4NH_3 + 5O_2 \rightarrow 4NO + 6H_2O}$ (Pt-Rh catalyst), then $\mathrm{2NO + O_2 \rightarrow 2NO_2}$, then $\mathrm{3NO_2 + H_2O \rightarrow 2HNO_3 + NO}$.

White phosphorus ($\mathrm{P_4}$, tetrahedral with $60^\circ$ bond angles) is strained and very reactive, so it is stored under water; red phosphorus is a stable polymer.

**Oxoacids of phosphorus:** the **basicity** (number of replaceable $\mathrm{H^+}$) equals the number of $\mathrm{P-OH}$ groups, not the number of hydrogen atoms. $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$ has two $\mathrm{P-H}$ bonds and one $\mathrm{P-OH}$ (monobasic, reducing); $\mathrm{H_3PO_3}$ has one $\mathrm{P-H}$ and two $\mathrm{P-OH}$ (dibasic); $\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$ has three $\mathrm{P-OH}$ (tribasic).

## Group 16

$\mathrm{O_3}$ is a powerful oxidant. $\mathrm{SO_2}$ is a reducing, bleaching, acidic gas. Sulfuric acid is made by the **Contact process** using $\mathrm{V_2O_5}$ as a catalyst, with $\mathrm{SO_3}$ absorbed in $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ (to give oleum) rather than water, to avoid an acid mist.

## Group 17

Oxidising power falls down the group: $\mathrm{F_2} > \mathrm{Cl_2} > \mathrm{Br_2} > \mathrm{I_2}$. Acid strength of the hydrogen halides **increases** down the group ($\mathrm{HF} < \mathrm{HCl} < \mathrm{HBr} < \mathrm{HI}$) because the H-X bond weakens. For the chlorine oxoacids, acidity rises with oxidation number: $\mathrm{HOCl} < \mathrm{HClO_2} < \mathrm{HClO_3} < \mathrm{HClO_4}$, since more oxygen atoms stabilise the anion.

## Group 18

Noble gases form compounds only with highly electronegative atoms. $\mathrm{XeF_2}$ is linear, $\mathrm{XeF_4}$ square planar, $\mathrm{XeF_6}$ distorted octahedral. Complete hydrolysis of $\mathrm{XeF_6}$ gives $\mathrm{XeO_3}$ (pyramidal, an explosive solid).

## Quiz

1. What are the basicities of $\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$, $\mathrm{H_3PO_3}$ and $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$, respectively?
   - [ ] 3, 3, 3
   - [ ] 3, 3, 1
   - [x] 3, 2, 1
   - [ ] 3, 1, 2
   > Only hydrogens in $\mathrm{P-OH}$ groups are acidic; hydrogens bonded directly to phosphorus ($\mathrm{P-H}$) are not. $\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$ has three $\mathrm{OH}$, $\mathrm{H_3PO_3}$ two, and $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$ one.

2. What is formed on complete hydrolysis of $\mathrm{XeF_6}$?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{XeOF_4}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{XeO_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{XeO_2F_2}$
   - [ ] Xe
   > $\mathrm{XeF_6 + 3H_2O \rightarrow XeO_3 + 6HF}$. Partial hydrolysis gives $\mathrm{XeOF_4}$ and $\mathrm{XeO_2F_2}$, with oxidation state remaining $+6$ throughout.

3. Which sequence correctly shows decreasing basic strength of the hydrides?
   - [x] $\mathrm{NH_3} > \mathrm{PH_3} > \mathrm{AsH_3} > \mathrm{SbH_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{SbH_3} > \mathrm{AsH_3} > \mathrm{PH_3} > \mathrm{NH_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{PH_3} > \mathrm{NH_3} > \mathrm{AsH_3} > \mathrm{SbH_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{NH_3} > \mathrm{AsH_3} > \mathrm{PH_3} > \mathrm{SbH_3}$
   > As the central atom gets larger, the lone pair is spread over a bigger region and is less available to accept a proton, so the basic strength decreases steadily down the group.
