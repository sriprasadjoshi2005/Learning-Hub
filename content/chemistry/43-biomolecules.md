## Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds that give them on hydrolysis. Glucose is an **aldohexose**, $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}$. Its open chain has four **chiral centres**, so it has $2^4 = 16$ stereoisomers (D- and L-forms included). In solution glucose exists mostly as a cyclic **hemiacetal** (the pyranose form) with two **anomers**, $\alpha$ and $\beta$, which differ at C-1.

- **Reducing sugars** have a free hemiacetal group that can open to the aldehyde: glucose, fructose, maltose, lactose. They reduce Tollens' and Fehling's reagents.
- **Sucrose** is $\alpha$-D-glucose joined to $\beta$-D-fructose through both anomeric carbons, so it is **non-reducing**. Its hydrolysis gives "invert sugar" (the optical rotation changes sign).
- **Starch** (amylose: $\alpha$-1,4 links; amylopectin: also $\alpha$-1,6 branches) and **cellulose** ($\beta$-1,4 links) are polymers of glucose.

## Amino acids and proteins

An $\alpha$-amino acid has $-\mathrm{NH_2}$ and $-\mathrm{COOH}$ on the same carbon. In solution it exists as a **zwitterion** (dipolar ion). At its **isoelectric point**, the net charge is zero. Amino acids join by **peptide bonds** ($-\mathrm{CO-NH-}$).

- **Primary structure:** the sequence of amino acids.
- **Secondary:** $\alpha$-helix and $\beta$-pleated sheet, held by hydrogen bonds.
- **Tertiary:** the overall 3-D folding (disulfide bridges, ionic and hydrophobic interactions).
- **Quaternary:** the arrangement of several chains, as in haemoglobin.

**Denaturation** destroys the secondary and tertiary structures (e.g. boiling an egg) but leaves the primary structure intact. Enzymes are protein catalysts that are highly specific.

## Vitamins

Fat-soluble: A (night blindness), D (rickets), E, K (clotting). Water-soluble: B group (B$_1$: beri-beri) and C (scurvy), which must be supplied regularly because they are not stored.

## Nucleic acids

DNA contains deoxyribose, the bases A, G, C and T, and forms a double helix; RNA has ribose and U instead of T. Base pairing is **A-T** (two hydrogen bonds) and **G-C** (three), so in double-stranded DNA $[\mathrm{A}] = [\mathrm{T}]$ and $[\mathrm{G}] = [\mathrm{C}]$ (Chargaff's rules).

## Quiz

1. How many stereoisomers does open-chain glucose have?
   - [x] 16
   - [ ] 4
   - [ ] 8
   - [ ] 32
   > Glucose (an aldohexose) has four chiral carbons, C-2 to C-5, so there are $2^4 = 16$ stereoisomers. Cyclisation adds a fifth stereocentre at C-1 (the anomeric carbon), but that applies to the ring forms, not to the open chain.

2. A sample of double-stranded DNA contains $28\%$ adenine. What percentage of guanine does it contain?
   - [ ] 28%
   - [ ] 44%
   - [ ] 72%
   - [x] 22%
   > A pairs with T, so thymine is also 28%, giving $56\%$ for A+T. The remaining $44\%$ is split equally between G and C, so guanine is $22\%$.

3. Which of the following sugars is a non-reducing sugar?
   - [ ] Glucose
   - [ ] Maltose
   - [x] Sucrose
   - [ ] Lactose
   > In sucrose, the anomeric carbons of both glucose and fructose are tied up in the glycosidic bond, so neither ring can open to give a free aldehyde or ketone. Maltose and lactose each have one free anomeric hydroxyl.
