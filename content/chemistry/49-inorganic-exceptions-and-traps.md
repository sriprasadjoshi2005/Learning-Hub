title: Inorganic Exceptions and Traps
level: exam
---

Inorganic questions often hide a rule's **exception**. The trends below are the ones examiners return to most often. Learn each trend together with its reason, because the reason lets you handle new variations.

## Trends that look regular but are not

- **Ionisation enthalpy:** B is below Be, and O is below N (subshell and half-filled stability).
- **Electron gain enthalpy:** Cl is more negative than F (the small $2p$ subshell of F causes repulsion). The same pattern holds for S versus O.
- **Bond angles in hydrides of Group 16:** $\mathrm{H_2O}$ ($104.5^\circ$) $>$ $\mathrm{H_2S}$ ($92^\circ$) $>$ $\mathrm{H_2Se}$ ($91^\circ$) $>$ $\mathrm{H_2Te}$ ($89^\circ$). In heavier hydrides the central atom uses nearly pure $p$ orbitals, so the angle approaches $90^\circ$.
- **Boiling points of hydrides:** $\mathrm{NH_3}$, $\mathrm{H_2O}$ and $\mathrm{HF}$ are anomalously high due to hydrogen bonding.
- **Hydrated ion size:** the smallest ion is the most hydrated. In water, ionic mobility of the alkali ions **increases** down the group: $\mathrm{Li^+} < \mathrm{Na^+} < \mathrm{K^+} < \mathrm{Rb^+} < \mathrm{Cs^+}$, because $\mathrm{Li^+}$ drags the largest shell of water.

## Thermal and chemical stability

- Carbonates of Group 2 become **more** stable down the group: $\mathrm{BeCO_3} < \mathrm{MgCO_3} < \mathrm{CaCO_3} < \mathrm{SrCO_3} < \mathrm{BaCO_3}$.
- Hydrogen halides: acid strength $\mathrm{HF} < \mathrm{HCl} < \mathrm{HBr} < \mathrm{HI}$, and the **reducing** power increases in the same order.
- Inert pair effect: lower oxidation states become more stable down groups 13, 14 and 15.

## Oxides

Acidic character rises across a period and falls down a group: $\mathrm{Na_2O}$ (basic), $\mathrm{MgO}$ (basic), $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$ (**amphoteric**), $\mathrm{SiO_2}$, $\mathrm{P_4O_{10}}$, $\mathrm{SO_3}$ (acidic). $\mathrm{CO}$, $\mathrm{NO}$ and $\mathrm{N_2O}$ are neutral.

## Exam habits

When a question says "which is the exception" or "which is incorrect", scan the options for the classic exceptions above first. When two options look alike, check the **reason**: examiners often swap the reason between two similar trends.

## Quiz

1. Which of the following correctly arranges the hydrides in increasing order of bond angle?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{H_2O} < \mathrm{H_2S} < \mathrm{H_2Se} < \mathrm{H_2Te}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{H_2S} < \mathrm{H_2Se} < \mathrm{H_2Te} < \mathrm{H_2O}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{H_2Te} < \mathrm{H_2Se} < \mathrm{H_2S} < \mathrm{H_2O}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{H_2Se} < \mathrm{H_2Te} < \mathrm{H_2S} < \mathrm{H_2O}$
   > Bond angles fall from $104.5^\circ$ (water) toward $90^\circ$ as the central atom becomes larger and less electronegative. So the increasing order is $\mathrm{H_2Te}$ ($89^\circ$), $\mathrm{H_2Se}$, $\mathrm{H_2S}$, $\mathrm{H_2O}$.

2. Which sequence correctly shows the ionic mobility of the alkali metal ions in aqueous solution, from lowest to highest?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{Cs^+} < \mathrm{Rb^+} < \mathrm{K^+} < \mathrm{Na^+} < \mathrm{Li^+}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{Li^+} < \mathrm{Na^+} < \mathrm{K^+} < \mathrm{Rb^+} < \mathrm{Cs^+}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{Na^+} < \mathrm{Li^+} < \mathrm{K^+} < \mathrm{Cs^+} < \mathrm{Rb^+}$
   - [ ] All have equal mobility
   > Mobility depends on the size of the **hydrated** ion. $\mathrm{Li^+}$ is the smallest bare ion, so it is most heavily hydrated, giving the largest hydrated radius and the lowest mobility.

3. Which of the following correctly arranges the carbonates in increasing thermal stability?
   - [x] $\mathrm{MgCO_3} < \mathrm{CaCO_3} < \mathrm{SrCO_3} < \mathrm{BaCO_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{BaCO_3} < \mathrm{SrCO_3} < \mathrm{CaCO_3} < \mathrm{MgCO_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CaCO_3} < \mathrm{MgCO_3} < \mathrm{BaCO_3} < \mathrm{SrCO_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{SrCO_3} < \mathrm{BaCO_3} < \mathrm{MgCO_3} < \mathrm{CaCO_3}$
   > A small, highly charged cation such as $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$ polarises the carbonate ion strongly and makes it decompose easily. Larger cations polarise less, so stability rises down the group.
