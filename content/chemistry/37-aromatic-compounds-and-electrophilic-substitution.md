## Aromaticity

A compound is **aromatic** if it is cyclic, planar, fully conjugated, and has $4n+2$ $\pi$ electrons (**Hückel's rule**). Benzene has 6 and is exceptionally stable. Compounds with $4n$ $\pi$ electrons in a planar conjugated ring are **antiaromatic** (very unstable), as in cyclobutadiene. Cyclooctatetraene is non-planar, so it behaves like an ordinary alkene.

Ions can be aromatic: the cyclopentadienyl **anion** (6 $\pi$ electrons) and the cycloheptatrienyl (tropylium) **cation** (6 $\pi$ electrons) are aromatic, whereas the cyclopentadienyl cation (4 $\pi$ electrons) is antiaromatic.

## Electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS)

Benzene keeps its aromaticity by **substituting** rather than adding. The mechanism has two steps: the electrophile attacks the ring to form a resonance-stabilised **arenium ion** (the slow step), then a proton is lost to restore the ring. Key reactions and electrophiles:

| Reaction | Reagents | Electrophile |
|---|---|---|
| Nitration | conc. $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ + conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ | $\mathrm{NO_2^+}$ |
| Halogenation | $\mathrm{Cl_2/FeCl_3}$ or $\mathrm{Br_2/FeBr_3}$ | $\mathrm{Cl^+}$ / $\mathrm{Br^+}$ (polarised) |
| Sulfonation | fuming $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ | $\mathrm{SO_3}$ |
| Friedel-Crafts alkylation | $\mathrm{RCl/AlCl_3}$ | $\mathrm{R^+}$ |
| Friedel-Crafts acylation | $\mathrm{RCOCl/AlCl_3}$ | $\mathrm{RCO^+}$ |

## Directing effects

A substituent already on the ring controls both the **rate** and the **position** of the next substitution.

- **Activating, $o/p$-directing:** $-\mathrm{OH}$, $-\mathrm{NH_2}$, $-\mathrm{OR}$, $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ (they donate electrons to the ring).
- **Deactivating, $m$-directing:** $-\mathrm{NO_2}$, $-\mathrm{CN}$, $-\mathrm{CHO}$, $-\mathrm{COOH}$, $-\mathrm{SO_3H}$ (they withdraw electrons).
- **Deactivating, $o/p$-directing:** the halogens ($-I$ outweighs $+M$ for the rate, but $+M$ controls the position).

## Friedel-Crafts limits

Alkylation does not work on strongly deactivated rings (nitrobenzene), may involve **carbocation rearrangement**, and can polyalkylate. Acylation avoids rearrangement because the acylium ion does not rearrange.

## Quiz

1. Which of the following is aromatic?
   - [ ] Cyclobutadiene
   - [ ] Cyclopentadienyl cation
   - [x] Cyclopentadienyl anion
   - [ ] Cyclooctatetraene
   > The cyclopentadienyl anion is cyclic, planar and conjugated, with 6 $\pi$ electrons ($4n+2$ for $n=1$). The cation has 4 $\pi$ electrons (antiaromatic), as does cyclobutadiene, and cyclooctatetraene is not planar.

2. Which of the following arranges the compounds in decreasing reactivity toward electrophilic substitution?
   - [ ] Benzene > toluene > phenol > nitrobenzene
   - [x] Phenol > toluene > benzene > nitrobenzene
   - [ ] Nitrobenzene > benzene > toluene > phenol
   - [ ] Toluene > phenol > nitrobenzene > benzene
   > $-\mathrm{OH}$ is a strong electron donor ($+M$), $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ a weak one, and $-\mathrm{NO_2}$ a strong withdrawing group. Donors make the ring more nucleophilic, and withdrawing groups make it less so.

3. What is the major product when benzene reacts with 1-chloropropane and $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$?
   - [x] Isopropylbenzene (cumene)
   - [ ] Propylbenzene
   - [ ] 1,2-Dipropylbenzene
   - [ ] Chlorobenzene
   > The primary carbocation $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2^+}$ rearranges by a hydride shift to the more stable secondary cation $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2CH^+}$, which then attacks benzene.
