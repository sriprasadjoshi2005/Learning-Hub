---
title: Redox Reactions and Oxidation Numbers
level: foundation
---

A **redox** reaction involves the transfer of electrons. **Oxidation** is loss of electrons (oxidation number increases); **reduction** is gain (oxidation number decreases). The substance that is oxidised is the *reducing agent*; the one reduced is the *oxidising agent*.

## Rules for oxidation numbers

1. An element in its standard state is $0$.
2. Simple ions have an oxidation number equal to their charge.
3. Hydrogen is $+1$ (except in metal hydrides, $-1$); oxygen is $-2$ (except in peroxides, $-1$, and in $\mathrm{OF_2}$, $+2$).
4. The sum of the oxidation numbers equals the overall charge.

Oxidation numbers can be fractional. They are bookkeeping averages, not real charges. In $\mathrm{Fe_3O_4}$ iron averages $+8/3$ because it is really $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ and two $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$.

## Balancing by the ion-electron method (acidic medium)

1. Split into two half-reactions.
2. Balance atoms other than O and H.
3. Balance O with $\mathrm{H_2O}$, then H with $\mathrm{H^+}$.
4. Balance charge with electrons.
5. Multiply so the electrons cancel, then add.

For permanganate and iron(II):
$$\mathrm{MnO_4^- + 8H^+ + 5e^- \rightarrow Mn^{2+} + 4H_2O}$$
$$\mathrm{Fe^{2+} \rightarrow Fe^{3+} + e^-}$$
so one $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}$ oxidises five $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$. This $1:5$ ratio is the heart of many titration questions.

## Special types

A **disproportionation** reaction is one in which a single species is both oxidised and reduced, as in $\mathrm{2H_2O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O + O_2}$, where oxygen goes from $-1$ to $-2$ and to $0$. The reverse, **comproportionation**, has two species of the same element at different oxidation numbers forming one product at an intermediate value.

## Quiz

1. What is the average oxidation number of sulfur in sodium tetrathionate, $\mathrm{Na_2S_4O_6}$?
   - [ ] +2
   - [ ] +3
   - [x] +2.5
   - [ ] +6
   > $2(+1)+4x+6(-2)=0$ gives $4x=10$, so $x=+2.5$. This is an average: two sulfur atoms are $0$ (bonded to each other) and two are $+5$.

2. How many moles of $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ are oxidised to $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ by $0.020$ mol of $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}$ in acidic solution?
   - [ ] 0.020 mol
   - [x] 0.100 mol
   - [ ] 0.060 mol
   - [ ] 0.140 mol
   > $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}$ gains 5 electrons (Mn from $+7$ to $+2$) and each $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ loses 1, so the ratio is $1:5$. Therefore $0.020\times5=0.100$ mol.

3. Which of the following is a disproportionation reaction?
   - [x] $\mathrm{2H_2O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O + O_2}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{Zn + CuSO_4 \rightarrow ZnSO_4 + Cu}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{NH_4NO_2 \rightarrow N_2 + 2H_2O}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{2Na + Cl_2 \rightarrow 2NaCl}$
   > Only in the first one does one element (oxygen, $-1$) end up both higher ($0$) and lower ($-2$). The second is displacement, the third is comproportionation (N at $-3$ and $+3$ both become $0$), and the fourth is a combination reaction.
