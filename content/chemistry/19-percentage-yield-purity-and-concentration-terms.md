# Lesson 19: Percentage Yield, Purity and Concentration Terms

Chemical reactions do not always produce the maximum amount of product predicted by stoichiometric calculations. Some reactants may remain unreacted, side reactions may occur, or products may be lost during separation and purification.

Percentage yield compares the actual amount of product obtained with the theoretical amount predicted by the balanced equation. Purity describes how much of a sample consists of the desired substance. Concentration terms describe the amount of solute present in a given quantity of solution or solvent.

## 1. Theoretical and Actual Yield

### 1.1 Theoretical Yield

Theoretical yield is the maximum amount of product that can form from the given reactants, assuming the reaction proceeds completely according to the balanced equation.

It is calculated using stoichiometry and the limiting reactant.

### 1.2 Actual Yield

Actual yield is the amount of product experimentally obtained from a chemical reaction.

It may be lower than the theoretical yield because of:
- Incomplete reactions.
- Side reactions.
- Product loss during filtration or transfer.
- Decomposition of products.
- Experimental limitations.

## 2. Percentage Yield

### 2.1 Definition

Percentage yield is the ratio of actual yield to theoretical yield, multiplied by 100.

\[
\boxed{\%\text{ yield}=\frac{\text{Actual yield}}{\text{Theoretical yield}}\times100}
\]

Both quantities must be expressed in the same units.

### 2.2 Worked Example

A reaction has a theoretical yield of 50 g, but only 40 g of product is obtained.

Calculate the percentage yield.

\[
\%\text{ yield}=\frac{40}{50}\times100
\]

\[
\boxed{80\%}
\]

### 2.3 Calculating Actual Yield

If percentage yield and theoretical yield are known:

\[
\boxed{\text{Actual yield}=\frac{\%\text{ yield}}{100}\times\text{Theoretical yield}}
\]

Example:

The theoretical yield is 80 g and the percentage yield is 75%.

\[
\text{Actual yield}=0.75\times80
\]

\[
\boxed{60\,g}
\]

### 2.4 Calculating Theoretical Yield

If actual yield and percentage yield are known:

\[
\boxed{\text{Theoretical yield}=\frac{\text{Actual yield}\times100}{\%\text{ yield}}}
\]

Example:

Actual yield = 36 g.

Percentage yield = 90%.

\[
\text{Theoretical yield}=\frac{36\times100}{90}
\]

\[
\boxed{40\,g}
\]

## 3. Percentage Purity

### 3.1 Definition

Percentage purity is the percentage of a sample's total mass that consists of the desired pure substance.

\[
\boxed{\%\text{ purity}=\frac{\text{Mass of pure substance}}{\text{Total mass of sample}}\times100}
\]

Impurities are substances present in the sample other than the desired material.

### 3.2 Worked Example

A 25 g sample contains 20 g of pure calcium carbonate.

Calculate its percentage purity.

\[
\%\text{ purity}=\frac{20}{25}\times100
\]

\[
\boxed{80\%}
\]

### 3.3 Calculating Pure Substance Mass

A 60 g sample has a purity of 85%.

\[
\text{Pure mass}=\frac{85}{100}\times60
\]

\[
\boxed{51\,g}
\]

The remaining mass is impurity:

\[
60-51=9\,g
\]

## 4. Stoichiometry with Impure Reactants

When a reactant is impure, only the pure portion is assumed to participate in the reaction unless the impurities are also reactive.

### 4.1 Worked Example

A 50 g sample of limestone contains 80% calcium carbonate. Calculate the mass of calcium oxide produced on complete thermal decomposition.

Reaction:

\[
CaCO_3\rightarrow CaO+CO_2
\]

Molar masses:
- CaCO₃ = 100 g mol⁻¹.
- CaO = 56 g mol⁻¹.

**Step 1: Calculate the mass of pure calcium carbonate.**

\[
m=0.80\times50
\]

\[
m=40\,g
\]

**Step 2: Calculate moles of calcium carbonate.**

\[
n=\frac{40}{100}=0.4\,mol
\]

**Step 3: Apply the mole ratio.**

The equation shows:

\[
CaCO_3:CaO=1:1
\]

Therefore:

\[
n(CaO)=0.4\,mol
\]

**Step 4: Calculate the mass of calcium oxide.**

\[
m=0.4\times56
\]

\[
\boxed{22.4\,g}
\]

## 5. Concentration of Solutions

### 5.1 Definition of a Solution

A solution is a homogeneous mixture containing one or more solutes dissolved in a solvent.

**Solute:** The substance dissolved.

**Solvent:** The substance that dissolves the solute.

For example, in a sodium chloride solution:
- Sodium chloride is the solute.
- Water is the solvent.

### 5.2 Concentration

Concentration describes the amount of solute present in a specified quantity of solution or solvent.

Different concentration units are used depending on the purpose of the calculation.

Important concentration terms include:
- Molarity.
- Molality.
- Mole fraction.
- Mass percentage.
- Volume percentage.
- Mass-by-volume percentage.
- Parts per million.

## 6. Molarity

### 6.1 Definition

Molarity is the number of moles of solute present in one litre of solution.

It is represented by \(M\).

\[
\boxed{M=\frac{n}{V}}
\]

Where:
- \(M\) = Molarity in mol L⁻¹.
- \(n\) = Moles of solute.
- \(V\) = Volume of solution in litres.

### 6.2 Worked Example

Calculate the molarity of a solution containing 0.5 mol of NaCl in 2 L of solution.

\[
M=\frac{0.5}{2}
\]

\[
\boxed{0.25\,mol\,L^{-1}}
\]

### 6.3 Molarity Using Mass

Since:

\[
n=\frac{m}{M_r}
\]

Molarity can also be written as:

\[
\boxed{M=\frac{m}{M_rV}}
\]

Where:
- \(m\) = Mass of solute in grams.
- \(M_r\) = Molar mass in g mol⁻¹.
- \(V\) = Solution volume in litres.

### 6.4 Worked Example

Calculate the molarity of a solution prepared by dissolving 5.85 g of NaCl in enough water to make 500 mL of solution.

Molar mass of NaCl:

\[
M_r=58.5\,g\,mol^{-1}
\]

Moles of NaCl:

\[
n=\frac{5.85}{58.5}=0.1\,mol
\]

Volume:

\[
500\,mL=0.5\,L
\]

Molarity:

\[
M=\frac{0.1}{0.5}
\]

\[
\boxed{0.2\,mol\,L^{-1}}
\]

## 7. Molality

### 7.1 Definition

Molality is the number of moles of solute dissolved in one kilogram of solvent.

It is represented by \(m\).

\[
\boxed{m=\frac{\text{Moles of solute}}{\text{Mass of solvent in kg}}}
\]

Unlike molarity, molality depends on the mass of solvent rather than the total volume of solution.

### 7.2 Worked Example

A solution contains 0.5 mol of glucose dissolved in 2 kg of water.

\[
m=\frac{0.5}{2}
\]

\[
\boxed{0.25\,mol\,kg^{-1}}
\]

### 7.3 Molarity Versus Molality

| Feature | Molarity | Molality |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Moles per litre of solution | Moles per kilogram of solvent |
| Symbol | M | m |
| Depends on volume | Yes | No |
| Temperature dependence | Changes with volume | Generally independent of temperature |
| Common unit | mol L⁻¹ | mol kg⁻¹ |

## 8. Mole Fraction

### 8.1 Definition

Mole fraction is the ratio of the number of moles of one component to the total number of moles of all components in a mixture.

For component A:

\[
\boxed{X_A=\frac{n_A}{n_{\text{total}}}}
\]

For a binary mixture:

\[
X_A+X_B=1
\]

Mole fraction has no unit.

### 8.2 Worked Example

A mixture contains 2 mol of ethanol and 3 mol of water.

Total moles:

\[
n_{\text{total}}=2+3=5
\]

Mole fraction of ethanol:

\[
X_{\text{ethanol}}=\frac{2}{5}=0.4
\]

Mole fraction of water:

\[
X_{\text{water}}=\frac{3}{5}=0.6
\]

Verification:

\[
0.4+0.6=1
\]

## 9. Percentage Concentration

### 9.1 Mass Percentage (w/w)

Mass percentage expresses the mass of solute per 100 units of total solution mass.

\[
\boxed{\%\,(w/w)=\frac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Mass of solution}}\times100}
\]

Mass of solution:

\[
m_{\text{solution}}=m_{\text{solute}}+m_{\text{solvent}}
\]

### 9.2 Volume Percentage (v/v)

Volume percentage expresses the volume of solute per 100 units of total solution volume.

\[
\boxed{\%\,(v/v)=\frac{\text{Volume of solute}}{\text{Volume of solution}}\times100}
\]

It is commonly used for liquid-liquid mixtures.

### 9.3 Mass-by-Volume Percentage (w/v)

Mass-by-volume percentage expresses the mass of solute in grams per 100 mL of solution.

\[
\boxed{\%\,(w/v)=\frac{\text{Mass of solute in g}}{\text{Volume of solution in mL}}\times100}
\]

### 9.4 Worked Example

A solution contains 10 g of glucose in 200 g of solution.

\[
\%\,(w/w)=\frac{10}{200}\times100
\]

\[
\boxed{5\%}
\]

## 10. Parts Per Million

### 10.1 Definition

Parts per million (ppm) is a concentration unit used for very small quantities of a substance.

For mass-based concentration:

\[
\boxed{ppm=\frac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Mass of solution}}\times10^6}
\]

For dilute aqueous solutions with density close to 1 kg L⁻¹:

\[
1\,ppm\approx1\,mg\,L^{-1}
\]

This approximation is valid only under suitable dilute-solution conditions.

### 10.2 Worked Example

A water sample contains 5 mg of a dissolved substance in 1 L of water.

Assuming the density is approximately 1 kg L⁻¹:

\[
\boxed{5\,ppm\approx5\,mg\,L^{-1}}
\]

## 11. Dilution of Solutions

### 11.1 Meaning of Dilution

Dilution is the process of reducing a solution's concentration by adding more solvent.

During dilution, the amount of solute remains constant, assuming no solute is lost or reacts.

Therefore:

\[
n_1=n_2
\]

Using:

\[
n=MV
\]

We obtain:

\[
\boxed{M_1V_1=M_2V_2}
\]

Where:
- \(M_1\) = Initial concentration.
- \(V_1\) = Initial volume.
- \(M_2\) = Final concentration.
- \(V_2\) = Final volume.

### 11.2 Worked Example

Calculate the volume of 2 M HCl required to prepare 500 mL of 0.5 M HCl.

Given:

\[
M_1=2\,M
\]

\[
M_2=0.5\,M
\]

\[
V_2=500\,mL
\]

Using:

\[
M_1V_1=M_2V_2
\]

\[
2V_1=0.5(500)
\]

\[
V_1=\frac{250}{2}
\]

\[
\boxed{125\,mL}
\]

Therefore, 125 mL of the original solution is diluted with solvent until the final solution volume reaches 500 mL.

## 12. Common Exam Traps

1. **Percentage yield compares actual yield with theoretical yield.**
2. **Percentage purity compares pure substance mass with total sample mass.**
3. **Impure reactants must be corrected for purity before stoichiometric calculations.**
4. **Molarity uses the total volume of solution, not the volume of solvent.**
5. **Molality uses the mass of solvent, not the mass of solution.**
6. **Mole fraction is dimensionless and the mole fractions of all components sum to one.**
7. **In dilution, the number of moles of solute remains constant.**
8. **Convert millilitres into litres when using molarity in mol L⁻¹.**
9. **The approximation \(1\,ppm\approx1\,mg\,L^{-1}\) is mainly suitable for dilute aqueous solutions.**

## Quiz

1. A reaction has a theoretical yield of 80 g and an actual yield of 60 g. What is its percentage yield?
   - [ ] 60%
   - [x] 75%
   - [ ] 80%
   - [ ] 90%

   > Percentage yield = (actual yield/theoretical yield) × 100 = (60/80) × 100 = 75%.

2. Which concentration unit is defined as moles of solute per kilogram of solvent?
   - [ ] Molarity
   - [x] Molality
   - [ ] Mole fraction
   - [ ] Mass percentage

   > Molality is calculated by dividing the moles of solute by the mass of solvent in kilograms.

3. A 2 M solution is diluted to prepare 500 mL of a 0.5 M solution. What volume of the original solution is required?
   - [ ] 50 mL
   - [ ] 100 mL
   - [x] 125 mL
   - [ ] 250 mL

   > Using \(M_1V_1=M_2V_2\), the required volume is \(V_1=(0.5\times500)/2=125\) mL.
