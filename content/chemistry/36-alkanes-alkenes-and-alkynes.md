## Alkanes ($\mathrm{C_nH_{2n+2}}$)

Alkanes are saturated and fairly unreactive. Their key reaction is **free-radical halogenation** (light or heat), a chain reaction of initiation, propagation and termination. The halogen radical removes a hydrogen to give an alkyl radical, which is more stable the more substituted it is ($3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$). Selectivity differs by halogen:

- **Bromination is highly selective** for the most substituted C-H (about $1600:82:1$ for $3^\circ:2^\circ:1^\circ$).
- Chlorination is much less selective ($5:3.8:1$), so it gives mixtures.

## Alkenes ($\mathrm{C_nH_{2n}}$)

The $\pi$ bond makes alkenes nucleophilic, so they undergo **electrophilic addition**.

- **Markovnikov's rule:** in the addition of $\mathrm{HX}$, hydrogen goes to the carbon with more hydrogens, because this gives the more stable carbocation. $\mathrm{CH_3CH{=}CH_2 + HBr \rightarrow CH_3CHBrCH_3}$.
- **Anti-Markovnikov addition** of $\mathrm{HBr}$ occurs only with peroxides (the radical mechanism) and only for HBr, giving $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2Br}$.
- **Ozonolysis:** $\mathrm{O_3}$ followed by $\mathrm{Zn/H_2O}$ cleaves the C=C bond and gives aldehydes or ketones on each side. $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2C{=}CHCH_3}$ gives propanone and ethanal.
- **Elimination** (dehydration of alcohols, dehydrohalogenation) follows **Saytzeff's rule**: the more substituted alkene predominates.
- Other additions: $\mathrm{H_2/Ni}$, $\mathrm{Br_2}$ (anti addition; the bromine water test), and cold alkaline $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ (syn diol, Baeyer's test).

## Alkynes ($\mathrm{C_nH_{2n-2}}$)

- **Terminal alkynes are weakly acidic** ($\mathrm{p}K_a \approx 25$): the $sp$ carbon holds the C-H bonding pair tightly. They react with $\mathrm{NaNH_2}$ and give precipitates with ammoniacal $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$ or $\mathrm{Cu_2Cl_2}$.
- **Hydration** ($\mathrm{HgSO_4/H_2SO_4}$) gives a ketone through an enol; only ethyne gives an aldehyde (ethanal).
- **Partial reduction:** $\mathrm{H_2}$ with **Lindlar's catalyst** gives the **cis** alkene; $\mathrm{Na}$ in liquid $\mathrm{NH_3}$ gives the **trans** alkene.

## Quiz

1. What are the products of reductive ozonolysis ($\mathrm{O_3}$ then $\mathrm{Zn/H_2O}$) of $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2C{=}CHCH_3}$?
   - [ ] Two molecules of propanone
   - [x] Propanone and ethanal
   - [ ] Two molecules of ethanal
   - [ ] Propanoic acid and methanal
   > Each end of the double bond becomes a carbonyl group. $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2C{=}}$ gives propanone (a ketone), and $\mathrm{{=}CHCH_3}$ gives ethanal (an aldehyde).

2. Which reagent converts but-2-yne into trans-but-2-ene?
   - [x] Na, liquid $\mathrm{NH_3}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{H_2}$, Lindlar's catalyst
   - [ ] $\mathrm{H_2}$ (excess), Ni
   - [ ] $\mathrm{HgSO_4/H_2SO_4}$
   > Dissolving-metal reduction adds hydrogen in an anti fashion, giving the trans alkene. Lindlar gives the cis isomer, excess $\mathrm{H_2/Ni}$ goes all the way to butane, and mercury(II) catalysis gives a ketone.

3. What is the major product of monobromination of 2-methylbutane with $\mathrm{Br_2}$ and light?
   - [ ] 1-Bromo-2-methylbutane
   - [ ] 2-Bromo-3-methylbutane
   - [ ] 1-Bromo-3-methylbutane
   - [x] 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane
   > Bromine radicals are very selective and remove the weakest C-H bond, the single tertiary hydrogen, giving the tertiary radical and so 2-bromo-2-methylbutane.
