## Group 13 (B, Al, Ga, In, Tl)

Boron is a non-metal; the others are metals, and aluminium is amphoteric (it dissolves in both acids and bases). Boron compounds are **electron-deficient**: in $\mathrm{BF_3}$ boron has only six electrons around it, so it acts as a **Lewis acid**.

- **Boric acid**, $\mathrm{B(OH)_3}$, is a weak monobasic Lewis acid. It does not donate a proton; it accepts $\mathrm{OH^-}$ from water: $\mathrm{B(OH)_3 + H_2O \rightarrow [B(OH)_4]^- + H^+}$.
- **Borax**, $\mathrm{Na_2B_4O_7\cdot10H_2O}$, gives coloured beads in the borax-bead test.
- **Diborane**, $\mathrm{B_2H_6}$, has two bridging hydrogens. It has four normal terminal 2c-2e B-H bonds and two **three-centre two-electron (banana) bonds**, and the bridging bonds are longer.

## Group 14 (C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb)

Carbon forms strong $p\pi$-$p\pi$ multiple bonds and long chains (catenation); silicon prefers single bonds. Allotropes of carbon:

| Form | Hybridisation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Diamond | $sp^3$ | hard, insulator |
| Graphite | $sp^2$ | layers, conducts electricity |
| Fullerene ($\mathrm{C_{60}}$) | $sp^2$ | 20 hexagons, 12 pentagons |

$\mathrm{CO_2}$ is a linear acidic oxide; $\mathrm{CO}$ is a neutral oxide that binds haemoglobin. $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ is a giant covalent solid, unlike $\mathrm{CO_2}$. Silicones are polymers with $-\mathrm{Si-O-Si}-$ chains and organic side groups.

## The inert pair effect

Down a group, the $s$ electrons become reluctant to take part in bonding, so the **lower oxidation state becomes more stable**. For Group 14, $+2$ becomes more stable than $+4$ going from $\mathrm{Ge}$ to $\mathrm{Pb}$; for Group 13, $+1$ is the stable state for thallium. Consequences:

- $\mathrm{PbCl_2}$ is more stable than $\mathrm{PbCl_4}$.
- $\mathrm{Pb^{4+}}$ compounds such as $\mathrm{PbO_2}$ are strong oxidising agents, and $\mathrm{Sn^{2+}}$ is a reducing agent (it prefers to reach $+4$).

## Quiz

1. Why is boric acid, $\mathrm{B(OH)_3}$, classified as a Lewis acid and not a Brønsted acid?
   - [ ] It donates a proton directly to water.
   - [x] It accepts $\mathrm{OH^-}$ from water, forming $\mathrm{[B(OH)_4]^-}$.
   - [ ] It donates an electron pair to the hydroxide ion.
   - [ ] It has no $\mathrm{O-H}$ bonds.
   > Boron has an empty $2p$ orbital, so it accepts an electron pair from $\mathrm{OH^-}$. The released $\mathrm{H^+}$ comes from the water, not from the boric acid itself.

2. How many two-centre two-electron (terminal) and three-centre two-electron bonds are there in $\mathrm{B_2H_6}$, respectively?
   - [x] 4 and 2
   - [ ] 6 and 0
   - [ ] 2 and 4
   - [ ] 8 and 0
   > Diborane has 12 valence electrons: 8 in the four terminal B-H bonds (2 electrons each) and 4 in the two bridging B-H-B bonds. Eight 2c-2e bonds would need 16 electrons, which boron cannot provide.

3. Which oxide is the strongest oxidising agent?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CO_2}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{SiO_2}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{SnO_2}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{PbO_2}$
   > Because of the inert pair effect, $\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}$ is more stable than $\mathrm{Pb^{4+}}$, so $\mathrm{PbO_2}$ readily gains electrons to reach the $+2$ state.
