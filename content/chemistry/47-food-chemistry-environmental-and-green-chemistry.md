# Lesson 47: Food Chemistry, Environmental Chemistry and Green Chemistry

Chemistry explains food composition, preservation, flavour, nutrition, and the environmental effects of human activities. This lesson connects chemical principles to food processing, pollution, atmospheric chemistry, water treatment, and sustainable chemical design.

## 1. Composition of Food

Food contains several major classes of nutrients.

| Nutrient | Main role | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | Energy | Rice, bread, fruit |
| Proteins | Growth and repair | Pulses, eggs, milk |
| Fats | Energy storage and cell structure | Oils, nuts |
| Vitamins | Regulation of biological processes | Fruits and vegetables |
| Minerals | Structural and physiological roles | Calcium, iron |
| Water | Solvent and transport medium | Drinking water |

The chemical composition of food affects its nutritional value, stability, and processing behaviour.

## 2. Food Additives

Food additives are substances added to food for technological or sensory purposes.

### 2.1 Preservatives

Preservatives slow spoilage caused by microorganisms or chemical reactions.

Examples include:
- Sodium benzoate
- Potassium sorbate
- Sulfur dioxide in certain food applications

### 2.2 Antioxidants

Antioxidants slow oxidation, which can cause rancidity and loss of flavour.

Examples include:
- Ascorbic acid
- Tocopherols
- Certain permitted synthetic antioxidants

### 2.3 Emulsifiers

Emulsifiers help stabilise mixtures of water and oil.

They contain regions that interact with both polar and nonpolar substances.

### 2.4 Artificial Sweeteners

Artificial or high-intensity sweeteners provide sweetness with relatively little material.

Examples include sucralose, saccharin, and aspartame.

Their permitted uses and acceptable intake limits depend on food regulations.

## 3. Food Preservation

Food preservation reduces microbial growth and slows chemical deterioration.

Methods include:
- Refrigeration and freezing
- Heating and pasteurisation
- Drying
- Salting and sugaring
- Fermentation
- Chemical preservation
- Modified-atmosphere packaging

### 3.1 Pasteurisation

Pasteurisation uses controlled heating followed by cooling to reduce harmful microorganisms and improve food safety.

It does not necessarily sterilise the food.

### 3.2 Fermentation

Microorganisms convert sugars into other compounds.

Alcoholic fermentation:

\[
C_6H_{12}O_6\rightarrow2C_2H_5OH+2CO_2
\]

Lactic acid fermentation:

\[
C_6H_{12}O_6\rightarrow2CH_3CH(OH)COOH
\]

These processes are used in products such as bread, yoghurt, and fermented foods.

## 4. Oxidation and Food Deterioration

Oxidation of fats and oils can produce unpleasant flavours and odours called rancidity.

Factors affecting oxidation include:
- Oxygen exposure
- Light
- Temperature
- Metal ions
- Fatty acid composition

Antioxidants can slow these processes by interrupting oxidation pathways.

## 5. Water Pollution

Water pollution occurs when harmful substances enter water bodies and reduce water quality.

Major pollutants include:
- Sewage
- Industrial chemicals
- Heavy metals
- Fertilisers
- Pesticides
- Oil
- Microplastics

### 5.1 Biological Oxygen Demand

Biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) measures the oxygen consumed by microorganisms while decomposing biodegradable organic matter under specified test conditions.

High BOD generally indicates substantial biodegradable organic pollution.

### 5.2 Chemical Oxygen Demand

Chemical oxygen demand (COD) measures the oxygen equivalent required to chemically oxidise substances in a water sample under specified conditions.

COD includes substances that may not be readily biodegradable.

### 5.3 Eutrophication

Excess nitrogen and phosphorus compounds can enter lakes and rivers.

This may cause excessive algal growth. When the algae die and decompose, oxygen levels can fall, harming aquatic organisms.

## 6. Water Treatment

A typical drinking-water treatment process may include:

1. Screening to remove large objects.
2. Coagulation and flocculation to gather fine suspended particles.
3. Sedimentation to allow particles to settle.
4. Filtration to remove remaining suspended matter.
5. Disinfection to reduce harmful microorganisms.

Disinfection methods include chlorination, ultraviolet treatment, and ozonation.

The exact treatment sequence depends on the source-water quality.

## 7. Air Pollution

Important air pollutants include:
- Carbon monoxide
- Sulfur dioxide
- Nitrogen oxides
- Particulate matter
- Ground-level ozone
- Volatile organic compounds

### 7.1 Carbon Monoxide

Carbon monoxide forms during incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels.

It binds strongly to haemoglobin, reducing the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity.

### 7.2 Sulfur Dioxide

Sulfur dioxide can form during the combustion of sulfur-containing fuels and certain industrial processes.

It contributes to acid deposition and respiratory problems.

### 7.3 Nitrogen Oxides

Nitrogen oxides form during high-temperature combustion and some industrial processes.

They contribute to photochemical smog and acid deposition.

### 7.4 Photochemical Smog

Photochemical smog forms through sunlight-driven reactions involving nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds.

Ground-level ozone is an important component.

## 8. Greenhouse Effect and Climate Change

Greenhouse gases absorb and emit infrared radiation.

Important greenhouse gases include:
- Carbon dioxide
- Methane
- Nitrous oxide
- Water vapour
- Certain industrial gases

The natural greenhouse effect makes Earth habitable. Increased greenhouse gas concentrations from human activities enhance warming.

Major sources include fossil-fuel combustion, deforestation, agriculture, and industrial processes.

## 9. Ozone Layer Depletion

Stratospheric ozone absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.

Certain chlorine- and bromine-containing compounds can release radicals that catalytically destroy ozone.

A simplified chlorine radical cycle is:

\[
Cl+O_3\rightarrow ClO+O_2
\]

\[
ClO+O\rightarrow Cl+O_2
\]

Overall:

\[
O_3+O\rightarrow2O_2
\]

The chlorine radical is regenerated and can participate in further reactions.

## 10. Acid Rain

Acid deposition is associated mainly with sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.

These gases undergo atmospheric reactions that produce sulfuric and nitric acids.

Effects include:
- Acidification of lakes and soils.
- Damage to vegetation.
- Corrosion of buildings and monuments.
- Changes in nutrient availability.

## 11. Green Chemistry

Green chemistry designs chemical products and processes to reduce or eliminate hazardous substances and waste.

### 11.1 Twelve Principles

Important principles include:

1. Prevent waste rather than treating it later.
2. Maximise atom economy.
3. Use less hazardous chemical syntheses.
4. Design safer chemicals.
5. Use safer solvents and auxiliaries.
6. Improve energy efficiency.
7. Prefer renewable feedstocks.
8. Avoid unnecessary derivatisation.
9. Use catalysts where suitable.
10. Design products for degradation after use.
11. Monitor processes to prevent pollution.
12. Choose inherently safer processes to reduce accident risks.

### 11.2 Atom Economy

Atom economy measures how effectively reactant atoms are incorporated into the desired product.

\[
\text{Atom economy}=
\frac{\text{Molar mass of desired product}}
{\text{Total molar mass of reactants}}
\times100
\]

A reaction with high atom economy generally produces less stoichiometric waste.

### 11.3 Catalysis

Catalysts can increase reaction rates and improve selectivity, potentially reducing energy use and waste.

A catalyst does not change the equilibrium constant of a reaction.

## 12. Sustainable Chemical Practices

Examples include:
- Replacing hazardous solvents with safer alternatives.
- Using renewable raw materials.
- Developing biodegradable materials.
- Recovering and recycling catalysts.
- Improving energy efficiency.
- Designing processes that minimise waste.

A process should be assessed across its life cycle rather than judged only by one isolated feature.

## 13. Common Exam Traps

- Pasteurisation reduces harmful microorganisms but is not necessarily sterilisation.
- BOD concerns oxygen consumption by microorganisms.
- COD measures chemically oxidisable material under specified test conditions.
- Eutrophication is associated with excess nutrients.
- Ground-level ozone is a pollutant; stratospheric ozone protects against ultraviolet radiation.
- The greenhouse effect and ozone depletion are different atmospheric phenomena.
- Green chemistry aims to prevent pollution at the source.
- High atom economy does not automatically guarantee that a process is safe or sustainable.

## 14. Summary

- Food chemistry involves nutrients, additives, preservation, and oxidation.
- Water pollution can be assessed using BOD and COD.
- Air pollution includes primary pollutants and secondary pollutants such as photochemical ozone.
- Greenhouse warming and ozone depletion involve different chemical processes.
- Green chemistry focuses on waste prevention, safer materials, and efficient processes.
