The last lesson brings the course together. Real papers mix topics inside a single question, so you must recognise which tool each part needs and combine them cleanly.

## Strategy for integrated problems

1. **Identify the topic of each sentence**: a titration, an equilibrium, a bond count. Break the problem into parts.
2. **Use the right concept for each part** (mole ratio, $K$, $\Delta G^\circ$, Nernst) and carry the result forward.
3. **Count carefully** in structure questions. Draw the molecule explicitly and tally every bond.
4. **Cross-check** with a rough estimate or a limiting case.

## Counting bonds

A single bond is one $\sigma$ bond; a double bond is one $\sigma$ plus one $\pi$; a triple bond is one $\sigma$ plus two $\pi$. Every C-H bond is a $\sigma$ bond. For $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH{-}C{\equiv}CH}$ (vinylacetylene), the carbon-carbon bonds contribute $3$ $\sigma$ and $3$ $\pi$, and the four C-H bonds add $4$ more $\sigma$ bonds.

## Linking thermodynamics and equilibrium

$$\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K$$

A positive $\Delta G^\circ$ means $K < 1$, but it does not mean the reaction never occurs: $K$ describes the equilibrium mixture, and the reaction still proceeds to a small extent. For gases, express $K$ as $K_p$ and use pressures in atm.

## Titration stoichiometry

For $a\,\mathrm{A} + b\,\mathrm{B}$, equivalents react in a fixed mole ratio, not volume ratio. Sulfuric acid is **diprotic**: one mole needs two moles of NaOH for complete neutralisation. Always convert volumes and molarities to **moles** first.

## Where to go from here

You have covered physical, inorganic and organic chemistry from atoms to named reactions. For exam preparation, work through past papers, time yourself, and review every mistake by identifying whether it was a concept, a calculation or a reading error.

## Quiz

1. How many $\sigma$ bonds and $\pi$ bonds are in vinylacetylene, $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH{-}C{\equiv}CH}$?
   - [ ] 6 $\sigma$ and 4 $\pi$
   - [ ] 8 $\sigma$ and 3 $\pi$
   - [ ] 9 $\sigma$ and 2 $\pi$
   - [x] 7 $\sigma$ and 3 $\pi$
   > The molecule has 4 C-H bonds (all $\sigma$) and three carbon-carbon bonds: a double bond ($1\sigma + 1\pi$), a single bond ($1\sigma$) and a triple bond ($1\sigma + 2\pi$). That gives $4 + 3 = 7$ $\sigma$ bonds and $1 + 2 = 3$ $\pi$ bonds.

2. $25.0$ mL of $0.100$ M NaOH exactly neutralises $20.0$ mL of a sulfuric acid solution. What is the molarity of the acid?
   - [ ] 0.0800 M
   - [ ] 0.125 M
   - [x] 0.0625 M
   - [ ] 0.250 M
   > NaOH: $25.0\times0.100 = 2.50$ mmol. Each $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ needs 2 $\mathrm{OH^-}$, so there is $1.25$ mmol of acid in $20.0$ mL, which gives $0.0625$ M. The value 0.125 M forgets that the acid is diprotic.

3. For $\mathrm{N_2O_4(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NO_2(g)}$ at $300$ K, $\Delta G^\circ = +4.8$ kJ mol$^{-1}$. What is $K_p$?
   - [ ] 0.85
   - [x] 0.15
   - [ ] 1.92
   - [ ] 6.9
   > $\ln K_p = -\Delta G^\circ/RT = -4800/(8.314\times300) = -1.92$, so $K_p = e^{-1.92} = 0.15$. A positive $\Delta G^\circ$ means $K < 1$. The value 6.9 is $1/K$, which would apply to the reverse reaction.
