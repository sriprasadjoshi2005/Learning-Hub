# Lesson 45: Amines and Diazonium Salts

Amines are derivatives of ammonia in which one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups. They are important in pharmaceuticals, dyes, polymers, and biological molecules. Diazonium salts are especially useful intermediates for preparing substituted aromatic compounds.

## 1. Classification of Amines

Amines contain nitrogen with a lone pair of electrons.

### 1.1 Primary Amines

One hydrogen atom of ammonia is replaced by an organic group.

\[
RNH_2
\]

Example: CH₃NH₂, methylamine.

### 1.2 Secondary Amines

Two hydrogen atoms are replaced.

\[
R_2NH
\]

Example: (CH₃)₂NH, dimethylamine.

### 1.3 Tertiary Amines

All three hydrogen atoms are replaced.

\[
R_3N
\]

Example: (CH₃)₃N, trimethylamine.

### 1.4 Aromatic Amines

An aromatic amine has nitrogen directly attached to an aromatic ring.

Example:

\[
C_6H_5NH_2
\]

Aniline.

Benzylamine, C₆H₅CH₂NH₂, is not an aromatic amine because nitrogen is attached to the side-chain carbon.

## 2. Nomenclature

Amines can be named using the suffix `-amine`.

Examples:
- CH₃NH₂: methanamine
- CH₃CH₂NH₂: ethanamine
- CH₃CH(NH₂)CH₃: propan-2-amine

For substituted amines, substituents attached to nitrogen are identified using the prefix N-.

## 3. Preparation of Amines

### 3.1 Reduction of Nitro Compounds

Nitro compounds can be reduced to primary amines.

\[
RNO_2+6[H]\rightarrow RNH_2+2H_2O
\]

Aniline can be prepared by reducing nitrobenzene using suitable reducing agents.

### 3.2 Ammonolysis of Haloalkanes

Haloalkanes react with ammonia to form amines.

\[
RX+2NH_3\rightarrow RNH_2+NH_4X
\]

Further alkylation can produce secondary and tertiary amines and quaternary ammonium salts.

### 3.3 Reduction of Nitriles

\[
RCN+4[H]\rightarrow RCH_2NH_2
\]

This method increases the carbon chain relative to the corresponding starting alkyl halide used to prepare the nitrile.

### 3.4 Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis

This method is useful for preparing primary aliphatic amines.

Potassium phthalimide reacts with a primary haloalkane, followed by hydrolysis or hydrazinolysis to release the primary amine.

It is not generally suitable for preparing aromatic primary amines through ordinary SN2 substitution.

### 3.5 Hofmann Bromamide Degradation

An amide reacts with bromine and aqueous alkali to produce a primary amine with one fewer carbon atom.

\[
RCONH_2+Br_2+4NaOH
\rightarrow RNH_2+Na_2CO_3+2NaBr+2H_2O
\]

This reaction is also called the Hofmann rearrangement or degradation.

## 4. Physical Properties

- Lower amines are often gases or volatile liquids.
- Primary and secondary amines can form intermolecular hydrogen bonds.
- Tertiary amines cannot donate hydrogen bonds to themselves because they lack N–H bonds.
- Lower amines are generally soluble in water.
- Solubility decreases as the hydrocarbon portion becomes larger.

## 5. Basicity of Amines

Amines act as bases because nitrogen has a lone pair that can accept a proton.

\[
RNH_2+H^+\rightarrow RNH_3^+
\]

Basicity depends on:
- Availability of the nitrogen lone pair.
- Inductive effects.
- Resonance effects.
- Solvation of the protonated amine.
- Steric effects.

### 5.1 Aliphatic Amines

Alkyl groups generally donate electron density through the inductive effect, often increasing amine basicity relative to ammonia.

However, the observed order in aqueous solution also depends on solvation and steric effects.

### 5.2 Aromatic Amines

Aniline is less basic than ammonia and many aliphatic amines because the nitrogen lone pair is delocalised into the aromatic ring.

The lone pair is therefore less available for protonation.

Electron-withdrawing substituents generally reduce aniline basicity, while electron-donating substituents generally increase it.

## 6. Chemical Reactions of Amines

### 6.1 Reaction with Acids

Amines form ammonium salts with acids.

\[
RNH_2+HCl\rightarrow RNH_3Cl
\]

### 6.2 Alkylation

Amines react with haloalkanes to form more highly substituted amines.

Further alkylation may produce quaternary ammonium salts.

### 6.3 Acylation

Primary and secondary amines react with acid chlorides or acid anhydrides to form amides.

\[
RNH_2+R'COCl\rightarrow R'CONHR+HCl
\]

Tertiary amines do not form ordinary amides by this N–H substitution route because they have no N–H bond.

### 6.4 Carbylamine Test

Primary amines react with chloroform and alcoholic potassium hydroxide on heating to form isocyanides.

\[
RNH_2+CHCl_3+3KOH
\rightarrow RNC+3KCl+3H_2O
\]

Isocyanides have a highly unpleasant smell.

The test is given by primary amines, both aliphatic and aromatic, but not by secondary or tertiary amines.

## 7. Reaction with Nitrous Acid

Nitrous acid is usually generated in situ from sodium nitrite and a mineral acid.

The reaction depends on the type of amine.

- Primary aliphatic amines generally form unstable diazonium intermediates that decompose to alcohols with nitrogen gas evolution.
- Primary aromatic amines form relatively stable diazonium salts at low temperature.
- Secondary amines generally form N-nitrosamines.
- Tertiary amines have different reactions depending on whether they are aliphatic or aromatic.

## 8. Diazonium Salts

Aromatic diazonium salts have the general formula:

\[
ArN_2^+X^-
\]

They are prepared by diazotisation of primary aromatic amines.

For aniline:

\[
C_6H_5NH_2+NaNO_2+2HCl
\xrightarrow{273-278K}
C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-+NaCl+2H_2O
\]

The temperature is maintained around 0–5°C because aromatic diazonium salts can decompose at higher temperatures.

## 9. Reactions of Diazonium Salts

The diazonium group can be replaced by several other groups.

### 9.1 Sandmeyer Reactions

Copper(I) salts help replace the diazonium group.

\[
ArN_2^+Cl^-
\xrightarrow{CuCl}
ArCl+N_2
\]

Similarly:
- CuBr can produce aryl bromides.
- CuCN can produce aryl nitriles.

### 9.2 Replacement by Iodine

\[
ArN_2^+Cl^-+KI\rightarrow ArI+KCl+N_2
\]

### 9.3 Replacement by Fluorine

The Balz–Schiemann reaction produces an aryl fluoride through a diazonium tetrafluoroborate intermediate.

### 9.4 Replacement by Hydrogen

Suitable reducing conditions can replace the diazonium group with hydrogen.

### 9.5 Hydrolysis

Warming an aqueous diazonium salt solution can produce a phenol.

\[
ArN_2^+Cl^-+H_2O
\rightarrow ArOH+N_2+HCl
\]

## 10. Azo Coupling

Diazonium salts react with activated aromatic compounds such as phenols and aromatic amines to form azo compounds.

General linkage:

\[
Ar-N=N-Ar'
\]

Azo compounds are important dyes because their extended conjugation allows absorption of visible light.

## 11. Important Distinctions

| Feature | Aliphatic primary amine | Aromatic primary amine |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrous acid reaction | Usually unstable diazonium intermediate | Stable diazonium salt at low temperature |
| Nitrogen gas | Usually evolved | Not immediately evolved during cold diazotisation |
| Example | Ethylamine | Aniline |

## 12. Common Exam Traps

- Aniline is an aromatic amine; benzylamine is not.
- Aromatic amines are generally less basic than aliphatic amines.
- The carbylamine test is given only by primary amines.
- Diazotisation is carried out at low temperature.
- Aromatic diazonium salts are valuable synthetic intermediates.
- Hofmann bromamide degradation reduces the carbon count by one.
- Gabriel synthesis is useful for primary aliphatic amines, not ordinary aromatic amines.
- Azo coupling produces compounds containing the –N=N– linkage.

## 13. Summary

- Amines are classified as primary, secondary, or tertiary.
- Their basicity depends on lone-pair availability and solvation.
- Aniline is less basic than many aliphatic amines because its lone pair is delocalised.
- Diazonium salts are prepared from primary aromatic amines at low temperature.
- Diazonium salts allow the synthesis of several aromatic derivatives.
- Azo coupling is an important method of dye preparation.
