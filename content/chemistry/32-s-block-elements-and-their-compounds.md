# Lesson 32: s-Block Elements and Their Compounds

The s-block consists mainly of Groups 1 and 2 of the periodic table. These elements have their valence electrons in s-orbitals and are commonly called alkali metals and alkaline earth metals.

They are highly electropositive and generally form ionic compounds. Their chemical reactivity, atomic size, ionisation energy, and reaction with water show important periodic trends.

## 1. Electronic Configuration

The general valence-shell configurations are:

- Group 1: \(ns^1\)
- Group 2: \(ns^2\)

Group 1 elements include lithium, sodium, potassium, rubidium, caesium and francium.

Group 2 elements include beryllium, magnesium, calcium, strontium, barium and radium.

## 2. General Physical Properties

### 2.1 Atomic and Ionic Size

Atomic size generally increases down each group because additional electron shells are occupied.

\[
Li<Na<K<Rb<Cs
\]

The same general trend occurs in Group 2.

### 2.2 Ionisation Enthalpy

Ionisation enthalpy is the energy required to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom.

It generally decreases down a group because the outermost electron becomes farther from the nucleus and more shielded.

Group 2 elements generally have higher first ionisation enthalpies than the corresponding Group 1 elements because their outer configuration is \(ns^2\).

### 2.3 Metallic Character

Metallic character increases down both groups.

The elements readily lose electrons and form positive ions.

\[
M\rightarrow M^++e^-
\]

\[
M\rightarrow M^{2+}+2e^-
\]

## 3. Alkali Metals: Group 1

Alkali metals are soft, highly reactive metals.

They generally form \(+1\) ions.

### 3.1 Reaction with Oxygen

Lithium mainly forms the oxide.

\[
4Li+O_2\rightarrow 2Li_2O
\]

Sodium commonly forms the peroxide.

\[
2Na+O_2\rightarrow Na_2O_2
\]

Potassium can form the superoxide.

\[
K+O_2\rightarrow KO_2
\]

The tendency to form larger oxygen-containing anions increases down the group because larger cations stabilise larger anions more effectively.

### 3.2 Reaction with Water

Alkali metals react with water to form hydroxides and hydrogen.

\[
2M+2H_2O\rightarrow 2MOH+H_2
\]

For sodium:

\[
2Na+2H_2O\rightarrow 2NaOH+H_2
\]

Reactivity increases down the group.

### 3.3 Reaction with Halogens

\[
2M+X_2\rightarrow 2MX
\]

For example:

\[
2Na+Cl_2\rightarrow 2NaCl
\]

### 3.4 Flame Colours

Several alkali and alkaline earth metals produce characteristic flame colours.

| Element | Flame colour |
|---|---|
| Lithium | Crimson red |
| Sodium | Golden yellow |
| Potassium | Lilac |
| Calcium | Brick red |
| Strontium | Crimson red |
| Barium | Apple green |

These colours arise from electronic transitions in excited atoms or ions.

## 4. Important Compounds of Alkali Metals

### 4.1 Sodium Chloride

Sodium chloride is an important industrial and biological compound.

It is obtained from seawater and underground deposits.

Uses include:

- Food preservation and seasoning.
- Manufacture of chlorine and sodium hydroxide.
- Preparation of sodium carbonate.
- Preparation of other sodium compounds.

### 4.2 Sodium Hydroxide

Sodium hydroxide is commonly manufactured by electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium chloride.

The overall reaction is:

\[
2NaCl+2H_2O\rightarrow 2NaOH+Cl_2+H_2
\]

It is used in soap manufacture, paper production and chemical industries.

### 4.3 Sodium Carbonate

Sodium carbonate is commonly known as washing soda when present as the decahydrate.

\[
Na_2CO_3\cdot10H_2O
\]

It is used in glass manufacture, water softening and cleaning products.

### 4.4 Sodium Bicarbonate

Sodium bicarbonate is also called baking soda.

\[
NaHCO_3
\]

On heating:

\[
2NaHCO_3\rightarrow Na_2CO_3+CO_2+H_2O
\]

It is used in baking, antacid formulations and certain fire extinguishers.

## 5. Alkaline Earth Metals: Group 2

Group 2 elements have two valence electrons and generally form \(+2\) ions.

They are harder and less reactive than alkali metals in the same period.

### 5.1 Reaction with Oxygen

\[
2M+O_2\rightarrow 2MO
\]

For magnesium:

\[
2Mg+O_2\rightarrow 2MgO
\]

### 5.2 Reaction with Water

The reaction becomes increasingly vigorous down the group.

Magnesium reacts slowly with cold water but more readily with steam.

\[
Mg+H_2O(g)\rightarrow MgO+H_2
\]

Calcium reacts with cold water.

\[
Ca+2H_2O\rightarrow Ca(OH)_2+H_2
\]

Barium reacts more vigorously with water.

### 5.3 Solubility Trends

The solubility of Group 2 hydroxides generally increases down the group.

The solubility of Group 2 sulfates generally decreases down the group.

For example, magnesium sulfate is soluble, while barium sulfate is extremely insoluble.

## 6. Important Compounds of Group 2

### 6.1 Magnesium Oxide

\[
2Mg+O_2\rightarrow 2MgO
\]

Magnesium oxide has a high melting point and is used in refractory materials.

### 6.2 Calcium Oxide

Calcium oxide is also called quicklime.

\[
CaCO_3\xrightarrow{\Delta}CaO+CO_2
\]

It reacts with water to form slaked lime.

\[
CaO+H_2O\rightarrow Ca(OH)_2
\]

### 6.3 Calcium Hydroxide

Calcium hydroxide is commonly called slaked lime.

It reacts with carbon dioxide to form calcium carbonate.

\[
Ca(OH)_2+CO_2\rightarrow CaCO_3+H_2O
\]

This reaction is used in the limewater test for carbon dioxide.

### 6.4 Calcium Carbonate

Calcium carbonate occurs naturally in limestone, chalk and marble.

It is used in construction, cement manufacture and industrial processes.

On heating:

\[
CaCO_3\xrightarrow{\Delta}CaO+CO_2
\]

### 6.5 Plaster of Paris

Plaster of Paris is calcium sulfate hemihydrate.

\[
CaSO_4\cdot\frac{1}{2}H_2O
\]

It is prepared by controlled heating of gypsum.

\[
CaSO_4\cdot2H_2O
\xrightarrow{\Delta}
CaSO_4\cdot\frac{1}{2}H_2O
+\frac{3}{2}H_2O
\]

When mixed with water, it sets back into hydrated gypsum.

## 7. Diagonal Relationship

Certain elements in the second period show similarities with elements diagonally placed in the third period.

Important pairs include:

- Lithium and magnesium.
- Beryllium and aluminium.
- Boron and silicon.

This is called the diagonal relationship.

It arises partly from similarities in ionic size, charge density and electronegativity.

### 7.1 Lithium and Magnesium

Similarities include:

- Both form nitrides directly with nitrogen.
- Their carbonates decompose on heating.
- Their fluorides and carbonates are relatively sparingly soluble.
- Their hydroxides are less soluble than those of the heavier members of their respective groups.

For example:

\[
6Li+N_2\rightarrow 2Li_3N
\]

\[
3Mg+N_2\rightarrow Mg_3N_2
\]

## 8. Anomalous Behaviour of Lithium and Beryllium

Lithium differs from other alkali metals because it has a very small atomic and ionic size.

Beryllium differs from other alkaline earth metals for similar reasons.

Their small size results in high charge density and stronger polarising ability.

Consequences include:

- Greater covalent character in some compounds.
- Formation of complexes.
- Unusual thermal stability and solubility trends.
- Amphoteric behaviour of beryllium oxide and hydroxide.

Beryllium oxide reacts with both acids and bases.

\[
BeO+2HCl\rightarrow BeCl_2+H_2O
\]

\[
BeO+2NaOH+H_2O\rightarrow Na_2[Be(OH)_4]
\]

## 9. Biological and Industrial Importance

- Sodium and potassium ions are essential for nerve impulses and fluid balance.
- Calcium is important for bones, teeth and muscle contraction.
- Magnesium is present at the centre of chlorophyll.
- Calcium compounds are widely used in cement and construction.
- Lithium compounds are important in rechargeable batteries.

## 10. Common Mistakes and Exam Points

- Group 1 elements form \(+1\) ions; Group 2 elements form \(+2\) ions.
- Alkali metal reactivity generally increases down the group.
- Sodium commonly forms peroxide, while potassium can form superoxide.
- Group 2 hydroxide solubility generally increases down the group.
- Group 2 sulfate solubility generally decreases down the group.
- Lithium resembles magnesium because of the diagonal relationship.
- Beryllium oxide is amphoteric.

## 11. Lesson Recap

- The s-block contains Groups 1 and 2.
- Group 1 elements have \(ns^1\) configurations and form \(+1\) ions.
- Group 2 elements have \(ns^2\) configurations and form \(+2\) ions.
- Important compounds include sodium hydroxide, sodium carbonate, calcium oxide and calcium carbonate.
- Lithium and beryllium show anomalous behaviour because of their small sizes.
- Diagonal relationships explain similarities between certain diagonally placed elements.
