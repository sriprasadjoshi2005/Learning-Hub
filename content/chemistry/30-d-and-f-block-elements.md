## Transition metals

Transition elements have partly filled $d$ orbitals in the atom or in a common ion. Zinc, cadmium and mercury ($d^{10}$) are therefore **not** counted as transition metals. Characteristic properties:

- **Variable oxidation states**, because $ns$ and $(n-1)d$ electrons have similar energy. The oxidation states often differ by one unit (Mn: $+2$ to $+7$).
- **Coloured ions**, caused by $d$-$d$ transitions. Ions with $d^0$ ($\mathrm{Sc^{3+}}$, $\mathrm{Ti^{4+}}$) or $d^{10}$ ($\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$) are colourless.
- **Paramagnetism**, from unpaired electrons. The spin-only magnetic moment is $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}$ BM, where $n$ is the number of unpaired electrons.
- **Catalytic activity** and the formation of interstitial compounds and alloys.

## Potassium permanganate and dichromate

$\mathrm{MnO_4^-}$ is a powerful oxidant whose product depends on the medium:

| Medium | Product | Electrons gained |
|---|---|---|
| Acidic | $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ (colourless) | 5 |
| Neutral/weakly basic | $\mathrm{MnO_2}$ (brown) | 3 |
| Strongly basic | $\mathrm{MnO_4^{2-}}$ (green) | 1 |

In acid, permanganate oxidises oxalate: $\mathrm{2MnO_4^- + 5C_2O_4^{2-} + 16H^+ \rightarrow 2Mn^{2+} + 10CO_2 + 8H_2O}$.

Chromate and dichromate are interconverted by pH: $\mathrm{2CrO_4^{2-} + 2H^+ \rightleftharpoons Cr_2O_7^{2-} + H_2O}$. Adding acid shifts it to orange dichromate; adding base gives yellow chromate. Dichromate in acid is a good oxidiser, reduced to green $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$.

## Lanthanoids and actinoids

The 14 lanthanoids ($4f$ filling) show mainly the $+3$ state. As $4f$ electrons shield poorly, the effective nuclear charge rises across the series and the ionic radii steadily shrink: the **lanthanoid contraction**. Its most important consequence is that the second and third transition series elements, such as Zr and Hf, have almost identical radii, which makes them very hard to separate chemically. Actinoids ($5f$) are radioactive and show a wider range of oxidation states.

## Quiz

1. What is the spin-only magnetic moment of $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ (atomic number 26)?
   - [ ] 2.83 BM
   - [ ] 3.87 BM
   - [ ] 4.90 BM
   - [x] 5.92 BM
   > $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ is $3d^5$ with five unpaired electrons: $\mu = \sqrt{5\times7} = 5.92$ BM. The value 4.90 BM belongs to $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ ($d^6$, four unpaired), and 3.87 BM to $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$.

2. $20.0$ mL of $0.10$ M $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ in acid exactly oxidises a solution of sodium oxalate. How many millimoles of oxalate were present?
   - [ ] 0.8
   - [ ] 2.0
   - [x] 5.0
   - [ ] 10
   > $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}$: $20.0\times0.10 = 2.0$ mmol. The stoichiometry is $2\ \mathrm{MnO_4^-} : 5\ \mathrm{C_2O_4^{2-}}$, so oxalate $= 2.0\times5/2 = 5.0$ mmol. The value 10 uses the $1:5$ ratio that applies to $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$, not to oxalate.

3. Zirconium and hafnium have almost the same atomic radius and very similar chemistry. What is the main reason?
   - [ ] The inert pair effect
   - [x] The lanthanoid contraction caused by poor shielding of $4f$ electrons
   - [ ] A diagonal relationship
   - [ ] Strong shielding by the $5d$ electrons
   > Hf follows the 14 lanthanoids. Poorly shielding $4f$ electrons let the nuclear charge pull the outer electrons in, cancelling the size increase you would expect from an extra shell.
