## Alcohols

Alcohols are classified as primary, secondary or tertiary by the carbon carrying the $-\mathrm{OH}$. Hydrogen bonding gives them much higher boiling points than ethers or alkanes of similar mass.

**Distinguishing them:** In the **Lucas test** ($\mathrm{ZnCl_2}$ in conc. $\mathrm{HCl}$), tertiary alcohols turn cloudy immediately, secondary alcohols in about 5 minutes, and primary alcohols not at room temperature. The order follows carbocation stability, since the reaction goes by $S_N1$.

**Oxidation:**
- Primary alcohols give aldehydes with mild reagents (PCC) and carboxylic acids with strong ones ($\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7/H^+}$).
- Secondary alcohols give ketones.
- Tertiary alcohols resist oxidation (no $\alpha$-hydrogen).

**Dehydration** with conc. $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$: at $443$ K ethanol gives ethene (elimination); at $413$ K it gives diethyl ether (substitution).

## Phenols

Phenol is more acidic than alcohols because the phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilised. It undergoes EAS readily, since $-\mathrm{OH}$ is strongly activating and $o/p$-directing. Two named reactions: **Reimer-Tiemann** ($\mathrm{CHCl_3/NaOH}$ gives salicylaldehyde) and **Kolbe** (sodium phenoxide with $\mathrm{CO_2}$ gives salicylic acid). Phenol gives a violet colour with neutral $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$.

## Ethers

Ethers are relatively unreactive. The **Williamson synthesis** makes them by $S_N2$ attack of an alkoxide on a **primary** alkyl halide:
$$\mathrm{RO^- + R'X \rightarrow ROR' + X^-}$$
Secondary and tertiary halides give elimination instead, so when one group is tertiary, the alkoxide must carry the bulky group.

Ethers are cleaved by $\mathrm{HI}$. With two primary or secondary groups, the halide attacks the **less hindered** carbon by $S_N2$. With a tertiary group, the reaction goes by $S_N1$ and the iodide ends up on the **tertiary** carbon.

## Quiz

1. What is the best way to prepare tert-butyl methyl ether by Williamson synthesis?
   - [x] $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CONa} + \mathrm{CH_3Br}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CH_3ONa} + \mathrm{(CH_3)_3CBr}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CH_3OH} + \mathrm{(CH_3)_3COH}$ with $\mathrm{NaOH}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CH_3Br} + \mathrm{(CH_3)_3COH}$
   > The alkyl halide must be primary for $S_N2$. A tertiary halide with an alkoxide gives mostly the alkene 2-methylpropene by E2, so the methyl group should come from $\mathrm{CH_3Br}$.

2. Which alcohol gives turbidity fastest in the Lucas test?
   - [ ] Butan-1-ol
   - [ ] Butan-2-ol
   - [ ] 2-Methylpropan-1-ol
   - [x] 2-Methylpropan-2-ol
   > The Lucas reaction proceeds through a carbocation, and the tertiary carbocation from 2-methylpropan-2-ol is by far the most stable, so the chloride forms immediately.

3. What are the main products when methyl tert-butyl ether, $\mathrm{CH_3OC(CH_3)_3}$, is treated with cold $\mathrm{HI}$?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CH_3I}$ and $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3COH}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CH_3I}$ and $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CI}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CI}$ and $\mathrm{CH_3OH}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CH_3OH}$ and $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3COH}$
   > Protonation of the ether oxygen lets the tert-butyl group leave as a stable tertiary carbocation ($S_N1$), which is captured by $\mathrm{I^-}$. Methanol remains. With an excess of HI, the methanol could later be converted to $\mathrm{CH_3I}$.
