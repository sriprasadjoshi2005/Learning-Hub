Two theories explain the shape, magnetism and colour of complexes.

## Valence bond theory (VBT)

The metal ion provides empty orbitals, which hybridise to accept electron pairs from ligands. Six ligands need $d^2sp^3$ (inner orbital, using $(n-1)d$) or $sp^3d^2$ (outer orbital, using $nd$). VBT links hybridisation to magnetism but cannot explain colour or the strength of ligand fields, so we mostly use crystal field theory.

## Crystal field theory (CFT)

Ligands are treated as point charges that repel the metal's $d$ electrons. In an **octahedral** field the five $d$ orbitals split into two sets:

- $t_{2g}$ ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{xz}$): **lower** energy, at $-0.4\Delta_o$ each.
- $e_g$ ($d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}$): **higher** energy, at $+0.6\Delta_o$ each.

The gap $\Delta_o$ depends on the ligand, following the **spectrochemical series**:

$$\mathrm{I^-} < \mathrm{Br^-} < \mathrm{Cl^-} < \mathrm{F^-} < \mathrm{OH^-} < \mathrm{H_2O} < \mathrm{NH_3} < \text{en} < \mathrm{CN^-} < \mathrm{CO}$$

For $d^4$ to $d^7$ ions, there is a choice. If $\Delta_o$ is **smaller** than the pairing energy $P$ (weak-field ligands), electrons spread out: **high spin**. If $\Delta_o > P$ (strong-field ligands), they pair up in $t_{2g}$ first: **low spin**.

**Crystal field stabilisation energy**, ignoring pairing energy, is
$$\text{CFSE} = (-0.4\,n_{t_{2g}} + 0.6\,n_{e_g})\,\Delta_o.$$

## Magnetism and colour

The spin-only moment is $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}$ BM, where $n$ is the number of unpaired electrons. Colour arises because a complex absorbs visible light to promote an electron from $t_{2g}$ to $e_g$, and we see the complementary colour. Ions with $d^0$ or $d^{10}$ configurations have no $d$-$d$ transition and are colourless. A larger $\Delta_o$ absorbs light of shorter wavelength.

## Tetrahedral fields

In tetrahedral complexes, the splitting $\Delta_t \approx \tfrac49\Delta_o$ is small, so tetrahedral complexes are almost always **high spin**.

## Quiz

1. What is the spin-only magnetic moment of $\mathrm{[Fe(CN)_6]^{3-}}$?
   - [ ] 0
   - [x] 1.73 BM
   - [ ] 3.87 BM
   - [ ] 5.92 BM
   > $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ is $d^5$. $\mathrm{CN^-}$ is a strong-field ligand, so the complex is low spin with configuration $t_{2g}^5e_g^0$: one unpaired electron, $\mu = \sqrt3 = 1.73$ BM. The high-spin value, 5.92 BM, applies to $\mathrm{[FeF_6]^{3-}}$.

2. What is the CFSE (ignoring pairing energy) of a high-spin octahedral $d^6$ complex?
   - [x] $-0.4\,\Delta_o$
   - [ ] $-2.4\,\Delta_o$
   - [ ] $-1.6\,\Delta_o$
   - [ ] $0$
   > High-spin $d^6$ is $t_{2g}^4e_g^2$: $4(-0.4) + 2(+0.6) = -0.4\,\Delta_o$. The value $-2.4\,\Delta_o$ is for the low-spin case ($t_{2g}^6$), and $-1.6\,\Delta_o$ forgets the two $e_g$ electrons.

3. Which of these hexaaqua ions is colourless?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{[Cu(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{[Ni(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{[Cr(H_2O)_6]^{3+}}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{[Zn(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}$
   > $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$ is $d^{10}$: the $d$ orbitals are full, so no $d$-$d$ transition is possible. $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ($d^9$) is blue, $\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}$ ($d^8$) green and $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$ ($d^3$) violet.
