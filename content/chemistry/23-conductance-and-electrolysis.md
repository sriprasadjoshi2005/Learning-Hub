## Conductance of electrolytic solutions

Ions carry current in a solution. **Conductivity** $\kappa$ (S cm$^{-1}$) is the conductance of a cube of solution 1 cm on each side. **Molar conductivity** accounts for how much electrolyte is present:

$$\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa \times 1000}{C}\ \ (\text{S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1},\ C \text{ in mol L}^{-1}).$$

As concentration falls, $\Lambda_m$ rises, because ions become more free to move (for strong electrolytes) and because more of a weak electrolyte ionises. At infinite dilution it reaches the limiting value $\Lambda_m^\circ$.

**Kohlrausch's law:** at infinite dilution each ion contributes independently to $\Lambda_m^\circ$. This lets us find the value for a weak electrolyte, which cannot be measured directly:

$$\Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{CH_3COOH}) = \Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{HCl}) + \Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{CH_3COONa}) - \Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{NaCl}).$$

The degree of dissociation of a weak electrolyte is then $\alpha = \Lambda_m/\Lambda_m^\circ$.

## Electrolysis and Faraday's laws

In an **electrolytic cell**, electrical energy drives a non-spontaneous reaction. Oxidation still occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode, but the anode is now the positive terminal.

**Faraday's first law:** the mass deposited is proportional to the charge passed. Using $Q = It$:

$$m = \frac{M}{zF}\,I\,t$$

where $z$ is the number of electrons per ion and $F = 96485$ C mol$^{-1}$. **Second law:** the masses deposited by the same charge are proportional to their equivalent masses.

## What is discharged?

At the cathode, the species that is easiest to reduce (the one with the **higher** reduction potential) is discharged first. At the anode, the species easiest to oxidise (the one with the **lower** reduction potential) goes first. In aqueous solutions water competes with the ions. In $\mathrm{Na_2SO_4(aq)}$ with inert electrodes, $\mathrm{Na^+}$ is too hard to reduce and sulfate too hard to oxidise, so water is split: $\mathrm{H_2}$ forms at the cathode and $\mathrm{O_2}$ at the anode.

## Quiz

1. A current of $2.00$ A is passed through aqueous $\mathrm{CuSO_4}$ for $30.0$ minutes. What mass of copper is deposited? ($M = 63.55$ g mol$^{-1}$)
   - [x] 1.19 g
   - [ ] 0.59 g
   - [ ] 2.37 g
   - [ ] 4.74 g
   > $Q = 2.00\times1800 = 3600$ C, which is $0.0373$ mol of electrons. Copper needs two electrons per atom, so $0.0187$ mol $\times63.55 = 1.19$ g. The value 2.37 g wrongly uses one electron per atom.

2. Given $\Lambda_m^\circ$ (S cm$^2$ mol$^{-1}$): $\mathrm{HCl}$ 426.2, $\mathrm{CH_3COONa}$ 91.0, $\mathrm{NaCl}$ 126.5. What is $\Lambda_m^\circ$ of acetic acid?
   - [ ] 299.7
   - [ ] 461.7
   - [ ] 643.7
   - [x] 390.7
   > $426.2 + 91.0 - 126.5 = 390.7$ S cm$^2$ mol$^{-1}$. The $\mathrm{Na^+}$ and $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ contributions cancel, leaving $\mathrm{H^+}$ and $\mathrm{CH_3COO^-}$.

3. What are the main products at the cathode and anode during the electrolysis of aqueous $\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}$ with inert electrodes?
   - [ ] Na at the cathode, $\mathrm{SO_2}$ at the anode
   - [ ] Na at the cathode, $\mathrm{O_2}$ at the anode
   - [x] $\mathrm{H_2}$ at the cathode, $\mathrm{O_2}$ at the anode
   - [ ] $\mathrm{H_2}$ at the cathode, $\mathrm{SO_2}$ at the anode
   > Water is easier to reduce than $\mathrm{Na^+}$ ($E^\circ = -2.71$ V) and easier to oxidise than $\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}$, so the solution simply loses water, and the concentration of the salt rises.
