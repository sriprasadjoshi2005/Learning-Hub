Both contain the carbonyl group, $\mathrm{C{=}O}$, in which carbon is $\delta^+$ and oxygen $\delta^-$. The carbonyl carbon is attacked by nucleophiles: **nucleophilic addition** is the signature reaction.

## Reactivity

Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones for two reasons. Electronically, a ketone has two electron-donating alkyl groups, which reduce the carbon's positive character. Sterically, the carbon of a ketone is more crowded. The order is

$$\mathrm{HCHO} > \mathrm{CH_3CHO} > \mathrm{CH_3COCH_3} > \mathrm{CH_3COC_6H_5} > \text{di-}\textit{tert}\text{-butyl ketone}.$$

Aromatic aldehydes are less reactive than aliphatic ones, because resonance with the ring reduces the positive charge on carbon.

## Characteristic reactions

- **Addition:** $\mathrm{HCN}$ gives cyanohydrins; Grignard reagents give alcohols; $\mathrm{NaHSO_3}$ gives bisulfite adducts.
- **Condensation:** with 2,4-DNP, hydroxylamine and hydrazine, giving coloured or crystalline derivatives used for identification.
- **Reduction:** $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$ or $\mathrm{LiAlH_4}$ gives alcohols; **Clemmensen** ($\mathrm{Zn/Hg}$, HCl) and **Wolff-Kishner** ($\mathrm{N_2H_4/KOH}$) reduce C=O to $\mathrm{CH_2}$.
- **Oxidation tests (aldehydes only):** Tollens' reagent (silver mirror) and Fehling's solution (red precipitate, for aliphatic aldehydes only). Ketones are not oxidised easily.

## $\alpha$-Hydrogen reactions

Hydrogens on the carbon next to C=O are acidic because the enolate is resonance-stabilised.

- **Aldol condensation:** two molecules of an aldehyde or ketone with an $\alpha$-hydrogen react in dilute base to give a $\beta$-hydroxy carbonyl compound, which can lose water to give an enone.
- **Cannizzaro reaction:** aldehydes with **no** $\alpha$-hydrogen (e.g. $\mathrm{HCHO}$, benzaldehyde) in concentrated base disproportionate into an alcohol and a carboxylate salt.
- **Haloform (iodoform) test:** compounds with a $\mathrm{CH_3CO-}$ group, or $\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)-}$ that oxidise to it, give yellow $\mathrm{CHI_3}$ with $\mathrm{I_2/NaOH}$.

## Quiz

1. Which compound gives a positive iodoform test?
   - [ ] Propan-1-ol
   - [x] Butan-2-ol
   - [ ] Pentan-3-one
   - [ ] Benzaldehyde
   > Butan-2-ol contains the $\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)-}$ unit, which iodine oxidises to $\mathrm{CH_3CO-}$ before the haloform reaction. Pentan-3-one has no methyl ketone group, and the others lack both units.

2. Which of the following undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction?
   - [x] Benzaldehyde
   - [ ] Ethanal
   - [ ] Propanone
   - [ ] Butanal
   > The Cannizzaro reaction needs an aldehyde with no $\alpha$-hydrogen. Benzaldehyde has none, whereas ethanal and butanal have $\alpha$-hydrogens and undergo aldol reactions instead. Propanone is a ketone.

3. Which sequence shows increasing reactivity toward nucleophilic addition?
   - [ ] Methanal < ethanal < propanone < di-tert-butyl ketone
   - [ ] Propanone < di-tert-butyl ketone < methanal < ethanal
   - [ ] Ethanal < methanal < di-tert-butyl ketone < propanone
   - [x] Di-tert-butyl ketone < propanone < ethanal < methanal
   > Less crowding and fewer electron-donating alkyl groups make the carbonyl carbon more reactive. Methanal has none of either, and the bulky ketone has the most of both.
