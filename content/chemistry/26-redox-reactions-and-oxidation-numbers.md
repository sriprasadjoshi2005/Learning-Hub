# Lesson 26: Redox Reactions and Oxidation Numbers

Oxidation-reduction reactions, commonly called redox reactions, involve the transfer of electrons or changes in oxidation states. They are fundamental to combustion, corrosion, electrochemical cells, metallurgy, respiration, and many industrial chemical processes.

## 1. Introduction to Redox Reactions

A redox reaction involves oxidation and reduction occurring simultaneously.

- Oxidation involves the loss of electrons.
- Reduction involves the gain of electrons.

Example:

\[
Zn+Cu^{2+}\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+Cu
\]

Zinc loses two electrons:

\[
Zn\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+2e^-
\]

Copper ions gain two electrons:

\[
Cu^{2+}+2e^-\rightarrow Cu
\]

Therefore:
- Zinc is oxidised.
- Copper ions are reduced.

## 2. Oxidation and Reduction

### 2.1 Oxidation

Oxidation can be identified through:
- Loss of electrons.
- Increase in oxidation number.
- Addition of oxygen.
- Removal of hydrogen.

Example:

\[
2Mg+O_2\rightarrow2MgO
\]

Magnesium is oxidised because its oxidation number increases from 0 to +2.

### 2.2 Reduction

Reduction can be identified through:
- Gain of electrons.
- Decrease in oxidation number.
- Removal of oxygen.
- Addition of hydrogen.

Example:

\[
CuO+H_2\rightarrow Cu+H_2O
\]

Copper changes from +2 in CuO to 0 in elemental copper.

## 3. Oxidation Number

Oxidation number is the hypothetical charge assigned to an atom when bonding electrons are assigned according to specified rules.

### 3.1 Rules for Assigning Oxidation Numbers

1. The oxidation number of an element in its free elemental form is zero.
2. The oxidation number of a monatomic ion equals its charge.
3. Group 1 metals generally have an oxidation number of +1.
4. Group 2 metals generally have an oxidation number of +2.
5. Fluorine has an oxidation number of -1 in its compounds.
6. Oxygen usually has an oxidation number of -2.
7. Hydrogen usually has an oxidation number of +1 with nonmetals and -1 in metal hydrides.
8. The sum of oxidation numbers in a neutral compound is zero.
9. The sum of oxidation numbers in a polyatomic ion equals its overall charge.

Important exceptions:
- Oxygen is -1 in peroxides such as H₂O₂.
- Oxygen is -1/2 per oxygen atom in superoxides such as KO₂.
- Oxygen can have positive oxidation numbers when bonded to fluorine.
- Hydrogen is -1 in compounds such as NaH.

### 3.2 Worked Example: Oxidation Number of Sulphur in H₂SO₄

Let the oxidation number of sulphur be x.

\[
2(+1)+x+4(-2)=0
\]

\[
2+x-8=0
\]

\[
\boxed{x=+6}
\]

### 3.3 Worked Example: Oxidation Number of Nitrogen in NO₃⁻

Let the oxidation number of nitrogen be x.

\[
x+3(-2)=-1
\]

\[
x-6=-1
\]

\[
\boxed{x=+5}
\]

## 4. Oxidising and Reducing Agents

An oxidising agent causes another substance to undergo oxidation.

It accepts electrons and is itself reduced.

A reducing agent causes another substance to undergo reduction.

It donates electrons and is itself oxidised.

For:

\[
Zn+Cu^{2+}\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+Cu
\]

- Zn is the reducing agent.
- Cu²⁺ is the oxidising agent.

## 5. Types of Redox Reactions

### 5.1 Combination Reactions

Two or more substances combine to form a product.

\[
2Mg+O_2\rightarrow2MgO
\]

### 5.2 Decomposition Reactions

A compound breaks into simpler substances.

\[
2HgO\rightarrow2Hg+O_2
\]

### 5.3 Displacement Reactions

One element displaces another element from a compound.

\[
Zn+CuSO_4\rightarrow ZnSO_4+Cu
\]

### 5.4 Disproportionation Reactions

The same element undergoes both oxidation and reduction.

Example:

\[
2H_2O_2\rightarrow2H_2O+O_2
\]

Oxygen in hydrogen peroxide has oxidation number -1.

It changes to:
- -2 in water.
- 0 in oxygen gas.

### 5.5 Comproportionation Reactions

Two forms of the same element with different oxidation numbers react to produce a form with an intermediate oxidation number.

## 6. Balancing Redox Reactions

Redox equations must satisfy both:
- Conservation of atoms.
- Conservation of electrical charge.

Two important methods are the oxidation-number method and the half-reaction method.

### 6.1 Oxidation-Number Method

General procedure:
1. Write the unbalanced equation.
2. Assign oxidation numbers.
3. Identify the atoms undergoing oxidation and reduction.
4. Calculate the total increase and decrease in oxidation numbers.
5. Choose coefficients so that the total increase equals the total decrease.
6. Balance the remaining atoms.
7. Check charge and atom conservation.

### 6.2 Half-Reaction Method

The reaction is separated into oxidation and reduction half-reactions.

For each half-reaction:
1. Balance all atoms except oxygen and hydrogen.
2. Balance oxygen using H₂O.
3. Balance hydrogen using H⁺ in acidic solution.
4. Balance charge using electrons.
5. Multiply the half-reactions to equalise electrons.
6. Add the half-reactions and cancel common species.

For basic solutions, add OH⁻ to both sides to neutralise H⁺, then simplify water molecules.

## 7. Redox Reactions in Everyday Life

- Rusting of iron.
- Combustion of fuels.
- Cellular respiration.
- Photosynthesis.
- Metal extraction.
- Batteries and electrochemical cells.
- Disinfection using oxidising agents.

## 8. Common Exam Traps

- Oxidation means an increase in oxidation number.
- Reduction means a decrease in oxidation number.
- The oxidising agent is itself reduced.
- The reducing agent is itself oxidised.
- The sum of oxidation numbers must equal the total charge.
- Oxygen is not always -2.
- Hydrogen is not always +1.
- A redox equation must conserve both atoms and charge.

## Quiz

1. What happens to the oxidation number of an atom during oxidation?
   - [ ] It decreases
   - [x] It increases
   - [ ] It always becomes zero
   - [ ] It remains unchanged

   > Oxidation involves electron loss and is associated with an increase in oxidation number.

2. Which species acts as the oxidising agent in the reaction \(Zn+Cu^{2+}\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+Cu\)?
   - [ ] Zn
   - [x] Cu²⁺
   - [ ] Zn²⁺
   - [ ] Cu

   > Cu²⁺ accepts electrons and is reduced, so it acts as the oxidising agent.

3. What is the oxidation number of sulphur in H₂SO₄?
   - [ ] +2
   - [ ] +4
   - [x] +6
   - [ ] -2

   > The total oxidation number must be zero: \(2(+1)+x+4(-2)=0\), giving sulphur an oxidation number of +6.
