**Stereoisomers** share the same connectivity but differ in the arrangement of atoms in space. Exams test three skills: finding stereocentres, counting isomers, and assigning $R/S$ and $E/Z$ labels.

## Chirality and optical activity

A carbon bonded to four **different** groups is a **stereocentre**. A molecule that is not superimposable on its mirror image is **chiral** and rotates plane-polarised light. Mirror-image pairs are **enantiomers**: identical in physical properties except for the direction of rotation, and they react differently only with other chiral substances. Stereoisomers that are not mirror images are **diastereomers**, with different physical properties. A 1:1 mixture of enantiomers is a **racemic mixture** and is optically inactive (external compensation).

A **meso compound** has stereocentres but also an internal plane of symmetry, so it is achiral and optically inactive (internal compensation).

## Counting stereoisomers

- With $n$ different stereocentres, there are at most $2^n$ stereoisomers.
- With **symmetry** (the same group at both ends), meso forms reduce the number: tartaric acid has 2 stereocentres but only **3** stereoisomers (a pair of enantiomers and one meso form).
- A double bond that can show $E/Z$ isomerism counts as an extra factor of 2.

## CIP priority rules ($R/S$)

1. Rank the four groups by the atomic number of the atom directly attached.
2. If tied, compare the next set of atoms outward, as a set (compare the highest first). For example, $-\mathrm{COOH}$ has $(\mathrm{O,O,O})$, $-\mathrm{CHO}$ has $(\mathrm{O,O,H})$, $-\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ has $(\mathrm{O,H,H})$ and $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ has $(\mathrm{H,H,H})$.
3. Treat a double bond as if the atom were duplicated.
4. View with the lowest priority group pointing away: clockwise $1 \to 2 \to 3$ is $R$, anticlockwise is $S$.

## Conformations

Rotation about a single bond gives conformers. For ethane the **staggered** form is more stable than the eclipsed form by about $12$ kJ mol$^{-1}$. For butane, the **anti** arrangement of the methyl groups is the most stable, with the **gauche** form about $3.8$ kJ mol$^{-1}$ higher and the fully eclipsed form the least stable.

## Quiz

1. How many stereoisomers does tartaric acid, $\mathrm{HOOC{-}CH(OH){-}CH(OH){-}COOH}$, have?
   - [ ] 2
   - [ ] 4
   - [x] 3
   - [ ] 6
   > It has two stereocentres, but they carry identical groups, so the $(R,S)$ form is a meso compound that is superimposable on its mirror image. The stereoisomers are $(R,R)$, $(S,S)$ and meso: three.

2. How many stereoisomers does pent-3-en-2-ol, $\mathrm{CH_3CH{=}CHCH(OH)CH_3}$, have?
   - [ ] 2
   - [x] 4
   - [ ] 3
   - [ ] 8
   > There is one stereocentre (the carbinol carbon, $2$ forms) and one double bond that can be $E$ or $Z$ ($2$ forms): $2\times2 = 4$.

3. Which sequence lists the groups in decreasing CIP priority?
   - [x] $-\mathrm{COOH} > -\mathrm{CHO} > -\mathrm{CH_2OH} > -\mathrm{CH_3}$
   - [ ] $-\mathrm{CHO} > -\mathrm{COOH} > -\mathrm{CH_2OH} > -\mathrm{CH_3}$
   - [ ] $-\mathrm{COOH} > -\mathrm{CH_2OH} > -\mathrm{CHO} > -\mathrm{CH_3}$
   - [ ] $-\mathrm{CH_2OH} > -\mathrm{CHO} > -\mathrm{COOH} > -\mathrm{CH_3}$
   > All start with carbon, so compare the sets attached to it: $\mathrm{COOH}$ is $(\mathrm{O,O,O})$, $\mathrm{CHO}$ is $(\mathrm{O,O,H})$, $\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ is $(\mathrm{O,H,H})$, $\mathrm{CH_3}$ is $(\mathrm{H,H,H})$. The aldehyde outranks the alcohol because the C=O is counted twice.
