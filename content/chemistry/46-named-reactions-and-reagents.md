title: Named Reactions and Reagents
level: exam
---

Entrance exams reward quick recognition of named reactions. The skill is to recall the **starting material, reagent and product** as one unit, and to know the common look-alikes.

## Making aldehydes and ketones

| Reaction | Starting material | Reagent | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rosenmund | acid chloride | $\mathrm{H_2}$, $\mathrm{Pd/BaSO_4}$ (poisoned) | aldehyde |
| Stephen | nitrile | $\mathrm{SnCl_2/HCl}$, then $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$ | aldehyde |
| Etard | toluene | $\mathrm{CrO_2Cl_2}$, then $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$ | benzaldehyde |
| Gattermann-Koch | benzene | $\mathrm{CO}$, $\mathrm{HCl}$, $\mathrm{AlCl_3/CuCl}$ | benzaldehyde |

## Carbon-carbon bond formation

- **Wurtz:** $\mathrm{2RX + 2Na} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \mathrm{R{-}R}$. A mixture of two different halides gives three alkanes ($\mathrm{R{-}R}$, $\mathrm{R'{-}R'}$ and $\mathrm{R{-}R'}$).
- **Wurtz-Fittig:** aryl halide + alkyl halide + Na gives an alkylbenzene.
- **Kolbe electrolysis:** electrolysis of sodium carboxylate gives an alkane ($\mathrm{R{-}R}$) at the anode.
- **Aldol, Cannizzaro, Friedel-Crafts, Grignard:** covered in earlier lessons.

## Halide exchange

- **Finkelstein:** $\mathrm{RCl/RBr + NaI} \rightarrow \mathrm{RI}$ in dry acetone. It works because $\mathrm{NaCl}$ and $\mathrm{NaBr}$ are insoluble in acetone and precipitate, driving the equilibrium.
- **Swarts:** $\mathrm{RCl} + \mathrm{AgF}$ (or $\mathrm{Hg_2F_2}$) gives $\mathrm{RF}$.
- **Sandmeyer and Gattermann:** diazonium salt to aryl halide (copper(I) salts for Sandmeyer, copper powder for Gattermann).

## Others to know

- **Reimer-Tiemann:** phenol with $\mathrm{CHCl_3/NaOH}$ gives salicylaldehyde. **Kolbe-Schmitt:** sodium phenoxide with $\mathrm{CO_2}$ gives salicylic acid.
- **Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky:** $\alpha$-halogenation of a carboxylic acid.
- **Hofmann bromamide:** amide to amine, one carbon fewer. **Gabriel:** primary aliphatic amine.
- **Clemmensen** (acidic) and **Wolff-Kishner** (basic) reduce $\mathrm{C{=}O}$ to $\mathrm{CH_2}$. Choose by whether the molecule tolerates acid or base.
- **Williamson:** alkoxide + primary alkyl halide gives an ether.

## Quiz

1. Which named reaction converts benzoyl chloride, $\mathrm{C_6H_5COCl}$, into benzaldehyde using $\mathrm{H_2}$ and $\mathrm{Pd/BaSO_4}$?
   - [ ] Stephen reaction
   - [ ] Etard reaction
   - [ ] Gattermann-Koch reaction
   - [x] Rosenmund reduction
   > The poisoned catalyst stops reduction at the aldehyde stage. Stephen starts from a nitrile, Etard from toluene, and Gattermann-Koch from benzene itself.

2. How many different alkanes can be formed when a mixture of $\mathrm{CH_3Br}$ and $\mathrm{C_2H_5Br}$ is treated with sodium in dry ether (Wurtz reaction)?
   - [ ] 1
   - [ ] 2
   - [x] 3
   - [ ] 4
   > Coupling of two methyl groups gives ethane, two ethyl groups give butane, and one of each gives propane: three alkanes, which is why the Wurtz reaction is poor for preparing unsymmetrical alkanes.

3. In the Finkelstein reaction, $\mathrm{RCl} + \mathrm{NaI} \rightarrow \mathrm{RI} + \mathrm{NaCl}$ goes to completion in dry acetone mainly because:
   - [ ] $\mathrm{I^-}$ is a weaker nucleophile than $\mathrm{Cl^-}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{NaCl}$ is insoluble in acetone and precipitates out
   - [ ] acetone acts as a catalyst
   - [ ] the C-I bond is stronger than the C-Cl bond
   > Removing $\mathrm{NaCl}$ as a solid shifts the equilibrium toward the products (Le Chatelier's principle). Iodide is actually a better nucleophile than chloride, and the C-I bond is weaker, not stronger.
