---
title: The Mole and Molar Mass
level: foundation
---

Atoms are far too small to count one by one, so chemists count them in bulk using the **mole**. One mole contains exactly $6.022\times10^{23}$ particles (the Avogadro constant, $N_A$), whether those particles are atoms, molecules or ions.

The key link between the lab balance and the particle count is **molar mass** $M$, the mass of one mole in g mol$^{-1}$. It is numerically equal to the relative atomic or molecular mass. For methane, $\mathrm{CH_4}$:

$$M = 12.01 + 4(1.008) = 16.04\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$$

Three relationships do almost all the work:

$$n = \frac{m}{M} \qquad N = n \times N_A$$

where $n$ is moles, $m$ is mass in grams and $N$ is the number of particles.

## Counting the right particles

Always ask what you are counting. One mole of $\mathrm{CH_4}$ molecules contains $N_A$ molecules but $5N_A$ atoms (one carbon and four hydrogens in each). Exam questions often ask for atoms when the amount is given in molecules, so multiply by the atoms per formula unit.

## Empirical and molecular formulae

The **empirical formula** is the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. To find it from percentage composition:

1. Treat the percentages as grams in a 100 g sample.
2. Divide each mass by its atomic mass to get moles.
3. Divide all by the smallest to get a ratio, and clear any fractions.

The **molecular formula** is a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula: divide the molar mass by the empirical formula mass to find the multiple.

## Quiz

1. How many atoms are in 4.00 g of methane, $\mathrm{CH_4}$ ($M=16.04$ g mol$^{-1}$)?
   - [ ] $1.50\times10^{23}$
   - [ ] $3.75\times10^{23}$
   - [ ] $1.20\times10^{24}$
   - [x] $7.51\times10^{23}$
   > $n=4.00/16.04=0.249$ mol of molecules, which is $1.50\times10^{23}$ molecules. Each has 5 atoms, giving $7.51\times10^{23}$ atoms. The value $1.50\times10^{23}$ counts molecules, not atoms.

2. A compound is 40.0% C, 6.7% H and 53.3% O by mass and has a relative molecular mass of 180. What is its molecular formula?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CH_2O}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{C_3H_6O_3}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O_5}$
   > Moles: C $3.33$, H $6.65$, O $3.33$, a ratio of $1:2:1$, so the empirical formula is $\mathrm{CH_2O}$ (mass 30.03). Then $180/30.03\approx6$, giving $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}$.

3. Which 10 g sample contains the greatest number of atoms?
   - [ ] $\mathrm{He}$
   - [x] $\mathrm{H_2}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{H_2O}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CH_4}$
   > Moles of atoms: He $10/4.00=2.5$; $\mathrm{H_2}$ $(10/2.016)\times2=9.9$; $\mathrm{H_2O}$ $(10/18.02)\times3=1.7$; $\mathrm{CH_4}$ $(10/16.04)\times5=3.1$. Hydrogen is so light that a given mass holds the most atoms.
