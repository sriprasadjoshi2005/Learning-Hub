# Lesson 48: Industrial Chemistry and Metallurgy

Industrial chemistry applies chemical principles to large-scale manufacturing. Metallurgy deals with extracting metals from ores, refining them, and preparing useful alloys. This lesson examines important industrial processes, their operating conditions, and the chemical principles behind metal extraction.

## 1. Industrial Chemical Processes

Industrial chemical production must balance:
- Reaction rate
- Chemical equilibrium
- Temperature and pressure
- Catalyst performance
- Raw-material availability
- Energy consumption
- Product separation
- Safety and environmental impact

The conditions that maximise laboratory yield are not necessarily the most economical industrial conditions.

## 2. Haber Process: Manufacture of Ammonia

Ammonia is manufactured from nitrogen and hydrogen.

\[
N_2(g)+3H_2(g)\rightleftharpoons2NH_3(g)
\]

### 2.1 Raw Materials

- Nitrogen is obtained mainly from air.
- Hydrogen is commonly obtained from natural gas or other suitable feedstocks.

### 2.2 Operating Conditions

Typical industrial conditions involve:
- High pressure, commonly around 150–250 atm.
- Moderate high temperature, commonly around 400–500°C.
- Finely divided iron-based catalyst with suitable promoters.

### 2.3 Effect of Conditions

The reaction is exothermic:

\[
\Delta H<0
\]

Lower temperature favours ammonia formation at equilibrium, but very low temperatures make the reaction too slow.

Higher pressure favours ammonia because the product side has fewer gas molecules.

The industrial conditions represent a compromise between equilibrium yield, reaction rate, cost, and safety.

### 2.4 Removal and Recycling

Ammonia is cooled and separated from unreacted gases.

Nitrogen and hydrogen are recycled to improve overall conversion.

## 3. Contact Process: Manufacture of Sulfuric Acid

Sulfuric acid is an important industrial chemical used in fertiliser production, petroleum refining, and chemical manufacturing.

### 3.1 Formation of Sulfur Dioxide

\[
S+O_2\rightarrow SO_2
\]

Sulfur dioxide can also be obtained by roasting suitable sulfide ores.

### 3.2 Oxidation to Sulfur Trioxide

\[
2SO_2+O_2\rightleftharpoons2SO_3
\]

The reaction is exothermic and is carried out using a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst.

Typical conditions involve a temperature around 400–450°C and a pressure near atmospheric pressure.

### 3.3 Absorption

Sulfur trioxide is absorbed in concentrated sulfuric acid to form oleum.

\[
SO_3+H_2SO_4\rightarrow H_2S_2O_7
\]

Oleum is then carefully diluted to produce sulfuric acid.

Direct absorption of SO₃ in water is avoided in the conventional process because it produces a difficult-to-handle acid mist.

## 4. Ostwald Process: Manufacture of Nitric Acid

The Ostwald process converts ammonia into nitric acid.

### 4.1 Oxidation of Ammonia

\[
4NH_3+5O_2\rightarrow4NO+6H_2O
\]

The reaction uses a platinum-rhodium catalyst at high temperature.

### 4.2 Oxidation of Nitric Oxide

\[
2NO+O_2\rightarrow2NO_2
\]

### 4.3 Absorption

Nitrogen dioxide is absorbed in water in the presence of oxygen to form nitric acid.

\[
4NO_2+O_2+2H_2O\rightarrow4HNO_3
\]

## 5. Solvay Process: Manufacture of Sodium Carbonate

The Solvay process uses brine, ammonia, and limestone to produce sodium carbonate.

### 5.1 Production of Carbon Dioxide

\[
CaCO_3\xrightarrow{heat}CaO+CO_2
\]

### 5.2 Precipitation of Sodium Hydrogen Carbonate

Carbon dioxide is passed through ammoniated brine.

\[
NaCl+NH_3+CO_2+H_2O
\rightarrow NaHCO_3+NH_4Cl
\]

Sodium hydrogen carbonate precipitates because of its relatively low solubility under the process conditions.

### 5.3 Formation of Sodium Carbonate

\[
2NaHCO_3\xrightarrow{heat}Na_2CO_3+CO_2+H_2O
\]

### 5.4 Ammonia Recovery

The calcium oxide produced from limestone is converted into calcium hydroxide.

\[
CaO+H_2O\rightarrow Ca(OH)_2
\]

Ammonia is recovered from ammonium chloride using calcium hydroxide.

This recycling makes the process economically attractive.

## 6. Metallurgy

Metallurgy is the science and technology of extracting, refining, and using metals.

The extraction method depends on:
- Chemical reactivity of the metal.
- Nature of the ore.
- Thermodynamic stability of the metal compound.
- Cost and energy requirements.

## 7. Concentration of Ores

Ore concentration removes unwanted earthy material called gangue.

### 7.1 Hydraulic Washing

This method separates materials based on differences in density.

### 7.2 Magnetic Separation

Magnetic properties are used to separate magnetic ore particles from nonmagnetic impurities or vice versa.

### 7.3 Froth Flotation

This method is especially useful for concentrating sulfide ores.

The ore particles attach to air bubbles and rise with the froth, while many impurities remain in the liquid.

### 7.4 Leaching

Leaching uses a suitable chemical solution to selectively dissolve the desired metal compound.

The metal is subsequently recovered from the solution.

## 8. Conversion of Concentrated Ore

### 8.1 Calcination

Calcination involves heating an ore in the absence or limited supply of air.

It is commonly used for carbonate and hydrated ores.

Example:

\[
ZnCO_3\xrightarrow{heat}ZnO+CO_2
\]

### 8.2 Roasting

Roasting involves heating an ore strongly in excess air or oxygen.

It is commonly used for sulfide ores.

Example:

\[
2ZnS+3O_2\rightarrow2ZnO+2SO_2
\]

### 8.3 Difference Between Calcination and Roasting

| Calcination | Roasting |
|---|---|
| Limited or no air | Excess air or oxygen |
| Often used for carbonates and hydrated ores | Often used for sulfide ores |
| May release CO₂ or water vapour | Often releases SO₂ |
| Thermal decomposition is important | Oxidation is important |

## 9. Reduction of Metal Oxides

Metal oxides can be reduced using carbon, carbon monoxide, hydrogen, or more reactive metals.

Example:

\[
Fe_2O_3+3CO\rightarrow2Fe+3CO_2
\]

The appropriate reducing agent depends on the stability of the oxide.

Highly reactive metals such as sodium and aluminium generally cannot be extracted economically by ordinary carbon reduction.

## 10. Extraction of Aluminium

Aluminium is extracted from purified alumina by electrolysis.

The Hall–Héroult process dissolves alumina in molten cryolite.

Cryolite lowers the operating temperature and improves electrical conductivity.

### 10.1 Cathode Reaction

\[
Al^{3+}+3e^-\rightarrow Al
\]

### 10.2 Anode Reaction

Oxide ions are oxidised at the carbon anode.

\[
2O^{2-}\rightarrow O_2+4e^-
\]

The oxygen reacts with the carbon anode, producing mainly carbon dioxide under normal operating conditions.

The carbon anodes are therefore gradually consumed.

## 11. Extraction of Iron in a Blast Furnace

Iron is extracted from iron ore using coke and limestone.

### 11.1 Formation of Carbon Monoxide

\[
C+O_2\rightarrow CO_2
\]

\[
CO_2+C\rightarrow2CO
\]

### 11.2 Reduction of Iron Oxide

\[
Fe_2O_3+3CO\rightarrow2Fe+3CO_2
\]

### 11.3 Formation of Slag

Limestone decomposes:

\[
CaCO_3\rightarrow CaO+CO_2
\]

Calcium oxide reacts with silica impurity:

\[
CaO+SiO_2\rightarrow CaSiO_3
\]

Calcium silicate forms slag, which can be separated from molten iron.

## 12. Electrochemical Extraction and Refining

Electrolysis is used to extract or purify certain metals.

In electrolytic refining:
- Impure metal forms the anode.
- Pure metal forms the cathode.
- A suitable electrolyte contains ions of the metal.
- The pure metal deposits at the cathode.
- Some impurities collect as anode sludge or remain in solution.

Copper is commonly purified by this method.

## 13. Alloys

An alloy is a metallic material containing two or more elements, at least one of which is a metal.

Examples:

| Alloy | Main components | Common use |
|---|---|---|
| Brass | Copper and zinc | Fittings and instruments |
| Bronze | Copper and tin | Bearings and decorative objects |
| Steel | Iron and carbon | Construction and machinery |
| Stainless steel | Iron, chromium, and often nickel | Corrosion-resistant equipment |
| Duralumin | Aluminium with copper and other elements | Lightweight structures |

Alloying can improve strength, hardness, corrosion resistance, and other properties.

## 14. Common Exam Traps

- Haber process produces ammonia and is exothermic.
- Higher pressure favours ammonia formation.
- The Contact Process uses V₂O₅ as a catalyst.
- The Ostwald Process begins with catalytic oxidation of ammonia.
- Froth flotation is particularly useful for sulfide ores.
- Calcination generally uses limited air; roasting uses excess air.
- Aluminium is extracted by electrolysis, not ordinary carbon reduction.
- Limestone in the blast furnace helps remove silica as slag.
- Cryolite lowers the melting temperature of the electrolyte.

## 15. Summary

- Industrial chemistry applies equilibrium, kinetics, catalysis, and separation methods on a large scale.
- The Haber, Contact, Ostwald, and Solvay processes manufacture important industrial chemicals.
- Metallurgy includes ore concentration, conversion, reduction, and refining.
- Electrolysis is essential for extracting highly reactive metals such as aluminium.
- Alloys are engineered to achieve useful combinations of physical and chemical properties.
