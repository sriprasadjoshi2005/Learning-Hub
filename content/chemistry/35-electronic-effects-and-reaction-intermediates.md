---
title: Electronic Effects and Reaction Intermediates
level: advanced
---

The behaviour of organic molecules depends on how electrons are distributed. Four effects matter.

## Electronic effects

- **Inductive effect** ($I$): permanent polarisation of $\sigma$ bonds by electronegativity, weakening with distance. Groups such as $-\mathrm{NO_2}$, $-\mathrm{CN}$, $-\mathrm{F}$, $-\mathrm{Cl}$ are electron-withdrawing ($-I$); alkyl groups are weakly donating ($+I$).
- **Resonance (mesomeric) effect** ($M$): delocalisation of $\pi$ electrons or lone pairs. $+M$ groups donate ($-\mathrm{OH}$, $-\mathrm{NH_2}$, halogens); $-M$ groups withdraw ($-\mathrm{NO_2}$, $-\mathrm{CHO}$, $-\mathrm{COOH}$).
- **Hyperconjugation:** donation from adjacent C-H $\sigma$ bonds into an empty or $\pi$ orbital. The more $\alpha$-hydrogens, the greater the stabilisation.
- **Electromeric effect:** a temporary shift of $\pi$ electrons to one atom in the presence of an attacking reagent.

## Reaction intermediates

| Intermediate | Description | Stability trend |
|---|---|---|
| Carbocation | positive carbon, $sp^2$, planar | $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{CH_3^+}$; resonance stabilisation (allyl, benzyl) helps |
| Carbanion | negative carbon with a lone pair | opposite: $\mathrm{CH_3^-} > 1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$ |
| Free radical | one unpaired electron | $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$ |
| Carbene | neutral carbon, two bonds, a lone pair | e.g. $:\mathrm{CCl_2}$ |

Alkyl groups donate electrons, which stabilises a carbocation but destabilises a carbanion.

## Acidity and basicity

An acid is stronger if its **conjugate base** is more stable. Electron-withdrawing groups stabilise the anion and raise acidity. For acetic acid derivatives, $\mathrm{p}K_a$ values are $\mathrm{CCl_3COOH}$ 0.65, $\mathrm{CHCl_2COOH}$ 1.29, $\mathrm{CH_2ClCOOH}$ 2.86 and $\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$ 4.76. Similarly, phenol ($\mathrm{p}K_a \approx 10$) is far more acidic than ethanol ($\approx 16$), because the phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance, and a $p$-nitro group ($-M$, $-I$) increases acidity further. Electron-donating groups reduce acidity.

## Quiz

1. Which sequence shows decreasing acid strength?
   - [x] $\mathrm{CCl_3COOH} > \mathrm{CHCl_2COOH} > \mathrm{CH_2ClCOOH} > \mathrm{CH_3COOH}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CH_3COOH} > \mathrm{CH_2ClCOOH} > \mathrm{CHCl_2COOH} > \mathrm{CCl_3COOH}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CCl_3COOH} > \mathrm{CH_3COOH} > \mathrm{CH_2ClCOOH} > \mathrm{CHCl_2COOH}$
   - [ ] $\mathrm{CHCl_2COOH} > \mathrm{CCl_3COOH} > \mathrm{CH_3COOH} > \mathrm{CH_2ClCOOH}$
   > Each chlorine exerts a $-I$ effect that stabilises the carboxylate anion, so more chlorine atoms mean a stronger acid. The first option has the order exactly reversed.

2. How many hyperconjugative structures can be drawn for the tert-butyl carbocation, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^+}$?
   - [ ] 3
   - [ ] 6
   - [ ] 12
   - [x] 9
   > Hyperconjugation counts the $\alpha$-hydrogens, which are the hydrogens on carbons attached to the positive carbon. Three methyl groups with three hydrogens each give 9, which helps explain why tertiary carbocations are so stable.

3. Which of the following correctly arranges the compounds in increasing order of acidity?
   - [ ] Phenol < ethanol < $p$-cresol < $p$-nitrophenol
   - [ ] $p$-Nitrophenol < phenol < $p$-cresol < ethanol
   - [x] Ethanol < $p$-cresol < phenol < $p$-nitrophenol
   - [ ] $p$-Cresol < ethanol < phenol < $p$-nitrophenol
   > Ethanol has no resonance stabilisation of its anion ($\mathrm{p}K_a \approx 16$). Phenol ($\approx 10$) is stabilised by the ring; the methyl group in $p$-cresol donates electrons and weakens the acid slightly; the $p$-nitro group withdraws electrons and strengthens it (about $7$).
