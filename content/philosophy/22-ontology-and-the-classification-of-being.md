# Lesson 22: Ontology and the Classification of Being

Ontology is the branch of metaphysics concerned specifically with being and existence. It investigates what kinds of things exist, how different entities relate to one another, and how existence can be classified. While metaphysics examines the broad nature of reality, ontology focuses particularly on the categories and structures of what exists.

## 1. Meaning of Ontology

The term ontology originates from the Greek words associated with being and the study of something.

Ontology asks fundamental questions:

- What kinds of things exist?
- Do abstract objects exist?
- Are numbers real?
- Do properties exist independently of the objects that possess them?
- What is the difference between an object and an event?
- What does it mean for something to exist?

Ontology attempts to develop a systematic account of the categories of existence.

## 2. Being and Existence

Being and existence are closely related philosophical concepts.

**Being** refers broadly to what it means for something to be.

**Existence** refers to the actuality or presence of an entity in some sense.

Consider the following examples:

- A physical tree exists as a concrete object.
- The number 5 is an abstract mathematical entity.
- A fictional dragon exists as a character within a story.
- A past historical event existed, although it is no longer occurring.

These examples raise questions about whether all forms of existence are equivalent.

## 3. Categories of Being

Ontology commonly distinguishes several broad categories.

### 3.1 Concrete Objects

Concrete objects exist in space and time or participate in physical reality.

Examples include:

- Trees
- Mountains
- Human beings
- Buildings
- Planets

They can generally be investigated through observation and scientific methods.

### 3.2 Abstract Objects

Abstract objects are not ordinarily understood as physical objects located in space and time.

Examples include:

- Numbers
- Mathematical relations
- Logical principles
- Some proposed universals

Philosophers disagree about whether abstract objects exist independently of human thought.

### 3.3 Properties

Properties are characteristics that objects or entities possess.

For example, a red apple has properties such as:

- Redness
- Roundness
- Sweetness
- Weight

An important ontological question is whether properties exist independently or only as features of particular objects.

### 3.4 Relations

Relations describe how entities are connected.

Examples include:

- Being taller than another person.
- Being located beside a building.
- Being the parent of another individual.
- Being mathematically greater than another number.

Relations are important because reality consists not only of individual objects but also of connections between them.

### 3.5 Events and Processes

Events are occurrences that take place, while processes involve developments or sequences of changes.

Examples:

- A volcanic eruption is an event.
- Biological growth is a process.
- A chemical reaction is an event or process depending on the level of description.

Ontology investigates whether events and processes are fundamentally different from objects.

## 4. Universals and Particulars

One of the most important debates in ontology concerns universals and particulars.

A **particular** is an individual entity.

A **universal** is a general property or characteristic that can be shared by multiple particulars.

For example, consider three red objects:

- A red apple
- A red car
- A red flower

The objects are particulars, while redness is a shared property.

### 4.1 Realism About Universals

Realism about universals maintains that universals have an objective existence that is not reducible to individual instances.

Plato's theory of Forms is an influential example of a philosophical approach that gives independent reality to universal Forms.

### 4.2 Nominalism

Nominalism rejects the idea that universals exist as independent entities.

According to nominalist approaches, general terms such as "redness" are names or concepts used to classify similar individual objects.

### 4.3 Conceptualism

Conceptualism maintains that universals exist as concepts in the mind rather than as independently existing abstract entities.

## 5. Substance and Attribute

The distinction between substance and attribute is central to classical ontology.

A **substance** is traditionally understood as something that exists in itself or serves as the underlying bearer of properties.

An **attribute** is a characteristic associated with a substance.

For example, a particular apple may possess:

- Colour
- Shape
- Mass
- Texture

The apple is the object, while these characteristics describe its properties.

Philosophers disagree about whether substances exist independently of their properties.

## 6. Ontological Dependence

Some entities may depend on other entities for their existence.

Examples include:

- A shadow depends on an object and a light source.
- A hole depends on the material surrounding it.
- A biological organism depends on certain physical and environmental conditions.
- A fictional character depends on a story or representation.

This raises the distinction between independent and dependent existence.

An entity is ontologically dependent when its existence requires another entity or set of conditions.

## 7. Ontological Commitments

An ontological commitment is a commitment to the existence of certain kinds of entities within a theory or explanation.

For example:

- A mathematical theory may appear committed to numbers.
- A scientific theory may be committed to electrons.
- A theory of society may refer to institutions and social structures.

The philosopher W. V. O. Quine argued that the ontological commitments of a theory can be investigated by examining what entities must exist for the theory's statements to be true.

## 8. Why Ontology Matters

Ontology has applications in:

- Mathematics and the philosophy of mathematics.
- Physics and scientific theories.
- Computer science and knowledge representation.
- Artificial intelligence and classification systems.
- Philosophy of mind.
- The study of identity and personal existence.

In computer science, an ontology can also refer to a formal representation of entities, categories and relationships within a particular domain.

## 9. Summary

Ontology studies being and existence. It classifies entities into categories such as concrete objects, abstract objects, properties, relations and events.

Important ontological debates concern universals and particulars, substance and attributes, independent and dependent existence, and the commitments made by philosophical and scientific theories.
