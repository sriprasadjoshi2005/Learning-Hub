# Philosophy syllabus plan (50 lessons, foundation to university entrance-exam level)

**Phase 1: Foundations and historical development of philosophy (Lessons 1–10):** Introduction to philosophy and its branches, nature and scope of philosophical inquiry, philosophical methods and critical thinking, history of ancient Western philosophy, Socrates and the Socratic method, Plato and the theory of Forms, Aristotle and his philosophical system, ancient Indian philosophical traditions, ancient Chinese philosophy and major schools of thought, and the development of medieval philosophy, including major contributions from Islamic and Christian philosophers.

**Phase 2: Knowledge, reality and logical reasoning (Lessons 11–20):** Introduction to epistemology and theories of knowledge, rationalism and empiricism, skepticism and the limits of knowledge, theories of truth and justification, introduction to metaphysics and fundamental questions of existence, idealism, materialism and dualism, philosophy of mind and the mind–body problem, formal logic and propositional reasoning, deductive and inductive arguments, and logical fallacies, critical reasoning and philosophical argumentation.

**Phase 3: Ethics, society and political philosophy (Lessons 21–30):** Introduction to ethics and moral philosophy, virtue ethics and Aristotle's conception of human flourishing, deontological ethics and Kant's moral philosophy, consequentialism and utilitarianism, moral relativism and moral objectivity, applied ethics and contemporary moral dilemmas, introduction to political philosophy and theories of the state, social contract theories of Hobbes, Locke and Rousseau, justice, liberty, equality and individual rights, and democracy, authority, power and contemporary political philosophy.

**Phase 4: Advanced philosophical theories and specialised branches (Lessons 31–40):** Political philosophy and theories of justice, social contract theory and the legitimacy of political authority, rights, liberty and equality, applied ethics and contemporary moral problems, environmental ethics and animal rights, philosophy of language and theories of meaning, hermeneutics and philosophical interpretation, existentialism and questions of freedom, anxiety and authenticity, phenomenology and the structures of lived experience, and postmodernism, relativism and critiques of grand narratives.

**Phase 5: Contemporary philosophy, interdisciplinary debates and comprehensive examination (Lessons 41–50):** Ancient and modern philosophical synthesis, metaphysics of mind and consciousness, philosophy of science and scientific methodology, philosophy of religion and arguments concerning the existence of God, advanced logic, argumentation and philosophical fallacies, aesthetics and the philosophy of art and beauty, philosophy of technology and artificial intelligence, metaphysics of time, space and causation, contemporary philosophical methods and debates, and integrated philosophical reasoning with comprehensive examination practice.

The sequence progresses from the foundations and historical development of philosophical thought to epistemology, metaphysics, logic, ethics, political philosophy, specialised philosophical branches and contemporary debates. It incorporates conceptual explanations, major philosophical theories, historical developments, influential thinkers, logical arguments, thought experiments, comparative analysis, critical interpretations, real-world applications and interdisciplinary discussions. Mathematical and symbolic concepts are introduced wherever necessary to develop an understanding of formal logic, propositional reasoning, argument structures and philosophical analysis.

The final lessons consolidate important areas that cannot be explored individually within the 50-lesson structure, including major philosophical traditions, theories of consciousness, the mind–body problem, scientific realism and falsifiability, paradigm shifts, arguments for and against the existence of God, the problem of evil, formal and informal fallacies, aesthetic theories, artificial intelligence and machine understanding, theories of time and causation, determinism and free will, feminist philosophy, comparative philosophy, contemporary ethical problems and integrated philosophical argumentation. This ensures that the curriculum provides a broad introduction to foundational and advanced philosophy without exceeding the 50-lesson limit.

The examination-oriented content is designed to develop the conceptual, analytical, logical, historical and critical foundations needed for university-level philosophy and related entrance examinations. It also supports preparation for philosophy-related topics in examinations such as CUET UG, CUET PG and other university entrance examinations, where applicable. Check the latest official syllabus for the examination being attempted; this is a broad philosophy learning pathway, not an official examination syllabus.
